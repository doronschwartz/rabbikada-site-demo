General Overview

The lesson introduces chapter 21 of the Moreh and examines five meanings of עבר before applying them to ויעבור ה׳ על פניו ויקרא. Rambam’s preferred interpretation is that Hashem diverts Moshe from trying to comprehend the divine essence and instead reveals the ways God acts in the world, expressed through the thirteen attributes of mercy. The lesson also presents two ways to understand Targum Onkelos’s reading, distinguishing a created manifestation of divine glory from a prophetic experience.

Background and Questions

The previous chapter explained that terms such as “high” and “exalted,” when applied to Hashem, describe greatness, authority, and power rather than physical location. Chapter 21 returns to Moshe’s request to see Hashem and the response that he may see Hashem’s “back” but not His “face.” The chapter will address what it means for Hashem to pass, whose face is meant, how to read the call of the divine name, and what the thirteen attributes of mercy represent.

Five Meanings of עבר

Rambam gives five meanings of עבר. It can mean physical movement from one place to another, as when Yaakov passes in front of his family; the transmission of sound, as in a public announcement or rumor; and a prophetic experience passing through a person’s mind, as in Avraham’s vision and the account of Hashem passing through Egypt. It can also mean overreaching a limit, or skipping over one target to divert attention toward another, as Rambam understands Yonatan’s arrow signal to David.

A participant asks whether aveirah means crossing a limit or diverting from the right path. The lesson leaves that question open for further consideration, noting that biblical phrases about failing to keep a commandment or covenant may help clarify the word’s meaning.

Rambam’s Preferred Interpretation

Rambam interprets עבר according to its meaning of diversion: Hashem diverts Moshe from seeking knowledge of the divine essence, which the verse calls Hashem’s face. Moshe instead receives knowledge of the divine actions manifested in the world, which the passage calls Hashem’s back. The thirteen attributes of mercy describe these manifestations; they are not separate parts of God.

Rambam reads פניו as referring to Hashem’s face and ויקרא השם as “Hashem called,” followed by the attributes of mercy. He connects this interpretation to his explanation in Mishneh Torah that no human being can comprehend God’s essence. He also explains that pursuing knowledge beyond human capacity may lead to madness or death, while divine assistance protects Moshe during the encounter.

Onkelos’s Interpretations

Onkelos commonly avoids attributing physical actions directly to God by adding a word that assigns the action to a created manifestation, such as God’s glory or presence. Accordingly, his translation of ויעבור ה׳ על פניו describes the Shechinah passing in Moshe’s presence, rather than Hashem Himself passing. In this reading, פניו refers to Moshe, and “on his face” means in his presence; ויקרא השם means that Hashem calls the attributes of mercy.

The lesson presents a second, prophetic reading of Onkelos’s wording. On this reading, the divine presence passes through Moshe’s mind in a prophetic experience rather than appearing as a physical manifestation. Rambam points to the verse about Yaakov’s gifts passing before him as an example of “before his face” meaning “in his presence,” and notes that the surrounding account describes a night in the camp.

Next Steps

The lesson concludes that the passage has three interpretations so far: Rambam’s reading of divine diversion, Onkelos’s reading of a created manifestation passing in Moshe’s presence, and a prophetic reading of that passing. The next lesson will present a fourth interpretation, compare all four readings, and revisit the meanings of עבר and related words.
