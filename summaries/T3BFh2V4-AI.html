General Overview

Rambam interprets the biblical image of God’s feet and the sapphire brick without attributing a body to God. Onkelos removes the verse’s anthropomorphic implications, while Rambam further reads the sapphire as primordial matter and the feet as its cause. The vision also points, in the Sforno’s interpretation, to human intellect becoming receptive to Torah.

The Three Meanings of Regel

The lesson recalls three meanings of regel: a physical leg or foot, a following, and a basis or cause. Rambam’s discussion continues from his account of Onkelos’s efforts to remove anthropomorphic descriptions of God.

Zechariah’s Vision and the Cause of Miracles

Rambam reads Zechariah’s statement that God’s feet will stand on the Mount of Olives as referring to the lasting manifestation of God’s causes: the miracles of the messianic era will show that God is their source. Yonatan ben Uziel reaches a similar interpretation by rendering God’s limbs as His might, though Rambam derives the meaning from regel as “cause” or “basis.”

Onkelos and the Sapphire Vision

In the verse from Mishpatim, Moshe, Aharon, Nadav, Avihu, and the elders see God and a sapphire brick beneath His feet. Onkelos renders “beneath His feet” as beneath the throne of His glory, which Rambam identifies with the created light of the Shechinah. This avoids both the image of God having feet and the implication that God physically sits on a throne.

Onkelos’s purpose is to remove corporeal readings, not to explain the meaning of the vision or the sapphire. Rambam says that such deeper interpretations are uncertain, difficult, and not fundamental to faith, so Onkelos does not pursue them. Rambam, by contrast, undertakes to explain the vision.

Primordial Matter and the Sapphire’s Transparency

Rambam interprets “beneath His feet” as “because of Him” or “on His basis,” using regel to mean cause. The elders therefore perceive that God is the cause of the first matter from which created things arise. This rejects Plato’s view that matter existed eternally alongside God; Rambam holds that God created primordial matter.

The phrase “the work of the sapphire” points to matter because matter is passive and receives form, while form is active. Rambam treats the sapphire’s whiteness as transparency rather than a color: transparency has no color of its own and can receive any color. Likewise, primordial matter has no form of its own and can receive different forms.

The Vision’s Meaning at Sinai

The lecturer recalls Rambam’s earlier account that Nadav, Avihu, and the elders were punished after entering too deeply into the vision, though the precise error is not specified. The vision’s setting at Sinai links God’s giving of the Torah with His role as Creator of all existence, including primordial matter.

The Sforno reads the transparent sapphire as an image of the human intellectual soul, which is ready to receive knowledge. At Sinai, the giving of Torah also prepares human beings to receive deeper understanding. The Maharal’s interpretation of the Messiah’s donkey similarly uses simplicity as a sign of receptivity; the lecturer connects this idea to the ability to change and accept what is new.
