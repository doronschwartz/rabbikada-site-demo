Topic: A Kohen who cannot pronounce the Hebrew letters correctly, and the importance of precise reading

Sources: Rambam; the Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; the Aharonim

Daily Halacha: Birkat Kohanim & Prayer, Wednesday 22 June 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Rambam lists six things that prevent a Kohen from blessing. One, based on the Gemara and codified in the Shulhan Arukh, is being unable to pronounce the letters correctly.
  • His examples: confusing alef and ayin, het and hey, het and khaf, or shin and sin.
  • One who blesses the people must say the words correctly.
  • Many people, especially from European countries, could not distinguish these letters (shin and sin less so), which was a major problem for the Ashkenazi poskim.
  • The Aharonim wrote that where the whole community pronounces this way it is not an issue, and this is the accepted ruling among Ashkenazim.
  • But that is clearly not ideal: one who can learn to pronounce ayin and het properly should do so, since by the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh this disqualifies a Kohen.
  • The lesson applies to all of us: read precisely, giving our prayers, and especially Birkat Kohanim, the honour they need.

In one line

A Kohen should pronounce every letter correctly to bless; the leniency for whole communities is no reason not to learn.
