Topic: May a visitor keeping the second day in Israel ask an Israeli to do melakhah for him?

Sources: Shulhan Arukh, Hilchot Shabbat; Rav Shelomoh Zalman Auerbach; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Thursday 17 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • In conclusion of the previous discussions: one visiting Israel for Yom Tov, not spending the three festivals there and not owning property, who intends to return, keeps two days according to the general consensus.
  • On his second day, while Israelis are working, may he ask an Israeli Jew to do melakhah for him?
  • Strict view: forbidden, because between Jews there is shelihut (agency), so it is as if he did it himself. Asking a non-Jew is only rabbinically forbidden, since there is no true agency with a non-Jew. By this view one may not ask a Jew even for a misvah.
  • Lenient view: permitted. The Shulhan Arukh rules, and all accept, that one who accepted Shabbat early (in an English summer, at 7.30 when sunset is around 9.15) may ask a Jew who has not yet accepted Shabbat to do work for him, since accepting early was voluntary. Rav Shelomoh Zalman argues similarly: keeping two days rests on a minhag, and one could make aliyah and keep one day. By this view, which he gives as that of Hakham Ovadiah, one may ask even for a Torah-level melakhah and even for pure pleasure.
  • Middle view: as with a non-Jew, one may ask only for a rabbinic prohibition and only for the sake of a misvah.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh, Hilchot Shabbat: one who accepted Shabbat early may ask a Jew who has not to do melakhah for him.
  • Rav Shelomoh Zalman Auerbach: the same reasoning applies to the second day, which rests on a minhag one could leave.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: the lenient view.

In one line

There are three views on asking an Israeli to do melakhah on one's second day: never, only rabbinic melakhah for a misvah, or freely.
