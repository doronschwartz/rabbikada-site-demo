Topic: How much to give for Mahasit HaShekel, and on whose behalf

Sources: The Torah, Parashat Ki Tisa; the Rema; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Purim, Tuesday 8 March 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • It may be given from 1 Adar, and at the latest by the night of Purim. Though the Shulhan Arukh does not record it, Sepharadim also observe it.
  • The Rema: give half the local coin (50p in England, half a dollar in America, half a shekel in Israel). The custom is three halves, since "terumah" appears three times in the portion, so Ashkenazim give £1.50.
  • Sepharadim follow a custom closer to the original: Parashat Ki Tisa sets a shekel at 20 gerah, so half is 10 gerah, about 9 grams of silver. At about 60p a gram, that is roughly £5.40 to £5.50, assessed each year around Purim.
  • The Torah says "from twenty years and above", so the Rema limits it to age 20. Many say that was for the Mishkan, while the annual donation was from 13.
  • Later poskim add women, children (parents give for them) and even a fetus. A family of two parents, three children under bar or bat misvah and a pregnant mother gives six halves. Biblically only the father gave, but since the half shekel atones for the soul, it is customary for the whole family.
  • It is a custom, so one should not overstretch. Hakham Ovadiah writes that if it is difficult, 50p is good enough.

Sources quoted

  • Parashat Ki Tisa: a shekel is 20 gerah; from age 20.
  • The Rema: half the local coin, customarily three; from age 20.
  • Hakham Ovadiah: if difficult, 50p suffices.

In one line

Sepharadim give about 9 grams of silver (or 50p if hard) for each family member; Ashkenazim three half coins.
