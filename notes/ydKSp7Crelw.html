Topic: The prohibitions of greeting and of Torah study on Tisha BeAv

Sources: The Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Monday 24 July 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Seven things are forbidden on Tisha BeAv: the five of Yom Kippur, Torah study and greeting.
  • Like a mourner during shivah, we do not greet each other ("Shalom Aleikhem", "how are you"). The Rambam writes this of talmidei hakhamim, but most commentators read it as applying to all.
  • The Shulhan Arukh notes not everyone knows this law: if someone unaware greets you and would be offended, respond in a subdued way. If they are open to learning, explain the halacha.
  • Compare greeting before Shaharit, forbidden every day: a talmid hakham once answered his greeting with "Shalom Aleikhem, you are not allowed to say that before Shaharit", returning the greeting and teaching halacha at once.
  • This applies all day; only customs, like sitting on the ground, end at midday.
  • Hakham Ovadiah rules that strictly "good morning" or "good night" is permitted, though better avoided; if needed, say it subdued. Wishing "Mazal Tov" or "BeSiman Tov" on a recent simhah is a blessing, not a greeting, and one may even shake hands.
  • Torah study is forbidden all day because it gladdens the heart, as for a mourner. Sad parts are permitted: the destruction in Yirmiyahu, the book of Iyov (some communities read it all), and Talmud passages on the destruction.

Sources quoted

  • The Rambam: the prohibition of greeting, written of talmidei hakhamim.
  • Shulhan Arukh: answer one who does not know the law, in a subdued way.
  • Hakham Ovadiah: "good morning" and "good night" strictly permitted.

In one line

On Tisha BeAv one neither greets nor studies Torah all day, answers the unaware gently, and studies only the sad parts of Torah.
