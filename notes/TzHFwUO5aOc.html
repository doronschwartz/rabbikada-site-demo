Topic: The separate obligation to be married, and what fulfils "Peru urevu"

Sources: Bereshit ("It is not good for man to be alone"); the Rambam; the Mishnah (Beit Hillel and Beit Shammai); Shulhan Arukh; the Gemara on converts; the Minhat Hinukh; Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 20 January 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Besides the Torah misvah to have children, there is an independent obligation for a man to be married, even if he cannot have children or already has them. Hazal learn it from "It is not good for man to be alone". For the Rambam and most authorities it is rabbinic.
  • So far two obligations: having children (Torah) and being married (rabbinic). A third follows later. The Shulhan Arukh: a man should never be without a wife.
  • Beit Hillel: the misvah is one son and one daughter, as in "male and female He created them". Beit Shammai: two sons, like Moshe. The halacha follows Beit Hillel.
  • Is the misvah the result (children in the world) or the act of trying to have them? The Gemara rules that a convert who had a son and a daughter before converting has fulfilled it.
  • From this the Minhat Hinukh concludes the misvah is the result. Rav Moshe Feinstein holds it is the effort: one who tries and is not granted a son and a daughter is like anyone prevented from a misvah, and he cites "Did you engage in" being asked after death.

In one line

A man is obliged both to have a son and a daughter and, rabbinically, to be married; whether the misvah lies in the result or the effort is debated.
