Topic: Whether a doubt about the Megillah, a misvah of divrei kabbalah, is treated strictly like a Torah law or leniently like a rabbinic one

Sources: The Rashba; the Ritva; the Rishonim (a child reading for an adult); the Mishnah Berurah; the Aharonim

Daily Halacha: Purim, Thursday 6 March 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The basic principles: Safek deOraita lehumra, a doubt in a Torah misvah is treated strictly; Safek deRabbanan lekula, a doubt in a rabbinic misvah leniently.
  • In between are divrei kabbalah, misvot from the Neviim, the classic case being reading the Megillah on Purim day. One must hear every word; if one knows one missed words one must hear it again. The question is a genuine doubt: whether one heard a word, or whether one dozed off.
  • The Rishonim treat a doubt in divrei kabbalah leniently. The Rashba, the Ritva and others write that in a place where it is doubtful whether to read on the 14th or the 15th, strictly one need not read at all, since it is a rabbinic doubt; we read anyway as a pious practice.
  • The Rishonim ask why a child who has reached hinukh cannot read for an adult, and answer, for example, that the child's obligation is doubly rabbinic. Nobody answered that the Megillah has Torah status, and some even ruled (we do not follow this) that a child may read for an adult. So it is a prophetic misvah with rabbinic status.
  • Many poskim follow this. The Mishnah Berurah was stringent, following later Aharonim who take into account the view that divrei kabbalah have Torah status.
  • Strictly, the halacha is that one may be lenient with a doubt about the daytime Megillah, though in practice many say to read it again. At night, which is almost certainly rabbinic (the main misvah of Mordekhai and Esther was by day), all agree one may be lenient.

Sources quoted

In one line

A doubt about the Megillah is in principle treated leniently, even by day, though many are stringent and read again by day.
