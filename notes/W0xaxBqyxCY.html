Topic: How "Israel" and "outside Israel" are defined for keeping one or two days of Yom Tov, and the case of Eilat

Sources: Rambam; the Ritva; Parashat Masei; Melakhim (Shelomoh HaMelekh); Rav Herzog

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Thursday 3 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Rambam had a novel approach, not generally accepted: it depends on where the messengers arrived in time, not on the borders. A place within 10 days of travel to which messengers used to go keeps one day; a place beyond that, or one the messengers never reached, keeps two, even inside Israel.
  • That would lead to dramatic results: Eilat in the southern Negev, which messengers almost certainly never reached, would keep two days, while Damascus or places in Jordan that messengers did reach would keep one.
  • The other Rishonim held that once the calendar was fixed and the minhag continued, it was set by the borders of Israel: inside, one day; outside, two, including Damascus and Jordan. This became the majority view and is generally how we approach it.
  • Eilat is a question even on this view, since it was not within the borders described in Parashat Masei. Some poskim hold that one spending a festival there must keep two days.
  • Rav Herzog and others held one day in Eilat, for reasons including the sovereignty of the State of Israel, and that Shelomoh HaMelekh captured it, as stated in Melakhim, so it may have become part of Israel later.
  • To his knowledge some communities in Eilat keep one day and some two. One who goes should consult and find out the correct approach.
  • Places like Syria, even if historically reached by the messengers, kept two days.

Sources quoted

  • Rambam: the criterion is a place within 10 days to which messengers went.
  • The Ritva and the other Rishonim: the criterion is the borders of Israel.
  • Parashat Masei: the borders, which did not include Eilat.
  • Melakhim: Shelomoh HaMelekh captured Eilat.
  • Rav Herzog: one day in Eilat.

In one line

Today one day is kept within the borders of Israel and two outside, with Eilat a matter of dispute on which one should ask.
