Topic: Must someone who says they are Jewish bring proof? Two stories from the Gemara

Sources: Gemara Pesahim (the start of the tractate); the Gemara (a case brought to Rava)

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 24 February 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The question: if someone, say from a Middle Eastern country, arrives in England, America or Australia, knows the practices and says they are Jewish, can we accept it and allow marriage into the community? We will go from the Gemara through the Rishonim to the practice of Batei Din today.
  • Story one: a non-Jew boasted that each year he went up to Yerushalayim and ate from the Korban Pesah, which the Torah forbids to a non-Jew since it is a covenant with Israel. Rabbi Yehudah, living in the north, asked if he had been given the alyah, the fat tail, and told him to ask for it next time.
  • The Kohanim knew the alyah goes on the Mizbeah, asked who sent him, investigated and found he was not Jewish. The Gemara praises Rabbi Yehudah for catching him in Yerushalayim from the north.
  • Until then he was simply given the Korban Pesah with no proof asked. All agree this shows no verification was needed for the Korban Pesah; marriage may be different, which will be discussed.
  • Story two: a man whose father was not Jewish but mother was, and so Jewish, could not find a match because of his lineage. Rava told him either to marry someone like himself or to move to another city.
  • Moving only helps if he would be accepted there as Jewish on his word, so even for marriage a person's statement seems to be accepted.

Sources quoted

  • Gemara Pesahim: the non-Jew who ate from the Korban Pesah until Rabbi Yehudah exposed him.
  • Rava: "marry someone like you, or move to another place".

In one line

Both stories suggest a person who presents as Jewish was accepted without proof, though marriage may differ, as the next classes discuss.
