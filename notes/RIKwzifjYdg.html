Topic: When and how to dispose of Hames, in a year when Erev Pesah falls on Shabbat

Sources: The Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Tuesday 23 March 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Eating Hames during the seven days of Pesah carries Karet, which in the Rambam's philosophy means one has no existence in the World to Come.
  • From midday on Erev Pesah, when the Korban Pesah was offered, the Torah forbids eating, benefiting from or owning Hames, but without Karet.
  • Rabbinically one stops eating two hours before midday (end of the fourth hour), and must dispose of Hames one hour before midday, since owning is less severe than eating.
  • This year Erev Pesah is Shabbat. Hames may be eaten all Friday and on Shabbat morning until about 10 am in England.
  • Strictly one may dispose of Hames all Friday, as it is not Erev Pesah, but the Shulhan Arukh says it is good to do so at the usual time, about 11 am this year, so as not to be confused in other years. If not possible, one has the rest of the day.
  • Keep only the minimum needed for Friday night and Shabbat morning.
  • For Sepharadim, following the Shulhan Arukh, ordinary disposal suffices, such as throwing into the sea or flushing down the toilet; no burning is needed. The Rema has Ashkenazim burn it, but leftovers on Shabbat morning are not burnt this year, relying on the main halacha.
  • Sale of Hames contracts are made before 11 am on Friday but should state they cover Hames owned until just before Shabbat; check your contract.

Sources quoted

  • The Rambam: Karet means ceasing to exist in the World to Come.
  • Shulhan Arukh: when Erev Pesah is Shabbat, it is good to dispose of Hames by the usual time on Friday; burning is not required.
  • The Rema: Ashkenazim burn Hames.

In one line

This year eat Hames until about 10 am on Shabbat, dispose of the rest by about 11 am on Friday if possible, and simply discard any leftovers.
