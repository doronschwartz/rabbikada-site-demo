Topic: What someone who must sleep before the end of a long summer Shabbat does about Havdalah

Sources: The Rambam; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Tuesday 22 June 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The longest Shabbatot end around 10:30pm in London and after 11pm further north. There is nothing wrong with going to sleep early, but what of Havdalah?
  • According to the Rambam Havdalah is a Torah misvah, marking the end of Shabbat as Kiddush marks its start. Its core is Bore Peri HaGefen and HaMavdil; the berakhot on fire and spices are not intrinsic.
  • The Shulhan Arukh allows, in extenuating circumstances, Havdalah from Plag HaMinhah (about 7:30 now in London), for one who must set off straight after Shabbat for a misvah. No candle is lit, since all melakhah remains forbidden until Shabbat ends.
  • The later authorities are uneasy, fearing one may think Shabbat is over and do melakhah, and say to rely on it only when there is no other option.
  • Contemporary poskim differ for one going to sleep: some say wait and make full Havdalah on Sunday morning; others compare it to the Shulhan Arukh's case. Both are acceptable.
  • His view: with family who are still keeping Shabbat, early Havdalah looks bad, so better wait for Sunday. Alone and going straight to sleep, one may make Havdalah after Plag HaMinhah.
  • One must do no melakhah until Shabbat ends. Havdalah on Sunday morning is without the candle and spices.

In one line

One who must sleep early may make Havdalah on Sunday morning, or when alone after Plag HaMinhah and then go straight to bed.
