Topic: The two readings of the Megillah, why the daytime reading is primary, and Shehehiyanu by day

Sources: The Gemara; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; Tosafot; the Aharonim; the Rama

Daily Halacha: Purim, Monday 27 February 2023

The halacha

  • Of the four misvot of Purim, the Megillah is primary. It is a misconception that one reading suffices: the Gemara cites two verses requiring it at night and by day, as recorded in the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh, whereas Mishloah Manot and gifts to the poor are done once, by day.
  • Several Rishonim write that the daytime reading is more important. Tosafot and others give reasons: the other misvot of Purim can only be done by day; publicising the miracle is greater by day; and the victory in the war happened by day.
  • Some Aharonim distinguish further. The Megillah cannot be biblical, since the story came long after the Torah, but there is a middle category of misvot mandated by the Neviim, before the time of the Hakhamim. The night reading is rabbinic, a preparation, while the day reading was mandated by the Neviim, Mordekhai and Esther.
  • One must try to hear both. If only one is possible, for example because of a work shift, some poskim say to take the first misvah that comes, the night reading. But the ruling we follow is that one should hear the daytime reading, the primary obligation.
  • It is a misconception that the main reading is at night; people should come by day too.
  • At night three berakhot are said, including Shehehiyanu. The Shulhan Arukh rules not to repeat Shehehiyanu by day, relying on the night, and that is the Sepharadi minhag; the Rama, for Ashkenazim, repeats it by day since the main misvah is by day. This does not detract from the day reading being primary.

In one line

Hear the Megillah both night and day, and if you can manage only one, choose the daytime reading, the primary obligation.
