Topic: What the Hashmonaim achieved, why their dynasty is not celebrated, and what Hanukkah commemorates

Sources: Gemara Bava Batra; the Torah on kingship for Yehudah; Rambam; "Ki ner misvah veTorah or"

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Hashmonai dynasty was overtaken by its own servants led by Herod. The Gemara in Bava Batra says anyone claiming Hashmonai descent is either of slave lineage or a liar.
  • They were criticised for taking both the High Priesthood and the kingship, which the Torah reserves for Yehudah. So we celebrate their achievements, not the dynasty.
  • The miracle did not last politically: the struggles continued, the dynasty broke down, and about 200 years later the Romans destroyed the Bet HaMikdash.
  • What endures are the values they fought for: defeating the Hellenists and the Seddukim, and setting the boundaries of the Torah Shebeal Peh. The 36 candles of the eight nights (without the shamash) match the 36 tractates of Shas.
  • This is why the Gemara calls the misvah of Hanukkah everlasting: without their fight, rabbinic law and the framework of the Hakhamim would have been lost. The light of the Menorah represents the Torah, specifically the Oral Torah.
  • Rambam stresses the political independence they brought for a couple of hundred years together with the establishment of the Oral Law.
  • Hanukkah was a cultural and spiritual war, not only military, so it is marked with Hallel and thanksgiving, not with festive meals as on Purim.

In one line

On Hanukkah we celebrate not the Hashmonai dynasty but the Torah Shebeal Peh and independence they secured.
