Topic: Switching on incandescent (heat-generating) lights on Yom Tov, and dimmers

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Thursday 5 June 2025

The halacha

  • So far: on Yom Tov one may increase a fire, or light a new one from an existing flame, but normally not extinguish. Electricity divides into lights generated by heat, such as incandescent filament bulbs (today), and other electricity such as LEDs (part two).
  • On Shabbat, switching an incandescent bulb on or off is a Torah prohibition, since it becomes red hot: bishul, or more precisely mavir, lighting a new fire.
  • On Yom Tov it seemingly should be forbidden, since it creates a new fire rather than drawing from an existing flame. This is indeed the halacha of the vast majority of poskim.
  • Some poskim permitted it, for two reasons. Rav Uziel argued the potential of the fire is already present in the wires, so switching it on is like lighting from an existing flame rather than striking a match. Others argued it is grama, indirect, which is generally permitted on Yom Tov.
  • The vast majority, including Hakham Ovadiah, rule it is forbidden to switch on an incandescent light on Yom Tov, and that is generally the ruling followed.
  • According to everyone one may turn up a dimmer on Yom Tov, since the light is already on and one is only increasing an existing fire. One may not turn it down, since lowering is a form of extinguishing.
  • Next: electricity not generated through heat, and the customs of the different communities.

Do not switch on an incandescent light on Yom Tov, but a dimmer already on may be turned up, not down.
