Topic: Whether a non-Kohen may bless with the words of Birkat Kohanim, as parents and rabbanim do

Sources: Bemidbar 6 ("Koh tevarekhu"); the Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Birkat Kohanim & Prayer, Wednesday 15 June 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara reserves Birkat Kohanim to Kohanim: from "koh tevarekhu", "you" (the Kohanim) shall bless, and not a Yisrael. The Shulhan Arukh rules that a non-Kohen who does so violates a Torah commandment.
  • Yet parents widely bless their children, and rabbanim their students, with "Yevarekhekha". Being outside the synagogue does not help, since Birkat Kohanim is not intrinsically part of tefillah; the Hakhamim merely placed it in the repetition of the Amidah.
  • One opinion, about 400 years ago: it is permitted because it is done with one hand on the child's head, not with hands outstretched like the Kohanim. But the poskim write that it is better to bless with both hands.
  • The other view, which Hakham Ovadiah rules: when blessing children or students one is not performing the misvah of Birkat Kohanim, only borrowing the Torah's beautiful words.
  • Saying the words with outstretched hands intending the misvah, for example standing up with the Kohanim, would violate the prohibition.
  • So one may bless one's children with these words, even with hands on their head.

In one line

A non-Kohen may bless with the words of Birkat Kohanim, as long as he does not intend to perform the Kohanim's misvah.
