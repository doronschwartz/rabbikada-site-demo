Topic: Books and the garden in the search for Hames, and the custom of the ten pieces

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Wednesday 6 April 2022

The halacha

  • Books need not be cleared of crumbs, since we search for pieces of Hames. Books used with food (at the table or in the kitchen) must be checked even for crumbs, which is why one is advised not to use the year-round Birkat HaMazon booklets on Pesah unless every crumb is removed.
  • A garden where Hames is regularly brought needs a cursory search for pieces of a kezayit, since it is part of one's property.
  • The custom of putting out ten pieces of Hames before the search is old. The Raavad of Provence (12th century) called it a custom of the unlearned: the search begins the disposal, as the Berakhah Al Biur Hames shows, so it is a Misvah even if nothing is found. The Hakham Svi of Amsterdam also opposed it.
  • Some argue that today, when the house is cleaned well before the night of the search, the pieces prevent the search from being in vain. Our custom is to put them out; the number ten is from the Arizal, followed by many Sepharadim and Ashkenazim.
  • Each piece should be less than a kezayit, so that if one is lost there is no need to turn the house upside down, as one can rely on the nullification.
  • The search must not become a hunt for the ten pieces. One searches the house for Hames and finds the pieces along the way; if the pieces become the whole purpose, it is better not to put them out.

Sources quoted

  • The Raavad: the custom is unnecessary, since the Misvah is to search and dispose, not to find.
  • The Hakham Svi: also against the custom.
  • The Arizal: the source for putting out specifically ten pieces.

In one line

Put out ten pieces of less than a kezayit each, but search the whole house rather than just hunting for them.
