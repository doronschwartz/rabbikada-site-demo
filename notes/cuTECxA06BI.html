Siman 447 (Continued) – Mixtures of Hames on Pesah

Recap of Key Principles from Last Week

  1. Be-mashehu on Pesah: During the year, lach be-lach (liquid mixtures) are nullified at 1 in 60. On Pesah, even 1 in 1,000 is not nullified (be-mashehu) — this is de-rabbanan (Rabbeinu Tam disagrees entirely).
  2. Two reasons for this chumra: (a) Severity of hames (karet). (b) Davar she-yesh lo matirin — hames will become permitted after Pesah, so don't rely on nullification now.
  3. Two hot solids touching: Kedei netilah / kedei klipah still applies on Pesah — no change from the rest of the year. Be-mashehu only applies where you'd normally need 1 in 60.
  4. Timing: Be-mashehu only applies to mixtures that occurred during Pesah itself. Mixtures from before Pesah (even from hasot on Erev Pesah) follow the regular 1 in 60 rule.

Se'if 4 – Does Nullified Hames "Reawaken" on Pesah? (חוזר וניעור)

This is one of the most consequential halachot for everyday Pesah observance.

The scenario: Hames got mixed into non-hames before Pesah, and there was 60 times more non-hames — so it was properly nullified according to regular kashrut rules. Now Pesah arrives. Does the nullified hames "come alive again" (chozer ve-ne'or) and become prohibited?

Two Opinions in the Shulhan Arukh

Opinion

Position

Who Holds It

Ramification

Stam (default opinion)

It does NOT reawaken. Once nullified before Pesah, it stays nullified throughout Pesah. You may eat the food on Pesah.

Rosh, Rashba, and others

More lenient — food nullified before Pesah is permitted on Pesah

Yesh Cholkim (some disagree)

It DOES reawaken. When Pesah begins, the nullified hames becomes active again and prohibits the mixture.

Understood to be the Rambam (as interpreted by the Ravad HaMagid — not explicit in the Rambam)

More strict — even food nullified before Pesah cannot be eaten on Pesah

If It Reawakens — What Was the Point of Nullification Before Pesah?

The second opinion still acknowledges that nullification before Pesah has real effects:

  1. Erev Pesah afternoon (from hasot until nightfall): Even according to the yesh cholkim, the food is permitted during this window — it was nullified, and it only reawakens when Pesah itself begins at nightfall.
  2. Taam (taste) vs. Mamashut (substance): Even the yesh cholkim agree that if only taste remains (no physical particles of hames), the taste does not reawaken. Only when there is mamashut — actual physical substance/particles of hames in the mixture — does it reawaken.

Practical example of the distinction:

  • Bread fell into a large pot of soup before Pesah. You strained the soup and removed all pieces. Only taste remains. → Everyone agrees the soup is permitted on Pesah. Taam doesn't reawaken.
  • Bread fell into soup before Pesah. You did not strain it. Tiny particles of bread remain dissolved in the soup. → First opinion: permitted (doesn't reawaken). Second opinion: prohibited on Pesah (mamashut reawakens).

Does Reawakening Require Heat?

Even among those who hold it reawakens, there is a further mahloket:

View

Position

Some Aharonim

Reawakening only occurs when heat is applied on Pesah — it doesn't happen automatically. If you don't cook the food on Pesah, it doesn't reawaken even according to the yesh cholkim.

Mishnah Berurah

Seems to be mahmir (strict) — reawakening may happen even without heat

Important to note: We are stacking multiple layers of chumra here:

  1. The entire be-mashehu rule on Pesah is de-rabbanan (Rabbeinu Tam disagrees entirely)
  2. Within this de-rabbanan, there's a mahloket whether it reawakens
  3. Even among those who say it reawakens, there's a mahloket whether heat is required
  4. All of this assumes hames was actually present in the first place

The Halacha

Shulhan Arukh (Sephardim): Follows the stam — it does not reawaken. Once nullified before Pesah, the food is permitted on Pesah. This is the opinion of Hacham Ovadia Yoseph.

Rama (Ashkenazim): Makes a compromise:

Type of Mixture

Rama's Ruling

Lach be-lach (pure liquid with liquid — completely intermingled, no solid particles)

Lenient — follows first opinion. Does not reawaken.

Yavesh be-yavesh or solid in liquid (any element of solid involved — e.g., bread fell into wine)

Strict — follows second opinion. Does reawaken on Pesah.

Practical consequence: Since most real-world mixtures involve some element of solid (even tiny particles), the Rama is strict in most cases. Only pure liquid-to-liquid mixtures (e.g., two liquids poured together) get the leniency.

Massive Practical Implications: Do You Need a Hekhsher on Natural Products?

Understanding Se'if 4 has enormous practical ramifications for what foods require a Pesah hekhsher.

The Logic (Following the Shulhan Arukh / Sephardi Psak)

Take the example of olive oil (or salt, sugar, coffee, tea, yoghurt, rice cakes, crisps, milk, etc.):

  1. Check the ingredients — if the label says 100% olive oil, that's what you're getting. We live in a country with strict manufacturing regulations and allergy/allergen disclosure requirements. What's on the label is what's in the product.
  2. Theoretical concern: Perhaps in the production facility, a non-Jewish worker's bread crumb fell in, or some cross-contamination occurred.
  3. Even if that happened (which is highly unlikely in modern clinical manufacturing conditions) — it would have been nullified before Pesah (well over 1 in 60).
  4. Concern: When you heat the oil on Pesah, it reawakens.
  5. Shulhan Arukh: It does not reawaken → permitted.
  6. Even for the yesh cholkim: You don't have to assume hames was even present (see Se'if 5 below).

The Ruling

For Sephardim (following Shulhan Arukh and Hacham Ovadia):

All natural products whose ingredients are clearly listed and contain no hames grains (wheat, barley, oats, rye, spelt) do not need a Pesah hekhsher, provided you buy them before Pesah. This includes: olive oil, vegetable oil, salt, sugar, coffee, tea, yoghurt, rice cakes, crisps, milk, and similar products.

If you want to be mahmir and follow the Rama / Ben Ish Chai / yesh cholkim — that is praiseworthy.

Should You Buy Before Pesah?

Timing

Recommendation

Before Pesah (ideal)

Buy natural products before Pesah and store/freeze them. This covers all opinions — even the yesh cholkim agree nullification before Pesah is effective for ta'am.

On Hol HaMoed

There is a concern that the product was manufactured on 1st/2nd day Hag (during Pesah) and arrived at the supermarket on Hol HaMoed — in which case nullification before Pesah wouldn't apply. However, based on Se'if 5 (below), there are strong grounds to say this is also permitted since you don't have to assume hames was present.

Ashkenazi Guests

If you are Sephardi, hosting Ashkenazi guests, and bought products without a Pesah hekhsher:

  • Based on Se'if 4 alone, there could be a problem (the Rama holds it reawakens in most cases).
  • Based on Se'if 5 (below), there are strong grounds for leniency even for Ashkenazim.
  • Best practice: At minimum, disclose to your guests what you're following. There are grounds for leniency, but this is a question that requires further discussion among the poskim.

Se'if 5 – Salting Before Pesah; The Principle of "Don't Assume Hames Is Present"

Shulhan Arukh: If you had dried meat, chicken, fish, or cheese that was salted before Pesah, and you weren't careful about the salt (didn't check that the salt was free of hames), and you salted it in a hames utensil — you may eat these foods on Pesah.

Why Is It Permitted? Three Reasons

Reason 1 — Salting doesn't extract from utensils:

  • Melichah (salting) does not have the ability to extract bliot (absorbed tastes) from the walls of utensils.
  • This is an important principle in all of kashrut — only heat (cooking, boiling, frying) extracts bliot. Salting does not.
  • Therefore, salting in a hames utensil is not a concern.

Reason 2 — Stam kelim einam ben yoman:

  • The default assumption about any utensil is that it hasn't been used in the last 24 hours (stam kelim einam ben yoman).
  • After 24 hours, any absorbed taste becomes pagum (degraded) — noten ta'am li-fgam — which is permitted (see Se'if 10 below).

Reason 3 — The crucial principle: אין מחזיקין איסורא / We do not presume prohibition:

The Shulhan Arukh states: We do not assume that hames is present. Don't presume things are problematic or prohibited without evidence.

  • Some people approach Pesah thinking hames is "lurking in every corner, waiting to catch you."
  • The halacha says: the default presumption is that things are not hames.
  • Unless you actually saw hames, or there's a real, specific reason to suspect it — you don't have to worry.
  • This applies even to the salt used — you don't have to assume there was hames in the salt.

What Does "Salting" Mean in This Context?

  • Salting in kashrut means salting to the extent that the food is inedible because of the salt (heavy salting for preservation, then washing off).
  • This is not regular seasoning or light salting.

Exception: Salting on Pesah Itself in Hames Utensils

Shulhan Arukh: If you salted food on Pesah itself in hames utensils and then soaked it in water in those utensils — yesh lehachmir (one should try to be stringent and not eat it).

  • Note: "Yesh lehachmir" (one should be stringent) is not the same as "yesh cholkim" (some disagree). It means: ideally be strict, but bedi'avad it would be permitted.

The Rama's Position

Rama: The custom in Ashkenazi lands is to be strict in the first instance and not eat foods salted with salt that wasn't verified hames-free. However, even the Rama says: if you washed the meat three times thoroughly (soaked, rinsed, removed all traces of salt), you may eat it.

Why is the Rama strict?

  1. He holds it reawakens (chozer ve-ne'or) — see Se'if 4.
  2. He doesn't fully rely on the presumption that hames isn't present.

However: The Rama was discussing salt that was "lying around" — loose salt in an open kitchen, hundreds of years ago, where hames could plausibly be mixed in. In modern manufacturing conditions — sterile factories, single-product production lines, allergy regulations, cross-contamination controls — even the Rama might agree the concern is negligible.

Se'if 10 – Noten Taam Lifgam on Pesah (נותן טעם לפגם)

This is another fundamental mahloket between the Shulhan Arukh and Rama with far-reaching practical consequences.

Background: Noten Taam Lifgam During the Year

  • When food taste has been absorbed in the walls of a utensil for more than 24 hours, the taste becomes pagum (degraded/spoiled).
  • If you then cook in that utensil, the degraded taste that transfers into the food is called noten ta'am li-fgam — "giving a spoiled taste."
  • Year-round halacha: Lechatchila (ab initio) — don't cook in a non-kosher pot. Bedi'avad (post facto) — the food is completely permitted. The spoiled taste doesn't prohibit.
  • From the Torah: Even within 24 hours, noten ta'am li-fgam is permitted. The Hachamim forbade within 24 hours as a precaution.

The Question on Pesah

Since we found that the Hachamim imposed a special stringency on Pesah (be-mashehu — even a drop prohibits), should we extend this stringency to noten ta'am li-fgam as well? Should we say that on Pesah, even degraded taste prohibits?

The Mahloket

Opinion

Ruling

Reasoning

Shulhan Arukh

Noten ta'am li-fgam is permitted on Pesah — same as the rest of the year

We don't extend chumrot beyond what the Hachamim explicitly stated. What we found (be-mashehu), we found. We don't add to it.

Rama

Noten ta'am li-fgam is prohibited on Pesah

Since we found the Hachamim were strict in one area, we should extend the stringency to other areas as well. This was the custom in Ashkenaz.

The Rama adds: Even a "double" leniency — be-mashehu + noten ta'am li-fgam combined — is still asur according to the Ashkenazi custom.

Practical Scenario

You accidentally cooked Pesah food in a hames pot that was clean and hadn't been used for more than 24 hours:

According to...

Is the food permitted?

Shulhan Arukh (Sephardim)

✅ Yes — noten ta'am li-fgam, same leniency as the rest of the year

Rama (Ashkenazim)

❌ No — on Pesah, even degraded taste prohibits

Important: This is one of the major reasons Ashkenazi Pesah practice is significantly more stringent than Sephardi practice. The Shulhan Arukh refuses to extend chumrot beyond what the Gemara explicitly stated; the Rama extends them based on the spirit of the Pesah stringency.

Summary of Key Halachot

Se'if

Key Takeaways

447:4 (chozer ve-ne'or)

Hames nullified before Pesah (1 in 60): Shulhan Arukh (stam) = does NOT reawaken on Pesah. Yesh cholkim = does reawaken. Halacha for Sephardim: does not reawaken (Hacham Ovadia). Rama compromises: lach be-lach = lenient; solid involved = strict.

447:4 (ta'am vs mamashut)

Even the yesh cholkim agree: if only taste remains (no physical particles — e.g., you strained the soup), it does NOT reawaken. Only mamashut (substance) reawakens.

447:4 (heat)

Even among those who say it reawakens: mahloket whether heat is needed to trigger reawakening. Some Aharonim: no heat = no reawakening.

447:4 (practical)

For Sephardim: natural products (oil, salt, sugar, tea, coffee, yoghurt, rice cakes, milk) do NOT need a Pesah hekhsher if ingredients are clearly listed and hames-free. Buy before Pesah ideally. Strong grounds to be lenient even on Hol HaMoed.

447:5 (salting)

Food salted before Pesah with unchecked salt, in hames utensils: permitted. Three reasons: melichah doesn't extract from utensils; stam kelim einam ben yoman; ein machzikin issura (don't assume hames is present).

447:5 (don't assume)

Critical principle: we do NOT presume hames is lurking. Default is things are kosher. Don't assume hames contamination in modern manufacturing without specific evidence.

447:5 (Rama)

Ashkenazi custom: strict about unchecked salt. But was discussing loose salt in pre-modern conditions — in modern factories, even the Rama might agree it's fine.

447:10 (noten ta'am li-fgam)

Cooked in a hames pot not used in 24+ hours: Shulhan Arukh = permitted on Pesah (don't extend chumrot). Rama = prohibited (extend the Pesah stringency). Major practical difference between Sephardi and Ashkenazi Pesah.
