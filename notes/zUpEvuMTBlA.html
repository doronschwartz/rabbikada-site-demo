Topic: Practical cases of injury by man or by Heaven

Sources: The Pithei Teshuvah; the Rambam; the Semag; the Beit Meir

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • About 250 years ago, and more than once since, as brought in the Pithei Teshuvah: a child was bullied by youths who tied a string tightly around his organ, causing an illness that eventually left him impotent.
  • Seemingly this is bidei adam, but the poskim are lenient: the human act must directly cause the impotency. Here it caused an illness, which caused the impotency, so it counts as illness, bidei shamayim, even according to the Rambam. By the Semag, since he was not negligent, it is also fine.
  • The poskim always try to find a way out, since the situation is very sad and serious.
  • The Beit Meir, also about 250 years ago: a man's illness, from Heaven, forced doctors to cut his reproductive organs. Based on one reading of the Rambam, since it was necessary and originated from Heaven, it is bidei shamayim though done by a human.
  • Back to prostate surgery: besides being internal, the sperm tubes were cut out of necessity, so there is further room to call it bidei shamayim. Not everyone agreed, and there is room to disagree.
  • In most cases the poskim find a way to define it as bidei shamayim. Real castration is bidei adam.

Sources quoted

  • The Pithei Teshuvah: the bullied child, an indirect cause through illness, is not a pesua dakka.
  • The Beit Meir: surgery forced by illness is bidei shamayim.

In one line

The poskim usually define indirect or medically necessary injuries as bidei shamayim, so the man is not a pesua dakka.
