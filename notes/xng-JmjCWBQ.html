Topic: The three methods of kashering utensils, and why no berakhah is said on kashering

Sources: Torah, Parashat Mattot and Parashat Sav; the Gemara; the Rambam

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Monday 24 March 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • With Pesah under three weeks away, the coming weeks cover kashering utensils (Hagalat Kelim) and then the laws of Erev Pesah falling on Shabbat.
  • There are three ways of kashering: Hadahah (simply washing and cleaning), Hagalah (using heat to extract absorbed taste) and Libbun (the most severe: heating until, in the halachic definition, sparks fly out).
  • Libbun is needed for anything that absorbed taste through heat alone without liquid, the classic example in the Gemara being barbecue spits (a little oil does not change this).
  • Hagalah is for taste absorbed through heat with liquid. The Hakhamim understood from the Torah that such absorption is less intense, so heating to boiling suffices.
  • Anything that falls into neither category needs only Hadahah, washing and cleaning, for Pesah and the whole year.
  • According to the Rambam, kashering is not a misvah in its own right, only a way to remove what is forbidden. A minority of Rishonim held it is a misvah like Tevilat Kelim, but the halacha does not seem to follow them.
  • One may simply buy or use other utensils instead. Therefore no berakhah is said on kashering, unlike Tevilat Kelim, which is a misvah and carries a berakhah.

Sources quoted

  • Parashat Mattot: after the war with Midian, the Benei Yisrael are told that anything used with fire must be purged through fire; this is the main source for kashering.
  • Parashat Sav: kashering also appears regarding the sacrifices.
  • The Gemara: barbecue spits as the example of dry absorption needing Libbun.
  • The Rambam: kashering is removal of a prohibition, not a positive misvah.

In one line

Utensils are kashered by Libbun, Hagalah or simple washing according to how they absorbed taste, and since kashering is not a misvah, no berakhah is said.
