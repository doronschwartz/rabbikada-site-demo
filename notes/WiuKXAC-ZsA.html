The halacha

  • Siman 1 covered the misvah to marry, Siman 2 Jewish identity, Siman 3 Kohanim. Siman 4, long and fundamental, deals with pesulei kahal, those who may not marry a regular Jew, chiefly the mamzer, the child of specific forbidden adulterous unions.
  • The Shulhan Arukh opens with the Torah's list. A male convert from Ammon or Moav may never marry a Jewish woman, though he may marry others such as a mamzeret. A female convert from Ammon or Moav may marry in.
  • This is why David's descent from Rut, though some tried to taint him, was valid: the Hakhamim said the ban covers only males. It is recorded in the Gemara and Shulhan Arukh.
  • Converts from biblical Misrayim and Edom, male or female, may not marry in for three generations: their child may not, but their grandchild may.
  • Today, as the Gemara and Shulhan Arukh rule, Sanheriv mixed up the nations. We follow the majority (kol deparish merubah parish), and most nations are permitted, so any convert today may marry into Am Yisrael.
  • A convert from modern Egypt is not what the Torah means.

In one line

The Torah bars male Ammonites and Moabites forever and Egyptians and Edomites for three generations, but since the nations are mixed, any convert today may marry in.
