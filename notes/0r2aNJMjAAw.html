It fills a gap in his Derech Hashem series at Tiferet Eyal, where these paragraphs were taught but not recorded.

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Review: the soul can refine the body

The previous class ended with Adam before the sin: body and soul, good and bad before him. Had he chosen the good, his period of challenge would have ended and he would have been perfected.

The short paragraph 7 explains how. We usually think the neshamah gives us two things: life and intellect. Take the soul away and the body is "just a piece of meat." But the soul has a third power: to refine the body and its material nature, "elevation after elevation", until the two can live together in harmony. Had Adam not sinned, his soul would have refined his body in this world, and there would have been no death. In the ultimate Olam HaBa, body and soul live together, but the body follows the soul instead of pulling it down.

So why does a true saddik's body not become more spiritual each time he does a misvah? That is what the next paragraphs explain. A participant asked whether it is fair that one sin on one day changed everything. The Rabbi suggested waiting for the next paragraphs: "I don't think it's so much a punishment. I think it's a reality."

Paragraph 8: three changes

Before the sin there was already deficiency in the world, since there must be bad for there to be choice. But the sin brought three changes:

  1. The bad entered man himself. Before the sin, good and bad were outside Adam, like the tree standing outside him. Afterwards, the deficiencies became part of man's own being, and of the world. The Rabbi noted that Rav Hayim of Volozhin develops this in Nefesh HaHayim.
  2. Repair became harder. At first the bad was simply how Hashem created the world, and Adam was not responsible for it. Now that he has drawn it into himself, he is partly responsible, so leaving the bad behind is harder.
  3. The work is double. We must first return to the level of Adam before the sin, and then go further, as Adam was meant to.

This, the Rabbi said, may explain why Adam had one misvah and we have 613: more repair is needed.

Daat: knowledge as connection

The tree is the Es HaDaat Tov VaRa. Daat is usually translated knowledge, but its true meaning is closeness and connection, which is why the Torah uses "and he knew" for the closeness of husband and wife.

He explained the three levels named in the weekday Amidah and in the word Habad: Hokhmah, Binah and Daat. Hokhmah is the plain facts you are taught, such as a lecture. Binah is working out something new from what you learned. Daat is when it becomes part of you and you live with it: a student of Eruvin who walks down the street noticing walls and wires, or a medical student who meets a real case and knows what to do. That is why the blessing ends "Honen HaDaat": you cannot jump straight to Daat.

So "the tree of Daat of good and bad" means the joining of good and bad in one being. Adam was told not to bring that mixture into himself. He did, and that is the state we live in now.

A participant asked whether repair is harder now than a thousand years ago. The Rabbi said it should be easier, because of what the Ramhal later calls the tikkun hakelali: the world as a whole is always moving towards its final repair.

Paragraph 9: death and resurrection

Paragraph 9 answers the question from before. Hashem decreed that neither man nor the world can reach perfection in their damaged form. The soul cannot refine the body until it leaves it, the body breaks down in the grave, and the two are rebuilt and reunited at the resurrection. The world too will be destroyed and renewed: the Gemara says the world will exist for 6,000 years and then lie desolate, and Hashem will renew it.

The decomposition in the grave returns the body to its state before the sin. Then the soul returns and begins to elevate it, step by step. "We see very holy people with very coarse bodies," because in this life the soul's hands are tied.

How far the soul can refine the body depends on how we lived. The more spiritual our life, the more refined the body will be, the less of a barrier between us and Hashem, and the closer we can cleave to Him. So the ultimate world to come is only after the resurrection; the world of souls is a waiting room.

Questions

  • What is the soul? "Soul is you": your character, your identity. The body is the spacesuit you wear to live on earth. After years on the moon you may grow attached to the spacesuit, but it is not who you are.
  • How will a decomposed body come back? "I've got no idea," the Rabbi said, and no one can say. As the Rambam writes about Mashiah, when it comes you will see; there is no need to speculate.

The group was asked to go over paragraph 9 before the next class.

Summary

  • Beyond life and intellect, the soul can refine the body; had Adam not sinned, this would have happened in his lifetime.
  • The sin brought the bad into man himself, made repair harder and doubled the work.
  • Daat means connection; the tree of Daat mixed good and bad within us.
  • Now the body must die and be rebuilt before the soul can refine it, and the world too will be renewed.
  • How refined our body becomes depends on how we live now.
