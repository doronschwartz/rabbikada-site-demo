Topic: The structure of Malkhuyot, Zikhronot and Shofarot in the Rosh Hashanah Musaf

Sources: The Gemara; Bereshit chapter 1; Tehillim 150; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Thursday 7 September 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Piyutim are customary, not obligatory. The heart of Rosh Hashanah prayer is the Shaharit Amidah and above all the Musaf, the longest Amidah of the year, longer even than any on Yom Kippur.
  • The Hakhamim instituted three long berakhot: Malkhuyot (Hashem's sovereignty and unity), Zikhronot (His remembering all that was and will be), and Shofarot (the shofar of Sinai, of the end of days, and of the Akedah of Yishak).
  • They form one unit: one who cannot say all three should say none, since part alone is a berakhah levatalah.
  • Each opens with an introduction to its theme, contains ten verses, and closes with a berakhah on the theme, the third ending "shomea kol teruat amo Yisrael".
  • The ten verses are three from the Torah, three from Ketuvim, three from Neviim, and a tenth from the Torah.
  • The Gemara gives three reasons for ten: the ten utterances of creation, the Ten Commandments, and the ten "halleluhu" of Tehillim 150, which mentions the shofar.
  • The Rishonim explain that Ketuvim precede Neviim because all those verses come from Tehillim, written by David HaMelekh before the later prophets, in his honour.
  • The shofar is blown at the end of each section, between the first three and last three berakhot.

In one line

Musaf's three sections, each with ten verses and a shofar blast, form one indivisible unit at the heart of Rosh Hashanah.
