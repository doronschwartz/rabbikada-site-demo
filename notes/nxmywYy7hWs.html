Topic: When women and children may light the household Menorah

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; Rav Hayyim Palagi; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the Ben Ish Hai

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Sepharadim light one Menorah per household. Women are equally obligated: though the misvah is time-bound, they too were saved from the Greeks' persecution.
  • So a mother may say the berakhah and light for the whole family, husband included. But the Gemara says that when husband and wife are both obligated it is better for the husband, so the minhag is for the father to light.
  • If the father is unwell and cannot come down, the mother may light with a berakhah for everyone.
  • Once the father has said the berakhah and lit one candle, he may hand over to his wife to light the rest.
  • Children under Bar or Bat Misvah: the Shulhan Arukh brings two opinions, so the halacha is that they should not light with the berakhah for the household.
  • After the berakhah and the first candle, a child who has reached the age of hinukh (about six; opinions range from five to seven) may light the extra candles, which are only hiddur, according to Rav Hayyim Palagi and Hakham Ovadiah. The Ben Ish Hai disagreed, but there is plenty to rely on.
  • Younger children should not light any of the candles, but anyone may light the shamash, if safe, since it has no halachic significance.

In one line

The father lights, or the mother if needed; children of hinukh age may add the extra candles after the first, and anyone may light the shamash.
