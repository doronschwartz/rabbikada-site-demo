Shiur: Part 7 of the series on the Rambam's Introduction to Perek Helek, given by Dayan Daniel Kada on Zoom, Tuesday 18 November 2025 (the week of Chayei Sarah)

These notes are based on the transcript of the recording.

Where We Are

On כל ישראל יש להם חלק לעולם הבא, the Rambam explained Olam HaBa and its "corollary" forms (Gan Eden, Gehinom, Mashiach, resurrection): Olam HaBa is the essence of everything we do. Now:

ומה שצריך שנזכיר בכאן, והוא הראוי מכל המקומות, שעיקרי דתנו ויסודותיה שלשה עשר יסודות.

Here is the most fitting place: the roots (ikkarim) of our religion and its foundations are thirteen.

Since the Mishnah discusses who has no portion, this is where to set out the foundations. "He's in his twenties, and he's the first to do this."

Part 1: The First Jewish Creed

"Judaism has a dogma of dogmalessness": a line attributed to Moses Mendelssohn, and argued by some Jewish academics: Judaism is about practice, not belief. "That is completely wrong, in my opinion, and definitely in the Rambam's."

There are beliefs every Jew must hold, scattered across Torah and Chazal. Rav Saadia (Monday nights) says we must believe in Mashiach, in resurrection, but made no list. The Rambam (12th century) is the first to collate a creed. "That part, everyone can agree is authentic. The emphasis is uniquely his": intellect in serving Hashem; mitzvot must be accompanied by understanding.

Belief over practice: the convert

The Gemara (Yevamot 47a)

The Rambam (Issurei Bi'ah 14:1–2)

Why convert? Don't you see Israel is afflicted, oppressed, harried? If he says I know, and I'm unworthy, accept him immediately

The same, almost word for word

Teach him some lighter and some weightier mitzvot; leket, shichechah, pe'ah, ma'aser ani; the punishment (until now forbidden fat and Shabbat carried no karet or stoning; now they do) and reward

ומודיעין אותו עיקרי הדת, שהוא יחוד השם ואיסור עבודה זרה, ומאריכין בדבר הזה. Teach him the foundations of the faith, Hashem's unity and the prohibition of idolatry, and elaborate at length. Then some mitzvot, ואין מאריכין בדבר זה: and don't elaborate

"He's turned the Gemara on its head, and added something he made up himself. How can he? For the Rambam it was obvious: you can't accept a convert who doesn't know the fundamentals of belief." (Halachot 3 and 4, on Olam HaBa, are from the Gemara, "and we still say them at the end of a conversion today.")

Part 4: Then Why Principles at All? Three Answers

1. Rav Yosef Albo: true vs foundational

The whole Torah is emet. Some things are also ikkarim. The difference is focus: you must believe everything, but some things need more attention.

2. Here, ignorance is no excuse

Principle 3: Hashem has no body. The Rambam: believe He does, and you're a heretic with no portion, even if that's how you were naively raised (Hashem sits on a throne in heaven).

The Ra'avad: of course He has no body (Rav Saadia devotes an essay to it), but greater men than you believed He did (some French Tosafists). Call them heretics for a mistake?

The famous answer (usually said in Yiddish): misken, I'm sorry for you, but if you "believe in God" and to you "God" means a pencil, you don't believe in God. "Saying the words doesn't make you a believer. A physical God, however big, isn't God. I'm sorry that's how you were taught, but you have no understanding of Godliness."

Elsewhere, an honest mistake costs nothing: a naive, even "Islamic" picture of Gan Eden and Gehinom is just an error. These 13 you must know, with no excuse. A nebach apikoros is also an apikoros.

Mark: wasn't the Rambam reacting to Islam and Christianity around him?

"Interesting: all 13 have sources in Torah or Chazal except the third. Nowhere do they say Hashem has no body. It's his own reasoning. Rav Saadia was equally adamant." Perhaps a reaction: "Now I think of it, in one of his letters he writes that he formulated the 13 because he saw great scholars, who knew all of Shas, with babyish beliefs. I think he meant exactly this."

3. They define who is "in Klal Yisrael"

At the end of the 13:

וכאשר יאמין האדם אלה היסודות כולם ונתבררה אמונתו בהם, הוא נכנס בכלל ישראל, ומצוה לאהבו ולחמול עליו… ואפילו עשה כל העבירות מחמת תאותו ותגבורת יצרו הרע… וכשיפקפק אדם ביסוד מאלו היסודות, הרי זה יצא מן הכלל וכפר בעיקר, ונקרא מין ואפיקורוס וקוצץ בנטיעות, ומצוה לשנאתו ולאבדו.

Believe them all, and you enter Klal Yisrael: we must love and care for you, even if you've committed every sin out of desire. Doubt one, and you've left the community: a min, an apikoros.

Part 6: What "Emunah" Really Means

ונתבררה אמונתו בהם: his belief has become clear to him. That's the Rambam's emunah sheleimah.

The Christian distortion. "Many concepts in English, and even in Judaism, have been severely affected by Christian philosophy: I've said it of reward and punishment, and it's true of belief." The Oxford Dictionary: faith: strong belief… based on spiritual conviction rather than proof. An early Christian writer (the line is usually attributed to Tertullian): I believe because it is absurd. "The more absurd, the more you need faith.**"

"That is absolutely not what emunah means. The complete opposite."

- אמן is the root: ותקעתיו יתד במקום נאמן, a peg in a firm place; ומצאת את לבבו נאמן לפניך, Avraham's heart steadfast; ויהי אומן את הדסה, to raise and train.

- "There's a creature in Australia I've never seen: I trust you, I believe you. That's not emunah."

- The Ramchal: כל איש מישראל צריך שיאמין וידע: believe and know. Emunah is tantamount to knowledge.

- So באמונה שלמה isn't strictly necessary; אני מאמין already means it.

Rav Moshe Feinstein (Igrot Moshe): may religious parents eat at a non-observant child's home? A child isn't believed as a witness (that's trusting testimony). But if the parents know with certainty the child respects them and wouldn't deceive them, it's barur to them: they know. "The difference between believing something and knowing it. That's real emunah, and why it's a lifelong project, and why the Rambam counts it among the 613."

Haran in the furnace

וימת הרן על פני תרח אביו באור כשדים. The simple meaning: Haran died in his father's lifetime. Targum Yonatan ben Uziel: Haran watched Avraham thrown into the furnace, undecided: If he comes out, I'll follow his God; if not, I won't. Avraham emerged unscathed, Haran jumped in, and died.

"One of my rabbis said: his problem was living with doubt: an agnostic's wager." Pascal's wager (believe, since 120 years' inconvenience beats eternal hell) fails, "not least because it works for every religion. You can't live your life with constant doubts. Make decisions, have convictions, even if it's the wrong one."

Why send to Ur Kasdim for a wife?

Avraham and Sarah's kiruv in Canaan brought thousands and tens of thousands. Yet Yaakov's family goes down to Egypt seventy souls. Where did they go? The Ran (Derashot): easy come, easy go: people inspired by one shiur leave after one contrary book. In Ur Kasdim they had such conviction they threw Avraham into a fire. "Avraham wanted a girl with conviction, even in the wrong faith. That's what we mean by emunah."

Part 7: The First Principle

היסוד הראשון: להאמין מציאות הבורא יתברך, והוא שיש שם נמצא שלם בכל דרכי המציאות, והוא עילת מציאות הנמצאים כלם, בו קיום מציאותם וממנו קיומם. ואל יעלה על הלב העדר מציאותו, כי בהעדר מציאותו נתבטלה מציאות כל הנמצאים ולא נשאר נמצא שיתקיים מציאותו. ואם נעלה על לבנו העדר כל הנמצאים זולתו, לא תתבטל מציאות השם יתברך ולא תגרע… כי הוא מסתפק במציאותו ודי לו בעצמו, ואין צורך במציאות זולתו. וכל מה שזולתו מן המלאכים וגופי הגלגלים ומה שבתוכם ומה שמתחתיהם, הכל צריכים למציאותם אליו. וזה היסוד הראשון, מורה עליו הדבור אנכי ה׳ אלהיך.

"People say the First Principle is just believe in God. There's much more. He has to define what God means, because a distorted understanding isn't belief in God."

The Mishneh Torah opens with exactly this, a halachic book: יסוד היסודות ועמוד החכמות, לידע שיש שם מצוי ראשון, והוא ממציא כל נמצא. "You can't learn halachah until you know this properly." That's ה׳ אלהים אמת: everything else needn't exist; He must.

Summary

  1. The Rambam is the first to formulate a creed. "A dogma of dogmalessness" is wrong. His emphasis on belief: converts are taught the foundations at length, mitzvot briefly, his own addition to the Gemara.
  2. 13 in three groups: God (1–5), revelation (6–9), reward and punishment (10–13). Not immediately accepted: Albo 3, Crescas fewer, the Radbaz none (the whole Torah is an ikkar).
  3. Why principles? (a) Albo: all true, some need focus; (b) no excuse of ignorance: a God who's "a pencil" isn't God (Ra'avad vs Rambam on corporeality, the one principle without a source); (c) they define entry into Klal Yisrael: kiddushin still works, shechitah doesn't.
  4. Yigdal and Ani Ma'amin: later, not always precise.
  5. Emunah isn't "belief without proof": from amen, firmness. Believe and know (Ramchal; Rav Moshe on the non-observant child). Conviction: Haran's wager; Avraham's wife from Ur Kasdim.
  6. Principle 1: five elements, Creator, perfect, self-sufficient, sustaining all, necessary: the opening of the Mishneh Torah.
