Topic: What Bedikat Hames requires, and what it does not

Sources: The Mishnah Berurah

Daily Halacha: Pesah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Bedikat Hames is a rabbinic Misvah. From the Torah it is enough to renounce ownership of one's Hames.
  • The Hakhamim required searching and removal for two reasons: one may not renounce it wholeheartedly (would one with a cupboard of expensive whisky really let a non-Jew take it over Pesah?), and even if one does, one may be tempted to eat it.
  • The main halacha, followed by Sepharadim and seemingly by the Mishnah Berurah for Ashkenazim, is that we search for a Kezayit, roughly 18 to 27 grams by most opinions, about half a piece of bread.
  • Crumbs are not part of the Misvah; any one owns are covered by the nullification on the night of the search and on Erev Pesah. There is no need to take things apart outside the kitchen and dining room. Spring cleaning is fine, but should not be blamed on Pesah.
  • Places where Hames is not usually brought, such as a bedroom where one does not eat, need only a quick scan. Where children go, check whatever they can reach, again for pieces, not crumbs.
  • The rest of the house can be done in an hour or so, opening cupboards and scanning.
  • Where food will be prepared or eaten on Pesah, such as the kitchen, be meticulous even about crumbs, lest they fall into Pesah food.

In one line

Outside the kitchen and dining room, Bedikat Hames is a search for pieces the size of a Kezayit, not a hunt for crumbs.
