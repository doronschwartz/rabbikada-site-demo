Topic: Whether an umbrella may be opened or carried on Shabbat and Yom Tov where there is an eruv

Sources: The Noda BiYehudah (Rabbi Yehezkel Landau of Prague, 1782); the Hatam Sofer

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Wednesday 26 May 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The question applies only on Yom Tov or on Shabbat with an eruv; without an eruv, carrying in a public domain on Shabbat is strictly forbidden.
  • Umbrellas became popular for men in the mid 18th century through Jonas Hanway in England, so for about a century the question was discussed by European Hakhamim before it reached the Middle East.
  • Building a permanent tent is the Torah melakhah of Boneh; the Hakhamim also forbade a temporary tent (ohel arai), which an umbrella seems to be.
  • A rabbi from England asked the Noda BiYehudah in 1782, wishing to permit it like a folding chair (kise traskal), which may be opened because the tent already exists.
  • The Noda BiYehudah disagreed: opening a ready-made tent is allowed when the space on top is used, as with a chair, but not when one uses the space beneath as protection from sun or rain. He forbade it sharply.
  • The Hatam Sofer permitted it for two reasons. A tent held by a person is not a tent, as with the tallit spread over children on Simhat Torah, which no one questions. And its walls do not reach the ground.
  • What the Sepharadi Hakhamim later said, and the halacha today, follows tomorrow.

In one line

The Noda BiYehudah forbade opening an umbrella as a tent, while the Hatam Sofer permitted it as a hand-held tent without walls.
