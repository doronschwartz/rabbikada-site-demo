Introduction

Last time (Seifim 1-5) we saw the cases where Beit Din forces a get. Today's two Seifim are, in the Rabbi's words, quite complicated, and still relevant.

  • Seif 6: the husband is not yoreh kahes, meaning his seed does not shoot out "like an arrow" and so does not reach her. Is the wife believed when she says so?
  • Seif 7: gevur anashim, the husband cannot have an erection. Is she believed here?

The Rabbi told the class to write down each Rishon's view as we go, because there are seven approaches to Seif 6. Most of them reappear in the Shulhan Arukh and the Rema.

The two sugyot and the contradiction

Nedarim. The Mishnah says that a woman who tells her husband "shamayim beini uveinekha" (Heaven is between us), that is, "you are not reaching me", is not believed. We suspect she simply wants out, perhaps to marry someone else.

Yevamot. The Gemara there speaks of a couple married ten years with no children. We tell him he has not fulfilled peru urevu, so he must divorce and pay the ketubah (it is his misvah, not hers). Then they argue about whose fault it is. Rav Ami says "devarim shebeino levein neemenet", she is believed, since she can tell better than he can whether he shoots like an arrow. That is the direct contradiction with Nedarim.

Other points from the Gemara:

  • If he says "let me marry another woman and check", we allow it. A man may marry more than one wife if he can support them.
  • If she demands a divorce only "because I want children", for the misvah, we do not listen, as she has no misvah of peru urevu. We listen only if she says "hutra leyadi, mora lekeverah", a stick for my hand and a spade for my burial, meaning she wants someone to care for her in old age. The Rabbi noted that most women today would say exactly this.

The Rabbi also noted the logic of the contradiction. Whether he is yoreh kahes is hard to know for sure, so her claim does not clearly contradict his. That is why she is not simply believed. In Seif 7, the question is a straight factual argument ("I have an erection", "you do not"), so she is believed more readily. He called this "ironic".

The shittot in Seif 6

Rishon

How he reconciles Rav Ami with Nedarim

Rav Hai Gaon

Rav Ami follows the first Mishnah in Nedarim, which was later superseded. Hard to accept, since Rav Ami is an Amora, and nobody follows it.

Rif and Rambam

Yevamot speaks of after ten years. We no longer suspect she wants someone else, since he must divorce anyway. The dispute is about the ketubah only: she is believed, so she keeps it.

Rosh

Almost the same, but within ten years, in a case where the husband wants to divorce her anyway. Again only the ketubah is at stake.

Tosafot, first answer

After ten years, but he already has children from another marriage, so he could not be forced to divorce. Since ten years have passed and she claims hutra leyadi, we accept that the problem arose later. He is forced to give a get.

Tosafot, second answer

Within ten years, but the added claim of hutra leyadi is a genuine concern, not a wish to leave, so she is believed. This is about the get.

Rivam

The case is her third husband, within ten years. A hazakah is only formed after three times, so she is still believed and gets her ketubah. If it were a fourth husband, she would get nothing.

The Rabbi stressed that the Rif, the Rambam and the Rosh speak about the ketubah, not about forcing a get. The Tosafot answers are about the get.

The Shulhan Arukh and the Rema (Seif 6)

  • If she says he is not yoreh kahes only to get children, we do not listen. Following the Rif and the Rambam, after ten years she is believed and receives her ketubah.
  • If she comes brazenly after money, this weakens her credibility.
  • Following Tosafot, even if he has children from another woman we may believe her, since the problem may have arisen afterwards. Beit Din forces him to give a get, and he must pay the ketubah.
  • If he says "let me marry another woman and check", we let him. If the second wife has a child, the first loses her ketubah. If the second also has no child, he divorces both and pays both ketubot. He may even ask to try a third wife, if he can support them.
  • How long does he get with the second wife? The Rema says another ten years, in all cases. The Tur says ten years, but if the second wife confirms the first wife's claim, he must divorce straight away. The Gra reads these as a mahloket. The Rabbi's view was that the Shulhan Arukh itself is like the Rema (ten years each), and the Tur is a separate view.
  • If it is known that he is infertile, no waiting is needed.

Tosefet ketubah. Does she also receive the addition to the ketubah? Rabbenu Tam says yes, she receives everything. Rabbenu Hananel, the Rosh, and the Shulhan Arukh say she receives only the ketubah itself. The reason: he wrote the tosefet out of affection, "if I die or if we divorce by choice". He never agreed to pay it when he is forced to divorce. The Rosh's own case (he wanted to divorce anyway) is different, and there she does get the tosefet.

The Rema adds a note on the Rivam's third husband. The Rabbi said honestly that he did not fully understand it, and the main point is that the Rema follows the Rivam and this does not contradict what we have learned.

Today. The class raised the point that nowadays this is checked medically, so most of Seif 6 is no longer needed in practice. The Rabbi agreed: "nowadays a lot of this is not relevant." Where the problem can be diagnosed, or treated, or is temporary, one would not force a get.

Seif 7: gevur anashim

This case is not in the Gemara. It comes from the Rishonim, and the source is the Rosh and Tosafot. The wife says her husband does not have an erection.

  • He is forced to divorce at once. There is no ten-year wait.
  • Because the claim is about a clear fact, it is believable. A woman is unlikely to lie to her husband's face about something both of them know, "especially in those days", given the balance of power between a man and a woman.
  • The Rashba was unsure, because of a Yerushalmi. The Shulhan Arukh rules that she is believed. The Rabbi noted that the Shakh, who is very stringent about eini zon, agrees here that he is forced to divorce.
  • The ketubah. The Shulhan Arukh says she does not receive it. The Rabbi said the Gra and the Shakh rule that she does. A key point is a woman who says "I only want the divorce, I waive the ketubah": then even those who suspect women today of being bold would believe her, as no money is involved.
  • The Rema (quoting the Mordekhai) says that women today are bolder. But if Beit Din sees an umdana, some evidence that she is telling the truth (for example, they have been married for five or six years, or he had an operation on that organ), she is believed even so.

"Before her husband". She must make the claim for the first time in front of her husband, not through a messenger and not first to Beit Din in his absence. The Rashbas says that if she first said it elsewhere, she has "got it off her chest", and loses her credibility. The Binyamin Zeev and the Maharik say she keeps her credibility, since she is not strengthened by the earlier statement and has said it to his face in the end.

If he proves himself. Say he offers to prove that he does have an erection with a second wife. She can still claim: "you do not have one with me." The Rabbi called this interesting.

Curable cases. The Rema, quoting the Ritva, says that if things will improve with time (for example, a first marriage where it is difficult at first), Beit Din does not force. The Rabbi added, as his own extrapolation, that nowadays with medical treatment the same logic would apply, and that he did not know of a posek who says so explicitly.

The class did not finish the end of the Rema, and the Rabbi said he would finish it quickly next time, before the remaining shorter Seifim.

Practical summary

  • A wife's claim that her husband is not yoreh kahes is believed only in limited cases. The Shulhan Arukh follows the Rif and Rambam (after ten years, ketubah) and Tosafot.
  • Distinguish the get from the ketubah. Many shittot are about the ketubah only.
  • If a husband asks to marry another woman to check, we allow it, with ten years for each wife, as the Rema rules.
  • If she is forced to receive a divorce, she gets the ketubah but not the tosefet (Shulhan Arukh, Rosh, Rabbenu Hananel).
  • For gevur anashim, she is believed, since the claim is clear. He must divorce at once, and the Shulhan Arukh says without the ketubah, though the Gra and Shakh differ.
  • She must raise this first before her husband and Beit Din, and Beit Din should check for an umdana today. Where there is a medical cure, we should not force.
