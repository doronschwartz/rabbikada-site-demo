Topic: The origins, story and status of Megillat Antiochus

Sources: Megillat Antiochus; Rav Saadiah Gaon; Baal Halakhot Gedolot; Tosafot Rid (Rabbi Yeshayah di Trani) on Sukkah

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Many festivals have a Megillah (Esther, Rut, Eikhah, and in some communities Shir HaShirim and Kohelet). Hanukkah seems to have none, but there is an ancient Megillat Antiochus.
  • The Geonim disputed its origin: Rav Saadiah Gaon held it was written by the five sons of Matityahu after the miracle; the Baal Halakhot Gedolot held it was written by the students of Hillel and Shammai, in the first century CE.
  • The story: Antiochus Epiphanes, in the 23rd year of his reign (about 175 BCE), waged religious war on the Jews. His general Nicanor conquered Jerusalem, set up an idol in the Bet HaMikdash and offered a pig on the altar.
  • Yohanan son of Matityahu pretended to change sides, arranged a private meeting with Nicanor and killed him. The next general, Bagris, failed twice but killed Yehudah. The elderly Matityahu then joined the war with his sons; they were victorious, killed and burnt Bagris, and Antiochus fled.
  • It is not part of Tanakh nor mentioned in the Talmud, so it lacks the sanctity of Tanakh. Yet it was accepted: the Tosafot Rid writes it was read publicly in Italian synagogues on Hanukkah, and the Yemenites read it too.
  • It is about 79 verses, available online with an English translation, and worth reading on Hanukkah.

In one line

Megillat Antiochus is an ancient, accepted though non-canonical account of the Hanukkah story, worth reading this Hanukkah.
