Topic: The seven restrictions of Tisha BeAv, and the last meal when the fast falls on Sunday Source: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Simanim 552 and 554 Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada in his Thursday Shulhan Arukh class, in the week before Tisha BeAv

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

When Tisha BeAv falls on Sunday

This was the last class before the summer break, a review of Tisha BeAv for a year when it fell on Sunday, straight after Shabbat. The Three Weeks and the Nine Days (Siman 551) had been covered in the Rabbi's daily halachot that week. The laws of the final meal (552-553) mostly do not apply when Erev Tisha BeAv is Shabbat, apart from one.

Siman 552:10. When Tisha BeAv falls on Sunday, or falls on Shabbat and is postponed, you may eat meat and drink wine at seudah shelishit, even a feast like King Shelomoh's, but you must stop at sunset. Eat normally, sitting at the table, with a zimmun if there are three men. The Mishnah Berurah brings authorities who say not to eat together with others. The Rabbi said it depends: if you always have guests for seudah shelishit, leaving them out would be a public display of mourning on Shabbat, which is forbidden; if you never do, a big gathering this week might be inappropriate.

No Tahanun. Eikhah calls Tisha BeAv a moed, an appointed time with a sense of festivity, because in the future it will become a day of joy. False messiahs have always tried to turn it into a feast; Shabtai Svi held one.

Seven restrictions, the whole 25 hours

Five are like Yom Kippur: eating and drinking, washing, anointing, leather shoes and marital relations. Tisha BeAv adds two found on no other day: Torah study and greeting. Everything forbidden by the halacha applies for all 25 hours. "No one's going to say on Yom Kippur after hasot you can be more lenient." People chatting and laughing in the afternoon because it is after midday are making a mistake.

1. Torah study

The Gemara forbids reading Tanakh, Mishnah, Midrash and Gemara, because "the words of HaShem gladden the heart." What may be studied: Iyov (skip the consoling verses), the harsh chapters of Yirmiyahu, the commentaries on Eikhah, the chapter in Moed Katan on mourning, and, as the Mishnah Berurah brings, the chapter in Gittin on the destruction. "There is so much to study": the sixty chapters on mourning in the Shulhan Arukh alone.

A question to keep in mind. Is there still a misvah to study, limited to these subjects? Or did the Hakhamim suspend the misvah, so one may simply sit and reflect? The Rabbi asked the class to spot the proof later in the siman.

Children. Schools are closed. Why, when we do not usually train children in mourning? One view: the problem is the teacher, who enjoys teaching. The Taz argues the same way, with a smile: what child enjoys being made to learn? On this view a child who chooses to learn alone may. The other view forbids even that. Since it is a rabbinic dispute, the Rabbi's view was: "if a child wanted, of their own accord, to study... let them study it. What else are they going to be doing nowadays?" From bar or bat misvah they keep the full halacha.

This reminds us that Torah is meant to be enjoyed. He told again of the Rogachover Gaon, who said he simply could not stop himself learning even on Tisha BeAv. "May that be our test."

Tehillim and musar. Rav Ovadia Yosef permits reading Tehillim or musar works such as Mesillat Yesharim and Hovot HaLevavot, though permitted Torah study is better. Far better than watching things or sleeping all afternoon, which defeat the purpose of the day.

Thinking Torah. Normally thought is not like speech. But the Shulhan Arukh brings an opinion forbidding even thinking over Torah, since the issue is joy, and that has become the consensus.

From the questions. Someone asked whether Tisha BeAv is becoming a Holocaust memorial day. The Rabbi pointed to the kinot: the Spanish and Portuguese recall the expulsion from Spain, Ashkenazim recall pogroms, and the ancient kinot of Hannah and her seven sons and the Ten Martyrs have nothing to do with the Temple. Tisha BeAv is about the whole exile, so serious study of the Holocaust falls under the permitted sad subjects. Turning it into entertainment is a different question.

Other points. A din Torah or a get is not done on Tisha BeAv unless it cannot wait. He told of a get written on Hol HaMoed Sukkot because a husband who had refused for many years suddenly agreed. The same would be done on Tisha BeAv. The korbanot in the morning prayers are said as usual, and the Rema allows the reader to prepare the Torah reading.

2. Eating and drinking

The Gemara, the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh rule that pregnant and nursing women fast on Tisha BeAv as on Yom Kippur. When the fast is postponed from Shabbat, Sephardim exempt them, and Rav Ovadia Yosef counts as "nursing" any woman within two years of giving birth. This year it was not postponed. Some hakhamim, in Baghdad because of the heat, and also some Ashkenazi authorities, gave a blanket exemption, but Rav Ovadia and most Sephardi poskim reject this. So such women should begin the fast, and may break it as soon as they feel weak or the baby is affected. Any complication needs medical advice.

  • Within 30 days of birth, a woman need not fast at all, and within 7 days she may not fast. The Rema and Mishnah Berurah are stricter after seven days.
  • The sick. Of the five levels of illness, a person ill in the whole body, not in danger but unable to function at work, is exempt, unlike the Rema's view. Someone who knows a fast always leaves them in bed for days afterwards is also exempt. Not so on Yom Kippur.
  • Those exempt eat what they need, not chocolates and treats.

3. Washing and 4. anointing

Even putting a finger in water is forbidden. That this is a separate law shows that not showering in the Nine Days is a custom, so a quick hygienic shower can be treated more leniently in those days.

  • Dirty hands, washing with soap after the bathroom, and getting wet while cooking for after the fast are all permitted: none is washing for pleasure.
  • In the morning, wash to the knuckles; pass moist hands over the eyes, or wash away discharge.
  • Teeth. Rav Ovadia Yosef allows someone who is sensitive to brush, head down over the sink. A touch of mouthwash so as not to be unpleasant to others is "just common sense."
  • One may cross water to reach a parent or one's rav, and even return. This is the proof: going to one's rav means learning Torah, so the misvah of study continues on Tisha BeAv. Tired feet after a journey may be washed, and a damp towel is fine.
  • A bride within thirty days of her wedding may wash her face and use make-up.
  • Anointing is forbidden only for pleasure. Medical creams and daily deodorant, even roll-on, are permitted.

5. Shoes

Only leather is forbidden; trainers and other materials are fine. Should one say sheasah li kol sorki? The Mishnah Berurah says yes. Many Sephardim omit it, following the Arizal, the Hida and the Ben Ish Hai. Rav Ovadia Yosef says it, since we do wear shoes. Follow your custom; without one, the Rabbi suggests saying it. The old leniencies for travellers and for those living among non-Jews hardly apply today, when trainers are normal footwear. The Mishnah Berurah criticised wearing ordinary shoes after midday.

6. Marital relations and 7. greeting

Couples sleep in separate beds; ordinary touch is permitted, though some forbid it. When the fast is postponed, Sephardim treat that Shabbat as a regular Shabbat, while the Rema forbids relations. This year, with the fast on Sunday, there is no restriction on Shabbat for anyone.

Do not ask after people's welfare. If someone greets you, answer briefly. Rav Ovadia permits "good morning" and "good night". Hanging around shops is also against the spirit of the day; buy what you need and go home.

Work and the strange hour

Work is not forbidden, only customary to avoid, and "it will not see blessing". Someone who must go to work may. The Rabbi ended with the hour between sunset and nightfall on Saturday night: do nothing active against Shabbat, such as changing shoes, and nothing active against Tisha BeAv, such as eating. The class resumed in Elul with Hilchot Shabbat.

Practical summary

  • When Tisha BeAv is on Sunday, seudah shelishit is a normal Shabbat meal, ending at sunset.
  • All seven restrictions apply for the full 25 hours; nothing relaxes at midday.
  • Study only the sad parts of Torah; Tehillim or musar are better than wasting the day.
  • Pregnant and nursing women begin the fast and may break it when they feel weak; within 30 days of birth there is no fast.
  • Washing for hygiene, deodorant and non-leather shoes are permitted; "good morning" is fine, conversation is not.
