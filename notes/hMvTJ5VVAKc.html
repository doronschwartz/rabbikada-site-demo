Topic: How one who cannot fast for medical reasons should eat and drink on Yom Kippur

Sources: A story of Rav Hayyim of Brisk

Daily Halacha: Yom Kippur

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Rav Hayyim of Brisk, known for strictness, was lenient with anyone who needed to break the fast on Yom Kippur, explaining that he was being strict about the Misvah of guarding health. Just as Hashem wants the healthy to fast, He wants those who medically cannot to eat; they should not feel they are doing wrong.
  • We rely on a doctor, even a non-Jewish one, and if doctors disagree we follow the one who says to eat.
  • If all doctors see no danger but the person genuinely feels it would be dangerous, he may eat and drink, as only he knows how he feels.
  • In imminent danger to life one eats as much as needed. One who knows from the outset he cannot fast should spread out small amounts rather than eat three meals.
  • Karet applies only to eating the size of a date (over about 30 grams) or drinking 44 ml or more within about five minutes. Less, or the same over a longer span, is still forbidden by the Torah but without Karet.
  • So eat under about 30 grams and drink under 40 ml, then wait about 10 minutes before the next portion.
  • Measuring is generally not allowed on Shabbat, Yom Tov or Yom Kippur, so measure beforehand. If not possible, one may measure on Yom Kippur with a scale or measuring cylinder.

In one line

One who medically must eat on Yom Kippur fulfils Hashem's will, and unless in imminent danger should eat small measured amounts spread across the day.
