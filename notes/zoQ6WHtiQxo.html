Topic: Extinguishing or lowering a flame on Yom Tov, and extinguishing indirectly through grama

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Thursday 29 May 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Recap: one may light from an existing flame; without one, a non-Jew may light if needed for candles or cooking, and otherwise one may not, barring the exceptional cases discussed.
  • Extinguishing is forbidden on Yom Tov, since it is generally not for okhel nefesh. This includes lowering or weakening a flame, not only putting it out.
  • When extinguishing serves the food it is permitted. The Shulhan Arukh's example: putting meat on the fire although its juices will extinguish some coals.
  • A more common example: food cooking too fast that will burn and not taste as it should. One may not switch off the flame, but may lower it so the food cooks properly.
  • What of a gas flame one does not want left on for two or three days of Yom Tov and Shabbat? Grama, indirect action, is permitted on Shabbat with any need, certainly for a misvah: for example, rainwater on a sukkah roof opened on Shabbat may fall on concrete and trickle into the grass, but not fall directly on the grass.
  • On Yom Tov, Hakham Ovadiah rules grama is certainly permitted with any need, and some say it is permitted outright. So one may put a full pot of water on the flame and let it boil over and extinguish it.
  • In sum: no extinguishing or lowering unless for the food, such as to stop it burning, but one may extinguish indirectly.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: meat juices extinguishing coals is permitted, as it serves the cooking.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: grama is permitted on Yom Tov with any need.

In one line

On Yom Tov you may lower a flame only to keep food from burning, but may extinguish it indirectly by letting a pot of water boil over.
