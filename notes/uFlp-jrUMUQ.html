Topic: The origins of a fifth cup at the Seder and the customs that followed

Sources: Gemara Pesahim (last chapter); Rabbi Tarfon; the Rif; the Rambam, Hilchot Hames UMassah; the Rosh; Rashi and Tosafot; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; the Vilna Gaon

The halacha

  • The four cups are in the last chapter of Pesahim. Our texts say Hallel is said over the fourth cup, including Hallel HaGadol ("Hodu LaShem ki tov, ki leolam hasdo").
  • The text of the Rif, the Rambam and the Rosh says Hallel on the fourth cup and Hallel HaGadol on a fifth, the view of the Tanna Rabbi Tarfon. Rashi and Tosafot said "fifth" is an error, and our texts came to read four; older Sepharadi texts had five.
  • Since it is one opinion, the Rambam rules four cups are required and a fifth is voluntary, and the Rosh likewise. The Temanim drink a voluntary fifth cup, following the Rambam.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules four cups and does not mention a fifth. The Rema writes, based on the Rambam and the Rif, that one who is thirsty may drink a fifth cup.
  • Pouring a fifth cup without drinking it developed much later in Europe. The Vilna Gaon explained it as the doubt between four and five, linked to Eliyahu HaNavi, who will resolve unresolved questions; this link is much later.
  • The Sepharadi custom is four cups, without pouring a fifth or the link to Eliyahu. Each should keep their own custom and not add. Still, one who wishes to drink a fifth cup has plenty to rely on.

Sources quoted

  • Gemara Pesahim: in the Rif's, Rambam's and Rosh's text, a fifth cup for Hallel HaGadol.
  • The Rambam and the Rosh: the fifth cup is voluntary.
  • Rashi and Tosafot: the correct text is four.
  • The Rema: a thirsty person may drink a fifth cup.
  • The Vilna Gaon: a fifth cup poured but not drunk, for the unresolved doubt.

In one line

Four cups are required; a fifth is voluntary per the Rambam, poured for Eliyahu by Ashkenazim, and not part of the Sepharadi custom, though one may drink it.
