Topic: The source and scope of the prohibition of castration

Sources: Parashat Emor; the Sifra; the Tosefta; the Gemara; the Rambam

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Castration is in this chapter, as in the Rambam, because one reason a pesua dakka may not marry is to deter castration.
  • The source is Parashat Emor, on blemishes that disqualify a sacrifice. After four blemishes of the reproductive organs, the verse adds "and in your land you shall not do" (uvearsekhem lo taasu).
  • Simply, it forbids offering such an animal and also forbids castrating, or neutering, an animal.
  • From these words the Hakhamim, in the Sifra, the Tosefta and elsewhere, learn four things. One: it applies even to an animal already blemished.
  • Two, as the Gemara says: even to a non-kosher animal that can never be offered, such as a dog.
  • Three: "in your land" is not geographic, as the Rambam explains; it applies everywhere in the world, whoever does it.
  • Four: it includes castrating a human being. Females will be discussed later.
  • Next: females, and indirectly causing impotency.

Sources quoted

  • Parashat Emor: "and in your land you shall not do".
  • The Sifra and Tosefta: the derivations from the verse.
  • The Rambam: the prohibition applies everywhere.

In one line

The Torah forbids castrating any animal, blemished or non-kosher, anywhere in the world, and a human being too.
