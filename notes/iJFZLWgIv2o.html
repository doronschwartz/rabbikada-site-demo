Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Even HaEzer, Hilchot Ishut Siman: 11, Seifim 1-3 Shiur: A Dayanut shiur by Dayan Daniel Kada on Zoom, Tuesday 19 November 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Where we are

The Rabbi continued Siman 11, Seifim 1 and 2: a married woman who is suspected of being with another man (the nikhshedet; the man is the nitan). Last week covered four of the opinions of the Rishonim. Today he finished the other three (the Maharam, the Rosh and the Ramban), then went through the Shulhan Arukh and the Rema, and the main points of the Beit Shemuel and the Helkat Mehokek. He followed the argument on a chart of all the opinions.

The Gemara in brief

If a woman is suspected with another man, must her husband divorce her (motsi), and may she later marry that man?

  • Rav says you need edim, which everyone reads as edei tumah: witnesses to the forbidden act itself.
  • Rebbe says a kol delo pasik (a rumour that does not stop) is already enough to force the husband out.
  • The conclusion: when there is a kol delo pasik together with davar mekhoar (a suspicious sign seen by witnesses, such as being found in a compromising situation), the halakhah is like Rebbe and she goes out. When there is only davar mekhoar with edim but no kol, the halakhah is like Rav and we do not force.
  • Two beraitot add that the nitan is not forced out if she has children from him, or if she married a third man in between.

The Maharam changes the picture. When Rav said "edim", he meant edei davar mekhoar, so witnesses to the suspicious behaviour count like witnesses to the act. The discussion of kol in the Gemara is then about a case with no such witnesses, only the husband's own sight. The Rabbi found it hard to understand why the Maharam still distinguishes cases of children (banim), since no one says that children help against edei tumah.

The Rosh goes back to the plain reading of the Sheiltot, and answers Rabbenu Tam's difficulty by saying that davar mekhoar with a kol delo pasik has the same status as edei tumah. But then the Rosh should not distinguish between children and no children, which is the problem the Helkat Mehokek raises later.

The Ramban says the idea of a kol matters only for the husband. For the nitan you do not need it, because when he marries her, that is the strongest kol. A marriage to a third man in between breaks the link.

The Shulhan Arukh

Seif 1. If the husband warned her (kinui) and she was alone with the man (setirah), and she did not drink the sotah waters, she is forbidden to her husband and in the same way to the nitan. Even if they married, and even with children, she must leave him.

The Rabbi then turned to a case without kinui: witnesses came and saw a davar mekhoar. If the husband divorced her because of this, she may not marry the nitan. The Rabbi stressed that this is the precise wording of the Rambam, and the Shulhan Arukh follows it. If the husband continued to live with her and later died, or divorced her for an unrelated reason, then the nitan is not forbidden to her. The husband is encouraged to divorce her in such a case, and she loses her ketubah. The condition for the nitan is a real kol delo pasik (defined in the Gemara as a day and a half, not started by enemies, and going continuously) together with the davar mekhoar. If they married anyway, she must leave, but if they have children, they may stay.

The Rabbi mentioned a variant in the text. Most editions read as if the husband himself found the signs, which would suggest the Maharam. The Gra and the old printings read it as the witnesses finding them, which is why the Shulhan Arukh is not following the Maharam.

Seif 2. If she married a third man in between, they do not have to separate, though they should not have married. This is clear: the Shulhan Arukh follows the Rif and the Rambam. For the husband you always need edei zenut. Where there are real edei zenut, even with many children she must be divorced.

Seif 3. The Rabbi only mentioned it at the end: whenever we say she must leave, it concerns her ketubah.

The Rema

The Rema is two lines but hard to place. If there are all the details of a kol (a kol delo pasik), together with davar mekhoar, the husband is forced to divorce her, but only if there are no children from the husband, so as not to bring suspicion of mamzerut onto them. If only one of the two is present, the husband is not forced out. For the nitan, if he married her, either one alone is enough to force him out (unless there are children).

Whom does he follow? The Rabbi worked it out:

  • Not the Maharam, for whom edei davar mekhoar alone is enough.
  • Not Rabbenu Tam, the Rif or the Rambam, who always need edei zenut for the husband.
  • So it must be the Rosh or the Ramban. The Beit Yoseph and the Darkhei Moshe bring the Rosh and not the Ramban.

For the nitan, a kol alone fits the Ramban, but a davar mekhoar alone does not obviously fit, since the Ramban's reason (the marriage strengthens the kol) does not work in the other direction. The Beit Shemuel explains that the Rema is making a compromise: for the husband he follows the Rosh, and for the nitan the Maharam. In both he adds the distinction of children, which the Rosh and the Maharam do not really hold.

The Rema adds a point about edut meyuhedet. Two witnesses who disagree about the time do not work in capital cases (dinei nefashot), but they do for monetary cases. Here the Rema says they do count: one saw a suspicious sign in the morning and one in the afternoon, or one saw them kissing and the other saw her putting on a belt.

In practice

Sefaradim follow the Shulhan Arukh. For the Rema's compromise, the Gra, the Arukh HaShulhan and the Pithei Teshuvah all rule like the Shulhan Arukh. The Pithei Teshuvah is concerned about a get meusah (a coerced get), so it is better to follow the Rif and the Rambam. The Rabbi said that many Aharonim therefore follow the Shulhan Arukh and not the Rema.

The Helkat Mehokek and the Beit Shemuel are both unhappy with the Rema's distinction of children, because according to the Rosh davar mekhoar with a kol is equated to edei tumah, and no one distinguishes children there.

The sign must be a real possibility. The Helkat Mehokek asks about a case where witnesses saw the couple do terrible things, but it is clear there was no time for an act. The Beit Shemuel says clearly that this is not like the Helkat Mehokek. Learning from the Rambam, he says the sign must suggest something that really happened. Seeing improper behaviour does not give licence to suspect an act at another time.

Only one element. If there is only a davar mekhoar or only a kol, the husband is not forced out, but the Helkat Mehokek and the Beit Shemuel say that lehatehilah the couple should not marry. The Beit Shemuel adds that the husband need not divorce, but he may, and has a right to.

Witnesses who spread the story. According to the Beit Shemuel, if the same witnesses who saw the davar mekhoar then go and spread it, that does not make a kol. There must be a kol that goes around anyway. The Drisha brings a mahloket here, but the Beit Shemuel rules leniently.

If the husband died or divorced later. The Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh apply the law only where the husband divorced because of the scandal. The Beit Shemuel rules like the Rosh: even if the husband died, a kol delo pasik is enough to forbid her to the nitan. The Rabbi said this is probably not how the Rambam holds, but that is how the Beit Shemuel rules.

Practical summary

  • To forbid a woman to the man she was suspected with, the Shulhan Arukh needs both a kol delo pasik and a davar mekhoar.
  • The Shulhan Arukh follows the Rif and the Rambam: the husband is forced out only on edei zenut, and for the nitan children, or a marriage in between, mean they need not separate.
  • The Rema makes a compromise between the Rosh and the Maharam, and adds a distinction of children that the Helkat Mehokek and the Beit Shemuel find difficult.
  • The Gra, the Arukh HaShulhan and the Pithei Teshuvah rule like the Shulhan Arukh, partly from concern about a get meusah.
  • According to the Beit Shemuel, a rumour spread by the witnesses is not a kol, and a sign that could not have led to an act is not enough.
  • If only one element is present, they are not forced to separate, but they should not marry lehatehilah.
