The halacha

  • The first berakhah: "lehadlik ner Hanukkah" or "lehadlik ner shel Hanukkah"? The Gemara, Rambam and Rif have "ner shel Hanukkah".
  • The Shulhan Arukh writes it without "shel", without comment in the Bet Yoseph, presumably the common practice of his time. It spread among the Oriental Sepharadim because the Arizal supported it: the initials of the three words spell Nahal, standing for three words of the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy.
  • Yet on Friday night all say "ner shel Shabbat". The Hida explains that the ner is the essence of Hanukkah, whereas lighting is one of many aspects of Shabbat.
  • The Spanish and Portuguese say "ner shel Hanukkah", as in the Gemara, Rambam and Rif and the old S&P siddur, without insisting on the acronym. Ashkenazi customs vary.
  • The second berakhah: Oriental Sepharadi and Ashkenazi siddurim say "bayamim hahem bazeman hazeh", in those days at this season (25 Kislev).
  • Many Rishonim had "uvazeman hazeh", "and in this time": miracles then, and miracles we await in our own lifetime. Many poskim disliked this and changed it.
  • The Spanish and Portuguese siddur preserves the old text, a powerful prayer for miracles in our days.

In one line

The Spanish and Portuguese say "lehadlik ner shel Hanukkah" and "uvazeman hazeh", preserving the older texts.
