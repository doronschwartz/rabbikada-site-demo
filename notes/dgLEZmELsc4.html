Topic: The berakhah on returning to a place where one was miraculously saved, and what counts as a miracle

Sources: The Mishnah; the Gemara; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Meiri; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Tuesday 18 February 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Unlike public miracle sites, this is relevant today. The Mishnah, codified by the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh, says one who returns to the place of a personal miracle blesses "sheasah li nes bamakom hazeh", each time after 30 days.
  • His children say "sheasah nes leavi bamakom hazeh", and his grandchildren "sheasah nissim laavotai bamakom hazeh".
  • What counts as a miracle is debated. The Gemara's examples: a traveller dying of thirst in the desert who finds water, or one seized by a lion and saved. Must it be supernatural, or a situation most people would not survive?
  • The Meiri holds any danger to life qualifies, even without a spectacular rescue: hiding from armed burglars, or being shot at and missed.
  • Other Rishonim require something out of the norm that most people would not survive, such as being found alive after days under a collapsed building. Some read this as requiring the supernatural, but that does not seem implied.
  • So being shot at and missed qualifies only for the Meiri, while surviving several shots, which usually kills, qualifies for both.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules like the most stringent view: the berakhah is said only where most people would have died, such as surviving a lion attack or being trapped under a large vehicle. Escaping danger that people often survive is not enough.

In one line

One returning to where he survived what usually kills says "sheasah li nes bamakom hazeh", as do his children and grandchildren.
