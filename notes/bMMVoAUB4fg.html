Topic: Pregnant and nursing women on Tisha BeAv, and Havdalah when Tisha BeAv follows Shabbat

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Sedakah Hussein

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Thursday 15 July 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara and Shulhan Arukh obligate pregnant and nursing women, and women more than 30 days after giving birth, to fast on Tisha BeAv.
  • Hakham Sedakah Hussein, sent from Aleppo to restore Torah in Baghdad in 1743 after a plague killed many of its Hakhamim, enacted that pregnant and nursing women should not fast, because of the danger of the heat.
  • It is wrong to take this as a blanket permission. They must begin the fast, and if they feel unable to complete it, they may eat.
  • On a postponed Tisha BeAv (the 9th on Shabbat, kept on Sunday), pregnant women are exempt.
  • Those fasting say only Borei Meorei HaEsh on Mosei Shabbat, with no spices, which restore the soul and are inappropriate now. On Sunday night they make Havdalah with HaGefen and HaMavdil, without spices.
  • One who must eat (a pregnant or nursing woman who feels weak, or anyone ill) must first make Havdalah, even on grape juice despite the severity of wine on Tisha BeAv, since it is for the misvah.
  • He or she should not make it on Mosei Shabbat in anticipation, but when the point of needing to eat is reached. The rest of the family may hear it then and fulfil their obligation.

In one line

Pregnant and nursing women begin the fast and eat if they cannot continue, and anyone who must eat after Shabbat first makes Havdalah on grape juice.
