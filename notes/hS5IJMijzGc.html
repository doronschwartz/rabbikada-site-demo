Topic: How the laws of mukseh apply on Yom Tov, and the two ways they differ from Shabbat

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Monday 30 June 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Mukseh on Shabbat is a rabbinic prohibition, mainly a safeguard: if people could move everything, they would end up tidying and rearranging the house and come to melakhah, losing the atmosphere of Shabbat.
  • Basically all the categories of mukseh on Shabbat apply equally on Yom Tov, with two exceptions.
  • First, foods. On Shabbat raw foods that cannot be eaten until cooked (flour, raw meat, raw potatoes) are mukseh, since they cannot be cooked. On Yom Tov one may cook and bake, so flour and the like are not mukseh. Nor are utensils used for food preparation (a grater, a masher, a bowl to knead dough), which on Shabbat would be a keli shemelakhto leissur. Mukseh means setting something aside in one's mind, and one does not set aside what one may use on Yom Tov.
  • Second, for okhel nefesh one may move mukseh normally and directly to reach food permitted on Yom Tov. If a phone, money, a purse or a credit card is left on a bowl of fruit, on Shabbat one could only shake it off with a shinui or lift the fruit and let it fall; on Yom Tov one may simply pick it up and move it away.
  • Not all food is permitted: an egg laid on Yom Tov (besah shenoldah) is mukseh, and so is fish caught on Yom Tov, for example by a non-Jew or bought by a neighbour, unless one knows for sure it was caught before Yom Tov.

In one line

Mukseh on Yom Tov is as on Shabbat, except that foods and food utensils are not mukseh, and mukseh may be moved normally to get to permitted food.
