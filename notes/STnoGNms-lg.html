Topic: The origin of the 100 blasts, hearing shofar alone, and where in the service each community blows

Sources: The Gemara; the Rishonim; the Arizal; the Rama; the Mishnah Berurah

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Wednesday 14 September 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • From the Gemara's time there was always more blowing during the prayer, some ten and some 30. Through the medieval period most Rishonim blew 30 and then a set in Musaf; 100 is recorded only in some places.
  • Because the Arizal required 100 Kabbalistically, it gradually became accepted. Old Mahzorim do not have it; the extra 40 are a development of the last 400 to 500 years. As he understands it, Temanim still blow 30 and a set in Musaf only.
  • So: 30 are biblical, the Musaf set is rabbinic, and the last 40 are custom.
  • The Musaf blowing was instituted within communal prayer. An individual, such as someone at home unable to come to synagogue, needs only the 30, which is why only 30 are blown in homes.
  • Among Ashkenazim the common custom was 40; the Rama records 30 with the berakhah and ten in Musaf. The Mishnah Berurah writes it is good to do 60.
  • Oriental Sepharadim blow 30 in the silent Amidah and 30 in the repetition, then ten at the end.
  • Ashkenazim, the Spanish and Portuguese and some Moroccan communities do not blow in the silent Amidah, which disturbs those praying. They blow 30 in the repetition, 30 in the Kaddish after it, and ten at the end.

In one line

Of the 100 blasts, 30 are biblical, the Musaf set rabbinic and the remaining 40 a later custom, and someone praying alone needs only the 30.
