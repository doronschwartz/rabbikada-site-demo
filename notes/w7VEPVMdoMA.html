Topic: The berakhah on zikim, comets or meteors

Sources: The Talmud; the Mishnah; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Thursday 6 February 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Talmud gives a special berakhah on zikim. The Rishonim explain it as either a comet or a meteor; both are accepted in halacha, so both require it.
  • A comet is an icy body that releases gas as it nears the sun, forming a tail. One visible in October 2024 was called the Comet of the Century; one who saw it in a clear sky would have said the berakhah.
  • A meteor is the streak of light when a meteoroid burns up entering the atmosphere.
  • Unusually, one may choose the berakhah: the Mishnah gives Oseh Maaseh Bereshit, and the Gemara adds "shekoho ugevurato malei olam", since these show Hashem's might. Most Rishonim read the Gemara's "two" as either one; some say both, but because of safek berakhot we say only one, whichever one prefers.
  • Earthquakes, thunder and lightning, coming next week, offer the same choice.
  • Fixed features such as seas and mountains may be blessed later while still there. These pass, so the berakhah must be said within toh kedei dibbur, about three or four seconds; after that it is unclear whether it may be said.

In one line

On a comet or meteor say either Oseh Maaseh Bereshit or "shekoho ugevurato malei olam", immediately on seeing it.
