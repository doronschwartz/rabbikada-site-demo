Topic: The status of glass, glass induction hobs and other stoves for Pesah

Sources: Rabbenu Tam; the Rosh; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; the Pri Hadash; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the Ben Ish Hai

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Thursday 3 April 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Earthenware (Keli Heres) cannot be kashered, as implied in the Torah and clear in the Hakhamim. The Shulhan Arukh holds that glazed or coated ceramics also cannot be kashered for Pesah.
  • The Rishonim disputed glass. Rabbenu Tam, the Rosh and others held that smooth, hard glass does not absorb, so it needs no kashering at all, even between meat and milk.
  • Many, mainly Ashkenazi, Rishonim likened glass to earthenware (made from sand at extreme heat), so it cannot be kashered.
  • A little-known third view, stated explicitly by the Rambam: glass is like metal, it can be kashered but needs kashering.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules like the Rosh and Rabbenu Tam: glass neither absorbs nor expels. One may use the same glass for coffee with milk and later chicken soup, and on Pesah all glassware, even ovenware, needs only washing.
  • The Rema follows the strict view, and this is the Ashkenazi practice. Some say his stringency was only for Pesah, not for meat and milk.
  • Sepharadi poskim, the Pri Hadash and Hakham Ovadiah follow the Shulhan Arukh. The Ben Ish Hai required Hagalah. Without a family custom, follow the Shulhan Arukh; with a strict family custom, keep it.
  • So for Sepharadim a glass induction hob needs only thorough cleaning.
  • Other stoves: one view required Libbun, but the authorities disagreed, since any hames that fell on it is burnt by the same fire. Strictly, cleaning would suffice; for Pesah we pour hot water on it, and one may be stricter by leaving it on high heat.
