Topic: The origin and status of giving Mahasit HaShekel before Purim, and when to give it

Sources: Masekhet Soferim; the Rosh; the Hida; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Beit Yoseph; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Purim, Monday 7 March 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Parashat Shekalim is the first of the four special portions. In Temple times the daily Korban Tamid came from communal funds, raised by every Jew giving half a shekel each year, renewed annually from Nisan. So from 1 Adar they announced the collection, and we read Parashat Shekalim to recall it.
  • The minor tractate Masekhet Soferim, written after the Talmud (so the Rosh; the Hida places it in the times of the Geonim, about the 7th or 8th century), records the custom of giving half a coin before Parashat Zakhor, the Shabbat before Purim, to recall the Temple half shekel. This is the first source.
  • Since it is not in the Talmud, the Rambam, the Shulhan Arukh and the Beit Yoseph do not record it. The Rema brings Ashkenazi Rishonim who mention it.
  • It later became the custom among Sepharadim too, but it is a custom. The four obligatory Misvot of Purim are Megillah reading, gifts to the poor, Mishloah Manot and the festive meal, and this does not share their status.
  • The Rema says it was given on the night of Purim, others on Taanit Esther afternoon. Many poskim say it may be given from 1 Adar, and that earlier is preferable.
  • How much, who gives, where the money goes and which funds may be used come tomorrow.

Sources quoted

  • Masekhet Soferim: the first source for giving half a coin before Purim.
  • The Rosh and the Hida: Soferim is post-Talmudic.
  • The Rema: the custom among Ashkenazi Rishonim; given on the night of Purim.

In one line

Mahasit HaShekel is a post-Talmudic custom recalling the Temple half shekel, best given early in Adar.
