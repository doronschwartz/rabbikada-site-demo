Topic: The festivals as moadim, meeting times, and dividing the day "half for HaShem, half for yourselves"

The points

  • Besides Yamim Tovim, the Torah calls the festivals moadim ("Elleh moadei HaShem"). Moed comes from meeting, as in the Ohel Moed: a time of meeting between us and HaKadosh Barukh Hu.
  • Shabbat is our weekly meeting, set by HaShem; the festivals are meetings we set. The Gemara asks how a berakhah can end with two things, "Mekaddesh Yisrael vehazemanim", and answers it is one: HaShem sanctifies Israel, and Israel sanctify the times. Once through the Sanhedrin fixing the month, and now through our fixed calendar, we put the meeting in the diary.
  • One verse calls the festival a time "for you", another "for HaShem". In the Gemara, Rabbi Yehoshua reconciles them: half the day for HaShem in tefillah and Torah, half for ourselves in eating and drinking. Rabbi Eliezer says each person may choose either.
  • The Rambam, the Shulhan Arukh and the Rishonim follow Rabbi Yehoshua. The Rambam describes it: rise, pray and study until midday, then eat, drink and enjoy the rest of the day, "hesyo laShem vehesyo lakhem". It need not be measured exactly.
  • Tefillah counts toward the half for HaShem, as the Rambam makes clear. The Maharshal (Rabbi Shlomo Luria, Yam shel Shlomo) adds that a hazzan drawing out the tefillah at great length does not add to it: everything spiritual counts, within reason.
  • Torah is a major aspect of the festival, which is why there are derashot in synagogue on the hag and Hol HaMoed.
  • Each person must balance not neglecting family and self with not neglecting HaKadosh Barukh Hu, using the time off work for one's spiritual identity.

Sources quoted

In one line

The festivals are meetings we set with HaShem, divided between Torah and tefillah and wholesome enjoyment.
