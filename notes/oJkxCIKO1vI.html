Topic: Why no berakhah is said on the Dead Sea, and the berakhah on rivers

Sources: The Mishnah; the Midrash quoted by Rashi in Parashat Lekh Lekha; Rambam; Tosafot; Shulhan Arukh; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Tuesday 4 February 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • On an ordinary sea, such as the North Sea or the Red Sea, one says Oseh Maaseh Bereshit.
  • The Dead Sea, Yam HaMelah, is called a sea in the Torah, yet the poskim conclude no berakhah is said on it. The berakhah praises what was made at creation, and the Midrash, quoted by Rashi in Lekh Lekha, says it formed after the destruction of Sedom.
  • These berakhot apply only after a gap of 30 days; one who goes regularly says none.
  • The Mishnah also gives Oseh Maaseh Bereshit on rivers, and the Rambam writes it without limitation, which would include the Thames after 30 days.
  • But Tosafot and other Rishonim, followed by the Shulhan Arukh, say it only on rivers known to exist from creation. Since we cannot know how a river formed or what human intervention it had, in practice only the four rivers of Bereshit (Hiddekel, Gihon, Pishon and Perat) qualify, and even these are not clearly identified.
  • So Sepharadim do not say a berakhah on rivers. Whether one may assume a big river is natural is left doubtful (sarikh iyun), so even for Ashkenazim it seems not to be said, though one may say "Barukh Oseh Maaseh Bereshit" without Hashem's name.

In one line

No berakhah on the Dead Sea, and in practice none with Hashem's name on rivers, only on seas.
