Topic: Why women are obligated in Hanukkah, and the minhag of women not working while the lights burn

Sources: Tosafot; Rashi; the Tur; the Ben Ish Hai; Mishnah Berurah

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Women are equally obligated in Hanukkah although lighting is a time-bound positive misvah.
  • Tosafot: they too were under the Greeks' decree and were saved, so they share the duty of thanksgiving. Rashi: the miracle came through a woman, Yehudit daughter of Yohanan, whose execution of a Greek general set the victory in motion.
  • Work is permitted on Hanukkah, for men and women; only fasting and eulogies are forbidden. Yet a medieval minhag, brought by the Tur, has women refrain from work at certain times.
  • Refraining all eight days is deemed inappropriate by the poskim, with no real source; he knows of no community that does it. Refraining on the first and eighth days is brought by the Ben Ish Hai, who told the women of his community to do so.
  • The generally accepted practice, for Sepharadim and Ashkenazim (Mishnah Berurah), is to refrain during the first half hour after the candles are lit: a good, ancient custom.
  • Two reasons: women were more often near the Menorah and might use its light, which is forbidden; or, since the miracle came through a woman, they took on this voluntary minhag.
  • "Melakhah" here means arduous work forbidden on Hol HaMoed, such as laundering and sewing; cooking and frying are fine.
  • The Ben Ish Hai was told of a Jerusalem practice: a woman needing a particular salvation would refrain for a day or two of Hanukkah, to connect more to it.

In one line

Women share fully in Hanukkah, and by an old custom refrain from arduous work for the first half hour the lights burn.
