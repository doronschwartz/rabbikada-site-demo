The Five Fundamental Principles of Melechet Tzeidah

Principle 1: Be-Mino Nitzod (Commonly Trapped Species)

Core Rule: Only trapping a creature that is commonly trapped (be-mino nitzod) is forbidden min HaTorah (from the Torah).

Background: The purpose of trapping in the Mishkan was to obtain animal hides for the second roof covering. The animals were trapped specifically to be used.

Practical Implications:

  • Trapping a creature that is not commonly trapped = only assur mi-derabbanan (rabbinically prohibited)
  • Example: Trapping a mouse for a scientific experiment would only be derabbanan, since mice are not commonly trapped
  • Example: Trapping flies is only derabbanan for two reasons:
  1. Flies are not commonly trapped (ein be-mino nitzod)
  2. You don't want to use the fly

Key Point: To be forbidden min HaTorah, you must trap the creature and intend to use it.

Principle 2: Bishchiya Achat (Accessible in One Hand Gesture)

Core Rule: To be chayav (liable) min HaTorah, the trapped creature must be accessible in one hand gesture (bishchiya achat).

What This Means:

  • Either the creature is literally in your hand, OR
  • It's in an enclosed space where you can grab it in one motion without extra effort

Examples:

  • ✓ Deer in a small, one-room house = trapped (chayav)
  • ✓ Fish in a small bowl = trapped (chayav)
  • ✗ Monkey in a large house = NOT fully trapped (only derabbanan) - requires effort to catch
  • ✗ Fish in a swimming pool = NOT fully trapped (only derabbanan)
  • ✗ Lion in a regular room = NOT fully trapped - requires a very tight enclosure

The Rambam's Formulation (Hilchot Shabbat, Chapter 10):

"If you trap them in a place where they are not sufficiently trapped... you are exempt."

Principle 3: No Opposite Melacha

Core Rule: Unlike other melachot, there is NO corollary/opposite melacha for Tzeidah.

Comparison to Other Melachot:

  • Kotev (writing) ↔ Mochek (erasing)
  • Boneh (building) ↔ Soter (demolishing)
  • Tofer (sewing) ↔ Ko'reiah (tearing)
  • Tzad (trapping) ↔ Nothing

Practical Implication: It is completely mutar (permitted) to release an animal from captivity on Shabbat. There was no such action in the Mishkan, therefore no prohibition exists.

Principle 4: Nitzod Ve-Omed (Already Considered Trapped)

Core Rule: If a creature is already incapable of escaping, it's considered "nitzod ve-omed" (already trapped), and further "trapping" is only derabbanan or completely mutar.

Principle 5: The Melacha is the Action, Not the Result

Core Rule: The prohibition is the action (ma'aseh) of trapping. There is no problem with animals being trapped on Shabbat without your direct action.

Practical Application (Siman 252): You may set up a trap before Shabbat and allow animals to be trapped during Shabbat. The melacha requires your active involvement in the trapping.

Shulchan Aruch - Detailed Analysis

Se'if Aleph: Basic Definition of Tzeidah

Text Summary: If you trap a sparrow (tzipor dror) into a tower where it's trapped, or other birds/deer into a house or small enclosure where they're trapped - chayav. If the enclosure is too large and they're not "trapped" - patur aval assur.

Important Chayei Adam (Mishnah Berurah):

Scenario: A bird flies into your house on Shabbat through an open window. Now you want to close the window because it's cold. Can you?

Analysis:

  • Closing the window seems like psik reisha (inevitable consequence) - you'll trap the bird
  • But the bird isn't accessible in one go in a large room = only derabbanan

Ruling: The Chayei Adam permits closing if:

  1. You're closing because you're cold, NOT to trap the bird
  2. The bird cannot be accessed in one go (too small/elusive)

Halachic Reasoning:

  • For Sephardim: Psik reisha d'rabbanan = mutar (following Shulchan Aruch)
  • For Ashkenazim: Multiple factors combine:
  • Psik reisha (unintended)
  • Not accessible in one go (derabbanan)
  • Melacha she-eina tzricha l'gufa (don't need the result)
  • The Rashba's view that unintended trapping isn't tzeidah at all

Critical Caveat: If you want to keep the creature, you CANNOT close the door, even if you're also cold. Having dual intentions doesn't create a heter.

The Rama: Using a Dog to Hunt

Rama's Statement: "One who incites a dog after an animal on Shabbat - this is tzeidah."

Difficulty: This seems to imply it's d'oraita, but where is the person's direct action?

Rambam's Position: You're only chayav if you COMBINE your efforts with the dog:

  • You chase alongside the dog
  • You stand in front of the animal to startle it while the dog approaches
  • This is a toladah (derivative) of tzeidah

Resolution (Mishnah Berurah): The Rama means tzeidah derabbanan. Sending a dog alone to trap = assur derabbanan (not d'oraita).

Or Zarua's Addition: Using dogs for hunting is "not proper practice" - it's the way of non-Jews who do it for entertainment. The Pri Megadim clarifies: for parnassa (livelihood) it's permitted.

Noda Bi'Yehudah's Famous Teshuvah on Hunting:

  • Hunting for food or practical use = permitted
  • Hunting purely for entertainment/leisure = not proper Jewish practice

Se'if Gimmel: The Ein Be-Mino Nitzod Rule

Text: "Any creature whose species is commonly trapped - chayav. Whose species is NOT commonly trapped - patur aval assur."

Application to Flies:

  • Not commonly trapped = patur
  • Still assur to trap

Rama's Important Addition: Therefore, one should be careful not to close a small box or container that has flies in it on Shabbat.

Why "small" box?

  • Small box = accessible in one go = one derabbanan
  • Large box = NOT accessible in one go = two derabbanans

Key Principle: Psik reisha with TWO derabbanans is mutar even for Ashkenazim.

Major Chiddush: Safek Psik Reisha (Taz's Innovation)

The Question

Scenario: You have a small box. You're not sure if there are flies inside or not. Can you close it?

Why This is Different from Regular Davar She-Eino Mitkaven:

Type

Nature of Doubt

Example

Davar She-Eino Mitkaven

Doubt is about whether the ACTION will cause a melacha

Dragging a bench - will it make a groove?

Safek Psik Reisha

Doubt is EXTERNAL to the action - is there something there?

Closing a box - are there flies inside?

The Conceptual Difference:

  • Davar she-eino mitkaven: The action itself is uncertain (will dragging cause a groove?)
  • Safek psik reisha: The action is certain (closing definitely traps), but an external fact is uncertain (are there flies?)

The Machloket

Taz's Position: Safek psik reisha = mutar (like davar she-eino mitkaven)

  • Even though the doubt is external, there's still uncertainty about whether a melacha will occur
  • Therefore, it has the same status as davar she-eino mitkaven

Biur Halacha's Initial Objection:

  • If there IS a creature inside, you're DEFINITELY doing a ma'aseh tzeidah
  • This should be safek d'oraita!
  • The doubt being "external" makes it WORSE, not the same

Rabbi Akiva Eiger's Parallel Case (Yoreh De'ah - Basar B'Chalav):

  • You're in a communal cooking area with non-Jews
  • If you stoke the fire, and a non-Jew's pot has milk and meat, you're cooking them together
  • Initial thought: This should be assur!
  • Conclusion: It must be mutar - otherwise Jews could never stoke fires in shared spaces

The Philosophical Underpinning: Why is Psik Reisha Assur?

Two Possible Reasons:

View 1 - Kavana Theory (Taz's understanding):

  • Because the result is 100% certain, you MUST have some subconscious intention for it
  • You can't sincerely say "I didn't intend this"
  • Therefore: If there's uncertainty (safek), you CAN sincerely say you didn't intend it → Mutar

View 2 - Mityaches Theory (Biur Halacha's initial understanding):

  • Psik reisha isn't about kavana at all
  • Because the result is 100% certain, it's attributed (mityaches) to your action
  • It's inherently included in your action, regardless of intent
  • Therefore: Even with safek, if the creature IS there, the result is attributed to you → Assur

Attribution: Rav Shimon Shkop explains this machloket in his analytical work.

Halachic Conclusion

Biur Halacha's Final Ruling: The Taz is correct.

He brings proofs from:

  • Tosafot (inconclusive)
  • Rambam (conclusive)
  • The practical necessity argument

Practical Halacha: Safek psik reisha is MUTAR.

Chacham Ovadia's Psak: Agrees - safek psik reisha is mutar.

Summary: When performing an action that would definitely cause a melacha IF a certain external condition exists, but you're uncertain whether that condition exists - it is permitted.
