Topic: Two principles behind which foods need a Hekhsher for Pesah

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Monday 8 March 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Knowing what needs a Hekhsher can save people a great deal of money, and the Gemara says in many places that the Torah cares about the money of Israel.
  • Usually a forbidden food less than one sixtieth of a mixture is nullified, as a drop of milk in a meat dish with sixty times as much meat. On Pesah, Hames is forbidden in any amount (Hames BeMashehu): even a drop forbids the whole dish.
  • The reason is that Hames will become permitted after Pesah (Davar SheYesh Lo Mattirin), unlike other prohibitions, which would forbid the dish forever.
  • This applies only when Hames falls into food on Pesah itself. If it happened before Pesah with sixty times as much other food, it is nullified and may be eaten on Pesah.
  • Some argue the nullified Hames reawakens when Pesah arrives. The Shulhan Arukh and most Sepharadi Hakhamim hold it does not, so Sepharadim may eat such food. The Rema, for Ashkenazim, is stricter.
  • Second principle: in Kashrut we do not presume something forbidden has happened unless we see it. The Shulhan Arukh and most Rishonim apply this to Pesah too, so food not guarded from Hames remains permitted. The Rema is again stricter.
  • The Rema, since Pesah has the unique stringency of no nullification, adds further stringencies; for the Shulhan Arukh that is the only one, and everything else stays as in ordinary Kashrut.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: the Torah cares about the money of Israel.
  • Shulhan Arukh: nullified Hames does not reawaken on Pesah, and we do not presume Hames fell in.
  • The Rema: stricter on both points.

In one line

For Sepharadim, Hames nullified before Pesah stays nullified and we do not presume Hames is present, which determines what needs a Hekhsher.
