Topic: Why we eat karpas, which vegetable, and how much

Sources: The Rishonim; the Gemara; the Rambam

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Friday 24 March 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Karpas is a rabbinic institution, with two reasons in the Rishonim. One: to make the evening unusual and arouse curiosity, since we end up washing twice and eat a vegetable before the meal. Two: it is a mark of aristocracy, like hors d'oeuvres before a meal.
  • The Gemara does not say karpas, only a vegetable. Unlike maror, any vegetable does the job, which is why many Ashkenazim use potatoes, probably for lack of celery or parsley in Europe.
  • Karpas became the custom. One reason given: its letters contain perekh, hard labour, plus samekh, numerically 60, alluding to the 600,000 Jews in Egypt. Sepharadim use celery or parsley, according to custom.
  • The Borei Peri HaAdamah on the karpas is meant to cover the maror, eaten within the meal, which is why we say only "al akhilat maror" on it.
  • So we eat less than a kazayit (about 27 grams) of karpas, to avoid an after-blessing that would close the berakhah. The Rambam and others held karpas, as a rabbinic eating, needs a kazayit.
  • In practice we eat less than a kazayit, and one who ate a kazayit still does not say an after-blessing, letting the HaAdamah cover the maror.

Sources quoted

  • The Rishonim: the two reasons for karpas.
  • The Gemara: any vegetable.
  • The Rambam: karpas requires a kazayit.

In one line

Karpas is any vegetable dipped before the meal, to stir curiosity or as an hors d'oeuvre, eaten in less than a kazayit with no after-blessing.
