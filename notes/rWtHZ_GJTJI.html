Topic: Megillat Taanit and the first reason: the Torah translated into Greek on 8 Tevet

Sources: Megillat Taanit (attributed in the Gemara to Hananiah ben Hizkiyah); the Gemara on the 72 sages

Daily Halacha: Asarah BeTevet

The points

  • The fast of Asarah BeTevet is from Divrei Nevi'im, a middle category between Torah and rabbinic misvot, like reading the Megillah. It is the only fast that can fall on a Friday.
  • Megillat Taanit, which precedes the Gemara and is attributed to Hananiah ben Hizkiyah in the time of Hillel and Shammai, records the days of tragedy, and gives three reasons why this period is sad.
  • First: on 8 Tevet, about 300 BCE, King Talmai (Ptolemy), successor of Alexander of Macedon, had the Torah translated into Greek (the Septuagint) for his great library. To avoid a "politically correct" version he had the Kohen Gadol send 72 sages and shut them in separate rooms.
  • The Gemara says that in 13 places, where a literal translation would mislead or offend the king, all 72 made the same change. For example "Naaseh adam", "Let us make man", became "I shall make man".
  • Hazal say darkness came on the world for three days. Translation loses the original context, as Shakespeare does; and in Hebrew a word (davar) is also a thing (davar): the language is not only descriptive but causative.
  • Today we learn in many languages to make Torah accessible, but the original way is from the Hebrew text itself.

In one line

8 Tevet, when the Torah was first translated into Greek, is the first of three sad days leading to Asarah BeTevet, because the Torah's meaning lives in its own language.
