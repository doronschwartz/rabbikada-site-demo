Topic: The Rambam's "presumed mamzer" and Rabbi Akiva Eiger's leniency; a child born soon after the husband returns

Sources: The Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; Rabbi Akiva Eiger; the Gemara; the Rama

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 15 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Rambam writes that a child born more than 12 months after the husband left is "presumed" (behezkat) a mamzer. Some infer that even for him it is only a presumption, so another doubt may allow leniency.
  • Rabbi Akiva Eiger: if this happened in a city whose majority is non-Jewish, the child could be kasher even according to the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh.
  • His reasoning: a child of a married Jewish woman from another man is a mamzer only if that man is Jewish. With a non-Jewish man, though it is a terrible thing to do, the child is not a mamzer. This will be explained later in the chapter.
  • So the doubts combine: the Rambam's presumption is not certainty, some hold the child is kasher after 12 months, and the majority of the city is non-Jewish. Every factor would need to be weighed in such a question.
  • The opposite case: a husband returns after years away and his wife gives birth a few months later. At seven months it is certainly fine, as was known in the Gemara's times and today.
  • Some Rishonim, brought by the Rama, accept even five months; less than that, the child would be a mamzer as it is not possible. Today we know very premature births happen and can be assessed medically, so this is less practical.
