Topic: Why the Greeks banned Shabbat, Rosh Hodesh and Berit Milah

Sources: The Gemara (Rabbi Akiva and the Roman general)

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Hanukkah commemorates an ideological victory, not only a physical one. The Greeks pressed Hellenism on the Jews, banning Shabbat, Rosh Hodesh and Berit Milah, and above all Torah study.
  • Greek philosophy was hedonism: life is enjoyment of the body, "eat and drink for tomorrow we die", the person being the sum of his drives.
  • There are three reasons a person would not try to change his nature: he does not believe in God; he is a deist, believing God created the world and left it without Hashgahah; or he believes in God's involvement but thinks one cannot transcend one's nature ("that's who I am"), which contradicts everything we believe, Yom Kippur above all.
  • The three banned misvot answer these: Shabbat affirms a first Creator; Rosh Hodesh symbolises renewal and God's constant involvement and Hashgahah; Berit Milah teaches that we must change ourselves.
  • When a Roman general asked Rabbi Akiva why God did not create man circumcised, he answered that the point is that we do it, mastering our passions.
  • Above all is Torah, without which we lose everything. This is why on Hanukkah, when Rosh Hodesh always falls, we strengthen Shabbat, Torah and our commitment to tradition in dark times.

In one line

The Greeks targeted Shabbat, Rosh Hodesh and Milah because they affirm a Creator, His providence and our power to rise above our nature.
