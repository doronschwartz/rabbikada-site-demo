Topic: Why the Hakhamim instituted Eruv Tavshilin, and what must be set aside

Sources: The Gemara; the Rambam; the Rif; Tosafot; the Rosh; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Sunday 29 September 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • This year, outside Israel, Rosh Hashanah, the first days of Sukkot and Shemini Aseret with Simhat Torah each fall on Thursday and Friday into Shabbat. In Israel this applies to Rosh Hashanah.
  • On Yom Tov one may cook only for that day of Yom Tov, not for a weekday, nor from the first day to the second, which is Yom Tov only rabbinically.
  • By Torah law one may cook on Yom Tov for Shabbat, since Shabbat is far holier than Yom Tov, so this is not preparing for a weekday.
  • The Hakhamim nevertheless required Eruv Tavshilin, beginning Shabbat preparations before Yom Tov. The Gemara gives two reasons: so that the needs of Shabbat are not neglected amid the Yom Tov, harming both days; and so that people, many of whom think Yom Tov holier than Shabbat, do not conclude one may cook from Yom Tov for a weekday.
  • One sets aside a cooked dish before Yom Tov, showing one has Shabbat in mind.
  • Most Rishonim (the Rambam, the Rif and others) say a cooked dish, such as meat, fish or eggs, suffices. Tosafot and the Rosh require a cooked dish for cooking and a baked one for baking.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules to set aside both in the first instance, but after the fact a cooked dish alone permits cooking and baking for Shabbat.

In one line

Eruv Tavshilin, ideally a cooked and a baked item set aside before Yom Tov, permits preparing on Yom Tov for Shabbat.
