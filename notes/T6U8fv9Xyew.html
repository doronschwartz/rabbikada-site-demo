Topic: Where the minhag of dressing up on Purim is first recorded, and four reasons given for it

Sources: The Mahari Mintz; the Megillah; Rashi on Parashat Hukkat; Tosafot at the beginning of Rosh Hashanah; a piyyut for Shabbat Zakhor

Daily Halacha: Purim, Tuesday 19 March 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • For many, dressing up is an essential part of Purim, yet it is not mentioned in the Talmud, the Rambam, the Rif or even the Shulhan Arukh.
  • To the best of his knowledge it is first recorded by the Mahari Mintz, Rabbi Yehudah Mintz of Italy in the late 15th century, who writes that people dress up and even discusses cross-dressing, to be covered later.
  • First reason: the whole story is about reversal ("venahafokh hu"). Haman wanted to be paraded and ends up parading Mordekhai; he wanted to kill the Jews and is himself killed. So we dress up and cannot be recognised.
  • Second: to preserve the dignity of the poor receiving Matanot LaEvyonim, so they cannot see who gave to them.
  • Third: Rashi on "the Kenaani dwelling in the south" in Parashat Hukkat says it was really Amalek, who spoke the Kenaani dialect to confuse Israel. Tosafot at the start of Rosh Hashanah adds they changed their clothing too, as a piyyut said on Shabbat Zakhor mentions. We dress up to remember this.
  • Fourth: the Megillah is about hiddenness. HaShem's name is never mentioned and He acts discreetly, so we mask ourselves to recall HaKadosh Barukh Hu directing events unseen.
  • These are lovely ideas, but tomorrow we will see the origins may be murky and problematic.

Sources quoted

  • The Mahari Mintz: the first record of dressing up on Purim.
  • Rashi and Tosafot: Amalek disguised its speech and its clothes.

In one line

Dressing up on Purim is first recorded in 15th-century Italy and is explained by reversal, the dignity of the poor, Amalek's disguise and HaShem's hiddenness.
