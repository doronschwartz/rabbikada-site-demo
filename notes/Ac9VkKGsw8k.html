Topic: The first two solutions for a mamzer: a child through a non-Jewish woman, and annulling the first marriage

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Wednesday 3 June 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A mamzer's children remain mamzerim even when he or she marries someone permitted, such as a mamzeret or a convert. There are three possible solutions.
  • First: a male Jewish mamzer's child with a non-Jewish woman follows the mother and is a non-Jew with no taint; once converted, the child is a regular ger or giyoret and may marry a Jew.
  • Its problems: it works only for a male mamzer, since a mamzeret's child with a non-Jew remains a mamzer. A Beit Din will not normally convert a child under 18 without full parental consent, and it would be hard to find an Orthodox Beit Din to convert a child living with a non-Jewish mother. Above all, a relationship with a non-Jewish woman is forbidden, so this is not acceptable advice. If it has already happened, each case would be judged on its merits.
  • Second: retroactively annul the first marriage. If, for example, she divorced only civilly and had children with a second man, annulling the first marriage means she was never married to the first husband.
  • Then the children of the second man are not mamzerim, and any children of the first husband are simply born out of wedlock, which in halacha has no effect on them.
  • The basic method: a kosher marriage depends on its two witnesses, not the rabbi or Beit Din. If one witness was not fully kasher, for example not fully observant of Shabbat, the marriage never took effect. That is for a Beit Din to investigate.
  • This is not always possible, as the witnesses may be of impeccable character. That leads to the third solution, the most controversial, complicated and effective.

In one line

A mamzer's line can be freed through a convert child of a non-Jewish mother, which is forbidden, or by annulling the first marriage through a flaw in its witnesses.
