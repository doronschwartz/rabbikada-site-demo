Sefer: Derech Hashem by Rabbi Moshe Hayim Luzzatto (the Ramhal) Section: Part 1, Chapter 3, paragraphs 9-11 Shiur: From Dayan Daniel Kada's Derech Hashem class for the Hazak girls' group (Wednesday evenings), Wednesday 20 January 2021. It fills a gap in his Derech Hashem series at Tiferet Eyal, where these paragraphs were taught but not recorded.

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Review

Body and soul pull in opposite directions for the limited time we live; afterwards the soul is in control. The sin of Adam made three changes: the bad became part of us, repair became harder, and there is more to do, since we must first return to Adam's level before the sin and then rise higher.

Paragraph 9: decay and renewal

Because of the sin, we cannot reach perfection in our present bodies. There must first be a period of hefsed, loss or decay: death for man, and destruction for the world. The soul can refine the body only after it has left it, the body has broken down, and the two are rebuilt at the resurrection. The world too will be destroyed and renewed: the Gemara says it will exist for 6,000 years, counted from Adam, then lie desolate, and then Hashem will renew it.

Before the sin, had Adam chosen well, the soul would have elevated his body and there would have been no death. Now every good choice creates that refinement in potential, and it is realised only after death and resurrection.

So, the Rabbi stressed, "Olam HaBa isn't up there. Olam HaBa is right down here": this physical world, renewed and more spiritual, with refined bodies that no longer have the same physical pulls. The body takes part in the reward because it took part in the work.

The Rambam, he explained, held a different view: the world will not be destroyed, and eternity is for souls alone, without bodies; he held that the resurrection is only for a limited period. Most thinkers follow the view the Ramhal presents here.

Where is the reward now?

"Someone died yesterday, a great righteous person. What's happening to him?" He is waiting. The waiting period is Gan Eden and Gehinnom, which is not the world to come; Olam HaBa comes after the resurrection.

The reward will also differ from person to person. The more the soul has overcome the body in this life, the more it will refine the body later, the less of a barrier there will be between us and Hashem, and the more we can cleave to Him. Karet, being cut off, means having no eternal identity at all; the Ramhal says later that almost everyone reaches Olam HaBa, but entering is only the beginning.

Questions

Why did the Rambam see it differently? The Rambam regarded our physical nature as something lowly, and held that a relationship with God is impossible within a physical body. The Ramhal agrees that it is impossible in this body, but holds that we will have a different, refined body. The Rabbi returned to his analogy of someone coming home after years on the moon: one view says throw the spacesuit away, the other says keep it, with respect, as part of who you were.

Karet and teshuvah. There are 36 sins that carry karet out of the 613, about half of them forbidden relationships, and others such as eating on Yom Kippur or not having a berit milah. Teshuvah still helps, as with every sin.

What about Jews who never kept these laws? Many Jews eat hames on Pesah. The Rabbi showed the Rambam's ruling on the tinok shenishba, the child raised in captivity: someone raised without Torah is considered compelled, even if he later hears about it. "Why should you be held responsible for something you weren't taught? That's a very, very important principle."

Where does halacha fit, if the body counts for so little? A participant pointed out that halacha is all about raising the physical. The Rabbi said this is complementary, not a contradiction. Halacha means walking: it brings Hashem into the whole day, not only Shabbat and Yom Tov. When I put tefillin on my arm, the arm is the means by which I connect to Hashem. In Olam HaBa there will be no misvot of this kind; whether misvot continue in the days of Mashiah is a separate discussion.

Could the world's "decay" be climate change? "Maybe. I don't know." We would have to be cautious, because while we are in this world Hashem wants us to look after it.

Paragraph 10: the time of reward

The time and place of the reward is after the resurrection, in the renewed world, body and soul together. There will be levels, according to the work each person did and how much they sought perfection: the soul "will glow and set fire to the body", and both will enjoy Hashem's goodness.

Paragraph 11: the world of souls

During the separation, each part needs its place. The body returns to the earth it came from and breaks down there: "you are dust, and to dust you will return." The Rabbi noted the stories of great saddikim whose bodies did not decay; whether or not they happened, the idea is that a truly great person's body needs less of that process.

For the soul, Hashem created Olam HaNeshamot, the world of souls, which we call Gan Eden. There the souls rest and receive "me'ein", a taste, of the reward to come. Just as Shabbat is me'ein Olam HaBa, one sixtieth of it, Gan Eden is a foretaste, not the real thing. So blessing someone with Gan Eden limits the blessing: what we really want is Olam HaBa. Even there, each soul's place depends on its deeds.

Gehinnom, which the Ramhal discusses in Part 2, is a cleansing for those who reach the world to come with bruises still to be removed. Some of that cleansing happens through suffering in this world; it never lasts more than twelve months, which is why Kaddish is said for twelve months.

Summary

  • Because of the sin, man must die and the world be destroyed before both are renewed.
  • The true Olam HaBa is in this world, renewed, with body and soul together, after the resurrection.
  • Until then the soul waits in Gan Eden, the world of souls, which is only a foretaste.
  • Each person's reward depends on how far they refined themselves in this life.
  • Someone raised without Torah is not held responsible as one who sinned knowingly.
