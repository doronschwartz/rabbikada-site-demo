Topic: The mountain held over Sinai: coercion or protection

Sources: The Gemara (kafah aleihem har kegigit); Mekhilta, Parashat Yitro

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Thursday 13 May 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Gemara says HaKadosh Barukh Hu held the mountain over the people: accept the Torah, or here will be your burial. It is a negative image of coercion.
  • The Mekhilta on Parashat Yitro says He held the mountain over them to protect them, as they were afraid of the thunder and lightning.
  • The two accounts complement each other: we were coerced to accept the Torah, but the coercion was an act of love, for our good and our protection.
  • We can feel the Misvot as a mountain about to bury us, a burden, or as a mountain sheltering and elevating us.
  • We are all forced to do things at some stage, but as we mature and learn we come to see the Torah's beauty and that it is our protection; without it life is meaningless.

In one line

The mountain over Sinai was both coercion and protection, as the Torah ultimately shelters and gives meaning.
