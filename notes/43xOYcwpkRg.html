Topic: The restrictions of Tisha BeAv itself: Torah study, washing, anointing, shoes, marital relations and work Source: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Siman 554 (25 paragraphs, taken at speed) Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada at Tiferet Eyal, Thursday 4 August 2022, three days before Tisha BeAv

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Torah study is forbidden

Tisha BeAv is forbidden for washing, anointing, leather shoes and marital relations, as well as eating and drinking (covered in the previous class). The Shulhan Arukh adds that one may not read Tanakh, or study Mishnah or Gemara, whether the legal or the homiletic parts. The reason is the verse "the precepts of Hashem are upright, gladdening the heart": Torah brings joy, and Tisha BeAv is a day of sadness. For the same reason schools close.

All these restrictions begin at sunset, not at nightfall.

Why do schools close? The Rabbi asked the class. The Taz holds that the problem is the teachers, who cannot teach Torah. So if a keen nine-year-old picks up Mishnayot by himself, nobody needs to stop him: children do not get that kind of pleasure from study. The Rabbi said he would be very hesitant to discourage a child who wants to learn. "I would not encourage him, but if you see a child studying, leave him alone."

What you may study

  • Iyov (Job), and the sad parts of Yirmiyahu (skipping the verses of comfort).
  • Eikhah, the Midrash on Eikhah and the commentaries on both.
  • The chapter of Moed Katan on the laws of mourning, and, adds the Mishnah Berurah, the story of Kamtsa and Bar Kamtsa in Gittin.
  • The laws of mourning and of excommunication in the Shulhan Arukh. "There are 64 chapters on mourning, and they will take you more than 24 hours."

What you may not do is deep analysis, a lecture, a debate, or searching for a contradiction. Read plainly what is written. The Rabbi urged the class not to waste the day: take a book you will never otherwise open, such as Iyov, and spend two or three hours understanding Eikhah with the Midrash. "This time is precious. Always in life, never waste time."

Questions from the class

  • Films about the Holocaust or the Spanish Inquisition? Fine. They are sad Jewish tragedies and they are meant to make you sad.
  • Thinking Torah instead of reading it? Some forbid even this. Normally thought is not like speech, but here the issue is joy, and thinking in Torah also brings joy. The Shulhan Arukh writes this as "yesh omerim" (some say). The Rabbi explained that this wording usually means the Shulhan Arukh found the ruling in only one source, not that he disagrees with it.
  • Tehillim and Musar? The order of the day is allowed: the prayers, the korbanot, the Akedah and the psalms within the service, since it is "seder hayom". Musar books are permitted if you need something to read, as long as you do not waste time.

The weekly parashah, and the reading of the day (Ki Tolid Banim), may be studied, and a Baal Koreh may prepare the Minhah reading.

Parts of the service are changed

Kinot are added in both. One approach makes the day strange by changing the service; the other, based on the Mekubalim and the Jerusalem custom, keeps everything as usual. Asked about singing, he said Az Yashir sung as on Shabbat would be grossly inappropriate; read it as on a weekday.

Fasting: pregnancy, nursing and illness

Pregnant and nursing women fast on Tisha BeAv like on Yom Kippur. On the three other fasts they are exempt, though they should not eat delicacies. Rav Sedaka Hussain, who came to Baghdad after the plague of 1743, ruled that pregnant women need not fast at all in an ordinary year, against the Shulhan Arukh, perhaps because of the heat. The Rabbi's approach: begin the fast, and eat the moment there is a real complaint. "It should not be a blanket permission."

A sick person (the definition always means bedridden, or about to become so) and a woman within 30 days of childbirth need not fast, and we do not investigate how bad it would get. "Only you know how ill you feel." The Rema sets a much higher bar for breaking the fast; we do not follow him.

Washing

All washing is forbidden, hot or cold, even putting a finger in water. Yet there are several permitted cases.

  • Mikveh. Women do not immerse on Tisha BeAv night. In the Gemara's day immersion was fixed to the exact seventh day; today it is not, and mikvaot are closed. All preparation, bathing and combing is done on Erev Tisha BeAv so that the preparation after the fast is brief, as on a Friday afternoon.
  • Dirt. Mud, or after using the toilet, one may wash, but only what is needed. Today it is normal hygiene to wash with soap after the toilet, and the same goes for someone who always washes his hands on coming home or into shul, but only up to the knuckles. Spraying hand gel just for the fresh feeling is not the same.
  • Indirect washing. Washing food or fruit for after the fast, where your hands get wet, is fine.
  • Morning. Netilat yadayim up to the knuckles. If hands are still damp you may wipe them over your eyes. Eye discharge may be washed off, and sweat that makes you feel dirty. A shower for the pleasure of feeling fresh is not allowed. Someone muddied on a walk washes limb by limb, not a full bath. The Mishnah Berurah allows a person who truly cannot function without washing his face to wash it.
  • Teeth. If it really bothers you, you may brush, with head down and never more than a mouthful of water. The Rabbi would not use mouthwash.
  • Journeys. Someone going to his teacher, his father or to perform a misvah may wade through water and also come back. For a property, he may go across but not return, since he would go anyway.
  • Medicine and babies. Tablets may be taken with water if needed, dry if possible. A baby may be washed as needed.

Anointing and shoes

Anointing is forbidden for pleasure only. Cream for eczema or a scalp condition is fine, and so is anything needed to avoid bad odour, which is preventive and not pleasure. A bride within her first 30 days may wash her face and anoint.

Leather shoes are forbidden, including wood covered with leather. Wood, bamboo or rubber is fine. Whether to say the berakhah for shoes follows your custom, and either way is fine. A mourner or an excommunicated person who must travel may wear leather outside but removes them at home. The Rema permits leather shoes among non-Jews, and the Rabbi applied this to a strict workplace dress code on a weekday Tisha BeAv, where trainers are not possible.

Marital life, walks and work

Marital relations are forbidden. One opinion adds that couples should not share a bed, and that is the custom: separate beds and blankets (not a full niddah style separation). On touching, the Mishnah Berurah rules that only intimate contact is forbidden. An innocent touch is allowed.

The Kolbo says not to go on long strolls on Tisha BeAv. A nature walk is inappropriate, but exercise and fresh air for a spinning head are common sense, and the Rabbi stressed it is only the advice of one Rishon.

Work: where it is the custom not to work, one does not. A person who wants to be a talmid hakham may refrain even where others work, and that is not arrogance. The Rema compromises: refrain from work and business until hasot, unless there would be a loss. After hasot, work is allowed if necessary. The Rabbi said real circumstances count, such as work that would cost a person annual leave.

The paragraph ends: one who works on Tisha BeAv will see no blessing from it, one who eats or drinks will not see the joy of Jerusalem, and one who mourns will merit to see it in its glory. And one who eats meat and drinks wine at the last meal in an ordinary year, his sins are on his bones.

Practical summary

  • Torah study, even by thought, is forbidden, apart from the sad texts: Iyov, Eikhah and Midrash, Yirmiyahu, the laws of mourning. Read plainly, no analysis.
  • Do not waste the day. Take a book you never open and read it.
  • All washing is forbidden, but dirt, hygiene after the toilet, morning knuckles, an inability to function, and journeys for a misvah or teacher are permitted within limits.
  • Anointing is allowed for illness or odour, not for pleasure. No leather shoes; wood, rubber and bamboo are fine.
  • Pregnant and nursing women fast on Tisha BeAv. The sick and women within 30 days of childbirth do not.
  • Separate beds, no work until hasot unless there would be a loss, and no long nature strolls.
