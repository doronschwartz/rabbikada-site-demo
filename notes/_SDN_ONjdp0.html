Topic: The deadlines for eating and disposing of hames when Erev Pesah falls on Shabbat

Sources: Torah; the Rambam; the Hakhamim; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Monday 7 April 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Eating hames on Pesah carries karet, which in the Rambam's understanding means being spiritually cut off after death. Karet applies only during the seven days.
  • From the Torah, hames is already forbidden from midday on Erev Pesah, when the Korban Pesah was offered; this year in England that is just after one o'clock on Shabbat afternoon.
  • Since people had no clocks and could confuse the times, the Hakhamim set eating to stop two hours before midday (about 10:45 in London) and owning to stop one hour before midday (about 11:54).
  • So people pray early this Shabbat, eat a combined breakfast and lunch around 9:30 or 9:45, finish hames by 10:45, and dispose of any remainder by about 11:50.
  • On Friday, the 13th, one may eat hames all day. Strictly, hames not needed for Friday night or Shabbat morning may be disposed of at any time on Friday.
  • The Shulhan Arukh writes from the Rishonim that it is good practice to dispose of it by the usual time, an hour before midday on Friday (about 11:54 in London), so as not to confuse other years. If one did not, the whole of Friday remains.
  • The rabbi's sale of hames (Mekhirat Hames) takes place before 11:54, but the contract continues to cover hames put away in the sold locations up until Shabbat.

Sources quoted

  • Torah: karet for eating hames on Pesah, and the prohibition from midday on Erev Pesah.
  • The Rambam: karet as spiritual cutting off.
  • Shulhan Arukh: good practice to dispose of hames on Friday by the usual time.

In one line

This year finish eating hames by about 10:45 on Shabbat morning, and ideally dispose of what is not needed by 11:54 on Friday, though all Friday is allowed.
