Topic: When davar haaved extends to gain: a great profit, and an opportunity that will not return

Sources: The Ritva; the Yerushalmi; the Rosh; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 4 November 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Selling shares one already owns that are about to fall is permitted as davar haaved. Buying shares simply for profit would seemingly not be.
  • But the Rishonim and Shulhan Arukh give cases where gain was permitted. The Ritva writes that one may perform kiddushin on Hol HaMoed, with its writing, lest the other side change their mind, though that is only a gain.
  • The Ritva explains that something so essential, or so great a profit, is considered like a loss. The heter exists so that one is not upset on the moed: people are less upset by a missed profit, but watching a huge one slip away is very upsetting.
  • What counts as a great profit depends on the person. Marriage is objectively substantial. A small expected gain on shares: better not. Shares that have plummeted and are clearly rising, where hundreds or thousands of pounds are at stake: permitted.
  • Second, based on the Yerushalmi, the Rosh writes and the Shulhan Arukh rules that one may buy cheaply from travelling sellers who will not be around after the moed. The Shulhan Arukh also allows buying wine at harvest time, when it cannot be bought later.
  • Seemingly, then, a genuinely unique sale held once or twice a year on Hol HaMoed may be used. A sale running for a few weeks, with perhaps less stock afterwards, is probably not strong enough.

Sources quoted

  • The Ritva: kiddushin on Hol HaMoed; a great profit is like a loss.
  • The Yerushalmi, the Rosh and Shulhan Arukh: buying from passing sellers, and wine at harvest time.

In one line

Davar haaved can permit pursuing a gain on Hol HaMoed when the profit is very great or the opportunity will not come again.
