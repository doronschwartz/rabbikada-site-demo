Topic: Whether one may blow shofar on Rosh Hashanah after the misvah is done, to practise or for pleasure

Sources: The Rama; the Hida; Rabbi Yaakov Emden; the Gemara (taking the lulav); Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Tuesday 24 August 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The core of Rosh Hashanah is Yom Teruah; with all the customs we end up blowing 101 times on each day.
  • The question is blowing with no obligation, such as practising in the afternoon for the next day, not blowing for someone who has not yet heard.
  • On Shabbat the Hakhamim forbade playing any instrument, including a shofar, lest one come to make or fix an instrument (Makkeh BePatish). That is why a shofar is mukseh on Shabbat.
  • On Rosh Hashanah that prohibition does not apply to the blasts we need. The Rama writes that once one has blown and is no longer blowing for others, such as family or women who have not heard, it applies again and further blowing is forbidden.
  • The shofar is not mukseh, though, and may be moved all day, since one may find someone who has not yet heard.
  • This is the view of most poskim, including the Hida.
  • Rabbi Yaakov Emden disagreed: just as the Gemara praises taking the lulav more on Sukkot, so the more one blows on Rosh Hashanah the better. This was not accepted; the Hida writes one should not, and this is Hakham Ovadiah's ruling.

In one line

Once the misvah is done and no one else needs to hear, one may move the shofar but not blow it for practice or pleasure.
