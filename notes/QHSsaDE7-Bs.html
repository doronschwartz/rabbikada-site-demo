Topic: Can one fulfil Mishloah Manot by giving to a parent or a spouse when in isolation?

Sources: The Megillah ("ish lereehu"); Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; Rabbi Akiva Yoseph Schlesinger; the Gemara (a hatan seeing the kallah); "Love your neighbour as yourself"

Daily Halacha: Purim, Tuesday 23 February 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • This year many will be in isolation. One living alone can do little, but can one fulfil Mishloah Manot by giving to immediate family? The question was rarely discussed until about 150 years ago, as it was uncommon.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph discusses only a child giving to a parent, and rules clearly that he fulfils the misvah. His concern: the Megillah says "each man to his rea", his friend, so treating a parent as a friend might slight kibbud av vaem. He answers that earlier authorities allow giving to one's teacher or rav, and that is no slight; the same applies to a parent.
  • He does not discuss spouses. Generally what a wife owns belongs to her husband (how that works today is for another time), so the question is whether a gift clearly given as hers alone counts, and whether a spouse is a rea.
  • Rabbi Akiva Yoseph Schlesinger, in Hungary about 150 years ago, concludes one may give Mishloah Manot to one's spouse. His source: the Gemara obliges a hatan to see the kallah before marriage, since arranged marriages were common, because of "love your rea as yourself"; if he disliked her afterwards he would violate it. So the Gemara treats husband and wife as reim.
  • So a child may give to a parent, a wife to her husband, and a husband to his wife with intent that it belongs fully to her.
  • This should only be relied on if one cannot go out and do the misvah in the regular way.

Sources quoted

  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: giving to a parent fulfils the misvah, as giving to one's rav does.
  • Rabbi Akiva Yoseph Schlesinger: from the Gemara on a hatan seeing the kallah, a spouse counts as a friend.

In one line

If one cannot go out, Mishloah Manot may be given to a parent or a spouse, a husband giving it fully into his wife's ownership.
