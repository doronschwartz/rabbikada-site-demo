Topic: Whether non-heat electricity is permitted on Yom Tov, and communities with a custom to use it

Sources: The poskim; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph, Teshuvot Yabia Omer

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 10 June 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • All agree electricity is forbidden on Shabbat, whether as makkeh bepatish, boneh or molid. The poskim debate whether it is from the Torah or rabbinic; Hakham Ovadiah held it is rabbinic, as we generally follow, which matters for questions like asking a non-Jew.
  • Building, makkeh bepatish and molid are equally forbidden on Yom Tov, so on these grounds electricity is equally forbidden on Yom Tov.
  • Yet some communities, mainly in Hus LaAres, allowed electricity on Yom Tov. They must have viewed it as fire: an extension of what is latent in the wires rather than a new flame, or as grama, and so permitted like lighting from an existing flame.
  • Hakham Ovadiah discusses this in Teshuvot Yabia Omer and concludes it is certainly forbidden on Yom Tov. But those with an established custom from such countries need not be strongly rebuked for switching lights on and off; one may explain mildly that the consensus of poskim forbids it.
  • Two points: this concerns only communities with that established custom; everyone else follows the vast majority, who forbid it at least rabbinically. And he was speaking of lights. He cannot imagine Hakham Ovadiah extending it to other electricity such as phones, which would certainly be wrong and arguably uvdin dehol, weekday activity.
  • So an established custom is limited to specific cases, not a carte blanche for computers and the like, has veshalom, which would destroy the fabric of Yom Tov and Shabbat.

Sources quoted

  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph, Yabia Omer: electricity is forbidden on Yom Tov, but those with an old custom for lights are corrected gently.

In one line

Electricity is forbidden on Yom Tov at least rabbinically, and even an established custom to switch lights never extends to phones or computers.
