Topic: Cutting nails on Hol HaMoed for Sepharadim and Ashkenazim

Sources: The Rif; Rambam; the Rosh; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Thursday 23 October 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Rif, Rambam, the Rosh and many Rishonim hold the decree on haircuts does not extend to nails. The Shulhan Arukh rules one may cut nails on Hol HaMoed in all circumstances, even if one did not before the festival.
  • The Rema writes that the custom is to be stringent, following Rishonim who held the decree included nails.
  • Even for the Rema this applies only if one did not cut them before the moed; one who did may cut them again.
  • Some have the custom, recorded long ago and in the Shulhan Arukh in Hilchot Shabbat, to cut nails every Friday. This year Sukkot fell midweek: one who cut on the Friday after Yom Kippur, had nothing to cut before Sukkot and cuts again the following Friday may do so, even according to the Rema, in honour of Shabbat.
  • Conclusion: for Sepharadim it is always permitted. Ashkenazim should cut before the moed, may cut on a Friday of Hol HaMoed if that is their weekly custom, and otherwise should refrain unless there is a pressing reason, such as very long, bothersome nails.

Sources quoted

  • The Rif, Rambam and the Rosh: the decree applies only to hair.
  • Shulhan Arukh: nails may always be cut on Hol HaMoed.
  • The Rema: the custom to be stringent unless cut before the moed.

In one line

Sepharadim may cut nails on Hol HaMoed freely; Ashkenazim should cut them before, unless cutting for Shabbat or for a pressing reason.
