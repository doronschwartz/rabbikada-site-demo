Topic: What the search for Hames requires, and why crumbs are not the target

Sources: The Torah, Parashat Bo

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Monday 4 April 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Torah, in Parashat Bo, forbids owning Hames on Pesah (bal yeraeh and bal yimase), even without eating it.
  • From the Torah, sincerely nullifying and abandoning ownership of one's Hames before Pesah is enough, even if it is still in the house.
  • The Hakhamim required searching for Hames and removing it (biur), for two reasons: a person may not genuinely abandon ownership (would he object if someone took his expensive bottles of whisky?), and he may forget and eat it during Pesah.
  • One opinion requires searching even for crumbs, because of the concern of eating them.
  • Many poskim disagree: karet applies only to eating a kezayit, and we are not genuinely concerned about eating crumbs. The Hakhamim therefore instituted a search for a kezayit, a substantial piece (normally about 27 grams) that one might come to eat.
  • The halacha, according to Hakham Ovadiah and many poskim, is that one must search for and remove a kezayit. Spring cleaning out crumbs is not required, and inedible Hames needs no search.
  • Spring cleaning is fine, but should not be confused with cleaning for Pesah.

Sources quoted

In one line

The rabbinic search is for a kezayit of Hames one might fail to nullify or come to eat, not for every crumb.
