The halacha

  • Over time it became accepted that opening an umbrella on Shabbat is forbidden, even with an eruv. Most Hakhamim of Sepharad agree: the Hida, Rabbi David Pardo, the Ben Ish Hai, and more recently Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph and Rav Ben Siyon Abba Shaul. This is the accepted ruling for Ashkenazim and Sepharadim alike.
  • Rav Ben Siyon Uziel, former Chief Rabbi of Israel, was slightly more permissive: since it is a mahloket, one should be strict oneself, but one has no authority to criticise those who use one.
  • Rabbi Yoseph Mashash, from Morocco and later Chief Rabbi of Haifa, wrote that a God-fearing person should refrain, yet in extenuating circumstances (a distant synagogue, torrential rain, missing the service or coming to wring out soaked clothes) the lenient have what to rely on. He himself wrote that it is better not to.
  • An umbrella opened before Shabbat: some forbade carrying it because of marit ayin. Hakham Ovadiah says this is incorrect, since after the Talmud we cannot make new gezerot. He still advises against it as a weekday activity (uvda dehol), not in the spirit of Shabbat, and that is generally the consensus.
  • He once read that in the Frankfurt synagogue of Rabbi Breuer, a student of Rabbi Hirsch, a non-Jew at the door closed people's umbrellas and opened them on the way out.
  • A non-Jew may open and carry an umbrella on one's behalf, as many poskim write.

In one line

The accepted ruling forbids using an umbrella on Shabbat, and one already open is best avoided, but a non-Jew may carry it for you.
