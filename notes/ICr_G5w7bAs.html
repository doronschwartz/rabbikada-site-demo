Topic: Shaving or haircuts during the Omer for work, a simhah, a Brit Milah or a Bar Misvah

Sources: Rav Moshe Feinstein, responsa; the Radbaz; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Sefirat HaOmer, Tuesday 3 May 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • From the day after Rosh Hodesh Iyar until Lag BaOmer all communities refrain from weddings and haircuts, and shaving is included.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein permits shaving, daily or weekly as needed, where there is a genuine concern of losing one's job or a deal, since this custom was never accepted where there is loss. In another responsum he clarifies it must be an actual loss of money; embarrassment or discomfort is not enough.
  • He contrasts the week of Tisha BeAv, a prohibition from the Hakhamim of the Gemara rather than a later custom, where even loss of money does not permit it.
  • If one shaves once a week for work, Friday is best, since the Radbaz held one may shave every Friday of the Omer for Kavod Shabbat. We do not necessarily follow that, but it adds Kavod Shabbat.
  • Attending a wedding held by a couple whose custom permits it when one's own does not: Rav Moshe permits a haircut or shave if otherwise it would detract from the simhah and he could not attend.
  • At a Brit Milah, the father, the sandak and the mohel may have a haircut and shave.
  • Hakham Ovadiah permits music at the seudat misvah on the day a boy turns 13; whether the boy may have a haircut is discussed and unclear, as it lacks the status of a Brit Milah or wedding and haircuts are more severe than music.
  • For Sepharadim this custom applies mainly to men; women are exempt.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: shaving permitted for real financial loss, and for attending a simhah.
  • The Radbaz: shaving on Fridays of the Omer for Kavod Shabbat.
  • Hakham Ovadiah: music at a Bar Misvah seudah on the day.

In one line

One may shave in the Omer to avoid real financial loss, preferably on Friday, and those central to a Brit Milah may shave, while for Sepharadim women are not bound by the custom.
