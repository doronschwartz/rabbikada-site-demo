Topic: Women's obligation in the Haggadah, fulfilling it by listening, and its essential parts

Sources: The Gemara; the Mishnah

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Thursday 18 April 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Women are obligated in Sippur Yesiat Misrayim just as men. Though time-bound, it is compared to Massah and the Korban Pesah, in which women are obligated. Some saw it as rabbinic, but the consensus of the poskim is that it is biblical, equal to men. "Levinkha" means one's child, son or daughter.
  • The Gemara shows that one person would read the Haggadah for everyone. It is not a prayer text but a springboard for discussion, so each person, including women and teenagers, fulfils the Misvah by listening and understanding. Hearing Hebrew read without understanding is not enough.
  • The key parts, from the Mishnah and Gemara, are the question and answer (Mah Nishtanah and Avadim Hayinu), the history from Mitehilah onwards, and Pesah, Massah and Maror with their reasons. The rest of Maggid was compiled in the times of the Geonim, about 1,000 to 1,500 years ago.
  • Hallel at the end of Maggid is like a Berakhah or Tefillah: everyone should listen attentively, or better, say it themselves or together.
  • The leader should explain the key parts clearly, translating them if Hebrew is not spoken at home; this is essential to the Misvah.
  • Any further discussion is "kol hamarbeh harei zeh meshubah".

In one line

Women are fully obligated in the Haggadah and, like everyone, fulfil it by listening to and understanding a leader who explains its key parts.
