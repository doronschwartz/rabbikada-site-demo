Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 282, seifim 4-7; 283; 284, seif 1 (introduction) Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 11 January 2024

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

Seif 4: the seventh aliyah and the Maftir

Last week covered how many are called up on each day, adding aliyot, repeating, and women and children. Seif 4 describes the custom: call seven, finish the parashah with them, say Kaddish, then the Maftir repeats some of what the seventh read and reads the Haftarah.

The Rabbi explained what lies behind it. The basic halacha is that only seven are called on Shabbat, and the seventh is the Maftir. If there were exactly ten men and only seven could be called, the seventh would go straight on to the Haftarah. There would be no Kaddish in between, and that is completely acceptable.

One opinion in the Gemara holds that the Maftir cannot be one of the seven and must be an extra person. The halacha does not follow it, but to accommodate it we add an eighth person. To show that he is an addition, not part of the seven, we say Kaddish before him. The Maftir must read from the Torah before the Haftarah, so nobody thinks the Prophets are equal to the Torah. "Which shows you, by the way, that people who fight over the Kaddish to get the Kaddish, it's ridiculous, because there was never any Kaddish in the first place."

On Yom Tov the custom is the same: five, Kaddish, then Maftir.

When only three are called. On a weekday we do not add aliyot, so we go back to the basic halacha: the third person is the Maftir, and there is no Kaddish before the Haftarah. Sefaradim read a Haftarah on a weekday only on Tisha BeAv, morning and afternoon; Ashkenazim do so on every fast day at Minhah. The Rabbi said that some siddurim mistakenly print a Kaddish there, but the old Spanish and Portuguese siddur and the correct mahzorim have none.

He also praised a Spanish and Portuguese custom: everyone called up is named, but the Maftir is called only as "Maftir," without his name, because he is not really a full aliyah.

More than one Sefer Torah. The Rema says the Maftir is always the last one called.

A child as Maftir. The Rema says a child may be called for Maftir, even for the four special parashiyot (Shekalim, Zakhor, Parah, HaHodesh), though some disagree. Those who disagree hold that the person called up for Zakhor should have in mind to fulfil the obligation for the whole congregation with his berakhah, which the Rabbi called a very late stringency. The main halacha allows it. He was once asked about a boy who had prepared the Maftir and Haftarah for Parashat Parah: "100%" he could be called up. With Zakhor it would be harder, but in pressing circumstances he would let an adult read Zakhor and the child read the Haftarah.

Seif 5: when only someone already called can read the Haftarah

Seven have been called and the only person who can read the Haftarah was one of them. What do you do?

  • If Kaddish has not yet been said: let the seventh person be the Maftir and go straight on to the Haftarah. You do not need to announce him as Maftir; the seventh is the Maftir automatically.
  • If Kaddish has already been said: call the person who can read up again as Maftir. He says the Torah berakhot a second time.

The Rema adds that, normally, someone already called up should not be called again if others can read.

Someone asked about the common situation where an aliyah is bought for a person who cannot read, and he is told "you say the berakhot, someone else will read." The Rabbi said there is something to rely on, but it is not the preferred way, and it hardly honours him. What may not be done is for one person to be called up for Maftir and someone else to say the Haftarah berakhot. The one called for Maftir is the one who says the berakhot on the Haftarah.

Seifim 6-7: mistakes in the reading

Seif 6. The reader finished the parashah with only six aliyot and Kaddish was said. Call a seventh person, who repeats part of what the sixth read. He counts as seventh and as Maftir, and no further Kaddish or extra Maftir is needed. If only five had been called, the Kaddish was wasted: you would need to call more and then say Kaddish.

Seif 7. If a verse was missed, even if the whole service is over, take the Sefer Torah out again and call one person to read that verse with two more, since one never reads fewer than three verses. No Kaddish follows. For Sefaradim, Kaddish always introduces the next part of the service; it is not a way of closing. That is why we do not say Kaddish after Aleinu at the end of the service. Some communities say Kaddish after a Mishnah at the end, but that Kaddish is on the Mishnah.

Siman 283: why not a second Sefer Torah on Shabbat?

On Yom Tov and Rosh Hodesh we read the day's offerings from a second Sefer Torah as Maftir. Why not on Shabbat? The Rishonim give two answers:

  1. The Rosh: the Shabbat offering in Parashat Pinehas is only two verses, and one never takes out a Sefer Torah to read fewer than three.
  2. Tosafot: when a second Sefer is used, the Haftarah connects to the Maftir. On Shabbat that would mean a Haftarah about Shabbat or offerings every week, and we would lose the freedom to choose a Haftarah connected to the parashah.

Siman 284: reading the Haftarah from a printed book

Siman 284 is on the Haftarah. First the Rabbi looked at a passage in the Mishnah Berurah. An authority from about 450 years ago asked: we would never read the parashah from a printed Humash, so how can we read the Haftarah from one? The answer given: the Haftarah began when reading the Torah was banned, and today it is only a remembrance (zekher) of that time, so a book is enough.

The Magen Avraham is still uneasy. He says to read at least from a printed book of the whole prophet, not a booklet of Haftarot. The Mishnah Berurah says that ideally each community should have parchment scrolls of each book of the Prophets and read from the right one each week, as was the custom of the Vilna Gaon and of many communities. So the order of preference is:

  1. A parchment scroll of that book of the Prophets.
  2. A printed book of the whole prophet or Tanakh.
  3. A Humash with the Haftarot printed after each parashah.

The beautiful Sefaradi Haftarah book carried by the boy has no sanctity, written or printed, since it collects separate passages. It is ceremonial. There is no need to stand for it, and if it falls it is like a Humash falling.

Why did Sefaradim never take on the scrolls? Probably cost, and a practice that happens in the middle of the main service every week is very hard to change. "This is one of the things Ashkenazim are more correct than us."

Seif 1. The Haftarah must come from the Prophets, connected to the parashah, and be at least 21 verses: seven people, three verses each, as when the Prophets were read in place of the Torah. If the topic ends naturally before that, as in Parashat Sav, one stops there. And there is no Haftarah with its berakhot unless the Torah has been read first.

Practical summary

  • Strictly, the seventh aliyah is the Maftir; the Kaddish and extra Maftir are a custom to accommodate a minority view.
  • When three are called (Tisha BeAv, and fast days for Ashkenazim), the third is the Maftir and there is no Kaddish before the Haftarah.
  • A child may be Maftir, even for Shekalim, Parah and HaHodesh; Zakhor is harder.
  • If the only Haftarah reader was already called and Kaddish was said, call him again as Maftir.
  • A verse left out: take out the Sefer Torah and read it with two more verses.
  • Ideally read the Haftarah from a scroll or a full book of that prophet.
