Topic: The Shulhan Arukh's ruling on the child, and what happens if a daughter married a Kohen

Sources: Shulhan Arukh, Even HaEzer, Siman 4; the Rambam; the Rosh; the Rif; the Helkat Mehokek; the Beit Shemuel

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 4 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The child is kasher even if the Jewish mother was married, since there is no kiddushin with a non-Jew. This is very different from a married Jewish woman's child with a Jewish man, who is a mamzer.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules: the child is kasher and may marry a Jew, but is blemished for kehunah (pagum likehunah), so a daughter may not marry a Kohen.
  • This is not like the Rambam; it follows the Rosh, or the Rif's doubt.
  • The commentaries, the Helkat Mehokek, the Beit Shemuel and many others, write that if a Kohen did marry her with kiddushin, the marriage takes effect and they may stay together, relying on the Rambam and on the Rif's doubt.
  • So a Kohen who asks is told it is forbidden, but if they married with a Jewish wedding they need not divorce.
  • Their children are of doubtful status, invalid for kehunah, and may not marry Kohanim; what a halal is will be explained in later chapters.

In one line

The child may marry any Jew, but a daughter should not marry a Kohen; if she did with kiddushin, they may stay together.
