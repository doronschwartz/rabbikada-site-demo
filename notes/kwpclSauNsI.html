Topic: Timing the Purim seudah when Purim falls on Friday, including joining it to the Shabbat meal

Sources: The Rama; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Purim, Wednesday 24 February 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • On a regular Friday one may eat an ordinary meal all day, even late afternoon, but not a special meal one is not accustomed to during the week, so as to enter Shabbat with an appetite.
  • A seudat misvah whose time falls on Friday, such as a brit milah, may be held all day. The Purim seudah is such a meal, so strictly it may be eaten any time on Purim day, even late afternoon.
  • The Rama says one should try to eat it before midday, to digest it and come to the Friday night meal with appetite. This is not strictly required, but if one can, it is better before hasot.
  • A further option is pores mapah umekadesh: pray Minhah early, begin the Purim seudah with bread late in the afternoon and continue into Shabbat. Since one is already in a meal one may keep eating after Shabbat begins. At nightfall (about quarter past six this year) cover the bread, make Kiddush, and continue the meal without washing again.
  • If one already drank wine during the seudah, omit Borei Peri HaGefen from Kiddush.
  • In Birkat HaMazon say Resseh. The majority of poskim say not to add Al HaNissim, since it would contradict Resseh, which belongs to Shabbat, the day after Purim. Arvit is prayed afterwards.
  • So there are three acceptable options: the seudah before midday; after midday (acceptable but perhaps not preferable), with Birkat HaMazon and a separate Shabbat meal at night; or one meal running into Shabbat.

Sources quoted

  • The Rama: hold the Friday Purim seudah before midday so as to eat the Shabbat meal with appetite.

In one line

When Purim is on Friday, eat the seudah before midday if possible, or join it to the Shabbat meal with Kiddush in the middle and Resseh alone in Birkat HaMazon.
