Topic: Three lessons from Sukkot for working on happiness

Sources: Sefer HaHinukh; Devarim ("Vesamahta behagekha... you, your son and your daughter")

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Friday 29 September 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • There is a misvah to rejoice on all three festivals, but the climax is Sukkot, the only one called zeman simhatenu. Rejoicing on Sukkot is a Torah misvah.
  • Sefer HaHinukh writes that every year we need a kind of seminar to renew our happiness, drawing joy from these eight days for the whole year.
  • Happiness is not binary but something we keep working on; the American constitution speaks of the pursuit of happiness.
  • First, gratitude: it is not what we own but how grateful we are. On Sukkot we leave our possessions yet beautify the sukkah with our best utensils, valuing what we have rather than what we lack.
  • Second, the journey: Sukkot alone celebrates no fixed place but forty years of travel. Fulfilment comes from stepping out of our comfort zone towards clear goals, which brings flow and contentment.
  • Third, a God-centred life: the Torah's call to rejoice always includes son, daughter, servants, convert, widow and orphan. Expecting the world to obey us breeds frustration; in the desert HaShem and the Aron were at the centre, and serving others is more rewarding than control.
