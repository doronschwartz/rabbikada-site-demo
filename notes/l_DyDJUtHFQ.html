Topic: Whether hydroponically grown vegetables are HaAdamah or Shehakol

Sources: The Shevet HaLevi; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the Hazon Ish; the Hayei Adam; the Torah on the man

Daily Halacha: Berachot, Monday 22 January 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Hydroponic vegetables are increasingly common. Vegetables are normally Boreh Peri HaAdamah, but these grow in water, which suggests Shehakol Nihyah Bidvaro, the catch-all berakhah.
  • The Shevet HaLevi held they remain HaAdamah: the berakhah follows the category, and saying different berakhot on two broccolis grown differently is unacceptable.
  • Hakham Ovadiah wrote a teshuvah that the berakhah is Shehakol, and that Sepharadim should certainly follow this, bringing proofs including from Ashkenazi poskim.
  • The Hazon Ish permits growing produce in water during Shemittah, since the Torah forbids only working the ground; so what grows in water lacks the status of the ground.
  • The Hayei Adam, about 200 years ago, wrote that bread from grain grown in a flower pot is Shehakol (not all agreed); it is how a thing grows that matters, not its category.
  • The man, "bread from heaven" in this week's parashah: some argue its berakhah, had they said one, would not be "hamosi lehem min haares". Mushrooms too are Shehakol, not HaAdamah, as they do not grow from the ground.
  • Since most vegetables are grown normally, one says HaAdamah on regular vegetables, and Shehakol only when one knows the vegetable is hydroponic.

In one line

Sepharadim follow Hakham Ovadiah and say Shehakol on vegetables known to be hydroponic, and HaAdamah on ordinary vegetables.
