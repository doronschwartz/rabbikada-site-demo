Topic: A thought on the day of Queen Elizabeth II's funeral, and not speaking during the first 30 shofar blasts

Sources: The Torah ("HaSur tamim paolo"); the Gemara (sur and sayar); the Hakhamim ("the work is not upon you to complete"); the Arizal; the Ben Ish Hai

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Monday 19 September 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • At a Jewish funeral we speak of HaKadosh Barukh Hu as "HaSur", the rock, perfect in His ways. Queen Elizabeth II was a rock for the United Kingdom: unswerving and unshakable.
  • The Gemara reads sur (rock) also as sayar (artist): the rock of stability is also the artist of life. Endurance and creativity are two sides of one coin; creativity must rest on solid foundations, and the Queen combined constancy with creativity.
  • She embodied the Hakhamim's teaching: the work is not upon you to complete, but neither are you free to desist from it. We must keep serving others and HaKadosh Barukh Hu to our last day.

The halacha

  • The first 30 blasts after the berakhah (tekiot meyushav, which Ashkenazim hear standing) fulfil the Torah misvah, so speaking during them is strictly forbidden.
  • The Arizal, brought by the Ben Ish Hai, says to verbalise viduy (confession) between the three sets of the first 30 (12, 9 and 9). Kabbalists and some who follow the Ben Ish Hai do so.
  • This is halachically problematic: the 30 are needed to cover every view of shevarim and teruah, so the misvah may not yet be fulfilled. Most poskim rule one should not speak it; one may think the confession and reflect on one's life.

In one line

No speaking at all during the first 30 blasts, even for confession, which should be in thought only.
