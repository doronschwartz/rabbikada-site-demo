These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

The Shulhan Arukh's surprising ruling

Last week we learned what may and may not be done at twilight. This week: when exactly are sunset, twilight and nightfall, in the context of the misvah of Tosefet Shabbat, adding to Shabbat.

Seif 2, introduced with "some say": one must add from the weekday onto Shabbat. The time for this addition begins at sunset, when the sun is no longer visible over the land, and runs for three and a quarter mil (about 58.5 minutes). You may make all of it Shabbat or part of it, as long as you add something. Then twilight lasts three quarters of a mil (about 14 minutes) before night.

In other words, according to this, what we call sunset is not the halachic sunset. It is still day for another 58.5 minutes, and twilight lasts only about 14 minutes.

In practice that week in Hendon, with sunset at 9.21pm on Friday:

"Isn't that shocking?" According to this, one could do Melakhah on Friday night until about 10.18pm.

Two Gemaras and two ways of learning

The Mishnah Berurah explains the source. The Gemara in Pesahim says that from sunset to nightfall is four mil, 72 minutes. The Gemara in Shabbat says twilight is three quarters of a mil and then it is night. How do we reconcile them?

The Rabbi explained two approaches to contradictions in the Gemara: - Tosafot and the French commentators make peace between the sources: this one speaks about one case, that one about another. - The Rambam and the Rif take the more apparent Gemara and set aside the other.

Rabbeinu Tam, true to the Tosafot approach, distinguished two "sunsets". Pesahim speaks of the start of sunset, when the sun disappears from view; from then to night is 72 minutes. Shabbat speaks of the end of sunset, when even the remaining rays are gone, 58.5 minutes later; only then does twilight begin.

The Rambam, living in Egypt and Morocco, said: use your eyes. In Israel, 58 minutes after sunset it is pitch dark; it cannot still be day. So twilight begins at what we call sunset. Rabbeinu Tam's view makes more sense in France or England, where summer evenings stay light for a long time.

Who keeps Rabbeinu Tam today?

Many Hasidic communities in Europe traditionally followed Rabbeinu Tam both ways, beginning and ending Shabbat late, which is why Hasidim are used to praying minhah late. The Magen Avraham, the leading Ashkenazi authority, also ruled like Rabbeinu Tam. Today almost everyone who keeps Rabbeinu Tam does so only as a stringency at the end of Shabbat. "Nobody keeps it lekula."

What do Sefaradim do, if the Shulhan Arukh rules this way? The Shulhan Arukh contradicts himself. In Hilchot Milah he follows the Rambam and the Geonim.

The Rabbi's example: a baby is born on Friday at 9.30pm, after sunset. According to Rabbeinu Tam it was born on Friday, and the brit is the next Friday. According to the Geonim, 9.30pm is twilight, a doubt whether it is Friday or Shabbat. The brit cannot be on Friday (maybe day seven) or on Shabbat (maybe day nine, and only the eighth day overrides Shabbat), so it is done on Sunday.

Why follow Hilchot Milah? Asked in class, the Rabbi agreed this is the key point: there the Shulhan Arukh is dealing with a serious question of whether to override Shabbat, so he was more concerned; here it is the relatively minor law of Tosefet Shabbat. The accepted practice became like the Geonim, and the Rabbi felt it was based on reality: the Rambam and the Geonim seem to be correct.

Different calendars (for example the United Synagogue and the Federation) differ by a few minutes at the end of Shabbat. These are slight variations within the opinion of the Geonim, about how to calculate three stars and how much to add. If Shabbat is published as ending at 10.28, "trust me, it's nightfall already".

Where the 15 minutes come from

A third, minority opinion, Rabbi Eliezer of Metz, places the roughly 14-minute twilight before sunset. We do not follow it. The custom of bringing in Shabbat a set time before sunset combines Tosefet Shabbat with this opinion: 15 minutes in London, 16 in Manchester, 18 in many places, longer in Jerusalem.

The Rabbi was explicit:

"Strictly speaking, you can do work tomorrow till 9.19."

In essence a couple of minutes of Tosefet Shabbat is enough. One who brings in Shabbat at 9.09 or 9.15 has done nothing wrong. Still, it is good practice to bring Shabbat in early and calmly, without rushing to the last second.

A common mistake: visitors from abroad add 18 minutes to the London calendar time, not realising it already includes 15. London traditionally takes the smallest margin. At worst, someone relying on the calendar time has about 13 minutes left before sunset, and must still leave a couple of minutes for Tosefet Shabbat.

How serious is Tosefet Shabbat?

The Rabbi asked: sunset is 9.21. If I drive at 11pm, I have broken Shabbat with the punishment of karet. At 9.45pm, during twilight, it is a doubtful karet. At 9.20, I have not broken Shabbat at all; I have lost the misvah of Tosefet Shabbat.

Tosefet Shabbat seems to be a Torah misvah, but there is no punishment for missing it. The source is in Parashat Emor about Yom Kippur: "you shall afflict yourselves on the ninth of the month in the evening". We fast on the tenth, so the verse teaches adding from the ninth onto the tenth, both at the start and at the end. Since it says "you shall rest your Shabbat", it applies to every day called Shabbat, including Shabbat and Yom Tov. Where is Yom Tov called Shabbat? In the famous dispute with the Sadducees over "the day after the Shabbat" for counting the Omer: they said it means Saturday, and Hazal said it means the festival itself.

Since twilight is already a doubt with its own prohibition, the Torah must be teaching us to add before twilight begins.

Practical summary

  • We follow the Geonim: from sunset it is twilight, and Melakhah is forbidden.
  • Rabbeinu Tam today is kept only as a stringency for the end of Shabbat.
  • The calendar's candle lighting time (15 minutes before sunset in London) is a good custom; the minimum Tosefet Shabbat is a couple of minutes before sunset.
  • Do not add another 18 minutes to the London calendar time.
  • Bring in Shabbat calmly, without rushing to the last second.
