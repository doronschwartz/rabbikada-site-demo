Topic: The correct time to light the Menorah, and lighting later in the night

Sources: Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Rishonim; the Meiri; Mishnah Berurah; the Ben Ish Hai; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Rambam calls the Hanukkah lights "a very beloved misvah", and the Shulhan Arukh says one must be very careful with it, expressions not found elsewhere. Lighting at the right time is an essential part.
  • Shulhan Arukh: the correct time is the end of sunset, just before nightfall (in London this year, just after half past four).
  • Lekhathilah one may still light while people are in the street, which the poskim give as about half an hour, because of pirsumei nisa.
  • Beyond that the Shulhan Arukh still allows the whole night, but bediavad.
  • The Rishonim write that once we light mainly for the household, the half hour is less critical. The Meiri reports that Torah scholars lit late, after their learning, with their families.
  • Halachically it is best to light at the start of the night or within half an hour, but when that is not possible (children back from school, people at work) one may light with a berakhah all night until dawn.
  • The Mishnah Berurah and the Ben Ish Hai say that late at night some family members should be awake for pirsumei nisa. But the Mishnah Berurah himself writes there is what to rely on even if no one else is home, and Hakham Ovadiah rules so: one need not wake sleeping children.

In one line

Light at the end of sunset or within half an hour; otherwise one may light with a berakhah all night, even alone.
