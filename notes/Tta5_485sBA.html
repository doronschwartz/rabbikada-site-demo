Topic: Women and Shemitat Kesafim, banks, a lost Prozbol, and loans wrongly claimed in the past

Sources: The Ben Ish Hai; the Gemara; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Shemitah & Prozbol, Wednesday 25 May 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The release of loans applies equally to women: although time-bound, it includes a negative commandment, "lo yigos", do not claim.
  • A woman who lent from her husband's money with his permission is covered by his Prozbol. A single woman, or a married woman with her own finances who made loans from them, needs her own.
  • The Ben Ish Hai writes that before the end of the seventh year it is commendable, for example, for a woman to lend a friend some oil, eggs or flour and not claim it back, so as to fulfil the misvah rather than rely on Prozbol.
  • Jewish-owned banks in Israel, with mortgages and loans, must make a Prozbol; one document covers all their loans.
  • A lender in Bet Din who says he wrote a Prozbol but lost it is believed, even without proof, since he could have done it permissibly and presumably did. If he admits he never wrote it, he cannot claim.
  • One who realises that in past years he claimed debts that Shemitah had cancelled: Hakham Ovadiah, in a long discussion, concludes that strictly he need not return the money, as it was done unintentionally, though it is good to seek forgiveness from the borrower if he can identify him.

In one line

Women must also make a Prozbol for their own loans, a lender who lost his Prozbol is believed, and past unwitting claims need not be repaid.
