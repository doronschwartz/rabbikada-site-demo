Topic: Why the new year for fruit trees, rather than for vegetables or animals, became a celebration

Sources: Mishnah Rosh Hashanah (first Mishnah); the Torah, Bereshit; the Talmud; the Shabbat psalm

Daily Halacha: Tu BiShvat, Wednesday 27 January 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The source of Tu BiShvat is the first Mishnah of Rosh Hashanah, which lists other new years too: for kings, festivals, animals and vegetables (the first of Tishri). Only the new year for fruit became a festive day with meals together.
  • In Gan Eden Adam could eat from every tree but the Tree of Knowledge. After the sin he was cursed to eat the grasses of the field.
  • The Talmud says Adam wept and said, "I and my donkey will eat from the same trough": moving from fruit to crops felt like falling to the level of an animal.
  • A vegetable or grain is cut and eaten and nothing remains. A fruit tree keeps its potential after its fruit is eaten, and the fruit itself holds seeds for more. Adam's capacity for spiritual growth was reduced, and his diet with it.
  • We know this innately: a person who can no longer function mentally is called a vegetable, never a fruit.
  • Fruit trees stand tall and strive upwards; on Shabbat we say the Saddik flourishes like a tamar, the palm, the tallest fruit tree. Vegetables lie on the ground, representing physicality.
  • So Tu BiShvat marks our potential and ability to grow, and we should try to grow like fruit.

Sources quoted

  • Mishnah Rosh Hashanah: the four new years, including the new year for trees.
  • The Talmud: Adam's tears, "I and my donkey will eat from the same trough".
  • The Shabbat psalm: the Saddik flourishes like a palm tree.

In one line

We celebrate the new year for fruit trees because fruit stands for potential and growth, which is what a person is meant to be.
