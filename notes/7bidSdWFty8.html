Topic: What counts as "after the fact" for a Kohen and a daughter of a non-Jewish father

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; Rav Moshe Feinstein; Rav Ovadiah Yoseph; the Rambam; a ruling of the Rabbanut

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 5 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Such a daughter should not marry a Kohen in the first instance, but if married with huppah and kiddushin, they may stay together (bedieved). Contemporary poskim ask what counts as bedieved.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: a civil marriage is not enough. A rabbi should not officiate at their kiddushin; they should divorce civilly and not live together, since the Shulhan Arukh forbids it in the first instance.
  • Rav Ovadiah Yoseph disagrees: by most opinions a civil marriage is no marriage, so why should it matter? He goes further: even without a civil marriage, if they are in a deep, long relationship or engaged and will marry anyway, one may arrange a proper wedding.
  • His reasoning: there is more to gain than to lose. Otherwise they may abandon family purity and all halacha, live together without marriage, or leave the fold. (He adds that they might also marry in another denomination.) One can rely on the Rambam and the great poskim who permit it.
  • The Rabbanut in Israel has ruled leniently, and one may add the doubt whether Kohanim today are certain.
  • He knows of a respected Beth Din that would not authorise such a wedding, but if another rabbi performed it with huppah and kiddushin, would register it afterwards.

In one line

Rav Moshe Feinstein allows them to stay together only after a Jewish wedding, while Rav Ovadiah Yoseph permits a wedding where they are already civilly married or in a deep relationship.
