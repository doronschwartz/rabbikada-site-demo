Topic: The time for blowing shofar, why it is delayed until before Musaf, and blowing for oneself alone

Sources: The Torah ("Yom teruah yihyeh lakhem"); the Gemara; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Wednesday 21 September 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • "Yom teruah" teaches that shofar is a daytime misvah, from dawn (Amud HaShahar) to sunset. As with any daytime misvah, we wait in the first instance for sunrise, as with Shaharit.
  • Normally the earlier the better (zerizim makdimim lemisvot), as with Megillah, tefillin or the Arbaah Minim. Yet we blow only just before Musaf, after Shaharit and the Torah reading.
  • The Gemara relates that in Roman times an early blowing was taken as a call to battle and many were killed. So it was instituted to blow later, clearly as part of the service.
  • The poskim write that one praying alone should not blow early, but wait until about three hours into the day, about 9:30 in London. The takkanah stands though the reason no longer applies.
  • The 30 blasts during Musaf were instituted only for communal prayer. One praying Musaf alone does not blow during it, which is why only 30 are blown for the homebound.
  • One alone needs only 30; he may blow more if he wishes, but not during Musaf.

In one line

Shofar is blown late in the morning even by one praying alone, who needs only the 30 blasts and none during Musaf.
