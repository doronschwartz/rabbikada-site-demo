Class Notes — Rabbi Daniel Kada

Sefer: מורה נבוכים / Moreh Nebuchim — The Guide for the Perplexed Author: רבינו משה בן מימון — Rambam (Maimonides) Section: Introductory Letter to Rabbi Yosef + Beginning of the Introduction Text: Sefaria (modern Hebrew translation from Arabic, likely Prof. Schwartz)

Preliminary Notes

  • Which text are we using? Sefaria has a modern Hebrew translation from the Arabic — not the Rambam's original words, but a translation. Rav Kapach's translation with footnotes is the primary study text for preparation.
  • Important: The Moreh is not really a "book" — Rambam never calls it that. He wrote essays/chapters and sent them to his student. They were later compiled into what we call the Moreh.
  • The headlines (rashei perakim) on Sefaria are NOT from the Rambam — they're modern editorial summaries. Same for bracketed text.

The Introductory Letter: Why Rambam Wrote This

Opening — בשם ה' אל עולם

  • Rambam opens with the pasuk from the end of Parashat Vayera: ויטע אשל בבאר שבע ויקרא שם בשם ה' אל עולם
  • Avraham = the father of monotheism, proclaiming the unity of Hashem to the world
  • Rambam is saying: I am continuing this mission — proclaiming the yichud Hashem

The Student: Rabbi Yosef ben Rabbi Yehuda

  • Rambam addresses his student Rabbi Yosef ben Yehuda ibn Shimon
  • We know: he was a member of the Beit Din in Egypt for a period, died in 1226
  • He came from far away to study under the Rambam in Egypt

The Progression — How Rambam Assessed His Student

Before meeting in person:

  • Rabbi Yosef sent letters, poetry, and prose from Alexandria
  • Rambam was impressed but cautious: שמא תשוקתו גדולה מהשגתו — "Perhaps his desire is greater than his reach"

After meeting:

  • Rambam guided him in astronomy → saw he already had a basis
  • Sent him to study more mathematics (prerequisite for astronomy)
  • Then more astronomy
  • Then logic
  • Only THEN: felt it appropriate to reveal the secrets of the Nevi'im

The Hierarchy of Study

There's a clear order the Rambam insists on:

  1. Mathematics (foundation)
  2. Astronomy (builds on maths)
  3. Logic (the discipline of reasoning)
  4. Secrets of Torah / Theology (only after all the above)

Imagine today: Going to a rav to study theology and he says "First, go study some more maths and science. Then come back to me." That's what the Rambam did.

The Mutakallimun (Kalam Philosophers)

  • Rabbi Yosef asked Rambam to explain the Mutakallimun — Islamic philosophers
  • Kalam (كلام) = "words" in Arabic (the school of Islamic philosophy)
  • They tried to reconcile Greek philosophy with Islamic theology (8th–9th centuries onward)
  • Saadia Gaon relied heavily on the Mutakallimun in Emunot VeDeot
  • Rambam generally didn't like them — felt they were too apologetic
  • Rav Kapach thinks when Rambam says "you learned from others," he's referring to Saadia Gaon

The Catalyst — Why He Actually Wrote It

וכאשר גזר האל את פרידתנו... עוררוני הפגישות הללו

  • When Rabbi Yosef had to leave → Rambam felt a lack
  • The time they spent studying together had aroused something in him
  • He had thought about writing these things before but never got round to it
  • Now that his student was gone → he felt compelled to continue teaching through writing

הניע אותי חסרונך לחבר את החיבור הזה

  • "Your absence motivated me to compose this work"
  • Wrote in chapters/essays → sent them one by one to Rabbi Yosef

The Teacher-Student Dynamic

  • The Gemara: יותר ממה שהעגל רוצה לינוק הפרה רוצה להניק — "More than the calf wants to suck, the cow wants to nurse"
  • Pirkei Avot: מתלמידי יותר מכולם — "From my students I learned the most"
  • Teaching satisfies the teacher — when the student left, the Rambam felt a void → that void became the Moreh

The Rambam's Introduction — Purpose of the Moreh

Three Opening Verses

  1. Tehillim: הודיעני דרך זו אלך כי אליך נשאתי נפשי — "Show me the path, Hashem — I look to You for guidance"
  2. Mishlei: אליכם אישים אקרא וקולי אל בני אדם — "To you men (ishim) I call, and my voice to humans (bnei adam)"
  3. Mishlei: הט אזנך ושמע דברי חכמים ולבך תשית לדעתי — "Listen to the sages, but put your heart to MY knowledge"

Why these three?

  • Verse 1: General — guide me on the path
  • Verse 2 (Efodi's explanation): The Moreh has multiple levels of meaning
  • אישים = the elect, the wise → will understand deeper levels
  • בני אדם = regular people → will understand the surface level
  • Same text, different depths of understanding
  • Verse 3 (Rav Kapach): Rambam will quote many philosophers and sages throughout
  • Listen to them all — BUT: ולבך תשית לדעתי — at the end of the day, follow MY view

Purpose 1: Explaining Terms in Tanach

מטרתו הראשונה של חיבור זה היא ביאור משמעויות של מונחים המופיעים בספרי הנבואה

Three categories of terms that cause confusion:

Category 1: Multi-meaning terms (מונחים רב-משמעיים)

  • One Hebrew word with multiple meanings in different contexts
  • Example: חי (chai) — can mean "live," "raw," or when applied to Hashem (חי ה') → neither alive nor dead, something beyond
  • Example: קרן (keren) — can mean "horn" or "ray" (as in קרן עור פניו)
  • Simple people understand only ONE meaning → miss the deeper layers

Category 2: Borrowed/metaphorical terms (מושאלים)

  • Words used metaphorically — people take them literally
  • Classic example: עיני ה' (the eyes of Hashem) — doesn't mean literal eyes with pupils → means hashgachah (divine oversight/attention)
  • This is the core of anti-anthropomorphism — a major theme of the entire Moreh
  • Rambam will later write (end of Part 1): believing in a corporeal God is worse than serving avodah zarah
  • During his lifetime, many people — even rabbis — believed God had some kind of physicality

Category 3: Ambiguous terms (מסופקים)

  • Sometimes borrowed, sometimes multi-meaning — unclear which
  • Context determines the correct understanding

Who Is This Book For?

אין מטרתו של חיבור זה להסביר את כולם להמון

NOT for:

  • The masses / simple people
  • Beginners in study
  • Even great Talmidei Chachamim who only know the legal aspects of Torah (halacha, Gemara, dayanut) but nothing beyond

Written FOR a person who has ALL of the following:

  1. Religious — observant, committed to Torah
  2. Clear belief in the truth and veracity of Torah
  3. Completed their character — good middot, wholesome personality
  4. Studied philosophy — understands philosophical terminology and concepts
  5. Has intellectual curiosity — naturally drawn to understand more
  6. But is PERPLEXED — reads verses in Tanach that seem to contradict what they've studied philosophically

The Perplexed Person's Dilemma

Such a person faces two terrible options:

Option A: Follow their intellect → throw away their Torah beliefs → think they've abandoned the foundations of faith

Option B: Stick with their simple Torah understanding → suppress their intellectual curiosity → constantly troubled, walking a tightrope

ויהיה שרוי תמיד בכאב לב ובמבוכה גדולה

  • Either way → constant pain and great confusion
  • The Moreh is written to rescue this person from the dilemma

Purpose 2: Explaining Veiled Imagery in Tanach

ביאור משלים חבויים מאוד הכתובים בספרי נביאים

  • Some passages in Tanach use imagery/analogy/parable — but it's not obvious that they're imagery
  • Simple people read them at face value and think there's nothing deeper
  • Even wise people who read them at surface level → become confused
  • When the deeper meaning is explained → the confusion is resolved
  • This is why it's called "The Guide for the Perplexed"

What This Book Will NOT Do

אין אני אומר שחיבור זה פותר כל קשייה

  • Won't solve ALL your difficulties — but will solve most of them and the hardest ones
  • Won't treat any topic exhaustively — even in person that would be impossible, all the more so in writing
  • Won't explain every image/parable to its deepest level

Why Not Exhaustive? Two Reasons

Reason 1: These are matters of Ma'aseh Bereishit (physics/cosmology) and Ma'aseh Merkavah (theology/metaphysics)

  • The Gemara says: don't teach Ma'aseh Bereishit to two people, don't teach Ma'aseh Merkavah even to one — unless he's wise, and even then only rashei perakim (headlines/rubrics)
  • Therefore: אל תבקש ממני יותר מראשי פרקים — "Don't expect more than general guidelines"

Reason 2: Deliberately not systematic

אפילו התחלות אלו אינן מסודרות... אלא מפוזרות ומעורבות

  • Rambam purposefully scattered and mixed the ideas throughout the book
  • He wants the truth to shine through but also to disappear again
  • He doesn't want to go against Hashem's intent: סוד ה' ליראיו — the secrets are for those who fear Hashem

The Bombshell About Bereishit

דע שגם בדברים הטבעיים אין ללמוד בפירוש

  • Even matters of physics — the Torah doesn't state them plainly
  • Even the Chachamim spoke about them in riddles and parables
  • Implication: The first chapter of Bereishit = Ma'aseh Bereishit = cosmology/physics
  • Rambam is saying: Bereishit is not to be understood at face value — it's analogy, it's imagery for something deeper
  • Physics is a prerequisite for theology → both have secrets → both are expressed through imagery in Torah

The Lightning Analogy — Levels of Understanding

לעתים האמת מציצה לנו כך שאנו חושבים אותה ליום ושוב... לחזור ולהיבלע לחושך

  • Humans grope in darkness in matters of physics and metaphysics
  • Sometimes we get a streak of light → then darkness returns

Key Points About the Analogy

  • Prophecy is not constant — even prophets don't always experience it
  • They "tune in": והיה כנגן המנגן — they would use music to enter the right state
  • Some prophets experienced more visions than others (did Yechezkel have more than Yeshayahu? We don't know)
  • For us without prophecy: the best we can hope for is occasional intellectual shining — how much depends on our own development
  • Rambam's key message: The more you develop your intellect AND your character → the more light you'll receive from this book

Yom Ha'atzmaut Thought

Magdil vs. Migdol in Birkat HaMazon

  • Weekdays: מגדיל ישועות מלכו (from Tehillim) — active, Hif'il → you are CAUSING salvation, bringing it about
  • Shabbat: מגדול ישועות מלכו (from Shmuel) — completed tower → everything is whole, the redemption is complete
  • Weekdays = ששת ימים תעשה — we work, we build, we bring about yeshuah. We are in magdil mode.
  • Shabbat = כאילו כל מלאכתך עשויה — we exist, we BE, everything feels complete. We are in migdol mode.
  • The prayer: that Eretz Yisrael will go from magdil (building, working) to migdol (the completed tower)

Rav Kook — Why Rabbi Abba Kissed the Stones

  • Gemara (Ketubot): Rabbi Abba would kiss the stones and dust of Eretz Yisrael when arriving
  • Rav Kook: why specifically stones? Not the earth?
  • If he kissed the earth → you'd think it's because of mitzvot ha-teluyot ba'aretz (agricultural mitzvot)
  • Stones have no growth, no agricultural mitzvot → he kissed them for the intrinsic kedushah of the land itself

Rav Reines — Ben Tzion

  • The Chida: if a woman's children are dying in infancy → name the next child Ben Tzion for protection
  • Rav Reines' metaphorical interpretation:
  • "Children dying" = people dying spiritually, losing faith, losing connection to Torah
  • "Call them Ben Tzion" = give them a love of Tzion, a love of Israel
  • Because if they love Israel → subconsciously they believe in Torah (the promised land = Torah's promise)
  • Eretz Yisrael can be the anchor that brings people back

Key Takeaways

  1. The Moreh was written because Rambam's student left → the void of not teaching drove him to write.
  2. Prerequisites for understanding the Moreh: maths → astronomy → logic → then theology.
  3. Purpose 1: Explain multi-meaning, metaphorical, and ambiguous terms in Tanach.
  4. Purpose 2: Explain veiled imagery in Tanach that isn't obviously metaphorical.
  5. Written for: Religious, philosophically educated people who are perplexed between Torah and philosophy.
  6. Won't be exhaustive — only rashei perakim (headlines). Deliberately scattered, not systematic.
  7. Bereishit is not to be read at face value — it's Ma'aseh Bereishit expressed through imagery.
  8. Lightning analogy: Different people receive different amounts of intellectual/prophetic light — from Moshe's constant illumination to complete darkness. Our level depends on our own development.
