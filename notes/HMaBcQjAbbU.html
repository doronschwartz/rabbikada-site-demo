Topic: What is the right age for men to marry today?

Sources: The Gemara; the Hida; the Rambam; Rav Eliezer Melamed

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 27 January 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The ideal age in the Gemara, the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh is 18 to 20. The Gemara itself allows one fully engaged in Torah study, who needs time to develop, to postpone marriage for a period (not indefinitely).
  • What of most young men today, who are not in full-time Torah study? The Rabbi presents the approach of Rav Eliezer Melamed, which he finds most reasonable, combining three principles: marriage and children are a great misvah; "Peru urevu" has no fixed time, which is why Hazal already moved it from 13 to 18; and one must prepare responsibly.
  • The Gemara, on the next page, mentions 24 as an acceptable age. Based on this the Hida writes that even one with a reason not to marry by 20 should try to marry by 24.
  • The Rambam, from the Gemara: the wise first find a livelihood, then a home, then marry; fools marry first and end up relying on charity.
  • Today one can hardly be financially prepared by 20, yet waiting for a job and a house could mean 30 to 40. Rav Melamed therefore suggests aiming to marry by about 24, with a plan for a livelihood. Earlier is better when one is ready; to say the age no longer matters and one may wait until 30 or 35 is, in the Rabbi's view, not correct.

In one line

Today young men should plan to marry by about 24, earlier if they are responsibly ready, with some leeway but no indefinite delay.
