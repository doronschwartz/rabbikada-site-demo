Topic: Kashering a Shabbat hotplate, an urn's lid and mouth, and a microwave for Pesah

Sources: Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph and the poskim

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Friday 4 April 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A Shabbat hotplate has spills and hallot placed directly on it, absorbing through dry heat above Yad Soledet Bo, so it would seemingly need Libbun.
  • Since that is not possible, many poskim, Hakham Ovadiah included, are lenient on a combination of factors: majority use, and the view that hames during the year is Hetera Bala. The consensus is to clean it, leave it 24 hours and heat it at the highest temperature for about an hour, relying on the view that Libbun may be done at the temperature of absorption.
  • Hakham Ovadiah goes further and says pouring boiling water on it is enough, though leaving it on for an hour is better. To satisfy all opinions one may cover it with foil or use a new hotplate.
  • An urn's lid needs kashering only if hallot were warmed on it during the year. Then dip it in a boiling pot, half at a time if needed. It is doubtful majority use applies to a lid.
  • The mouth of the urn needs no kashering: it is mostly used for tea and coffee, and occasional soup or pasta heated under it follows the majority. To be strict, let steam from boiling water rise into it.
  • The microwave's glass plate needs no kashering, since glass does not absorb.
  • The walls are disputed. Some say thorough cleaning suffices, since it mainly reheats cooked food and the walls rarely reach Yad Soledet Bo. Others require kashering: clean it, wait 24 hours, then heat a bowl of water (with soap, to satisfy all opinions) for 10 to 15 minutes.

Sources quoted

  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: lenient on the hotplate, even allowing pouring boiling water over it.

In one line

A hotplate is cleaned and heated on high for an hour, an urn's lid is dipped in boiling water if used for hallot, and a microwave is cleaned and steamed with a bowl of water.
