Topic: When to pray Arvit and make Kiddush when Shavuot falls on Mosei Shabbat

Sources: Shulhan Arukh, laws of Shabbat

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Tuesday 31 May 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • In a regular year Sepharadim may make Kiddush on Shavuot from sunset. On Mosei Shabbat (Shabbat ends about 10.15 in London) it is harder, since the holiness of Shabbat is greater than that of Shavuot.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules that one under duress, such as one leaving straight after Shabbat on a journey for a Misvah, may pray Arvit with Atah Honantanu and make Havdalah from Pelag HaMinhah, without the blessings on fire and spices. Havdalah marks the distinction even before Shabbat ends, though work stays forbidden until it does. The poskim allow this only in great need.
  • So this year, in the first instance, one should not pray Arvit from sunset and make Kiddush early, as that takes from the sanctity of Shabbat. The Kiddush also includes Boreh Meorei HaEsh, which cannot be said before Shabbat ends.
  • Since opinions differ on the exact end of Shabbat, one may pray Arvit slightly before, say from 9.45, and make Kiddush after 10.15.
  • In extenuating circumstances, such as an elderly or unwell person who cannot stay up, one of the poskim permits praying Arvit and making Kiddush early, without Boreh Meorei HaEsh. Likewise if a person or community would otherwise have no Kiddush or Yom Tov meal at all, it is better done after sunset.
  • One who in such circumstances makes Kiddush early may set the table and eat, since it is for now.

In one line

On Shavuot after Shabbat, wait until Shabbat ends for Kiddush and the meal, praying Arvit just before; earlier only in real need.
