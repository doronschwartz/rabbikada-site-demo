Topic: Fuels and wicks for Hanukkah, and using Shemitah olive oil from Israel

Sources: Mishnah Shabbat, chapter Bameh Madlikin

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • On Friday night we recite Bameh Madlikin, which lists oils and wicks not to be used for Shabbat candles, lest they burn poorly and one not enjoy the meal. That concern does not apply to Hanukkah, so on Shabbat Hanukkah it is not recited.
  • All oils and wicks, including wax candles, may be used for Hanukkah. Olive oil is best lekhathilah, better than wax, but wax candles fulfil the misvah.
  • This is a Shemitah year. Produce of the seventh year may only be used for consumption and benefit; wasting it is forbidden. Burning oil for light or heat counts as proper use, so Shemitah oil may be used for Shabbat candles.
  • Hanukkah lights may not be used for one's own benefit (for instance, reading by them), so some argue that Shemitah oil burnt for them is wasted and forbidden.
  • There is what to rely on to permit it: sitting by the lights and watching them is itself a benefit, so the oil is not wasted.
  • Moreover almost everyone lights a shamash, which allows benefit from all the candles; with a shamash one may use even Shemitah oil from Israel.

In one line

Any fuel may be used on Hanukkah, olive oil being best, and with a shamash even Shemitah oil from Israel is permitted.
