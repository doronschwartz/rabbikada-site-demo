Class Notes: Dayan Daniel Kada

Series: Rabbenu Avraham ben HaRambam on the Parashah: ideas from Rabbenu Avraham's commentary on the Torah (which survives on Bereshit and Shemot), taught each week at the Monday HaMaspik LeOvdei HaShem shiur.

Source: Taught at the HaMaspik shiur of the week of Vayetse 5787, HaMaspik 10.5.

Topic: Two last ideas on Toledot, then seven ideas on Vayetse. Most of them echo what we are learning in the chapter on perishut: a life that is not busy with the physical, and a mind that is in charge of the body.

The Rabbi: "It has become one of my favourite things to do on Shabbat now, going through Rabbenu Avraham's commentary. I'm specifically finding things which are unique, or related to what we've been learning."

Part One — Two Ideas on Parashat Toledot

1. Why lentils?

ויזד יעקב נזיד — Yaakov was cooking lentils when Esav came home. Why does the Torah tell us what he was cooking?

  • The Midrash: Avraham died that day, so that he would not see Esav go astray. Yishak was sitting shivah, and lentils are the mourner's food: round, like the cycle of life. A beautiful Midrash, but not the simple meaning.
  • Ibn Ezra: perhaps the family had lost its wealth, and Yaakov could only afford lentils. Rabbenu Avraham rejects this: "פירוש חסר טעם ודבר רחוק", an explanation without reason and far-fetched.
  • Rabbenu Avraham: ואפשר שהיה יעקב עם חבירי דרך השם מטפל בזרעונים ובקטניות בהרבה מסעודותיו, וממעט באכילת הבשר ומאכלי העונג, לא מפני חסרון דברים גשמיים, כמו שאמר בספר המספיק לעובדי השם. Yaakov came from a very wealthy family, yet he chose simple food, legumes and lentils rather than meat and rich dishes. Not because he lacked anything, but out of perishut. He quotes his own HaMaspik, the very chapter we are learning.

2. Why call a well "Esek"?

ויריבו רועי גרר עם רועי יצחק לאמור לנו המים, ויקרא שם הבאר עשק כי התעשקו עמו. Yishak re-dug his father's wells, the shepherds of Gerar fought over one, and he named it Esek, "business". Why name a well after an argument?

Rabbenu Avraham: יובן מזה סוד גדול, כי האבות עם כל עושרם הגדול ונכסיהם המרובים לא היו עוסקים בעניינים גשמיים. For all their wealth, the Avot spent their time and their thoughts on spiritual things. This well dragged Yishak into a dispute, "באר זו נתנה לנו עסק מה שאין כסדר נתעסק בו": it made him busy with something outside his routine. He named it Esek to remind himself: this is not what I normally do.

Both ideas on Toledot carry the same theme: don't busy yourself too much with the physical and mundane.

Part Two — Seven Ideas on Parashat Vayetse

1. ויפגע במקום: reaching, praying, striking

Yaakov left Beer Sheva, ויפגע במקום: he reached the place. Pegiah means to reach or meet. Rabbenu Avraham:

  • ומזה הושאל לתפילה פגיעה, לפי שהוא פגישת מתפלל אדונו. Prayer is called pegiah because in prayer we meet our Master. "We encounter the divine." (We will learn more in Chapter 12, on tefillah.)
  • ומזה הושאל להריגה פגיעה, לפי שההורג מתקרב להרוג. Killing is also pegiah, because the killer has to come close to his victim.

2. Even bread and clothes are tithed

ונתן לי לחם לאכול ובגד ללבוש... וכל אשר תתן לי עשר אעשרנו לך.

In HaMaspik (here and in the chapter on Bitahon) Rabbenu Avraham brought this verse to show that we ask Hashem only for essentials, not luxuries. The Rabbi shared a joke he was sent: someone asked a friend for a loan of a million pounds, and the friend answered, "I would be embarrassed to ask God for that!"

Here he adds: ואפילו מן הפחות ממה שנתן לי אני מקבל על עצמי לתת מעשר. Even the little Yaakov asked for, bread and a garment, he would tithe, לפי שחיובים כלפי הקדוש ברוך הוא... מן הפחות ומן המרובה. Our obligations to Hashem apply to little and to much alike.

A Midrash the Rabbi had never seen. Yaakov tithed even his sons. Count Efrayim and Menasheh with his twelve sons (as Yaakov said they would be like his own), giving fourteen. Take out the four firstborns (Reuven, Yoseph, Dan and Gad, one from each mother), and ten are left. The tithe of the ten is Levi, dedicated to the service of Hashem. Rabbenu Avraham: "מאמר נעים ונפלא... ואף על פי שאינו כוונת המקרא לגמרי", a lovely saying, but not the meaning of the verse. (The Rabbi later found it in Bereshit Rabbah.)

3. Yaakov lifted his feet

וישא יעקב רגליו וילך ארצה בני קדם. Why "lifted his feet"?

  1. He walked, and did not ride an animal.
  2. More deeply: אף על פי שלפי הנראה הרגליים הן נושאות את האדם בעל הנפש, הן באמת נישאות על ידי רצונו. It looks as though the legs carry a person, but really the person's will carries the legs.

Every movement should be a conscious decision. The Rabbi heard from one of his teachers that the Hazon Ish never moved his hand without thinking.

The Rabbi added his idea of מלך: מוח, לב, כבד. A king decides in the mind, then wants it in the heart, then does it. The reverse, כלם, is acting first and justifying it afterwards; that was the way of Bilam. Yaakov lifted his feet: the mind was in charge, not the body.

4. Why did seven years feel like a few days?

ויהיו בעיניו כימים אחדים באהבתו אותה. The famous question: when you long for something, the waiting feels longer, not shorter.

Rabbenu Avraham: they were כימים מועטים, כלי ערך בצד החשק, few in comparison with his desire, though in truth a long time. And אפילו הזמן הקצר ארוך לאוהב: to a lover, even a short time is long. So the verse shows something about Yaakov's love:

בכל אהבתו לא היתה מחשבתו עסוקה בתאוות החושים כדי שיאריך לו הזמן, אלא... במטרת אהבתו האמיתית, ולכך לא ארך לו הזמן.

Had it been a physical, sensual love, seven years would have dragged. Because his love was spiritual, the time passed quickly.

The Rabbi's addition (a parable he saw years ago, he thinks from Rav Shimshon Pinkus): your employer promises £1,000 for every day you come on time for 100 days. You don't count down; you tick each day off: "day one, £1,000." That is why we count the Omer upwards. When every day builds towards the goal, the days fly. Each day of the seven years was preparation for Yaakov's union with Rahel.

5. Why was Dinah called Dinah?

ואחר ילדה בת ותקרא את שמה דינה, from דין, judgement. Two reasons:

  1. הניגוד הנודע להולדת הבנות: the well-known (and, the Rabbi notes, to modern ears difficult) displeasure at the birth of girls, seen as a judgement.
  2. שהרגישה לאה בצרות העתידות לבוא בגללה: Leah sensed, prophetically, the troubles that would come through her daughter, the story of Shekhem. Through this daughter, האל יתעלה פועל דינו עלינו, Hashem's strict judgement would be revealed. "Imagine the trauma for Yaakov and Leah."

6. דלקת: pursuit in anger

After Lavan searched his tents for the idols, Yaakov protests: כי דלקת אחרי. Why not רדפת?

Rabbenu Avraham, quoting his grandfather Rabbi Maimon: הרדיפה שם לכל ריצה ויציאה אחרי חפץ או בן אדם, ואילו הדלקה שם לרדיפה בכעס בלבד. Redifah is any pursuit, even of honour. Delikah is pursuit in anger only. It makes sense: דלק is fire, as in להדליק נר חנוכה. Lavan pursued him with fire and venom.

7. Yaakov swore by פחד יצחק

At the covenant, Lavan says אלהי אברהם ואלהי נחור: שיתוף על ידי לבן בין אל האמת... ובין עבודה זרה. He joined the true God with idolatry.

ויתרחק יעקב עליו השלום משיתופו... וכינה אותו יתעלה בשם פחד אביו יצחק, כדי להתרחק מלשון לבן. Had Yaakov sworn "by the God of Avraham", it would sound as if he agreed with Lavan's oath. So he swore בפחד אביו יצחק, a name Lavan had not used, to keep away from any association with Lavan's avodah zarah.
