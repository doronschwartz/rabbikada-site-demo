Topic: The minimum quantity of dough for Hallah, with and without a berakhah

Sources: Parashat Shelah Lekha ("reshit arisotekhem"); the Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; Hazon Ovadiah; the Ben Ish Hai

Daily Halacha: Hallah, Monday 4 July 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Torah says "the first of your dough". The Gemara explains: the dough of the wilderness, an omer, which is a tenth of an eifah.
  • An eifah is 72 log and a log is 6 eggs, so a tenth of an eifah is 7.2 log, or 43.2 eggs. Everyone agrees on 43.2 eggs.
  • Shulhan Arukh gives this as 520 dirham, but the dirham's exact size and how compressed the flour must be are disputed, hence differing opinions.
  • Hazon Ovadiah: a dirham is about 3 grams, so about 1.6 kg of flour; at that amount one separates Hallah with a berakhah.
  • The Ben Ish Hai calculated 777 dirham of about 3.2 grams, about 2.5 kg. On this view, from about 1.6 to 2.5 kg one separates without a berakhah because of the doubt, and says the berakhah only from 2.5 kg.
  • Ashkenazim generally say about 2.25 kg; opinions vary but it is above 2 kg.
  • In practice it depends whom one follows, but many follow Hazon Ovadiah: 1.6 kg.

Sources quoted
