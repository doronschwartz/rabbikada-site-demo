Topic: Sperm banks outside Israel and in Israel, and the question of shetuki

Sources: Rav Moshe Feinstein; Rav Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 14 July 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Outside Israel, hardly any bank is in a city with a Jewish majority, so one may assume the donor is not Jewish, which is far less problematic halachically.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein writes that in the diaspora the great majority are non-Jews, so there is no concern of siblings marrying. It is good to verify if possible, which may be hard today with equality laws, but strictly one may rely on the majority, in the circumstances discussed.
  • In Israel the majority is Jewish, which raises the issue of shetuki: the mother is known and the father is not, so under the majority he is Jewish and may have had a blemish. A shetuki is a safek mamzer.
  • Rav Ovadiah Yoseph was asked by a woman who, single and not religious, had a child through donation and later became religious (hozeret biteshuvah): may her son marry a regular Jewish girl?
  • He permitted it, and the Batei Din follow this even in Israel. Most Jews are kasher, and the reason a shetuki is not decided by majority is the principle that something fixed (kavua) counts as half and half. A bank, however, is considered already separated from the population (parish), so the regular majority applies and the child is not a shetuki.
  • There are further reasons, but in short a child from a bank, even in Israel, is kasher and may marry.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: outside Israel one may rely on the majority being non-Jewish.
  • Rav Ovadiah Yoseph: a bank donation counts as separated, so the majority applies and the child is kasher.

In one line

A child from a sperm bank, outside Israel or even in Israel, is kasher and may marry.
