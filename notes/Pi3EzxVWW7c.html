Topic: Artificial insemination from a Jewish donor: the questions and the source in Rabbenu Peres

The halacha

  • Having finished Siman 4 on mamzerut and lineage (yohasin), and before Chapter 5, a few classes on artificial insemination and IVF. These are not discussed explicitly in the Rishonim or Aharonim, so the poskim of the last 50 to 60 years draw on earlier sources.
  • Artificial insemination means injecting a woman with another man's semen, for example when the husband cannot produce viable seed. IVF, fertilising the egg in a lab, is far newer, so most teshuvot deal with insemination.
  • The donor may be Jewish or non-Jewish. The first case: a married Jewish woman and a Jewish donor.
  • Three questions: is it permitted in the first instance; if done, is she forbidden to her husband, as a married woman who has relations with another man is; and is the child a mamzer?
  • The starting point is Rabbenu Peres, one of the Baalei HaTosafot about 800 years ago, also brought by the Hida: may a woman sleep on sheets a man has slept on, lest semen enter her and create a mamzer? Science now says this is impossible, but they thought it possible.
  • He applies it also to a husband's sheets when his wife is a niddah. He permits both, because there is no forbidden act of cohabitation (biat issur): even if she conceived that way, nothing prohibited took place.
  • How the Aharonim use this follows next time.

Sources quoted

  • Rabbenu Peres: a woman may sleep on a man's sheets, even a niddah on her husband's, since there is no biat issur.

In one line

Rabbenu Peres's ruling that conception without a forbidden act creates no problem is the starting point for artificial insemination.
