Topic: How to kasher kitchen countertops and sinks for Pesah

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Tuesday 1 April 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Countertops are mostly used with cold food, though hot food sometimes spills on them from a Keli Rishon. By majority use, if one is confident hot spills are rare, Sepharadim could strictly be lenient and not kasher at all. The custom, however, is to pour boiling water over them (Irui Keli Rishon).
  • One could argue full immersion is needed, since a pot straight off the fire may be placed on the counter. Because this is rare and immersion is impossible, even Ashkenazim rely on pouring.
  • This reflects the dispute about majority use: the Shulhan Arukh is lenient, and even the Rema holds one may strictly rely on it, though Ashkenazim ideally kasher as intensely as possible. Hence many Ashkenazim also cover their countertops.
  • For Sepharadim, pouring hot water suffices. If one is concerned about the material, covering it with thick foil is enough.
  • The surface must be clean and dry when the water is poured. Usually it takes several pourings from a kettle, and areas where water has trickled must be dried before the next pouring.
  • Sinks work the same way: start at the bottom of the basin in a circular motion, then the walls, making sure each spot is dry as you pour. Pouring suffices because hames would normally be absorbed only from hot tap water.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: lenient on majority use.
  • The Rema: majority use may strictly be relied on, but Ashkenazim kasher as intensely as possible.

In one line

Countertops and sinks are kashered by pouring boiling water on clean, dry surfaces, section by section, or the counters may be covered.
