Topic: Lighting candles for Yom Tov: the berakhah, its order, Shehehiyanu and the time of lighting

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; Tosafot (Rabbenu Tam); the Rambam; the Ben Ish Hai

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Wednesday 21 May 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Surprisingly, the Talmud nowhere says to light candles for Yom Tov. The vast majority of Rishonim compare it to Shabbat, and the Shulhan Arukh rules clearly that we light with a berakhah.
  • Tosafot cite Rabbenu Tam that there is no need: Shabbat candles were to provide light, since one cannot light on Shabbat and darkness would upset the household, whereas on Yom Tov one may light from an existing flame. Almost everyone disagreed, and the minhag of almost all Israel is to light with a berakhah.
  • Some Teimanim did not say a berakhah, since the Rambam mentions "lehadlik ner shel Shabbat" but never Yom Tov. Those with that custom should continue it.
  • For Shabbat there are different practices about lighting before or after the berakhah, but on Yom Tov almost all, Ashkenazim and Sepharadim, agree the correct practice is to say the berakhah first and then light.
  • Men say Shehehiyanu at Kiddush, and a woman fulfils it when she hears Kiddush. Some women, as the Ben Ish Hai records, say Shehehiyanu at candle lighting; those with an established minhag should continue. Otherwise it is probably best to say it at Kiddush, though there is nothing wrong with saying it at lighting, for example over a new dress.
  • On Shabbat the lighting time is strict. On Yom Tov, strictly, one who is running late may light after Yom Tov begins from an existing flame, since lighting is permitted, though as a misvah it should be done as soon as possible.

Sources quoted

  • Tosafot, citing Rabbenu Tam: no need for Yom Tov candles; the halacha does not follow this.
  • The Ben Ish Hai: women who say Shehehiyanu at lighting should keep their minhag.

In one line

Light Yom Tov candles with the berakhah said first, saying Shehehiyanu at Kiddush or by established custom at lighting, and if late, light from an existing flame.
