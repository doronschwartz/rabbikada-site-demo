Topic: Why the second day of Yom Tov is still kept outside Israel despite the fixed calendar

Sources: The Gemara; Rambam

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Wednesday 2 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • In the fourth century persecutions made monthly sanctification of the moon impossible. Hillel II (not Bet Hillel), a direct descendant of Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi, with his Bet Din, had the authority to institute a fixed calendar, in 369 CE, a remarkably precise achievement.
  • The Gemara then asks why a second day is needed if the date is known. Israel sent a message to Bavel: be careful with the custom of your forefathers, since the calendar may be disrupted again in the future.
  • So it is not merely a minhag: the Gemara instructs us to continue it, and that has been the continuous practice outside Israel (which places count as outside Israel is for a later discussion).
  • Today the fixed calendar cannot be disrupted, so seemingly there is no reason left. The answer connects to the Three Weeks: an essential part of the mourning is the loss of the Sanhedrin and the ability to interpret and decide law.
  • It is possible a Sanhedrin today would say the second day is no longer needed, though no one knows until it happens, and its composition would matter.
  • With the close of the Talmud, Rambam explains, nothing in it can be changed; the Talmud was the final Sanhedrin. There are Hasidic ideas about the diaspora needing two days to receive the holiness of the festival, but in strict halachic terms the reason is that the Talmud established it.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: the message from Israel to Bavel to keep the custom of the forefathers in case the calendar is disrupted.
  • Rambam: after the conclusion of the Talmud nothing established in it can be changed.

In one line

Outside Israel we keep two days because the Talmud, the final Sanhedrin, established it, and there is no body today that can change it.
