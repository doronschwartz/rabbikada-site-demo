Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 273, seifim 1-7 Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 16 November 2023

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

Kiddush only where you eat

After which wine may be used (Siman 272), Siman 273 adds another requirement: Kiddush must be made where the meal is eaten (Kiddush bimkom seudah). The Gemara in Pesahim learns it from the verse we say before Shabbat morning Kiddush, "and you shall call Shabbat a delight": in the place where you "call" Shabbat holy, there must be the delight of a meal.

The Rabbi stressed this: if you made Kiddush with the right wine, the right amount, everything correct, but did not eat there, "you have not done Kiddush." At least on a rabbinic level the misvah is not fulfilled. "Unfortunately people stumble in this week in, week out, especially when they go to kiddushim."

Seif 1: what counts as the same place

  • The same room is one place, from one end of a large hall to the other.
  • Another room in the same building is not, at least ideally.
  • The Rosh: if you can see the other place from where you made Kiddush, that is enough.
  • Some say: within the same building, if you intended while making Kiddush to eat elsewhere, that is enough, even if you cannot see it.
  • Never: making Kiddush in shul and eating at home, or moving to another house. That never works.

The ruling: ideally make Kiddush in the room where you eat. When there is a need, you may rely on seeing the place, or on intention within the same building. For example, your wife is cooking and wants to taste something, so you make Kiddush in the kitchen and then eat in the dining room. It is better at the table, but it works.

At a wedding on Shabbat, sometimes the washing station is outside the hall and bread is left there for HaMosi. Kiddush was made inside, so do not eat the bread outside: "take it inside."

Zimun is different. Two groups in rooms that open onto each other can join for zimun, because zimun is about eating together. That does not make them one place for Kiddush.

From house to sukkah. The Rema says going from the house to the sukkah is like moving within one room. The Mishnah Berurah explains this as a sukkah attached to the house. Other poskim say that even a sukkah in the garden works, because on Sukkot there is an assumed intention to eat in the sukkah. So if it rained, you made Kiddush indoors, and then it stopped, you may go out. During the rest of the year this does not apply. If you planned a garden picnic, made Kiddush inside because of clouds, and then the sun came out, you could not just go out and eat unless you had that intention or can see the place.

If the roof started leaking after Kiddush on Friday night and you had to go and eat at a neighbour's, you would make Kiddush again with its berakhot.

Seifim 2-3: you must actually eat, and soon

If you did not eat after Kiddush, you have not made Kiddush. The Rabbi gave a case that matters: on Shabbat morning you hear Kiddush in shul, take a sip of grape juice, and plan to eat properly at home. Now you may not eat even an apple or a sweet there, because as far as you are concerned you have not made Kiddush yet. If you are not going to eat the required amount, do not eat or drink anything.

Immediately. The Rema says you must eat right after Kiddush. What does that mean? Some say within half an hour. There is a strong case that it means straight away, with nothing in between. So:

  • Many people with large families bless the children before Kiddush, since going round children and grandchildren can take a long time. The Rabbi usually does it after Kiddush.
  • If you had to go out after Kiddush, the Hida allows it, but the Mishnah Berurah says not to. In any case, after more than half an hour, even through no fault of your own, you must make Kiddush again.
  • This is the origin of the custom to keep a basin for washing in the dining room, so nobody leaves the room between Kiddush and HaMosi.

Someone asked about Seder night, where Maggid comes between Kiddush and the meal. The Rabbi called it a famous question and said he would come back to it.

Seif 5: what counts as a meal

The Gemara does not define the meal; the Geonim do. Kiddush bimkom seudah is fulfilled by:

  • A kazayit of bread, or
  • A kazayit of mezonot. The Mishnah Berurah says the five grains are more substantial than wine, and everyone accepts mezonot, or
  • A reviit of wine (86 ml). Many Aharonim say wine is not enough and one relies on it only in need. The custom is to be lenient with a reviit, even on Friday night. The Mishnah Berurah brings a stringency that the reviit must be in addition to the Kiddush cheekful; the Rabbi said a reviit in total is enough.

Fruit does not count. Whisky does not count, and beer does not count even where it is the local drink.

Friday night Kiddush in shul. At the Rabbi's Friday night gatherings he drinks a reviit himself, so he is covered. But everyone else? "This is why whenever you have these kinds of kiddushim, I insist always" that there is mezonot on the table. On Pesah this is a real trap. The cakes are made of potato starch and eggs, which are not mezonot. People eat them before Kiddush, think they have made Kiddush, and do not make it again at home. He was not sure whether drinking wine alone at such a kiddush, without having mezonot, is allowed: "I would say better not to."

Shabbat day. Kiddush in shul with mezonot fulfils Kiddush. Lunch at home needs bread for the Shabbat meal, but strictly no new Kiddush. The Vilna Gaon did not accept this ruling of the Geonim and made Kiddush only where he was about to wash. Rav Moshe Feinstein says that even so, when you sit down to lunch at home you should make Kiddush again, even alone. "That's why I personally do as well." On Friday night you cannot repeat Kiddush like this.

Seif 4: making Kiddush for others

You may go to someone's home and make Kiddush for them without eating there yourself, because for them it is the place of the meal.

Why may you say Borei Peri HaGefen when you are not drinking? A berakhah on enjoyment (birkat hanehenin) can only be said for someone else if you are also eating or drinking. A berakhah on a misvah (birkat hamisvot) can be said for another person even after you have done the misvah yourself, because all Jews are responsible for each other. The Shulhan Arukh says the wine berakhah in Kiddush is part of the misvah of sanctifying Shabbat, so it takes on the status of a misvah berakhah. The Rema extends this to Shabbat morning, even though that Kiddush is only Borei Peri HaGefen.

Two conditions: - Only for someone who cannot say it themselves. The Mishnah Berurah's example is a widow who could not read; men would go to her home each Friday night to make Kiddush. - If you have not yet made Kiddush yourself and you are not staying for a meal, taste nothing there.

So if a man made Kiddush in shul on Friday night, he cannot come home and repeat it for his wife if she can read; she makes it herself. On Shabbat day he can.

Seifim 6-7: courtyards and candles

Seif 6. If your table is set and you hear a neighbour's Kiddush across the courtyard, with him intending to include you, you may answer amen and eat.

Seif 7. The Rosh held one should eat where the Shabbat candles are. This is not the halacha. In any case our electric lights count as Shabbat light, so it rarely comes up.

Practical summary

  • Make Kiddush in the room where you will eat. In need, another room you can see, or one you intended, in the same building.
  • Kiddush in shul never covers a meal at home on Friday night.
  • Eat straight after Kiddush; after more than half an hour, make Kiddush again.
  • A meal for Kiddush means a kazayit of bread or mezonot, or a reviit of wine. Fruit, whisky and beer do not count.
  • If you will not eat the right amount at a kiddush, taste nothing there. Synagogues should put out real mezonot, especially on Pesah.
  • On Shabbat day it is good to make Kiddush again at home before lunch.
