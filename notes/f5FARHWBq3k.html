Topic: Four cases where debts may be claimed after Shemitah without a Prozbol

Daily Halacha: Shemitah & Prozbol, Tuesday 24 May 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Without a Prozbol, loans may not be claimed after Rosh Hashanah 5783; claiming them violates a prohibition. But there are exceptions.
  • A loan secured by collateral (hamalveh al hamashkon): the lender acquires a proprietary right in the pledge, as if partly repaid, so he may claim indefinitely.
  • Store credit (hakafat hanut), such as a monthly account at a local shop, is a business transaction, not a loan, so the shopkeeper may claim it after Rosh Hashanah. The same applies to unpaid wages and to an invoice for work done, such as electrical work billed shortly before Rosh Hashanah.
  • A loan not yet due at Rosh Hashanah, for example lent today for repayment in six months, is not cancelled by this Shemitah (only by the next, if still unpaid).
  • A stipulation that Shemitah will not cancel the loan is void, as "matneh al mah shekatuv baTorah".
  • But the lender may lend on condition that the borrower waives his right to have the loan cancelled. This does not contradict the Torah, and if worded correctly it works.

In one line

Collateral loans, business debts, loans not yet due and loans where the borrower waived his right all survive Shemitah without a Prozbol.
