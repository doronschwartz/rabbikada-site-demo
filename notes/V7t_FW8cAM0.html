Topic: Applying the telephone opinions to Zoom, and why the daytime Megillah takes priority

Sources: Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach; Rav Ovadiah Yoseph; Rav Moshe Feinstein; Rav Sevi Pesah Frank; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Purim, Wednesday 17 February 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Recap: Rav Auerbach and Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph say the telephone does not work, as no human voice is heard; Rav Moshe Feinstein says it does, since even live speech is reproduced by sound waves; Rav Frank says it does, as hearing the voice indirectly suffices.
  • For Zoom: by the first view, all the more so it does not work. By Rav Frank's view it seemingly does.
  • By Rav Feinstein it is questionable: Zoom involves more steps and depends on an internet connection. He might extend his leniency, or he might say it is a step too far.
  • So hearing in person in a socially distanced, safe synagogue is certainly preferable, to accommodate Hakham Ovadiah and Rav Auerbach. One who is concerned or has no minyan nearby should know Zoom is certainly better than nothing, and a telephone may be better than Zoom, since Rav Feinstein ruled clearly on the telephone. The order is: in person, telephone, then Zoom.
  • We read twice, and people sometimes neglect the day reading after hearing it at night. The opposite is true: the night reading is purely rabbinic, while the day reading is divrei Neviim, the original misvah instituted by Mordekhai.
  • The poskim write that a doctor on shifts who can hear only one reading should choose the day. Likewise anyone who can come to synagogue only once should come by day.

In one line

Hear the Megillah in person if at all possible, otherwise by telephone and then Zoom, and above all be present for the daytime reading.
