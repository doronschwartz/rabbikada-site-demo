Topic: How to approach Shemini Aseret and Simhat Torah 5785, a year after 7 October

Sources: The Vilna Gaon; Rav Yissakhar Shlomo Teichtal, Em HaBanim Semehah; the fourth berakhah of Birkat HaMazon (Hakhamim of Yavneh); Kohelet; Devarim ("Israel dwells securely"); the Hakafot

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Tuesday 22 October 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Vilna Gaon is said (the attribution is not certain) to have called rejoicing continuously all Sukkot the hardest misvah. This year we also face the first Hebrew anniversary of 7 October.
  • On 7 October 2023 it was Simhat Torah in Israel but Shemini Aseret abroad. Outside Israel, Shemini Aseret is the natural day to remember: Yizkor for Ashkenazim, a Hashkavah for Sepharadim, as on Holocaust Memorial Day. Israel and, for us, Simhat Torah raise the question of how to dance.
  • It is not the first Simhat Torah massacre. Accounts tell of Krakow, perhaps in 1655, where attackers broke into the synagogue during the fourth hakafah. Until the Holocaust, Krakow danced three hakafot and turned the fourth into a kind of Tisha BeAv.
  • A different approach: Rav Yissakhar Shlomo Teichtal, a Hasidic Rav and fervent Zionist, author of Em HaBanim Semehah, who perished in 1945. Asked in 1940 whether to scale back a siyum and festive meal, he said not to diminish it at all: invite everyone, serve every delicacy, since rejoicing in a misvah is the best way to remove harsh decrees.
  • The fourth berakhah of Birkat HaMazon was added in Yavneh after Beitar, when, years after Hadrian forbade burial, the bodies were found undecomposed and could be buried. The Hakhamim thanked HaShem as good and doing good despite the massacre.
  • There is a time to mourn and a time to dance. Tears and outrage have their place but do not save a people; gratitude and hope do.
  • On Simhat Torah the whole world reads "Israel dwells securely": painful memories will return, but we hope to live confidently in our land. Our greatest victory is to keep thriving, celebrating and dancing.
  • Each community and person should mark what happened appropriately. We are a couple of thousand Jews fewer since 7 October, with the soldiers who fell since; we must fill their seats and recommit to Torah, for "Nesah Yisrael lo yeshakker".

Sources quoted

  • Rav Teichtal: do not diminish a misvah celebration even in persecution.
  • Birkat HaMazon: "Hatov vehametiv" instituted after the burial of Beitar's dead.

In one line

We remember 7 October fittingly, then dance on Simhat Torah with gratitude and hope, as Jews always have.
