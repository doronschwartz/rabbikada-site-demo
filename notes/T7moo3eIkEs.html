Topic: The first six melakhot (ploughing to winnowing) on Yom Tov compared with Shabbat

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; Rambam; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Wednesday 18 June 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Melakhot unrelated to food, such as writing and building, are forbidden on Yom Tov as on Shabbat. The discussion concerns the first 11 melakhot, which relate to food preparation.
  • Ploughing (horesh) and planting (zorea) are not for food on Yom Tov, so they are identical to Shabbat.
  • Harvesting (koser), such as taking an apple off a tree, is forbidden even to eat it on Yom Tov (whether from the Torah or rabbinically is debated), because harvesting is normally done in bulk for the long term. Gathering (meammer) fallen fruit is likewise forbidden.
  • Threshing (dash), extracting the edible from the inedible, is generally forbidden for the same reason. But the Shulhan Arukh and Rambam rule that by hand, where it cannot be done in bulk, it is permitted: one may shell peas from their pods normally on Yom Tov, whereas on Shabbat a shinui is needed.
  • Squeezing olives and grapes, a toladah of dash, is forbidden. A minority of poskim allowed squeezing other fruits such as oranges, but the vast majority, including Hakham Ovadiah, forbid it as on Shabbat.
  • Winnowing (zoreh) is a long-term activity and forbidden. So the first six melakhot are basically as on Shabbat, apart from shelling by hand.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh and Rambam: shelling by hand is permitted on Yom Tov.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: squeezing any fruit is forbidden on Yom Tov.

In one line

On Yom Tov the first six melakhot are as forbidden as on Shabbat, except that shelling peas and the like by hand is permitted.
