Topic: When a fast on Friday ends: at nightfall, or once Shabbat is accepted

Sources: Gemara Eruvin ("mitaanin umashlimin"); the Mordekhai quoting Rabbenu Yishak; the Maharam of Rothenburg; the Ran; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Asarah BeTevet

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Recap: for Sefaradim the fast begins 72 minutes before sunrise; one may eat earlier only if one stipulated before going to sleep (Ashkenazim allow drinking even without stipulating).
  • Every fast normally ends at nightfall (Set HaKokhavim), since from sunset it is already a doubt.
  • When Asarah BeTevet falls on Friday, the Gemara says "one fasts and completes". The Mordekhai, quoting Rabbenu Yishak, and the Maharam of Rothenburg record that they prayed Minhah and Arvit, went home and ate even before sunset, since Shabbat had been accepted; on this view the Gemara only permits completing the fast.
  • Most Rishonim, the Ran among them, reject this: one must complete the fast until nightfall even after accepting Shabbat.
  • Shulhan Arukh: one fasts until night. For a private fast (for example on a parent's anniversary) one who stipulated may eat earlier; the Rema allows a private faster to eat after praying even before nightfall. For a public fast, the consensus is to wait until nightfall.
  • In practice: one may say Shalom Aleikhem and the rest, but complete Kiddush only after nightfall. Someone weak or struggling, whose Shabbat is suffering, may rely on the lenient view after sunset once Shabbat is accepted.
  • A fast for a bad dream (taanit halom) is the exception: all agree it is completed until night, since it may even be kept on Shabbat.

In one line

On a Friday fast, healthy people wait for nightfall before Kiddush even after accepting Shabbat, but in difficulty one may rely on the Rishonim who allow eating once Shabbat is accepted.
