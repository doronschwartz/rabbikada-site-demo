Topic: Whether a couple who have fulfilled Peru Urevu and Shevet may stop having children

Sources: Gemara (the wife of Rabbi Hiyya); the Rema; the Arukh HaShulhan (Rabbi Epstein)

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 9 February 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • This discussion applies only once a couple have a boy and a girl (Peru Urevu) and have had further children for the misvah of Shevet. A family with only girls or only boys is still obligated to continue.
  • Many poskim hold that one must continue having children unless there are physical or mental concerns, and some say so even when one is unsure of being able to provide for them, trusting in HaKadosh Barukh Hu.
  • There are other views, and with so many variables in each family it is hard to give definitive rulings, so one must consult.
  • The Gemara tells of Yehudit, wife of Rabbi Hiyya, who had four children. In disguise she asked him whether a woman is commanded in Peru Urevu, he said no, and she made herself unable to have more children. The Gemara does not condemn this, though Rabbi Hiyya would have liked more children.
  • The Rema permits a widower with children to marry a woman who cannot have children if new children would cause friction with his existing ones.
  • The Arukh HaShulhan writes that the misvah to continue beyond Peru Urevu and Shevet applies when one can provide for the children, so some poskim allow stopping at that point.
  • All agree that every extra child is a great misvah, and one who has the financial and mental ability should continue.

Sources quoted

  • Gemara: the story of Rabbi Hiyya's wife, with the children's names given (Yehudah, Hizkiyah, Pazi and Tavi).
  • The Rema: the case of marrying a woman who cannot bear children to avoid strife between families.
  • The Arukh HaShulhan: the obligation beyond Peru Urevu and Shevet depends on the ability to provide.

In one line

After Peru Urevu and Shevet there are views that permit stopping, but more children is the greater misvah where one is able, and each case needs rabbinic guidance.
