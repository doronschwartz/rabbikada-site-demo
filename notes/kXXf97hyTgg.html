Topic: How Jewish identity is proved in practice today

Sources: Rav Moshe Feinstein, Teshuvah

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 13 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The simplest proof is the parents' ketubah from a recognised Orthodox body or rabbi, with birth certificates linking it to the person.
  • If the parents married in a Reform or other unrecognised setting, one can link maternally: for example a maternal aunt's Orthodox ketubah, plus birth certificates of the two sisters and of the person.
  • Where there are no records, as is common from some Middle Eastern countries, a combination is used. A person who practises and properly knows Jewish minhag and halacha and lives in a community will almost always be accepted; people from the Middle East have been verified this way.
  • Testimony from a recognised witness who knew the person and the parents as Jews in the original country is also often sufficient.
  • Burial of a parent in a Jewish cemetery is not definitive, since the standard for burial is lower than for marriage, but it can add to a case.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein implies that a clearly Jewish name can be added to the equation. It is hard to define, since names like David, Daniel and Saul are widely used; perhaps he means a name like Moshe.
  • Mitochondrial DNA, passed from mother to child, is by his understanding insufficient alone, but can bolster other evidence.

In one line

A recognised ketubah traced by birth certificates is the best proof; otherwise practice, testimony and supporting evidence are combined.
