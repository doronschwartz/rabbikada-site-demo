Topic: The Rosh and the Semag on injury by man or by Heaven, and the Shulhan Arukh's ruling

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

The halacha

  • Recap: for Rashi bidei shamayim is something from heaven, birth, and seemingly illness. For the Rambam bidei adam is physical force even through an object, while birth, illness, or cutting because of illness is bidei shamayim.
  • The Rosh, Rabbenu Asher, is the strictest: only one born that way is bidei shamayim; an illness counts as bidei adam.
  • Two explanations: the Rambam writes in Hilchot Deot that the vast majority of illnesses come from unhealthy eating, so illness is attributed to the person. Or the Rosh means an illness that began elsewhere in the body and spread; one directly in the reproductive organs he would agree is bidei shamayim.
  • The Semag, who often follows the Rambam, is the most lenient: bidei adam only through negligence (peshiah). A dog that bit him out of the blue would be bidei shamayim. This fits the Gemara's source in mamzerut, which comes from a wrongful act.
  • The Shulhan Arukh quotes the Rambam, then the Rosh, and closes with "the Rosh wrote that the Yerushalmi implies so", which some read as an unusual sign that here he follows the Rosh over the Rambam.
  • But the Aharonim, the Helkat Mehokek, the Beit Shemuel and the Pithei Teshuvah, all rule like the Rambam, whatever the Shulhan Arukh held, and the Semag also plays a part. The Rambam's distinction is the main one.

Sources quoted

  • The Rosh: illness is bidei adam.
  • The Semag: bidei adam only through negligence.
  • Shulhan Arukh: closes with the Rosh's citation of the Yerushalmi.
  • The Helkat Mehokek, Beit Shemuel and Pithei Teshuvah: the halacha follows the Rambam.

In one line

The Rishonim range from the Rosh, strictest, to the Semag, most lenient, but the Aharonim follow the Rambam's definition.
