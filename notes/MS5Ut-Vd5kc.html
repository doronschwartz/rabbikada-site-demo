The halacha

  • This discussion is about Jewish identity only; whether one must prove untainted lineage is the next topic.
  • Many Rishonim, among them Tosafot, Rabbenu Asher, the Ramban and the Rashba, learn from the two stories (and another Gemara) that one who presents as Jewish is believed, with all the privileges of a Jew, including marriage. The Rambam's view is less straightforward.
  • The Ritva's reason: something that can be checked, people do not lie about. Since most such claims could be verified, we assume the claim is true.
  • Tosafot's reason: even where most of the local population is not Jewish, most people who come to a Beth Din or a Kehillah presenting as Jewish are Jewish, and we rely on that majority. The two reasons lead to different results today.
  • A possible third reason, not explicit in the Rishonim: a Hazakah that a non-Jew does not present himself as Jewish.
  • Other Rishonim, including perhaps Rashi and Rabbi Meir HaLevi, hold he is not believed and must verify his identity.
  • They answer the proofs: eating the Korban Pesah is a prohibition but not as grave as yihus, where one must be far more particular. Rava's advice was only that in a new city checks were less likely to catch him out, not that there were none.

Sources quoted

  • The Ritva: people do not lie about what can be verified.
  • Tosafot: most who come to a community claiming to be Jewish are Jewish.

In one line

Most Rishonim believe a person who says he is Jewish, for different reasons, while others require verification.
