The halacha

  • The Omer days are not sad days. The Ramban calls them Hol HaMoed, since Pesah (freedom) and Shavuot (the Torah, how to use that freedom) are one long festival, with the days between building up to it.
  • The Gemara never calls them days of mourning; it says the students of Rabbi Akiva did not show one another enough kavod, and died. The Midrashim place this between Pesah and Aseret, "until halfway to Shavuot".
  • From the time of the Geonim, about 1200 years ago, came the custom to refrain from weddings and later haircuts. It is a custom after the Gemara, less binding, and wrong to equate with the Three Weeks or Nine Days.
  • Most poskim read this as 33 days. The general Sepharadi custom, as the Shulhan Arukh rules, is no weddings or haircuts for the first 33 days, until the 34th day. Some Ashkenazim keep from the day after Rosh Hodesh Iyar until Shavuot.
  • The Radbaz (Rabbi David ben Zimra, Egypt, a contemporary of Rabbi Yoseph Karo) asked how the days of Nisan, when eulogies are forbidden, or Rosh Hodesh could be days of mourning. He restricts only from after Rosh Hodesh Iyar until Lag BaOmer.
  • The Kabbalists keep all seven weeks, with no haircut until the day before Shavuot.
  • The Spanish and Portuguese custom follows the Radbaz: weddings until Rosh Hodesh, not from Rosh Hodesh until Lag BaOmer, and permitted again until Shavuot.

In one line

The Omer is a happy period with a few customs of mourning, and the Spanish and Portuguese, following the Radbaz, keep them only from Rosh Hodesh Iyar to Lag BaOmer.
