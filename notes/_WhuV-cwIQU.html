Topic: Why the borrower must own land for a Prozbol, and whether relatives may serve

Sources: Mishnah and Gemara; Rashi; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Shemitah & Prozbol, Tuesday 17 May 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Mishnah and Gemara require that the borrower own some land (karka) for a Prozbol to work.
  • Rashi: a landless borrower was unusual, and the Hakhamim legislate for the regular case.
  • Other Rishonim: the Prozbol treats the debt as if already collected, as the Bet Din transfers the borrower's property to the lender, which works with land but not with movables, which can be hidden from the court.
  • Either way, this is the halacha. So Prozbol documents state that if the borrower has no land, the lender grants him some, any amount ("kol dehu").
  • This works through "zakhin leadam shelo befanav": one may confer a benefit on someone without his knowledge, through a third party. Although it will let the lender collect from him, receiving land is intrinsically a benefit, so it works.
  • The dayanim may be relatives.
  • The two witnesses should not be related to the lender or the borrower, but if they are the Prozbol is still valid: since he could have done it permissibly, we assume he did not do it improperly, and he is believed.

In one line

The borrower must own (or rent) some land, or be granted a token plot; and the witnesses should preferably not be relatives.
