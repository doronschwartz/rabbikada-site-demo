Topic: The order of lighting the Hanukkah candles

Sources: The Gemara; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Arizal; the Vilna Gaon

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah, Wednesday 25 December 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A principle of the Gemara, codified by Rambam: whenever one turns, one turns to the right, so given a choice one goes from left to right.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules that each night one starts from the furthest left candle, the new one, and moves right: on the third night candle three, then two, then one; on the eighth night from the far left back to candle one.
  • His second reason: each night has a new miracle, so the berakhah and the first lighting go on the new candle.
  • This is the universal Sepharadi practice, and the Arizal held so as well.
  • The Vilna Gaon disagreed. One candle each night is the core misvah (and suffices if one is stuck), the rest being mehadrin, so the berakhah and lighting should begin with candle one and go from right to left. The Shulhan Arukh would reply: who says the first is the main one?
  • The Vilna Gaon also notes that the Menorah belongs as near as possible to the doorway, and candle one, on the right, is nearest.
  • Ashkenazi practice varies. He believes most Ashkenazim go left to right like the Sepharadim, but some go right to left, always saying the berakhah on candle one.

In one line

Sepharadim say the berakhah on the new candle and light from left to right; some Ashkenazim follow the Vilna Gaon and begin with candle one.
