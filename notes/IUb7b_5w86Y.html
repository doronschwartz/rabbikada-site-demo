The halacha

  • Most of this series follows a teshuvah of BeMareh HaBazak from Eres Hemdah, written about 15 years ago to a Jewish school in New York that wanted to open a veterinary programme.
  • The approach is not "don't be a vet". Relieving animals' pain (saar baalei hayim) is potentially a Torah misvah, and veterinary work has real benefits, so the aim is to understand the issues and avoid any Torah or rabbinic prohibition.
  • First, students and lecturers neutering male animals, the bigger issue. Directly destroying the organs is a Torah prohibition.
  • Injecting something that indirectly destroys the organs is forbidden too, at least rabbinically, some say from the Torah.
  • Option one: hormones that reduce the male's desire to reproduce, like the Gemara's hen's crest, are permitted. Rav Moshe Feinstein and many others wrote this.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein added that some desire should remain, as with the crest; in practice this hardly arises. Others permit even removing it completely, since the organs are not affected even indirectly.
  • Option two: hormones that leave the desire and the organs intact but make the semen unable to produce offspring. Its issues and possibilities follow next time.

Sources quoted

  • BeMareh HaBazak: a detailed teshuvah on the issues facing a Jewish veterinary school.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: hormones that reduce desire are permitted, ideally leaving some desire.

In one line

A Jewish vet may not neuter a male animal directly or indirectly, but may use hormones that reduce its desire to reproduce.
