Topic: The source of the ruling against teaching daughters Torah, its meaning, and the first exception

Sources: Mishnah Sotah; Rashi; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Riaz; the Maharil; the Perishah

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Monday 3 June 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The source is the Mishnah in Sotah. Ben Azzai held a father should teach his daughter the basics of Torah. Rabbi Eliezer said whoever teaches his daughter Torah, "ke-ilu lomdah tiflut".
  • Rashi reads tiflut as deviousness: the depths of Gemara sharpen the mind and may lead to improper use of Torah. The Rambam reads it as emptiness: "rov hanashim ein daatan mekhuvenet lehitlamed", most cannot fully concentrate and turn Torah into idle matters.
  • Almost all the Rishonim and the Shulhan Arukh rule like Rabbi Eliezer. The Riaz (Rabbi Yeshayah of Trani) ruled like Ben Azzai, but the halacha does not follow him.
  • For the Rambam the ruling concerns the Oral Torah. The Written Torah does not carry the problem, though teaching it is still not encouraged.
  • There are four caveats. The first, from the Maharil (Rabbi Yaakov Moelin, 14th-century Germany): the Mishnah speaks only of a father teaching; a woman who wishes to learn by herself may do so.
  • The Perishah (Rav Falk, about 400 years ago): the concern about most women applies to formal teaching; one who chooses to learn shows she is not of that majority.
  • The remaining exceptions and the practical halacha follow tomorrow.

Sources quoted

  • Mishnah Sotah: Ben Azzai and Rabbi Eliezer.

In one line

A father is discouraged from teaching his daughter the Oral Torah, but a woman who wants to learn by herself may.
