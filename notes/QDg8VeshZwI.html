Topic: May one write by hand on Hol HaMoed?

Sources: The Mishnah (igeret shel reshut); the Rif; Rambam; Rashi; the Baal Halachot Gedolot; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Monday 29 September 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The source is the Mishnah listing things that may be written on Hol HaMoed, including an igeret shel reshut.
  • The Rif and Rambam: reshut means a voluntary letter of friendship, such as a thank-you card after a Shabbat invitation. Ordinary joined-up writing without special effort is melekhet hedyot and permitted for the moed; careful block lettering, as in a Sefer Torah, would be melekhet uman.
  • Rashi and the Baal Halachot Gedolot: reshut means government, an essential letter. On this view all ordinary writing counts as professional and is forbidden; only writing in a changed way (left hand, sloppy, crooked letters) is permitted.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules like the Rif and Rambam: one may write normally anything needed for the moed, such as a card when invited out, or a shopping list for Hol HaMoed or the last days of Yom Tov.
  • The Rema writes that although the halacha follows the Rif, the Ashkenazi custom is to write in an altered way where possible; where not possible, even he agrees normal writing is allowed.
  • Conclusion: Sepharadim write normally for the moed. Ashkenazim might better write with the left hand if legible, and otherwise may write normally. Phones, texting, printing and computers follow.

In one line

Writing in one's normal hand for the needs of the moed is permitted, with Ashkenazim preferring an altered hand where it stays legible.
