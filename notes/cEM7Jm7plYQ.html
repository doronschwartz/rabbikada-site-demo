Topic: Why Pesah Sheni matters: wanting a misvah even when one cannot do it

Sources: The Torah; the Gemara; the Hazon Ish

Daily Halacha: Sefirat HaOmer, Wednesday 22 May 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The 14th of Iyar is Pesah Sheni. Men who were tamei (through a dead body or carrying the coffin of Yoseph; the Gemara gives two reasons) could not bring the Korban Pesah and asked Moshe why they should lose out.
  • They did not take "no" for an answer, since the Korban Pesah and Brit Milah are the only two misvot whose omission carries karet.
  • Moshe consulted HaKadosh Barukh Hu, who gave a second Pesah a month later, the only misvah with a second chance. It falls on the 14th because the Korban was offered on the eve of Pesah.
  • This should be our attitude: even when prevented through no fault of our own (ones), we should still want the misvah. Serving Hashem needs tenacity, and we mark the day though we have no Beit HaMikdash.
  • Tahanun is not said. Some, more among Ashkenazim and Hasidim, eat Massah on the night, the day, or the night of the 15th, when the Korban was eaten.
  • Sepharadim never really had this custom, and the Hazon Ish opposed it as having no source. There is nothing wrong with it, but it was not the Sepharadi custom.

Sources quoted

  • The Torah: the impure men's request and the second Pesah.
  • The Hazon Ish: eating Massah on Pesah Sheni has no source and adds misvot.

In one line

Pesah Sheni celebrates not giving up on a misvah, even one we are prevented from doing.
