Topic: How many days a visitor from outside Israel keeps when in Israel for Yom Tov

Sources: The Hakham Svi; the Shulhan Arukh HaRav; Rav Kook

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Thursday 10 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • One would expect the halachot to be symmetrical with travel from Israel abroad: it would depend on how long one intends to stay.
  • The Hakham Svi (Rav Svi Hirsh Ashkenazi, 17th century, father of Rav Yaakov Emden) was, to his knowledge, the first to hold that even a visitor to Israel who intends to return keeps only one day. The second day is a minhag of the place, binding only while one is in that place.
  • The Shulhan Arukh HaRav, by the first Rebbe of Habad, seemingly followed this view. It is a minority opinion.
  • The vast majority of poskim disagree: it depends on intent. A visitor for the festival or a short period who intends to return keeps two days even in Israel; one going for an extended period intending to stay keeps one. This is the practice of the vast majority of Am Yisrael, though certain groups follow the Hakham Svi.
  • A third, also minority, view, apparently held by Rav Kook, is "one and a half days": the visitor does no melakhah on the second day out of doubt, but wears tefillin and says the weekday prayers. That does not seem to be the minhag.
  • The details of length of stay and intent follow next week.

Sources quoted

  • The Hakham Svi: the second day is a minhag of the place, so a visitor in Israel keeps one day.
  • The Shulhan Arukh HaRav: seemingly follows the Hakham Svi.
  • Rav Kook: apparently held "one and a half days".

In one line

A visitor to Israel who intends to return home keeps two days, against the minority view of the Hakham Svi that one day is enough.
