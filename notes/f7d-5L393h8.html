Topic: Whether a self-declared Kohen may be called up first to the Torah: the Rambam and the Ramakh

Sources: The Rambam; the Ramakh (Rabbi Moshe HaKohen of Lunel), quoted by Tosafot; the Maggid Mishneh

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 14 April 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Rambam rules clearly that one who says he is a Kohen is not believed: he is not called up first to the Torah, does not do Birkat Kohanim, and gets none of a Kohen's privileges.
  • The Ramakh, Rabbi Moshe HaKohen of Lunel in Provence (13th century), writes that the minhag is to rely on him for the first aliyah only, not for anything else.
  • His reasons: anyone may strictly be called up first; the Kohen goes first for the sake of peace (darkhei shalom), or perhaps the misvah to honour him. It is not the end of the world if he is not really a Kohen.
  • Might people then give him Terumah, which a non-Kohen eating is liable to death at the hands of Heaven? No: Terumah is from the Torah only when most of the Jewish people live in Israel, so today it is rabbinic, and only in Israel; outside Israel there is none.
  • The Rambam makes no such distinction: without proof he is not called up first.
  • Many disagreed with the Ramakh. The Maggid Mishneh on the Rambam says the Gemara implies otherwise; perhaps the logic works outside Israel, but not in Israel, where there is Terumah, even if rabbinic.

In one line

The Rambam does not call up a self-declared Kohen first, while the Ramakh does, since nothing serious follows from it today.
