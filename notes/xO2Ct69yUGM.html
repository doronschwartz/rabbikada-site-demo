Siman 8: the child follows the father, with exceptions

The Rabbi said most of Siman 8 repeats what we have already learned, with a few points worth noting. The starting rule: where there is valid kiddushin and no transgression, the child follows the father (holkhin ahar hazakhar). So a halal who marries a daughter of a Levi or an Israelite has a child who takes the father's status, and a daughter of that marriage is a halalah and may not marry a Kohen.

A ger who marries a halalah. The Shulhan Arukh says the child is a full Israelite. The obvious question: by the rule above the child should follow the father and be a ger. The Beit Shemuel raises this and points back to Siman 4, where a ger who marries a mamzeret is also not treated by that rule. The conclusion: a ger has his own din, and the usual rule (kol makom sheyesh kiddushin ve-ein aveirah) does not apply when a ger is involved. The child is a full Israelite.

The Torah and the Beit Yoseph. The Tur seems to say that the child of a ger and a mamzeret is a ger, which is hard to reconcile with Siman 4. The Beit Shemuel's answer, as the Rabbi gave it: the point is only that this child cannot serve as a dayan. The Torah still sees "a small element of gerut" in him, even though he is an Israelite and forbidden to marry a mamzeret.

A girl. None of this reaches a daughter.

A ger and a halalah: why is the child fully kosher? Rav Akiva Eiger asks: whichever parent we follow, a daughter should be disqualified from a Kohen, either as a ger's child or as a halalah's. The answer the Rabbi gave: we never find a halalah passing on her status unless she married a Kohen. So the boy or girl is completely kosher and may marry a Kohen.

A non-Jew who marries a Jewish halalah. The child is kosher. The Minhat Hinukh raises a novelty: since the child's yihus comes from the mother, and she is a halalah, perhaps the child is a halal. The Rabbi explained that some statuses simply are not passed down, in the same way that a deaf mother's child is not deaf by status.

A Gerusha and a Kohen: what makes a halal?

A Kohen who has a child with a gerusha makes the child a halal. The Rabbi asked what happens if a woman became pregnant from a Kohen's seed without relations (the old idea of pregnancy in a bath, and today artificial insemination). The reasoning: the child is a halal because of the forbidden act (maaseh issur). With no act there is no halalut, and the child is a kosher Kohen if the father is a Kohen.

The Rabbi then mentioned a different view, which he called a big novelty and said he did not know how widely it is accepted: that for a halalah, the status comes from the seed itself, not the act. On that view insemination with a Kohen's seed might create a halal. He did not take a position.

Siman 11, Seif 5: someone rumoured to have been with a non-Jewish woman

Moving on, the Rabbi said Siman 10 was done earlier and Siman 11 is large, so he would do Seif 5 to the end of the Siman now, and the beginning (Seifim 1 and 2) in a separate shiur.

The Gemara: people were saying a Jewish man was involved with a shifhah or a non-Jewish woman, and she then converted. He should not marry her; if he did, he does not have to divorce her (lo yikanes, ve-im nikhnas lo yosi).

Why not? There are two reasons.

Source

Reason for the rumour being a problem

Rashi

The first rumour proves true: he really was with her before, and people should not be able to say so

Ramban

People will say she converted only for the sake of marriage (gerut leshem ishut), which is valid after the fact but not ideal

Tosafot say that if a Jewish couple are rumoured to have been together, the practice today is that they should marry if they wish to stay together. The Rambam and the Beit Yoseph rule that the law applies even if we know for sure they were together.

The question for a dayan today. A couple come to Beit Din: they live together, or are civilly married, and she wants to convert. Even if the conversion is completely sincere, why is this not exactly the case of the Shulhan Arukh? They are together, we convert her, and then marry them. The Rabbi called this a huge problem, and said it is symmetrical: a Jewish woman with a non-Jewish man is the same din.

The Rambam's responsum and how we rule today

The Rambam's responsum (Peer HaDor): a Jewish man was rumoured to be living with his non-Jewish servant, and she converted. The Rambam's answer: either send her away or marry her. The Rabbi noted the Rambam himself rules in his Mishneh Torah like the Shulhan Arukh, yet here he said that in such cases he told people to marry her. In the Rambam's words, as the Rabbi read them: if we say do not marry her, "she's going to stay a shifhah", and the man keeps having relations with her. The Rambam even said to give a date: marry or divorce by next month. The Rabbi explained that the Rambam ranks this as a rabbinic prohibition of the kind connected with appearances (lemarit ayin), and that this is far better than staying with a non-Jewish woman.

Rav Hayim Ozer Grodzinski (Ahiezer), Mishpetei Uziel and Rav Moshe. The Ahiezer asks the same question and brings the Nitan problem as well. The central ideas:

  • On Rashi's reading, the concern is that people will suspect a past relationship. But where there is a civil marriage there is nothing to suspect: it is a known fact.
  • On the Ramban's reading, the concern is gerut leshem ishut. But in a civil marriage the woman is already married in the eyes of the country, so there is no such concern.
  • The Ahiezer concludes that one may rely on the Rambam, even if one holds Rashi's reason as well. Mishpetei Uziel says the same, and says it more strongly when there is a civil marriage.
  • Rav Moshe combines Rashi and the Ramban into one concern (people think it is for the sake of marriage), and reaches a similar result where there is a civil marriage.

The Rabbi's conclusion, and the main practical point of the class: "a whole halacha in Shulhan Arukh is completely modified". Even though the Shulhan Arukh says lo yikanes, today the conversion is accepted and the couple are married, so we not only allow it but essentially encourage it. This is what Batei Din around the world rely on. The conversion itself must still pass all the usual checks of sincerity.

What does "nikhnas" mean? Kiddushin, nisuin and a warning

Back to im nikhnas lo yosi: what does nikhnas mean? The Rabbi recalled a previous class and the two versions of the Yerushalmi.

Ramban

Rashba

Leniency

If he did nisuin, he can stay even if Beit Din warned him

If only kiddushin, he can stay

Stringency

Kiddushin alone is not enough

If Beit Din warned him, he must divorce even after nisuin

The Shulhan Arukh's wording, that if they warned him he must divorce, points to the Rashba. That would mean nikhnas in Seif 5 means kiddushin. The Rivash holds this is a safek derabbanan, so we can rely on both leniencies. The Beit Shemuel follows the Rivash, and the Rabbi said this is what the Aharonim accept.

In practice. The Rabbi's summary: - Kiddushin without a warning: he stays. - Nisuin, even with a warning: he stays. - The one case where he must divorce: only kiddushin, with a formal warning from Beit Din.

The Rabbi then said the beginning of Siman 11 (Seifim 1 and 2) will be the subject of its own shiur.

Practical summary

  • Where there is valid kiddushin and no transgression, the child follows the father; a ger is an exception to this rule, and his child is a full Israelite.
  • A halalah does not pass on her status unless she married a Kohen, so the child of a ger and a halalah may marry a Kohen.
  • The Shulhan Arukh says a man rumoured to have been with a non-Jewish woman should not marry her after she converts, but if he did, he need not divorce.
  • Following the Rambam's responsum, the Ahiezer, Mishpetei Uziel and Rav Moshe, couples who are civilly married or already living together may convert and marry today, after the usual checks of sincerity.
  • The Rashba and the Ramban differ on whether kiddushin or nisuin counts; the accepted practice is that only kiddushin with a Beit Din warning requires divorce.
