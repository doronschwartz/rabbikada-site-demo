Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 271, seifim 4 and 7-9 Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 26 October 2023

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

Seif 4: nothing at all before Kiddush

Last week covered making Kiddush as early as possible, the obligation of women, and what to do when money is short. Seif 4 says one may not taste anything before Kiddush, not even water.

The seif continues: if someone began a meal on Friday afternoon and it ran on into Shabbat, he does not have to finish before Shabbat. At nightfall he stops, covers the bread and makes Kiddush. If he already drank wine during the meal, he does not repeat the berakhah on the wine. The Rabbi explained the debate that follows: one opinion says he must say HaMosi again, because the time when he was forbidden to eat counts as an interruption; the other says it is not enough of an interruption, and "that's what we hold." In practice he simply makes Kiddush and then eats at least a kazayit of bread as his Friday night meal, with no new washing.

When does this happen? When Purim falls on a Friday. The Sefaradi custom every year is to have the Purim meal in the morning: Megillah, gifts to the poor and mishloah manot, then a meal of a few hours that ends while it is still Purim. Starting the meal just before sunset and carrying on late into the night is an Ashkenazi practice, found in the Rema. Even Ashkenazim try to eat in the morning when Purim is on Friday. But if the meal only started at four o'clock, there is no need to rush it. At sunset he stops, makes Kiddush, and carries on.

Someone asked about Kabbalat Shabbat. The Rabbi said the halacha is only to accept Shabbat. Lekha Dodi and the psalms before it are lovely additions from the last few hundred years, but there is no halachic obligation to say them.

Why not even water?

The Rabbi set out the general rule. Whenever the time for a misvah has arrived, one may not sit down to a meal (bread, or a kabeisah of mezonot) until the misvah is done, because one may get carried away and forget it. This applies to Arvit, Minhah, Bedikat Hames, lulav, Sefirat HaOmer, and a father whose son's Brit Milah is later that day. Two things lift it: a fixed time (a regular shiur and Arvit every night), or a shomer to remind you, and he said a phone alarm can be a shomer. Fruit, vegetables and drinks are always permitted before a misvah.

So before Havdalah you may drink water, have a coffee, or eat fruit; you just cannot have bread or mezonot. "Can you have a cup of coffee before Havdalah? A hundred percent."

Kiddush on Friday night and Shabbat morning (and Yom Tov) is the exception: not even water. The only comparable case is the Rema's ruling not to drink after Taanit Esther before hearing the Megillah, while Rav Ovadia Yosef holds that from the letter of the law one may drink and eat something small there. Why is Kiddush stricter? The Rabbi said it is not entirely clear; it seems the Hakhamim wanted to stress its importance, so they said nothing at all until Kiddush. It remains a rabbinic prohibition, so an ill person who needs to drink may do so.

Shabbat morning: before Shahrit and before Musaf

Before Shahrit on Shabbat morning you may drink, since the obligation of Kiddush only starts after prayer. You may have water or coffee, with sugar and milk, exactly as on a weekday. The usual weekday rule against eating before praying still applies.

Rav Ovadia's view is based on the Hida, who writes that someone who made Kiddush before Musaf should make it again after Musaf. The Rabbi was careful to say this is the view of some Sefaradim, not all. So sweets thrown at a simhah after Shahrit are fine on this view, but not a meal or a real amount of cake.

For those who follow the first view, he said there is no justification for a hazzan or reader to drink water during the Torah reading without Kiddush unless he is ill; he should go out and make Kiddush.

On long services that end very late, many rely on the Baal HaTanya, who said it is better to eat in order to pray than to pray in order to eat. The reason not to eat before praying is that it is arrogant to look after yourself first; eating so that you can pray properly is not arrogance. The Rabbi called it "quite a weak heter," and said it has been abused: nobody permitted a full breakfast before shul. If you are worried, have some water, a piece of chocolate or a fruit, and only if you need it. Someone who is ill or weak eats whatever he needs.

Seifim 7-8: if you ate first, or missed Kiddush at night

If someone ate or drank before Kiddush, whether through illness or by mistake, he still makes Kiddush. He should not think he has spoiled it and must wait until the morning.

If he did not make Kiddush on Friday night at all, deliberately or because he could not (he was ill in bed, or busy with a birth), he makes it on Shabbat day, at any time until the end of Shabbat. He says the full night Kiddush with the berakhah Mekadesh HaShabbat, but, says the Rema, not Vayekhulu. Those verses describe God finishing creation and belong at the start of the seventh day, not to the morning. Others may fulfil their obligation by listening to him.

Kiddush at twilight. Suppose he was occupied all of Friday night and Shabbat day saving a life and is free only after sunset. Normally we do not make a berakhah when in doubt. But Kiddush is a Torah obligation, and a doubtful Torah obligation is done with its berakhah, as with Birkat HaMazon. So he makes Kiddush with the berakhah until nightfall. The exception: if he prayed Arvit on Friday night, he may already have fulfilled the Torah obligation there, so he would make it without the berakhah. On Yom Tov he would not make Kiddush with a berakhah at twilight.

Seif 9: covering the bread

The bread must be covered above and below during Kiddush. Two reasons are given:

  1. The Yerushalmi: normally the berakhah on bread comes before wine, so the bread is "embarrassed" when wine goes first. We cover it.
  2. The manna (zekher laman): the manna fell with dew beneath it and dew on top.

The Shulhan Arukh requires a cover underneath as well, which only makes sense with the manna reason. Today a tablecloth counts as the lower cover; on a bare wooden table you would need something under the bread.

He said there is no halacha that the hallah must be on the table during Kiddush: "It's a myth." The only requirement is to wash soon after Kiddush. Keeping the bread on the hotplate and bringing it in on the way back from washing is fine.

The Yerushalmi's reason still teaches something. "The true test of a person's character is how they treat things which can't respond to you." He told the story of Rav Yisrael Salanter, a guest at a home where the bread had been left uncovered. The host scolded his wife in front of the Rabbi to show that they usually covered it. Rav Yisrael said the whole point of covering the bread is not to embarrass anyone, and he had just embarrassed his wife.

Practical summary

  • Before Kiddush on Friday night and Shabbat morning, eat and drink nothing, not even water, unless you are ill.
  • Before Shahrit on Shabbat you may drink water or coffee. Between Shahrit and Musaf, most poskim forbid even water; Rav Ovadia permits water, a sweet or fruit.
  • If a meal (for example on Purim that falls on Friday) runs into Shabbat: stop at sunset, make Kiddush, then eat at least a kazayit of bread.
  • Before Havdalah, Arvit or any other misvah, drinks and fruit are fine; only a bread or mezonot meal is a problem without a fixed time or a reminder.
  • Missed Kiddush on Friday night? Make the night Kiddush, without Vayekhulu, any time on Shabbat day.
  • Cover the hallah above and below during Kiddush.
