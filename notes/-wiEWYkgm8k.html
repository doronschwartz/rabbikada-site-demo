Topic: The Maharashdam and Rabbi Yaakov Emden on Kohanim today as doubtful Kohanim

Sources: The Gemara; the Rambam; the Maharashdam (Rabbi Shemuel de Medina), Teshuvah; Rabbi Yaakov Emden

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 23 April 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara accepts one witness for a captive woman but not a witness who heard from another witness. Yet the Maharashdam allowed this captive to marry a Kohen.
  • His reason: Kohanim today are presumed but not definite Kohanim; their status is a safek. The ban on a captive is rabbinic, and with a doubt in a rabbinic matter one may be lenient.
  • This doubt does not permit what the Torah forbids: a Kohen still may not marry a divorcee. But in a rabbinic case one may rely on it. The question is how far it extends.
  • Rabbi Yaakov Emden of Germany also held forcefully that we do not really have certain Kohanim. On Pidyon HaBen, where some Kohanim return the money in a permitted way, he wrote that since there is no proof anyone is a Kohen, a God-fearing Kohen should strictly return it, or it could be theft, as money matters require strictness.
  • That is not today's practice: Kohanim may and many do keep the money. But it shows how prominent poskim regarded today's Kohanim.
  • The result: today's Kohanim are a safek, which matters first in rabbinic cases, and second where another doubt joins to make a double doubt (sefek sefeika).

In one line

The Maharashdam and Rabbi Yaakov Emden treat today's Kohanim as doubtful, allowing leniency in rabbinic cases but never against a Torah prohibition.
