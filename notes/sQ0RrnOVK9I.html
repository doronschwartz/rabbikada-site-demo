Topic: The history from Philip of Macedon to the Hashmonaim retaking the Temple, and the main miracle of Hanukkah

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

The points

  • The oil is one minor aspect of Hanukkah; its beauty is appreciated through the history, drawn here from Rav Eliezer Melamed's Peninei Halakha.
  • From about 400 to 300 BCE Greek culture (democracy, philosophy, art) spread. King Philip of Macedonia united the Greek states and had Aristotle teach his son Alexander.
  • Alexander took the throne around 334 BCE and by 331 BCE had conquered Asia Minor, Egypt, Israel and on to India; he died in 323 BCE. Jews lived under Greek rule without conflict. Later King Talmai had the Torah translated into Greek.
  • In 175 BCE Antiochus Epiphanes took over and Hellenism spread in Israel, even among some Kohanim Gedolim, who built a wrestling stadium near the Bet HaMikdash. In 169 BCE he entered the Bet HaMikdash and took its vessels; by 167 BCE Torah study, Berit Milah and Rosh Hodesh were banned.
  • In Modiin, Matityahu, whom they tried to force to serve idols, killed them with his sons and small army, showing that one can rebel. Yehudah HaMakkabi led the fighters and within two years retook Yerushalayim, entering the Bet HaMikdash on 25 Kislev.
  • They found one flask of oil, enough for one night, with seven days needed to bring more, and it lasted.
  • The main miracle is the victory of the few and weak over the many and strong, as Al HaNissim says. So too today the existence and successes of Israel and the IDF make no military sense; we pray it will be "in those days, at this time".

In one line

The real miracle of Hanukkah is the victory of the few against the mighty Greeks; the oil is its byproduct.
