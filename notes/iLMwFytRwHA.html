Topic: The abandoned child (asufi) and the status of the Karaites

Sources: Shulhan Arukh, Siman 4; the Gemara; Rav Eliyahu Bakshi-Doron; the Rama; Rav Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 3 July 2026

The halacha

  • An asufi ("gathered") is an abandoned child whose father and mother are unknown. He is a safek mamzer, since abandoning him suggests a serious problem, possibly mamzerut.
  • The Gemara says that if he was abandoned in a way showing the parents still care for him, or in a time of famine, he is not a safek mamzer; and in a city with a Jewish majority he is assumed Jewish.
  • A child left outside a hospital in Israel would not be a safek mamzer, since presumably the parents could not raise him.
  • Rav Eliyahu Bakshi-Doron, former Chief Rabbi of Israel, was asked about a baby found in a Jewish area. He answered to ask the police, who said there were signs the parents still cared for the child, so the child was not a mamzer.
  • The Rama rules that the Karaites, who reject the Oral Torah and follow only the written text (mikra), are all safek mamzerim, since they perform kiddushin but not proper gittin.
  • Rav Ovadiah Yoseph, writing in Cairo in 1949, held that a Karaite who becomes a God-fearing Jew is not a mamzer: their kiddushin are performed before witnesses invalid for testimony, so they are invalid, and he found they were not even performing kiddushin properly. This has become the accepted ruling.

In one line

An abandoned child is a safek mamzer unless the circumstances show otherwise, and a Karaite who joins the community is accepted as no mamzer.
