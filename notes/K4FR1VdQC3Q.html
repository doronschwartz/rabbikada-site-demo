Topic: The correct time of day for the Purim seudah in a regular year

Sources: The Gemara; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Terumat HaDeshen; the Rama; the poskim; the Mekubalim

Daily Halacha: Purim, Tuesday 11 March 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara, Rambam and Shulhan Arukh do not say when in the day to hold the seudah, only that night does not count. Like the other misvot of Purim it may be done any time before sunset, though the principle of doing a misvah as soon as possible suggests earlier is better.
  • Among the Ashkenazi Rishonim the widespread minhag was to begin in the late afternoon and continue into the night. The Terumat HaDeshen reports this, but says he, his colleagues and his teachers always held it in the morning, after the Megillah.
  • One explanation of the minhag: continuing into the night of the 15th joins in with Yerushalayim and marks the second day of Purim.
  • The Rama makes a compromise: do the misvot in the morning, pray in the afternoon, then begin the seudah early in the afternoon for a few hours. Beginning right at evening, so that the main mishteh is after Purim ends, is not best practice, though those who do so argue they began by day.
  • This is the practice of most people in a regular year. Having been busy with misvot in the morning, beginning in the afternoon and continuing into the evening is acceptable, as long as one does not start just before sunset.
  • The poskim write that the morning is certainly best, and the Mekubalim say the best time is straight after the Megillah and Mishloah Manot.
  • Tomorrow: when Purim falls on Friday, as this year.

Sources quoted

  • The Terumat HaDeshen: the custom of a late seudah, against his own teachers' morning practice.
  • The Rama: begin early in the afternoon, not at evening.

In one line

In a regular year the seudah may be any time by day, best in the morning, and at the latest begun in the afternoon well before sunset.
