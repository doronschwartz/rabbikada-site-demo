Topic: Whether a couple should undergo genetic testing before marriage

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; Rav Moshe Feinstein, Teshuvah

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 31 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara, recorded in the Shulhan Arukh, advises not to marry into a family in which three members have suffered from epilepsy (nikhpim) or leprosy (mesoraim).
  • In matters of danger a hazakah is usually set by two occurrences; for example parents who lost two boys through circumcision do not circumcise a third. Here, since it involves different members of a family, all agree three are needed.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein was asked whether a couple should undergo genetic testing to avoid recessive conditions in their children. One might argue "HaShem guards the simple" (Shomer Petaim HaShem) and trust in Him.
  • He rejected this: since testing is so easy, not doing it is like closing one's eyes, just as one cannot cross a road with eyes shut and rely on HaShem. One must take basic care of health and safety.
  • This is the common approach, certainly among Ashkenazim. One could see it as fulfilling the Gemara's concern about families with a repeated illness.
  • Many agree with Rav Moshe that one should test before marriage where there is any concern of an issue between the couple.

In one line

Following Rav Moshe Feinstein, a couple should undergo easy genetic testing before marriage rather than rely on "HaShem guards the simple".
