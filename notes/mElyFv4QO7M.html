Topic: Standing or sitting for Kiddush on Sukkot: Sepharadim and Ashkenazim

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rambam; the Kaf HaHayyim; the Rosh; the Rama

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Friday 7 October 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Sepharadim hold that when a misvah is done on behalf of others, all should be seated unless there is a reason to stand, since sitting together shows focus (keviut). So Sepharadim sit for Kiddush on Shabbat morning and for Havdalah.
  • Ashkenazim agree, but stand for Havdalah in honour of Shabbat leaving. All stand on Friday night in honour of Shabbat arriving.
  • Sukkot is the exception. Shulhan Arukh follows the Rambam that Leshev BaSukkah means the physical act of sitting, and a berakhah precedes the misvah. So every Kiddush of Sukkot, even Shabbat and Yom Tov morning, is said standing, and one sits after the berakhah. The Kaf HaHayyim says this clearly too.
  • The Rosh understands "leshev" as being in the sukkah, dwelling there. Strictly the berakhah would be said before entering, but it is joined to Kiddush since eating is the main part.
  • For the Rosh there is no point standing, since one dwells sitting or standing. So the Rosh and the Rama rule that Ashkenazim sit for Kiddush (standing only where they normally do, as on Friday night).

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh, following the Rambam, and the Kaf HaHayyim: stand for Kiddush on Sukkot.
  • The Rosh and the Rama: Kiddush is said seated.

In one line

Sepharadim stand for every Kiddush of Sukkot because the berakhah is on sitting; Ashkenazim, for whom it is on dwelling, stay seated.
