Topic: Adding the doubt that the father was a non-Jew to permit a safek mamzer

Sources: The Rishonim; the Rambam; the Aharonim; a 20th century Hungarian posek

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Wednesday 1 July 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A safek mamzer, such as a child born when the husband has been away over a year, may marry neither a mamzer nor a regular Jew. But with a double doubt (sefek sefeka) one may be lenient and assume the child is kasher.
  • A married Jewish woman's child from a non-Jewish man, though the relationship is forbidden, is not a mamzer; only a Jewish man creates a mamzer.
  • The question, touched on in the Rishonim and discussed at length in the Aharonim: may one add the doubt that the father was a non-Jew, making a double doubt?
  • Some Rishonim say yes. Another objects that she lives among Jews and most people around her are Jewish, so a non-Jewish father cannot be assumed; that is very different from today.
  • There is a huge debate on the Rambam's view, but in short he held one may add it, and many contemporary poskim relied on this to permit a child whenever there was another doubt.
  • A case in 20th century Hungary: a married woman left Judaism without a get, lived entirely among non-Jews, and on her deathbed told her daughter she was Jewish. The daughter came to the Beit Din. Since the mother lived as a non-Jew among non-Jews, it was assumed the father was a non-Jew, and the daughter was permitted to marry into the community.

In one line

Many poskim permit a doubtful mamzer by adding the possibility that the father was a non-Jew.
