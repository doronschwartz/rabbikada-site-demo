Class Notes: Meleches Tzad (Hunting/Trapping) & Shochet (Killing)

Part 1: Review of Key Principles from Previous Lessons

Principle 1: Min HaNitzod (מינו ניצוד)

  • Torah prohibition of Tzad only applies if the species is commonly hunted and trapped
  • If the species is not commonly trapped → Issur is Derabbanan (Rabbinic prohibition only)

Principle 2: Bi-Schicha Achat (בשחיה אחת)

  • The trapped creature must be accessible in one go once trapped
  • Both conditions must be met for Torah liability:
  1. Be-Mino Nitzod (commonly hunted species)
  2. Accessible immediately upon trapping

Principle 3: Sleeping/Blind vs. Ill/Elderly Animals

Animal Condition

Halachic Status

Reason

Sleeping or blind

Chayav

Can still jump away

Ill or elderly

Patur Mi-Derabbanan

Cannot effectively escape

Principle 4: Hunting with a Dog (Kelev)

  • According to Rambam (and Rama's psak): Only chayav if actively engaged in hunting together with the dog
  • Simply setting a dog onto prey → Derabbanan only

Practical Application – Cats and Mice:

  • Cat actively encouraged + rewarded to catch mouse → Chayav
  • Cat left alone and catches mouse on its own → Permitted (not your action)

Principle 5: Safek Pesik Reisha

  • The Taz's ruling (accepted as Halacha): Safek Pesik Reisha has the same status as Davar She-Eino Mitkaven
  • Result: Mutar – even for De-Oraita matters

Part 2: Trapping a Human on Shabbos

The Question

Can you trap/confine a human being on Shabbos? Examples:

  • Locking a misbehaving child in a room
  • Working as a prison guard and locking inmates
  • Confining an adult for any reason

Are Humans "Be-Mino Nitzod"?

  • Argument: Yes – people are regularly imprisoned, confined, and restrained

Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach's View

Ruling: Mutar (permitted) in many cases

Reasoning:

  • Uses example of rescuing someone drowning – they are "happy to be trapped"
  • When a person consents or submits to authority, it's like a domesticated creature
  • Children, though misbehaving, ultimately submit to parental authority

Limitation: Jailing an unwilling adult who wants freedom → Would be Tzad according to Rav Auerbach

Alternative View (Offered in Shiur)

Thesis: There is no trapping of a human at all

Supporting Arguments:

  1. Not discussed in Gemara – despite prisons existing in ancient times
  2. The essence of a human is not their physical body but their mind, consciousness, and intellect
  3. Physical confinement ≠ true "trapping" because the mind remains free
  4. The Mishkan's melacha of Tzad addressed trapping creatures whose primary essence is their physical being

Practical Difference (Nafka Mina):

  • Prison guard locking door on Shabbos:
  • According to Rav Auerbach → Assur (adult doesn't consent)
  • According to alternative view → Not Tzad at all

Connection to Parsha (Va'era)

Ramchal's interpretation of "תכבד עליהם העבודה" (increase their labor):

  • Pharaoh wanted to enslave not just bodies but minds
  • True freedom is intellectual freedom – the ability to think and question
  • This is why the Seder emphasizes questions and dialogue ("Mah Nishtana")
  • כל המרבה לספר ביציאת מצרים הרי זה משובח – More discussion = more praiseworthy

Part 3: Seif Dalet (ד) – Covering a Beehive

The Halacha

You may spread a mat over a beehive on two conditions:

  1. No intention to trap the bees
  2. Not a Pesik Reisha – bees have an exit route available

Explanation

  • Cover is not sealed tightly – bees can find their way out if they try
  • Purpose: Protect bees from rain or excessive sun

If It Were Pesik Reisha

  • Would be Assur
  • Implication: Bees are considered Be-Mino Nitzod (commonly trapped species)
  • If bees were NOT commonly trapped, it would only be Derabbanan, and Pesik Reisha De-Lo Nicha Lei Be-Derabbanan would be Mutar

Part 4: Seif Hei (ה) – Two People Trapping Together

The Gemara's Case

A deer enters a small house (where it's immediately accessible once door is closed)

Halachic Outcomes

Scenario

Status

One person closes door

Chayav

Two people close door together

Patur (exempt)

Heavy door requiring two people

Chayav for both

Principle: Shnayim She-Asu (שנים שעשו)

  • Source: "בעשותה אחת מעם הארץ" – requires one person to do the melacha
  • Two people doing what one could do alone → Derabbanan only
  • But if both are necessary → Chayav

Rama's Addition: Adding Extra Protection

  • If door is already closed → Permitted to lock it
  • Principle: Adding extra layers of protection to already-trapped animal is not forbidden

Practical Application: Entering a Room with Trapped Animal

Problem: If you open door, animal is no longer fully trapped; closing door = trapping again

Solution (Mishnah Berurah):

  1. Add secondary restraint before opening door (net, rope, etc.)
  2. Then freely enter and exit
  3. Secondary restraint maintains "trapped" status
  4. After door is closed, another person can remove the secondary restraint

New Pets Warning

Scenario: New parrot/bird in cage, not yet domesticated

Danger: Opening cage door = freeing the bird; closing it = Tzad

Caution: When feeding new pets on Shabbos:

  • Don't create situations where animal becomes "untrapped"
  • Feed through the cage without opening the door
  • Very easy to violate Tzad (at least mi-Derabbanan) with non-domesticated pets

Part 5: Seif Vav (ו) – Sitting in Doorway

The Case

  • Person A sits in doorway, trapping a deer inside (forbidden – did Tzad)
  • Person B sits down next to Person A (permitted – just adding protection)

If Person A Leaves

Ruling: Person B may remain – Patur U-Mutar (exempt and permitted)

Key Principle: This is one of three places in Masechet Shabbat where "Patur" means "Mutar"

  • Normally "Patur" = not Torah violation but still Rabbinically forbidden
  • Here = completely permitted

The Reasoning

  • At the moment Person B sat down, the animal was already trapped
  • Person B's action was permitted from the start (just extra protection)
  • When the original protection leaves, Person B's position remains valid

Side Discussion: Can You Undo Forbidden Tzad?

Question: If you trapped an animal forbidden on Shabbos at 12:00, and realize at 12:15 – should you release it?

Answer: No benefit in releasing

  • The melacha is already done
  • If done intentionally → Chayav
  • If by mistake → Chayav Chatas
  • Releasing doesn't undo the violation

Part 6: Seif Zayin (ז) – Trapping Dangerous Creatures

The Halacha

Snakes, scorpions, and other dangerous creatures:

Purpose

Status

For personal use (skins, blood, etc.)

Chayav

To prevent being bitten

Mutar

Why Is Preventing Harm Mutar?

This goes beyond Pikuach Nefesh (life-threatening danger)

Explanation: Melacha She-Eina Tzricha L'Gufa (מלאכה שאינה צריכה לגופה)

  • Trapping to prevent harm = different purpose than Mishkan trapping
  • In Mishkan: Trapped to use the creature
  • Here: Trapping to remove danger – no benefit from the trapped creature itself

General Rule:

  • Melacha She-Eina Tzricha L'Gufa is Derabbanan (according to most Rishonim)
  • When there is significant harm (hezek), Chachamim permit Melacha She-Eina Tzricha L'Gufa

Precedent: Siman 308, Seif 6-7 – Removing broken glass from public area

According to the Rambam

Rambam holds Melacha She-Eina Tzricha L'Gufa is Chayav (Torah level)

How to explain this Halacha?

Maggid Mishneh's Answer:

  • Potential danger to many people → Elevated to status of Sakanat Nefashot

Acharonim's Answer (cited by Mishnah Berurah):

  • This isn't even Melacha She-Eina Tzricha L'Gufa – it's Mitasek
  • You're not performing an act of trapping at all
  • Your entire focus is alleviating harm; trapping is merely incidental
  • Lower level than Melacha She-Eina Tzricha L'Gufa

Practical Halacha

Any creature that can cause significant harm (not necessarily life-threatening):

  • May be trapped on Shabbos
  • Includes dangerous dogs that might bite
  • Accepted by all authorities

Halacha Regarding Bruising (Chovel)

Eight Sheratzim: Bruising them (blood congeals under skin) = Chayav

Other Sheratzim: Only Chayav if blood actually exits the body

Reason: The eight sheratzim have orot (hides/skins) where blood can be trapped underneath

Understanding Shochet on Shabbos

Melacha of Shochet: Killing on Shabbos

Extracting Blood:

  • According to most Rishonim → Toledah of Shochet
  • Based on "כי הדם הוא הנפש" – blood represents the life force
  • Blood extraction = partial taking of life

According to Rambam:

  • Extracting blood = Toledah of Dash (threshing)
  • Called Mefarek – extracting something from its container
  • Blood is "within" the body like seeds in a pod

Bruising Without Purpose

  • Pinching someone causing bruise (no purpose) → Derabbanan
  • Just playing around → Derabbanan

Bruising Out of Anger (Machlokes)

Rambam's View:

  • If you bruise someone out of anger or revenge → Chayav De-Oraita
  • Reasoning: The anger relief is the purpose; bruising is necessary for that relief

Ra'avad's View:

  • Still Patur from Torah (Derabbanan only)
  • You don't benefit from the blood itself – just from hurting them
  • "Getting a kick" from it isn't productive enough for Torah liability

Practical Applications

Shabbos Table Scenario:

  • Pinching someone under table to send a message
  • If causes bruise:
  • According to Rambam → Potentially Chayav De-Oraita
  • Definitely Assur

Rubbing Boils:

  • Cannot rub boils on Shabbos that might release blood

Extracting a Tooth:

  • Cannot pull out a loose tooth on Shabbos
  • Blood extraction is inevitable
  • Discussion whether it's De-Oraita (Rambam reasoning) or Derabbanan

Tzad (Trapping) with the Eight Sheratzim

Eight Sheratzim:

  • Trapping them = Default assumption of L'Tzorech (for a purpose)
  • Because they have hides/skins people want

Other Sheratzim:

  • Default assumption = NOT for a purpose
  • Unless you specifically intend to use them

Note: This is separate from Be-Mino Nitzod

  • Example: Mouse (עכבר) is one of the eight but is Ein Be-Mino Nitzod
  • The issue here is whether the default purpose is productive or not

Summary of Key Principles

Tzad (Trapping)

  1. Torah liability requires: Be-Mino Nitzod + Bi-Schicha Achat
  2. Adding extra protection to trapped animal → Permitted
  3. Trapping dangerous creatures to prevent harm → Mutar
  4. Safek Pesik Reisha = Davar She-Eino Mitkaven → Mutar

Shochet/Chovel (Killing/Bruising)

  1. Blood extraction = Either Shochet (most Rishonim) or Dash (Rambam)
  2. Bruising = Blood congealed under skin = Same as extracting
  3. Eight Sheratzim → Chayav even for bruising (have skins)
  4. Other creatures → Only Chayav if blood actually exits
  5. Bruising out of anger → Machlokes if Chayav (Rambam) or Patur (Ra'avad)
