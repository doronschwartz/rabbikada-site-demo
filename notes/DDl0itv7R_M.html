Topic: Rabbi Yehudah HaHasid's instruction not to marry someone sharing a parent's name

Sources: Rabbi Yehudah HaHasid, his will; the Pithei Teshuvah; the Noda BiYehudah (Rabbi Yehezkel Landau of Prague); Rav Moshe Feinstein; Rav Ovadiah Yoseph; the Gemara on zugot

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Sunday 29 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Rabbi Yehudah HaHasid, of the pious of 12th century Germany, wrote instructions in his will that some adopted. They are not halachic in essence, as they are not in the Talmud or the codes.
  • One, brought here by the Pithei Teshuvah (not the Shulhan Arukh or Rema): a man should not marry a woman whose father has his name, or a woman who has his mother's name. The reason is unclear.
  • Sepharadim were never particular about this, and even for Ashkenazim it is limited in many ways.
  • The Noda BiYehudah: it was a will for his own descendants only. He could not make a new rule binding on everyone.
  • Some authorities: it applies only when the names double over, for example the father-in-law is Reuven ben Hayyim and the groom is Reuven son of Hayyim.
  • Aharonim: adding a name, such as Reuven Hayyim, makes it a different name.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein and Rav Ovadiah Yoseph are lenient. Rav Moshe compares it to zugot (pairs), where the Gemara says one who does not care need not worry: if the couple are worried they should not go ahead, but if not, they may marry.
  • One with a family tradition to be careful should seek advice.

In one line

This is not a Talmudic rule, Sepharadim never kept it, and most may marry regardless, while those with a tradition to be careful should ask.
