The halacha

  • The Torah says a mamzer may not enter the congregation. He is a full Jew who keeps the misvot and takes part in communal life, but may not marry into the community, forever, and his children almost always remain mamzerim.
  • The English "illegitimate child born out of wedlock" is not what a mamzer is in halacha.
  • The word appears in Zekhariah about the Philistine cities: "a mamzer shall dwell in Ashdod", meaning a foreigner will live there. It comes from zar, foreign.
  • The Ramban: the parents of a child from a forbidden union usually disown him and treat him as an outsider.
  • The Ibn Ezra and the Hizkuni: mamzer is like mizar, "from a stranger", a man foreign to the mother, as when a married woman has a child with another Jewish man.
  • The Rambam in Moreh Nevuchim: the Torah is so harsh to deter people from these forbidden relationships.
  • Is it fair to the child, who did nothing wrong? No, it is not fair at all. Not everything in life is fair, and sometimes a harsh deterrent is needed.

In one line

Mamzer means one who is estranged, and the harsh, permanent status serves as a deterrent against forbidden unions.
