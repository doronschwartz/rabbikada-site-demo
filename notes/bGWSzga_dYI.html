Topic: When one person may say a berakhah to fulfil another's obligation

Sources: Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Berachot, Monday 15 January 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The three categories (benefit, misvah, praise and request) matter in practice.
  • In general one may say a berakhah on behalf of another even after fulfilling the misvah oneself. One who heard shofar in synagogue may blow and say the berakhah at home for a man who has not yet heard it.
  • The chief example is the repetition of the Amidah, instituted so the hazan could fulfil the obligation of those unable to pray.
  • First caveat: birkot hanehenin can be said only by one who is benefiting. Saying "shehakol" for a friend's drink without drinking is a berakhah in vain and does not work. If both are drinking, one may say it for both, as with kiddush.
  • Second caveat: in the other categories, if the other person knows how to say the berakhah, it is debated. The Shulhan Arukh rules that in the first instance he should say it himself; so with shofar, he says the berakhah and you blow.
  • Some disagree, so in the first instance he says it himself, but if one does say it for him it still works. For one unable to say it, it certainly works.

In one line

A berakhah of benefit cannot be said for another unless you also benefit, while berakhot of misvah and praise can, ideally only for one who cannot say them himself.
