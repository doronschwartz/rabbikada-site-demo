Topic: How the laws of borer are relaxed on Yom Tov compared with Shabbat

Sources: The Rama; the poskim; the Hakhamim

Daily Halacha: Moadim

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • On Shabbat, selecting from a mixture, of food and per the halacha also non-food, requires three conditions: taking the wanted (okhel) from the unwanted (pesolet), for immediate use (about the next half hour), and by hand or ordinary cutlery, not a dedicated separating utensil. On Yom Tov the first two conditions do not apply; the third does.
  • This concerns household selecting. Selecting in the field or a factory on an industrial scale, like the borer of the Mishkan separating grain from stones, is forbidden on Yom Tov.
  • On Yom Tov one may take the unwanted from the wanted where that is easier: almonds out of a bowl mostly of pistachios, lemon pips out of a salad by hand, or knives out of a pile of mostly forks.
  • This is certainly permitted for Sepharadim. For Ashkenazim the Rama brings those who are strict, but that concerns flour; other kitchen items are generally permitted for them too.
  • On Shabbat some poskim permit removing fish bones even for Sepharadim, a separate discussion. On Yom Tov one may certainly remove bones from fish or chicken.
  • One may select even for later in the day, as long as it is for Yom Tov itself.
  • Still forbidden is a dedicated instrument such as a strainer or sieve, which the Hakhamim forbade because it looks industrial. Hands and ordinary cutlery, such as a knife to peel an orange, are fine.

In one line

On Yom Tov you may remove the unwanted from the wanted, even for later in the day, by hand or with ordinary cutlery, but not with a sieve or strainer.
