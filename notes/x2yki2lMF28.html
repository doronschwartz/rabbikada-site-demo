Orach Chaim Siman 308, Se'if Dalet and Beyond

REVIEW OF PREVIOUS CATEGORIES

Categories of Muktzeh Learned So Far:

  1. Muktzeh Machmat Chisaron Kis - Items too valuable/delicate for general use
  2. Kli Shemelachto L'Issur - Tools whose primary purpose is forbidden on Shabbat

SIMAN 308, SE'IF DALET - כלי שמלאכתו להיתר (KLIIM WHOSE PRIMARY PURPOSE IS PERMITTED)

Basic Definition and Laws

Shulchan Aruch Text: "כלי שמלאכתו להיתר, מותר לטלטלו אפילו אינו צריך הכלי שלא ישבר או יגנב, אבל שלא לצורך כלל אסור לטלטלו. וכתבי הקודש ואוכלים מותר לטלטלם אפילו שלא לצורך כלל."

Translation: A utensil whose primary purpose is for permissible activities may be moved even if you don't need the utensil itself - just so it shouldn't break or be stolen. But moving it for no purpose at all is forbidden. Holy books and food may be moved even for no purpose at all.

Historical Background

  • Nechemiah's Decree: The prophet Nechemiah saw people being lax with items on Shabbat
  • Temporary ban was instituted on ALL keilim (utensils)
  • When standards improved, the ban was lifted but with restrictions
  • Current Law: These items can only be moved when there is a particular purpose

Examples of Kli Shemelachto L'Heter

  • Plates, spoons, knives, forks
  • Dresses, clothing
  • Watches, clocks
  • Any utensil whose primary purpose is something permissible on Shabbat

Permitted Reasons for Moving

  1. Primary use - Using the item for its intended purpose
  2. Secondary use - Using it for a different purpose
  3. Protection - Moving it so it won't be:
  • Broken
  • Stolen
  • Damaged by sun/weather
  • Trampled

Forbidden Movement

"Shelo L'Tzorech Klal" - Moving for absolutely no purpose is prohibited

Example: You set out plates and cutlery Friday night for Shabbat lunch. On Shabbat morning you were invited out and won't be eating at home anymore. You want to put away the utensils just because you don't want them lying around on the table - this would seemingly be "shelo l'tzorech" and therefore forbidden.

Exceptions - Items That Can Be Moved Freely

  1. All food - Can be moved for any reason or no reason
  2. All sefarim (holy books) - Can be moved freely
  • Can organize bookshelf
  • Can move books around without restriction

Sephardim vs. Ashkenazim Regarding Utensils

  • Mishna Berura: Some are lenient and consider utensils like food
  • Our practice (Sephardim): Following Rambam and Shulchan Aruch - only food and books have unrestricted movement
  • All other utensils are prohibited to be moved without purpose

PRACTICAL APPLICATIONS AND QUESTIONS

The Fidgeting Question

Scenario: Person sitting at Shabbat table after meal, playing around with cutlery

Analysis:

  • Possible Heter: Could argue this serves a purpose (calming, fidgeting need)
  • Problem: If we allow this, when would the law ever apply?
  • Philosophical Issue: Normal humans don't do things without reason

Conclusion: Very difficult to find scenarios where "shelo l'tzorech klal" actually applies in practice, since people can usually find some justification for their actions.

Megillah on Shabbat

General Rule: Megillot are not muktzeh because they can be opened and learned from

Special Case - Megillat Esther:

  • In Jerusalem (walled cities): When Purim falls on Friday
  • Outside Jerusalem: Read Megillah on Friday (14th)
  • In Jerusalem: Normally read on 15th, but Chachamim decreed not to read on Shabbat
  • Therefore: Megillat Esther becomes muktzeh on the 15th in Jerusalem
  • Reason: Prevention of carrying in reshut harabim

Outside Jerusalem: Megillah may be moved freely on the 15th since there's no reading requirement

TEFILLIN ON SHABBAT - MAJOR MACHLOKET

Background - Why No Tefillin on Shabbat?

Gemara's Teaching: We need two "otot" (signs):

  1. Weekdays: Brit Milah + Tefillin
  2. Shabbat: Brit Milah + Ot of Shabbat (therefore no need for Tefillin)

Historical Practice

  • Some people wore Tefillin on Shabbat "lishmor min hamazikin" (protection from evil forces)
  • This was for protection, not for the mitzvah of Tefillin

The Core Machloket

Position 1: Tefillin is Kli Shemelachto L'Heter (Rama's Position)

  • Tosfot's view: Chachamim said you don't NEED Tefillin on Shabbat
  • If you WANT to wear Tefillin for the mitzvah, you may
  • Status: Regular utensil that can be moved when there's any need
  • Practical application: Can move Tefillin bag to get Tallit out

Position 2: Tefillin is Kli Shemelachto L'Issur (Chida, Ben Ish Chai, Chacham Ovadia)

  • Zohar's position: Not only don't you need Tefillin, it's FORBIDDEN to wear them
  • Reasoning: Wearing Tefillin on Shabbat is "mechalel Shabbat" - desecrates Shabbat
  • Shows you're saying the "ot of Shabbat" isn't sufficient
  • Status: Kli Shemelachto L'Issur - can only move l'tzorech gufo or l'tzorech mekomo

Practical Applications of Tefillin Status

Secondary Purpose (L'Tzorech Gufo)

Mishna Berura: Can move for protection from danger Chacham Ovadia: Broader interpretation includes protecting the Tefillin themselves from "bizayon" (disgrace)

Extenuating Circumstances

Mishna Berura: "B'makom hadchak yesh l'hakel" - in pressing circumstances, can be lenient

Practical Scenarios

Scenario 1: Tefillin and Tallit in Same Bag

Problem: Forgot to remove Tallit before Shabbat, Tefillin lying on top

Solutions:

  1. Preferred: Put hand underneath and move Tallit indirectly (Tefillin fall further into bag)
  2. If impossible and no other Tallit available: May rely on opinion that Tefillin is Kli Shemelachto L'Heter
  3. Best practice: Remove Tallit before Shabbat to avoid complications

Scenario 2: Finding Tefillin in Compromised Position

Examples:

  • Tefillin lying in street
  • Tefillin in sun getting damaged
  • Any situation where they face "bizayon"

Ruling: Everyone agrees you may move them to protect from disgrace

Practical Conclusion for Tefillin

  • First instance: Both Ashkenazim and Sephardim should be particular not to move Tefillin unnecessarily
  • Compromised situations: All agree movement is permitted
  • General advice: Remove Tallit from Tefillin bag before Shabbat

SHOFAR LAWS

Basic Status

Shulchan Aruch: "שופר אסור לטלטלו כי אם לצורך גופו ומקומו" Shofar may not be moved except for its place or secondary purpose

Reasoning

Primary purpose: Playing music (Rosh Hashana or general music) - forbidden on Shabbat Therefore: Kli Shemelachto L'Issur

Secondary Purposes (Historical)

  • Filling with oil for storage
  • Storing liquids to keep them cold

Contemporary Application

Modern Reality: People don't use Shofar for secondary purposes Poskim's Position: Nowadays Shofar should be categorized as "Muktzeh Machmat Chisaron Kis"

  • Too valuable/special to use for other purposes
  • Result: Cannot be moved at all (even with shinui - unusual manner)

Historical Context

Beit Hamikdash period: Shofar was blown on Shabbat (only in Temple) Outside Temple: Forbidden due to concern of carrying in reshut harabim Principle: "Ein shvut bamikdash" - no rabbinic prohibitions in Temple

SIMAN 308, SE'IF HEI - SPECIAL HETER FOR KLI SHEMELACHTO L'ISSUR

The Basic Heter

Shulchan Aruch Text: "יש מתירים לטלטל כלי שמלאכתו לאיסור אפילו מחמה לצל על ידי כיכר או תינוק"

Some permit moving Kli Shemelachto L'Issur even from sun to shade by means of bread or a baby.

Gemara Source: Garlic in Mortar

Clear ruling: Garlic crushed before Shabbat and left in garlic mortar may be moved freely on Shabbat

Two Interpretations of the Heter

Approach 1: Rashba (Limited Application)

Theory: The utensil becomes "batel" (secondary) to the food inside Reasoning:

  • Whole purpose of mortar is to have crushed garlic
  • When garlic is inside, we look at garlic, not the utensil
  • Utensil becomes secondary to its contents

Limitation: Very narrow application - only works when utensil contains its intended product

Approach 2: Rosh (Broad Application - Shulchan Aruch's Position)

Theory: When anything permissible is placed on Kli Shemelachto L'Issur, we consider you're moving the permitted item

Practical Application:

  • Empty pot lying in garden, will be damaged
  • Put slice of bread on pot
  • Move pot with bread together
  • Bring inside, put pot down, remove bread

Mishna Berura: "Hu hadin im noten sha'ar chefetz mutar" - Same law applies to any other permitted object

The Machloket and Practical Ruling

Ashkenazim

  • Follow Rashba's limited approach
  • Cannot use general heter of adding permitted item
  • Exception: When utensil contains its intended contents (like garlic in mortar)

Sephardim

  • Follow Rosh/Shulchan Aruch's broad approach
  • Major new heter: Can place any permitted item on Kli Shemelachto L'Issur and move freely
  • Additional to other heterim: Shinui, l'tzorech gufo, l'tzorech mekomo

Important Limitations

  1. Pre-Shabbat placement doesn't help: Cannot place item before Shabbat to enable movement
  2. Only for Kli Shemelachto L'Issur: Does not apply to other categories of muktzeh
  3. Must move together: Both items moved simultaneously

Practical Examples

Food in Pots

Principle: Pot with cooked food can be moved freely Reasoning: Whole purpose of pot is to contain cooked food

When pot becomes muktzeh:

  • During meal: Once all food is removed from pot on table
  • Exception: Can remove from table due to "graf shel rei" (unpleasant mess)
  • Cannot: Move empty pot freely around kitchen afterward

Dishwasher scenario: Cannot rearrange empty pots in dishwasher unless need space (tzorech mekomo)

SIMAN 308, SE'IF VAV - MUKTZEH MACHMAT GUFO

Definition

Muktzeh Machmat Gufo: Items that have no purpose Examples: Stones, rocks, coins (when not designated for specific use)

Broken Utensils - The Fundamental Rule

Shulchan Aruch: "כל הכלים שנשברו אפילו בשבת, מותר לטלטל שבריהם, ובלבד שיהיו ראויים לשום מלאכה"

All utensils that broke, even on Shabbat, it's permitted to move their fragments, provided they are suitable for some purpose.

Examples of Permitted Fragments

  • Trough fragments: To cover a barrel
  • Glass fragments: To cover a container opening

The Chiddush (Novel Teaching)

Key principle: Even though fragments weren't originally designated for their new use, since they came from a utensil, they retain "torat kli" (status of a utensil)

Distinction:

  • Regular stone/pebble: Even if could be used to cover bottle, it's muktzeh because never was a utensil
  • Broken utensil pieces: Can be moved if they have potential use, because they originated as utensils

Contemporary Application Debate

Position 1: Stricter View

  • Reality: Nowadays people don't reuse broken glass pieces
  • Conclusion: Fragments revert to Muktzeh Machmat Gufo status
  • Reasoning: Not fitting for any melacha in practice

Position 2: Lenient View

  • Reality: We have so many alternatives we don't need to reuse
  • Principle: If technically usable when pushed, remains permissible
  • Example: Less developed countries do reuse such items

Practical Applications

Empty Food Packages

Example: Empty Bamba package

Basic rule:

  • While containing food: Can move freely (food status)
  • After eating: Can move due to "graf shel rei" to throw away
  • For reuse: Can wash and use for other purposes (retains torat kli)

Exception: If thrown away before Shabbat, loses torat kli status

Israel Milk Containers

Scenario: Plastic milk sachets common in Israel Problem: When empty, have no practical reuse value Status: Would become Muktzeh Machmat Gufo when emptied Solution: Must tip container to empty, cannot pick up directly

Important Distinction - Tzorech Mekomo Limitations

Critical point: Muktzeh Machmat Gufo CANNOT be moved l'tzorech mekomo Reason: This heter only applies to Kli Shemelachto L'Issur Cannot move stone just because you need its place

Safety Exception (Rama)

Rule: If broken pieces are dangerous, may move freely regardless of usefulness Example: Glass fragments on living room floor - clear immediately Limitation: If in unused room where no one enters, cannot clean until after Shabbat

SUMMARY OF NEW CATEGORIES LEARNED

Category 3: Kli Shemelachto L'Heter

  • Basic rule: May move for any purpose except "shelo l'tzorech klal"
  • Exceptions: Food and sefarim may be moved freely
  • Practical challenge: Difficult to define "no purpose at all"

Category 4: Muktzeh Machmat Gufo

  • Basic rule: Items with no purpose cannot be moved
  • Exception: Broken utensil pieces if they have potential use
  • No l'tzorech mekomo: Unlike Kli Shemelachto L'Issur

New Heterim Learned

  1. For Kli Shemelachto L'Heter: Can move for protection/prevention of damage
  2. For Kli Shemelachto L'Issur (Sephardim only): Can add permitted item and move together
  3. Broken utensils: Retain status if pieces have potential use
  4. Safety: Dangerous items may be moved regardless of status
