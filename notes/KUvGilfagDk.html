Topic: Where the custom of Selihot comes from, and when Sefaradim and Ashkenazim begin

Sources: The Geonim (Rav Kohen Sedek, Rav Hai Gaon); the Rambam; Rabbi Yishak Ibn Ghiyyat; Rabbenu Nissim; the Meiri; the Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Elul, Monday 5 September 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Selihot are not mentioned in the Gemara or the Midrashim. They are a post-Talmudic minhag, first recorded among the Geonim of Babylonia in the 9th and 10th centuries, who said them during the Ten Days of Teshuvah between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur.
  • Already in the 11th century the Sefaradim rose to say Selihot from the beginning of Elul. This is the practice of the vast majority of Sefaradim today: from the day after Rosh Hodesh Elul.
  • The reason for Elul: Moshe went up for the second Luhot on Rosh Hodesh Elul and came down with them on Yom Kippur, the day of forgiveness and atonement.
  • Ashkenazim begin at least four days before Rosh Hashanah, like a korban that needed four days of examination for blemishes. They begin on the Sunday before Rosh Hashanah if four days remain; otherwise on the Sunday a full week before. Sunday was chosen as a fixed, easy day to remember.
  • Other customs existed, such as saying Selihot only on Mondays and Thursdays of Elul (recorded by the Meiri), but they are not practised today.
  • The Shulhan Arukh writes "it is customary to rise" for Selihot: it is a minhag, not a Torah or rabbinic obligation. So it must not come at the expense of other obligations. Just as teachers may not take on extra fasts because they owe their students full energy, someone whose early rising for Selihot would harm his work for his employer, or a talmid hakham whose Torah study would suffer, should weigh this.

Sources quoted

  • The Rambam, who normally records only what is in the Talmud, still writes that it has become customary for everyone to rise for Selihot in the Ten Days of Teshuvah.
  • Shulhan Arukh: "It is customary to rise early to say Selihot", the language of minhag.

In one line

Selihot are an ancient and beautiful minhag (from Rosh Hodesh Elul for Sefaradim, from the Sunday before Rosh Hashanah for Ashkenazim), but as a minhag they must not displace our real obligations.
