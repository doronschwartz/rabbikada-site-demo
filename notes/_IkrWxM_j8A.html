Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 271, seifim 1-3 (seif 3 begun) Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 19 October 2023

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

Into Shabbat itself: Kiddush

The Shulhan Arukh follows the order of the day. Having dealt with Erev Shabbat, the preparations and the candles, it now enters Shabbat itself. The Rabbi skipped the chapter on mistakes in the Shabbat prayers and began siman 271, the laws of Kiddush on wine, with 17 seifim. "This is a very important chapter."

Seif 1: when you get home, make Kiddush promptly

The Shulhan Arukh: when you come home, hurry to eat. The Mishnah Berurah explains that the point is to make Kiddush, which must come before the meal.

Why hurry? "Zakhor et Yom HaShabbat Lekadesho": remember Shabbat to sanctify it, and the Gemara says remember it as it comes in and as it goes out. Kiddush should be at the first opportunity, not hours after Shabbat has begun.

The same applies at the end of Shabbat. You may extend Shabbat as long as you like, but once you have prayed Arvit, do not delay Havdalah. If there are no spices until later, make Havdalah now and say the berakhah on spices later by itself. (The Rambam holds Havdalah is from the Torah; most hold only Kiddush is.)

Within reason. If you are not hungry yet, the Magen Avraham says you may rely on having said "Mekadesh HaShabbat" in Arvit. But if your wife, servants or guests, especially poor guests, are hungry, do not delay them. "You should never do humrot at the expense of others." Waiting a few minutes for guests to arrive is fine; if it will be an hour, make Kiddush.

The Rabbi once asked a great Rav in Jerusalem, who would give long shiurim after Arvit on Friday night and reach home very late, about this. He answered: read it carefully. The Shulhan Arukh says that once you come home, make Kiddush quickly. If you are in shul praying or learning, no one says you must rush out. Once you are home, do not hang around.

What is Kiddush from the Torah?

"Kiddush" means sanctification, not grape juice. (The Rabbi recalled someone at a shiva asking "is there Kiddush tonight?" when he meant refreshments.) Three opinions: - Kiddush on wine is from the Torah. - The words are from the Torah; the wine is rabbinic. This is the main view, brought by the Mishnah Berurah. - A minority: Kiddush on wine in the place of the meal is from the Torah.

If you miss Kiddush on Friday night, you say the full Friday night Kiddush during the day, any time until Shabbat ends.

Does Arvit count as Kiddush?

The Magen Avraham says that since the Torah obligation is words alone, saying "Mekadesh HaShabbat" in the Amidah fulfils it. So if you are unsure whether you made Kiddush on wine, it is only a rabbinic doubt. And a child, whose obligation is rabbinic, could make Kiddush for an adult who has prayed Arvit, since both are now rabbinic.

The Mishnah Berurah does not like this at all: - A Torah misvah needs intention. When you pray Arvit, you are not thinking of fulfilling Kiddush, since you plan to make it properly at home. By default a person does not want to fulfil a misvah in a "half-baked" way when he can soon do it properly. - The Biur Halacha adds that Kiddush must mention the Exodus from Egypt, which the Amidah does not.

So we do not rely on it: a child should not make Kiddush for his father. Only if you truly have no wine and no bread would you rely on your tefillah. (If you have no wine, you make Friday night Kiddush on bread.)

Seif 2: women and Kiddush

Women are obligated in Kiddush equally with men, even though it is a time-bound misvah. The reason is a hekesh: "Zakhor" and "Shamor" were said together. Women are bound by "Shamor", the prohibitions of Shabbat, so they are bound by "Zakhor", the positive misvot of Shabbat. There is no difference between men and women in any of the laws of Shabbat.

Asked why loving HaShem is not time-bound, since one cannot do it asleep, the Rabbi explained that sleep is external; the obligation never ceases. Sukkah, seven days a year, is time-bound.

Since her obligation is equal, a woman may make Kiddush for men, and may even make it for others after she has already made it, just as a man can.

Children: a common mistake is a mother telling her 12-year-old son to make Kiddush when the father is away. He is rabbinic, she is Torah; she must make Kiddush for everyone. A boy who has just become Bar Misvah should also not make Kiddush for others unless it is known that he has physical signs of maturity (the presumption that a 13-year-old has matured is relied on for rabbinic matters, not Torah ones). If unsure, he says Kiddush for himself and his mother repeats it word by word with him.

Women making Kiddush for men outside the household

The Mishnah Berurah brings the Shulhan Arukh, the Gra, the Magen Avraham and the Taz, all permitting a woman to make Kiddush for men. Then the Eliyah Rabbah, about 250 years ago, says it is better not to for men outside her household, because it is "zila milta", demeaning.

The Rabbi's view: - No one suggests any problem with a woman making Kiddush for her husband. - The concern was about a time when women were not educated, so it looked bad for men to rely on them. It is advice, not a takkanah of Hazal.

"I see no problem at all in a woman saying Kiddush on behalf of another man."

Perhaps for a whole community it might be inappropriate, though he did not think so. With guests at home, it is absolutely fine. If the husband has a headache or does not want to drink, she should make it.

A participant who cannot drink wine asked whether it is better to make Kiddush on grape juice himself, or for his wife to make it on non-mevushal wine. The Rabbi said that theoretically, in purely halachic terms, wine is better, so she could make it, though he was not saying one must, as there are other considerations; alternatively he could make Kiddush on the wine and she drinks it.

Listening is not laziness. Hearing Kiddush from someone else is fully equal to saying it, and even preferable: "berov am hadrat melekh", the more people doing a misvah together, the better. That is why one person makes Kiddush for everyone. But you must pay attention, not fiddle with the cup or look around. The old Sefaradi custom was one person making Birkat HaMazon for everyone.

Seif 3: if money is short

The class began seif 3: what comes first if you cannot afford everything, Friday night wine, daytime wine, Havdalah wine, and good food for the meals. This will continue next week.

Practical summary

  • Once home on Friday night, make Kiddush promptly; do not keep hungry family or guests waiting.
  • Do not rely on Arvit for Kiddush; make Kiddush on wine (or on bread if there is no wine).
  • Women are equally obligated and may make Kiddush for their husbands and for other men.
  • A child under Bar Misvah should not make Kiddush for adults; a mother should make it for her children.
  • After Arvit on Mosei Shabbat, do not delay Havdalah; spices can be added later.
  • Listen attentively when another makes Kiddush for you.
