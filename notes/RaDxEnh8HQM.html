Se'if 27: Bones, Peels, and Food Remnants

Basic Rule - Edible Items

Bones suitable for dogs, peels suitable for animals, and crumbs (even less than a כזית) - מותר להעבירם (permitted to move them) in a normal fashion from the table.

Rationale: These items retain their status as food because they have potential use - bones can be eaten by dogs, peels by animals, and crumbs are still technically food, even if small.

When Items Become Muktzah

אם אין הקליפין ראוין למאכל בהמה - If the peels are no longer suitable even for animals, אסור לטלטלן (forbidden to move them). This falls under מוקצה מחמת גופו - items with no purpose.

Permitted Methods of Removal

1. Shaking Method

מנערת הטבלה והן נופלין - You may shake the tablecloth and let the items fall off naturally. You cannot carry them directly to the bin.

2. With Other Food Present

אם יש פת על השולחן מותר לקנבה הטבלה או לטלטלה עם הקליפים - If there is bread (or any edible food) on the table, you may lift the entire tablecloth because שהם בטלים אגב הפת (the inedible items are nullified in relation to the bread).

3. Need for Space

ואם היה צריך למקום השולחן - If you need the space where the table/peels are located, you may move them directly.

4. Graf Shel Re'i (Unpleasant/Disgusting)

If there is a significant amount of waste that makes the area unpleasant to be in, you may remove it directly under the principle of גרף של רעי.

Modern Applications

  • Pistachio shells, eggshells that cannot be fed to animals become muktzah
  • Same rules apply to items on plates as on tablecloths
  • The היתר of גרף של רעי applies when continuing to use the space (e.g., multiple courses at a meal)
  • Must be genuinely uncomfortable, not just preference for cleanliness

Se'if 28: Which Animals Count as "Common"

Basic Principle

כל שהוא ראוי למאכל חיה ועוף המצויים, מותר לטלטלו - Anything suitable for commonly found animals and birds may be moved.

ואם אינו ראוי אלא למאכל חיה ועוף שאינם מצויים - If only suitable for uncommon animals, אם יש לו מאותו מין חיה ועוף, מותר לטלטלו - it's permitted only if you own that type of animal.

Examples and Applications

  • Dogs are מצוי (common) everywhere, so bones are generally not muktzah
  • Cats, hamsters would be considered מצוי in urban areas
  • Cows would only be relevant in rural areas where they're actually present
  • Must be animals that are commonly owned by people, not just present (e.g., stray pigeons don't count, but pet parrots do)

Important Limitation

אין מזונותיהם עליך - You have no obligation to feed stray animals on Shabbat (forbidden due to טירחה - unnecessary effort), so food suitable only for stray animals would remain muktzah.

Se'if 30: Date Pits and Fruit Seeds

Date Pits

גרעיני תמרים במקום שמאכילים אותם לבהמה מותר לטלטלן - Date pits may be moved in places where it's common to feed them to animals.

ואדם חשוב צריך להחמיר על עצמו שלא יטלטלם רק בשינוי - An important person should be strict and only move them in an unusual manner.

Modern Reality

Today, date pits are generally considered muktzah as they're not commonly fed to animals and may be dangerous.

Removing Pits While Eating

Permitted to remove pits while eating the fruit as this is the only way to consume the fruit safely. However, once removed, dry pits become muktzah.

Other Fruit Pits

  • Apricot pits - typically dry, become muktzah unless designated before Shabbat for games
  • Apple cores - usually retain some fruit, so not muktzah
  • Designation (ייחוד) must be done before Shabbat when the pit is already separated, not while part of the fruit

Se'if 30-31: Raw Meat and Fish

Raw Meat

בשר חי אפילו תפל שאינו מלוח כלל, מותר לטלטלו, משום דחזי לאומצא - Raw meat, even completely unsalted, may be moved because it can be chewed raw.

Even if spoiled and smelly, מותר לטלטלו משום דראוי לכלבים - still permitted because it's suitable for dogs.

Raw Fish

דג מלוח מותר לטלטלו - Salted fish may be moved. ושאינו מלוח אסור משום שאינו ראוי באף - Unsalted fish is forbidden as it's not suitable raw.

Modern Developments

The פוסקים note that customs change over time:

  • 40-50 years ago, some questioned if raw meat should become muktzah since people stopped eating it
  • Today, with carpaccio, sushi, and other raw preparations being common, both raw meat and raw fish have strong grounds to be permitted
  • ספק דרבנן לקולא - in cases of rabbinic doubt, we rule leniently

Frozen Meat/Fish

  • Muktzah if it cannot defrost in time to be eaten before Shabbat ends
  • Not muktzah if it could theoretically defrost and be eaten later on Shabbat
  • Depends on circumstances: size of item, ambient temperature, time remaining

Se'if 32: Amulets

קמיע שאינו מומחה אף על פי שאין יוצאים בו, מותר לטלטלו אותו - An unproven amulet, even though one may not wear it in רשות הרבים, may be moved at home.

Se'if 33: Graf Shel Re'i - Disgusting Items

Definition and Examples

כל דבר מתונף - Anything disgusting or filthy:

  • רעי (excrement)
  • קיא (vomit)
  • צואה (waste)
  • בין של אדם בין של תרנגולים - whether human or animal waste

When Permitted

אם היו בחצר שיושבים בה מותר להוציאם לאשפה או לבית הכסא - If in a place where people are present, permitted to take to refuse or toilet, אפילו בלא כלי - even directly without a utensil.

When Forbidden

אם היו בחצר שאין דרים שם, אסור להוציאם - If in a place where people don't frequent, forbidden to remove them.

Modern Extensions of Graf Shel Re'i

The משנה ברורה already extended this to include large amounts of table waste (egg peels, nut shells, etc.).

Contemporary applications (רב שלמה זלמן אורבך):

  • Hammer on Shabbat table - unpleasant, may be moved
  • Phone on Shabbat table - not in spirit of Shabbat, may be moved
  • Empty pots lying around - considered unpleasant in modern standards
  • Any item causing genuine discomfort in a space being used

Transportation Rule

Once picked up due to graf shel re'i, you may carry it all the way to its final destination (e.g., outside bins) even if you pass through areas where it would no longer be bothersome.

Se'if 33 (continued): Muktzah Mechamat Mius

Category: Inherently Disgusting Items

Items that are muktzah because they are מאוס (disgusting) even when clean.

Example: Clean potty - Even when empty and clean, still muktzah מחמת מיאוס.

Limited Permission

May only be moved for its intended purpose - bringing it to a child who needs to use it. Cannot be moved for general tidying or rearranging unless using it for its designated purpose.

Se'if 34: Not Creating Graf Shel Re'i

Prohibition on Intentional Creation

אין עושין גרף של רעי לכתחילה - Don't intentionally create situations requiring graf shel re'i.

Example: When eating sunflower seeds, shell them into a bowl rather than creating a pile of shells that will need to be removed as graf shel re'i.

If It Happens

ואם עבר עצמו מותר להוציאו - If it happened inadvertently, you may remove it.

Practical Application

  • Plan ahead to avoid creating unpleasant waste situations
  • Use containers or designated areas for shells, peels, etc.
  • If the nature of eating certain foods inevitably creates graf shel re'i, then it's unavoidable and permitted to clean up

Summary of Key Principles Learned

  1. Edibility determines muktzah status - potential use matters, not actual use
  2. "Common animals" means locally available owned animals, not wild or stray
  3. Graf shel re'i has expanded significantly in modern times due to heightened cleanliness standards
  4. Location matters - graf shel re'i only applies where people are present
  5. Times change halacha application - raw food consumption patterns affect muktzah status
  6. Don't create problematic situations when avoidable
  7. Multiple heterim exist - need for space, secondary to food, graf shel re'i
