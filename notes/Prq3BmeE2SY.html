Topic: How communities in the southern hemisphere pray for rain

Sources: The Gemara; Rav Yaakov Meir; Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; Rav Sitton and the Hakhamim of Argentina

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • In the northern hemisphere we pray for rain from the night of 4 December (5 December by day), or a day later before a civil leap year: 60 days after Tekufat Tishrei, as the Gemara set for Bavel.
  • As Jewish communities grew over the last century in Brazil, Uruguay, Argentina and Australia, where the seasons are reversed, the question arose. There are three opinions.
  • Rav Yaakov Meir, the first Rishon LeSion under the British Mandate (honoured with a CBE): simply reverse the prayers. From Sukkot to Pesah say "ten berakhah" and morid hatal, and pray for rain in their winter, since the Hakhamim spoke only of the northern hemisphere. This is not really practised today.
  • The second, favoured by Hakham Ovadiah and apparently the practice of Sepharadim there, builds on the Shulhan Arukh's ruling for a place that needs rain in summer: keep the standard text and add a private request in Shema Kolenu.
  • So all year they say morid hatal, never mashiv haruah umorid hageshem, and Barekhenu with "ten berakhah"; in their winter, from Pesah to Sukkot, they add "veten tal umatar livrakhah" in Shema Kolenu. This was the ruling of Rav Sitton and other Argentinian Hakhamim of the early 20th century, and is followed in Brazil too.
  • The third, which he understands to be the Ashkenazi practice: follow the northern hemisphere text, though it seems strange, since one prays for Kelal Yisrael as a whole.

In one line

In the southern hemisphere Sepharadim keep the summer text all year and add the request for rain in Shema Kolenu during their winter.
