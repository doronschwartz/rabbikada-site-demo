Siman 308, Seif 3: Keli Shemelachto L'Issur (כלי שמלאכתו לאיסור)

The Basic Halacha

Shulchan Aruch Text: "כלי שמלאכתו לאיסור מותר לטלטלו"

A utensil whose primary use is for prohibited purposes is permissible to move under two specific circumstances:

  1. בין לצורך גופו (For its own purpose) - When using it for a secondary, permitted function
  • Example: Using a hammer (normally for building - forbidden) to crack nuts
  • Example: Using an axe to cut a large cake
  1. בין לצורך מקומו (For its place) - When you need the space where the utensil is located
  • You need to use the place where the utensil is positioned

Additional Permission: Once you pick up the item for either of these permitted reasons, you may carry it and place it down wherever you want (ומותר לו ליטול משם ולהניחו באיזה מקום שירצה).

What is Forbidden

Moving from sun to shade (מחמה לצל) - Moving the item merely to protect it from:

  • Theft
  • Damage
  • Weather

This type of protective movement is forbidden.

Definition of Keli Shemelachto L'Issur

Requirements for This Category

  1. Must be a כלי (utensil) - If it's not a utensil, it falls under muktzeh machmat gufo
  2. Primary purpose must be for prohibited use - Not just majority use, but primary function

Primary Purpose vs. Majority Use - Key Distinction

The Debate:

  • Pri Megadim: You follow the majority use (רוב)
  • Biur Halacha: Disagrees - primary purpose is different from majority use

Biur Halacha's Position:

  • Primary purpose refers to the fundamental function of the item
  • Even if you use something mostly for issur, if it has regular permitted uses that you think about, it's not keli shemelachto l'issur
  • Example: A pot used mostly for cooking but also regularly for storage - not considered keli shemelachto l'issur

Practical Application:

  • Scissors used both for office work (cutting paper - issur) and kitchen use (cutting fruit - heter)
  • Even if used more for office work, if you think of them as having both functions, they're not keli shemelachto l'issur
  • However, if you have separate scissors for each purpose, then the office scissors would be keli shemelachto l'issur

Permitted Uses in Detail

1. L'Tzorech Gufo (For Secondary Function)

The Debate About Alternatives:

  • Mishna Berura: Only permitted when you don't have another utensil that can perform the secondary function
  • Chacham Ovadia and Sephardic Poskim: Permitted even if you have alternatives - it's an outright heter

Practical Ruling:

  • Sephardim: May use keli shemelachto l'issur for any secondary purpose even with alternatives available
  • Ashkenazim: More stringent - only when no alternatives exist

2. L'Tzorech Mekomo (For Its Place)

Broad Definition:

  • Not just the exact spot where the item sits
  • Includes the general environment/room where you need the space
  • You want to use the area without obstruction

Examples:

  • Alarm going off on Shabbat - can move phone to another room to stop noise disturbance
  • Muktzeh item on dining room floor when guests arrive - can move it for room aesthetics
  • Bathroom scales in the way - can move them to access bathroom facilities

Additional Halachot

Picking Up by Mistake

Keli Shemelachto L'Issur: If picked up by mistake, you may continue carrying it and put it down wherever you want

Other Types of Muktzeh: Must put down immediately where you are (may need to use shinui/unusual manner)

Using a Non-Jew (Goy)

Special Heter for Keli Shemelachto L'Issur:

  • May ask a non-Jew to move it even without great financial loss
  • Reason (Pri Megadim): Since there's theoretically a way for a Jew to do it (l'tzorech mekomo or l'tzorech gufo), you may ask a non-Jew outright

Parallel Example: When you've accepted Shabbat early but it's still day outside, you may ask a non-Jew to do melacha because theoretically you could have done it yourself

Practical Examples

Common Items That Are Keli Shemelachto L'Issur

  1. Measuring Scales:
  • Forbidden to measure on Shabbat (gezeira - might come to write)
  • Can move l'tzorech mekomo (need bathroom space, etc.)
  1. Sponges:
  • Forbidden due to sechita (squeezing water)
  • Can move if needed for sink use, etc.
  1. Matchbox:
  • After lighting candles, left on table
  • Can move l'tzorech mekomo
  1. Musical Instruments:
  • Shofar example from Gemara
  • Playing music forbidden rabbinically (gezeira shema yetaken kli shir)
  • Secondary use: storing water/drinks

Special Cases

Guns:

  • In Israel/Dangerous Areas: Not muktzeh at all - primary purpose is self-defense (mitzvah)
  • In Safe Areas: Either muktzeh machmat chesron kis or keli shemelachto l'issur depending on use and value

Watches with Calculators:

  • Primary purpose: telling time (heter)
  • Therefore: keli shemelachto l'heter, not l'issur

Ovens:

  • Generally keli shemelachto l'issur
  • Can open to remove food (l'tzorech gufo)
  • Can close door due to:
  1. Safety hazard (l'tzorech mekomo)
  2. Unpleasant appearance (l'tzorech mekomo)
  3. Protecting other food inside (l'tzorech gufo)

Additional Principles from Seif 3

1. Touching Muktzeh (נגיעה בעלמא)

Rama's Addition: Muktzeh is only forbidden to move, but touching without moving is permitted

Examples:

  • May touch synagogue candelabra if it doesn't move
  • May touch a lit oven
  • May lean against a car if it doesn't move the car

2. Indirect Movement for Permitted Purposes

Principle: May move non-muktzeh item on top of muktzeh item, even if this causes the muktzeh to move

Example: Taking ice cream from freezer, causing raw meat underneath to fall

Key Distinction:

  • Permitted: Indirect movement for permitted purpose
  • Forbidden: Indirect movement for forbidden purpose (like prodding muktzeh with stick)

3. Moving Through Blowing (ניפוח)

Halacha: May move any muktzeh through blowing

Reason: This is considered unusual movement (shinui), not regular tiltul

Siman 308, Seif 4: Introduction to Keli Shemelachto L'Heter

The Third Category

Definition: Items whose primary use is for permitted purposes

Examples: Manual watch, regular kitchen utensils, etc.

Basic Rule: May be moved freely, but with certain limitations (to be covered in detail next class)

Important Note: Even items that seem completely permissible have some restrictions in certain circumstances
