Topic: The structure of the Yom Kippur services, from Kol Nidre to Neilah: what is core and what was added Source: The Spanish and Portuguese mahzor Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada at Holland Park, Wednesday 9 October 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The plan of the day

After two weeks on Rosh Hashanah, the series turned to Yom Kippur. With only an hour, the Rabbi focused on the structure and the key points. Yom Kippur has five prayers: Arvit at night, and Shaharit, Musaf, Minhah and Neilah by day. Each follows the same pattern: - The Amidah, said silently, ending with a confession (viduy). - The repetition (in the daytime services), which also contains a viduy. - The selihot after it: poems, supplications and verses, added later to fill the day.

The Amidah with its viduy is the focus of each prayer. Since the page numbers differ between the old and new editions of the mahzor, he said he might announce both on the day.

Before Arvit: Kol Nidre

  • "Shema koli", by Rav Hai Gaon in the tenth century, also said on fast days, lists those who were answered (Avraham, Yishak, Yosef in prison, Yonah, Moshe at Sinai, Hannah) and asks: listen to our voice too. At Holland Park they also say "Lekha Eli," a confession chanted slowly, because one should confess just before Yom Kippur begins.
  • "Al daat hamakom": with the consent of the heavenly and earthly courts, "we permit prayer with those who have transgressed." The Rabbi attributed it to Rabbi Meir of Rothenburg in thirteenth-century Germany, who died in prison, probably as a response to the assimilation of his time. People still come back to synagogue once a year, and we welcome them.
  • Kol Nidre itself (he noted the correct pronunciation is "Kal Nidre") is not a prayer. It is a legal declaration annulling vows from the past year and declaring that vows in the coming year will not take effect. It became especially prominent after the Inquisition, when the conversos, outwardly Christian, would gather on Yom Kippur night: before they could pray, they annulled what they had been forced to declare and reclaimed their identity. That may explain its slow, haunting tune.

Kol Nidre was used against Jews by people who mocked that Jews could not be trusted because they annul their promises every year. That is not what it does. It only works for vows a person does not know about, has forgotten, or made by mistake (such as doing a good practice three times, which can become like a vow). If you remember a vow, you must go to a Beit Din, state the specific vow and go through the process with them. A promise to pay someone within seven days is not cancelled by saying Kol Nidre.

It must be said before nightfall, because vows are not annulled on Shabbat or Yom Kippur: annulment is a Beit Din procedure, and the hakhamim suspended all Beit Din business on those days, since it is associated with writing. It is in Aramaic because that was the spoken language; a legal declaration you do not understand has no value. That is why, the Rabbi said, he would explain it in English before it is said. Then come the Mi sheberakh prayers, for the royal family, the congregation, the State of Israel and the hatanim, and the hashkavot. These are not crucial.

Arvit

Arvit begins with the blessings of the Shema, then the Amidah. The first three blessings are like Rosh Hashanah, on Hashem's sovereignty; the fourth thanks Hashem for Yom Kippur; the rest is a regular Amidah, with the confession at the end. There is never a repetition at night, so the second viduy comes after the selihot.

About 1,500 years ago, he said, you would have gone home after the Amidah. Selihot were added after each of the five prayers so that people would not be idle. If you can only come for a short time, because you are unwell for example, come for Kol Nidre, the Shema and the Amidah.

The two key parts of the selihot: - The Thirteen Attributes of Mercy, "Hashem, Hashem, El rahum vehanun," said 26 times over the day, matching the numerical value of Hashem's name. - "Hatanu surenu," we have sinned, our Rock, said slowly line by line, with Shema Yisrael, Barukh shem and "Hashem Hu HaElokim": acceptance of the yoke of heaven.

After the Kaddish come the first four psalms of Tehillim, a Kabbalistic addition whose 310 words are said as spiritual protection for the night. He was not sure how it entered the Spanish and Portuguese books. Arvit ends with Alenu.

The night That year Yom Kippur began on Friday evening, with the service starting at six and ending at about 8.30. "If you're tired, go to sleep and wake up early." If you have energy, do not spend the night idle: study, read, meditate, think about teshuvah. Pages 50 to 66 of the mahzor are for private night-time reading and are not said in synagogue.

Shaharit

Shaharit begins like a Shabbat morning, with extra psalms added to the introductory section, read rather than sung. Leather shoes are forbidden; white clothing is a nice custom, not an obligation.

The Amidah is like Arvit, with its viduy. The viduy of the repetition is the longest of the day because it includes the confession of Rabbenu Nissim, about a thousand years old, said only once. He urged people to look at it beforehand or follow the English: it is raw, it goes through every sin you can think of, and "it's exactly who we are." Avinu Malkenu is not said when Yom Kippur falls on Shabbat. Selihot follow.

The Torah reading and Musaf

The Torah reading, from Aharei Mot, describes the Kohen Gadol entering the Holy of Holies and the laws of the day. The haftarah, from Yeshayahu, urges the people to do teshuvah.

Musaf is unique. After the silent Amidah with its viduy, the repetition includes the Avodah: a long, detailed account of the service in the Temple on Yom Kippur, introduced by poems, with the ark open. Because it is a technical and legal text, the custom is that only rabbis lead Musaf. A long viduy follows, then selihot. He aimed to begin Musaf at about 12.30 and end by 2.45, with a short break before Minhah at 3.15.

Minhah and Neilah

Minhah has the Torah reading of forbidden relationships, which follows straight on from the morning reading. He noted that the commentators give reasons for it, but the simplest answer is that it is the next passage, sparing the need to roll the scroll. The haftarah is the whole book of Yonah, in which the people of Nineveh repented: as Neilah approaches, it is time to do teshuvah if we have not yet. Then the Amidah, its viduy, the repetition and selihot, as before.

Neilah Neilah begins with a song and Ashrei. Its Amidah is different: the viduy in the silent Amidah is a single paragraph, and in the repetition there is only a very short one, because "now is the time to think about the future." Its closing paragraph says that Hashem wants us to return, even the wicked, and gives us the chance. A short selihot is followed by the finale: Shema Yisrael, "Hashem Hu HaElokim" seven times, and the shofar.

Summary

  • Each of the five prayers is an Amidah with a viduy, its repetition (also with a viduy), and later-added selihot.
  • Kol Nidre is a legal annulment of unknown or forgotten vows, not a prayer and not a licence to break promises.
  • The heart of the selihot is the Thirteen Attributes and the acceptance of the yoke of heaven.
  • Musaf contains the Avodah, and Rabbenu Nissim's long viduy in Shaharit is worth preparing in advance.
  • Use every moment of the day, and of the night, for teshuvah rather than idle talk.
