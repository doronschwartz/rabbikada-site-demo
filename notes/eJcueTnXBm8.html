Topic: An overview of Siman 5 and the basic principles of pesua dakka and kerut shofkhah

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

The halacha

  • Siman 5 has two parts. Seifim 1 to 10: the marriage restrictions on a pesua dakka or kerut shofkhah, a man whose reproductive organs were injured. Seifim 11 to 14: the prohibition of castration and sterilisation, direct and indirect, of humans and animals, by Jews and non-Jews, very relevant to vets today.
  • The Torah in Parashat Ki Tese forbids a pesua dakka or kerut shofkhah to marry a regular Jewish woman. As Rashi explains, pesua dakka concerns the testicles and kerut shofkhah the organ itself.
  • The key principle, implied in the Gemara and explicit in Rashi and the Rambam: the status applies only if the injury leaves him permanently unable to reproduce. If he can still reproduce, he may marry.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein permitted a man to have part of a testicle removed for IVF when doctors affirmed he could still reproduce; it is neither forbidden sterilisation nor a bar to marriage.
  • He went further: even where the Hakhamim classed an injury as preventing reproduction, if medicine today shows he can reproduce, he may marry.
  • The status applies only to an injury caused by man (bidei adam), not by Heaven (bidei shamayim); details follow.
  • The Torah's prohibition addresses men; women will be addressed briefly, and the reason is for tomorrow.

Sources quoted

  • Parashat Ki Tese: the prohibition for a pesua dakka or kerut shofkhah to marry.
  • Rashi: the distinction between the two terms.
  • Rashi and the Rambam: the status depends on inability to reproduce.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: removal of part of a testicle for IVF is permitted when reproduction remains possible.

In one line

A man is barred as pesua dakka or kerut shofkhah only if a man-made injury leaves him permanently unable to reproduce.
