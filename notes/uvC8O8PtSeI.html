The halacha

  • The Gemara in Kiddushin: divide one's days into thirds, Mikra, Mishnah and Talmud.
  • Rashi divides the week: two days for each, with the seventh split three ways. The Rambam divides each day's study time.
  • Mikra means all 24 books of Tanakh.
  • Rashi seems to take Mishnah as the 3rd-century Mishnah and Talmud as the Babylonian Talmud.
  • The Rambam takes Mishnah as the whole Oral Torah: his introduction to the Mishneh Torah defines it as everything from Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi and the Tannaim before him to Rav Ashi, Mishnah and Gemara alike, the facts of the law.
  • His third part is Talmud (some editions wrongly print Gemara; the Oxford manuscript reads Talmud): inferring and comparing concepts until one understands the principles on which halacha rests, the hardest level.
  • How these readings shaped different ways of learning today is the next topic.

Sources quoted

In one line

For the Rambam, each day's learning is a third Tanakh, a third the Oral Torah's facts, and a third its underlying principles.
