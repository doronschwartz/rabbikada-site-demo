Topic: Shushan Purim Katan, and which Adar to keep a nahalah (yahrzeit) in a leap year

Sources: The Rama; the Kaf HaHayim; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Purim Katan & Adar Rishon, Tuesday 15 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Purim Katan is a happy day: no eulogies or fasts, and according to the Rama a festive seudah.
  • As the 15th is marked in some way even outside walled cities in a regular year, so on both the 14th and 15th of Adar Rishon, even in England or America, one omits Tahanun and does not fast or eulogise.
  • The Kaf HaHayim writes to hold a seudah on the 15th too, which is a double humrah.
  • If someone died in Adar of a leap year, the nahalah is kept in that same Adar. For one who died in a regular Adar, the Shulhan Arukh rules to keep it in Adar Sheni: it is the main Adar, as Purim shows, and a sad day is not brought forward, just as a fast on Shabbat is postponed, not advanced. This is the custom of all Sepharadim.
  • The Rama, followed by most Ashkenazim, keeps it in Adar Rishon: one does not pass over misvot, and for some matters, such as vows, Adar Rishon is the main Adar.
  • Nothing prevents keeping both, since Kaddish may be said whenever there is need. This helps a family where one spouse is Ashkenazi and the other Sepharadi, keeping each custom.

In one line

Sepharadim keep a regular Adar nahalah in Adar Sheni, most Ashkenazim in Adar Rishon, and one may keep both.
