Topic: The rare year when Erev Pesah falls on Shabbat: the firstborns' fast, the search, disposing of hames, and the Shabbat meals Source: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Pesah, with the rulings of Rav Ovadia Yoseph Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada on Zoom, Tuesday 23 March 2021 (third of three Pesah preparation classes)

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

A year that comes rarely

After classes on what needs a hekhsher and on the Seder itself, this third class dealt with what is particular to 2021: Erev Pesah fell on Shabbat, which had not happened since 2008. The Rabbi went in order, from Thursday morning to Sunday morning. The times he gave were for London that year.

Thursday: the firstborns and the search

The fast of the firstborns. Its earliest source is Masekhet Soferim. The Rambam did not codify it, understanding the Yerushalmi to disagree, but many Rishonim held that one fasts, and the Shulhan Arukh rules that a firstborn fasts on Erev Pesah, in memory of the Jewish firstborns who were spared. Some say a firstborn daughter fasts, but the Shulhan Arukh records that this is not the custom. The Rema brings a custom for a father to fast for a firstborn son under bar misvah; some Sephardim do this, but it is not really our custom.

This year the fast cannot be on Shabbat, and we avoid fasting on Friday so as to enter Shabbat in the right frame of mind. Some say it moves to Thursday. Others say that since it cannot fall on its day, there is no fast at all. The Shulhan Arukh brings both, and the Rabbi explained that we generally follow the second opinion, so for Sephardim, firstborns need not fast this year at all.

Normally the custom is to join a festive meal of a misvah, a brit milah if one is available, or more usually a siyum. In a normal year you must be there, listen and eat. Taking food home for a firstborn son "doesn't work". This year, since the fast is not obligatory, it is good for a firstborn to attend a siyum, but listening over Zoom is enough. Last year (2020) Zoom was a necessity; this year it is simply sufficient.

Bedikat hames moves to Thursday night, since one cannot search with a candle on Friday night. Begin from nightfall, about 7.10pm (from sunset, about 6.30pm, if one must go to bed early). Do not sit down to learn or to eat dinner before searching.

  • After the berakhah, speak only about the search ("is this hames or not?"), not phone calls.
  • The ten pieces are a custom, not a halachic requirement. If you keep it, write down where they are and use pieces of less than a kazayit, about 15 grams, so a lost piece can simply be nullified.
  • The misvah is to search, not to find. "The objective of the evening isn't to find ten pieces."
  • Say Kol Hamira after the search, as every year, even though you will still eat hames the next day. If you missed the search on Thursday night, do it on Friday with a berakhah.

Friday: eating, disposing, working

The Rabbi explained the layers. Eating hames during the seven days carries karet. From midday on Erev Pesah, eating hames is a Torah prohibition without karet. The Hakhamim added a margin: stop eating two halachic hours before midday and stop owning hames one hour before. That year in London: stop eating at 10.00am, dispose by 11.04am, midday 12.07pm.

Since Friday is not Erev Pesah:

  • Hames may be eaten all day Friday, and on Shabbat morning until 10.00am.
  • Disposal may be done all Friday, but the Shulhan Arukh records that it is good practice to dispose of what you will not need by the usual time, 11.04am. The batei din sell before 11, and the sale contracts also cover hames put away until about an hour before Shabbat.
  • How to dispose. Ashkenazim must burn. For us it is enough to destroy it: flush it, crumble it into the outside bin, or pour bleach on it. Burning is good if possible.
  • Whiskey may be drunk on Friday night and Shabbat morning until 10.00am, but not from a bottle you have sold. Pour some into a cup before the sale.
  • Work and haircuts are permitted all Friday afternoon, unlike a normal Erev Pesah after midday.

Cooking. Ideally cook all Shabbat food in Pesah dishes. It is not strictly required, but if you cook hames, avoid sticky foods that cling to pots and never pour from hames pots into Pesah pots.

Setting the table. You cannot prepare on Shabbat for Pesah night. If you have room, set the Seder table on Friday and eat the Shabbat meals elsewhere, even at another table in the same room, as long as it is respectable. Not "stuck in a tiny place in the kitchen between the ovens."

Lettuce for maror must be checked for insects before Shabbat. Washing infested lettuce on Shabbat or Yom Tov would inevitably kill insects.

Friday night and the Shabbat morning meal

Friday night. You may eat hallot, or even real massah. The prohibition of eating massah on Erev Pesah begins, according to the Rambam whom we follow, from the morning, not the night before. That massah counts as your bread: Hamosi and Birkat HaMazon on a kazayit.

Shabbat morning is already Erev Pesah, so ordinary massah is out. Three options:

  1. Hallot before 10.00am. Pray early, wash and eat the hallah before 10. The meal can then continue with Pesah food. Clear the hames, flush or bin what remains, and say Kol Hamira before 11.04am. For teeth: floss if you do not usually bleed; or use mouthwash; or, relying on Rav Ovadia Yoseph's ruling that a manual toothbrush with toothpaste is permitted on Shabbat, brush as you normally would.
  2. Massah ashirah (egg massah), made with fruit juice and no water. It cannot become hames, which is why it cannot be used for the Seder and why it may be eaten on Erev Pesah. It has the status of mezonot, so for Hamosi and Birkat HaMazon you would need a full meal of over 160 grams, about four or five square massot. Ashkenazim do not eat it on Pesah at all.
  3. Massah mevushelet, Rav Ovadia Yoseph's preferred option. Remove all hames before Shabbat and say Kol Hamira on Friday. Before Shabbat, put the massot for Shabbat morning in hot soup for a couple of minutes. Cooked massah no longer counts for the Seder, so it may be eaten on Erev Pesah, and as long as it stays intact it is still Hamosi and Birkat HaMazon.

Seudah shelishit: only options 2 and 3, or fruit alone, eaten in the early afternoon so as to come to the Seder with an appetite. Cakes made with massah meal should be avoided on Erev Pesah, as Rav Ovadia writes.

In the questions, the Rabbi related what he heard from Rav Yishak Yoseph: his father entered the kitchen twice a year, to make the haroset and, when Erev Pesah fell on Shabbat, to prepare this cooked massah himself.

Shabbat afternoon and Mosei Shabbat

  • Shemurah massah set aside for the Seder is mukseh on Shabbat Erev Pesah. Ordinary massah that could be given to children is not.
  • Sleeping on Shabbat afternoon is fine; just do not announce that you are sleeping to have energy for the Seder.
  • Setting the table only after nightfall, about 7.15pm. If young children or elderly people cannot wait, one may begin from sunset, about 6.30pm.
  • Kiddush has five berakhot (Yaknehaz): wine, Kiddush, the candle, Havdalah and Shehehiyanu. Leave a flame burning from Friday that lasts 48 hours, for Havdalah and for lighting on Sunday night.
  • The clocks went forward that night. A clock that has stopped may not be restarted, as the Mishnah Berurah discusses. A working manual clock may be moved forward on Shabbat or Yom Tov. The Rabbi's leaning, which he said he wanted to double check, was that a clock with a battery is also permitted.

From the questions

  • Throwing away hallah is disrespectful, so prepare only what you will eat. If some is left, there is no choice: it may not be kept.
  • Massah ashirah is mezonot all year round. Chocolate-covered ordinary massah is still Hamosi on Pesah.
  • Hand-made massah: "Everyone agrees that's the real massah."

Practical summary

  • For Sephardim, firstborns do not fast this year; attending a siyum, even by Zoom, is good.
  • Search for hames on Thursday night; eat hames until 10am Shabbat; dispose of unneeded hames ideally by 11am Friday.
  • Shabbat morning: hallot before 10am, or massah ashirah in quantity, or massah cooked before Shabbat.
  • Do not handle the Seder shemurah massah on Shabbat; set the table after nightfall.
  • Kiddush on Mosei Shabbat has five berakhot; leave a 48-hour flame.
