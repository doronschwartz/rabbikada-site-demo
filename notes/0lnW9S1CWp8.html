Topic: Whether one may live from communal funds while studying Torah or holding a rabbinic post

Sources: Rambam, Hilchot Talmud Torah and Commentary on Avot; Mishnah Avot; the Maharal; the Tashbes; the Beit Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Wednesday 29 May 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Rambam, in the Mishneh Torah and his Commentary on Avot, writes unusually strongly against those who do not work and live from communal funds, calling it forbidden.
  • He cites "do not make the Torah a spade to dig with", "love work and hate positions of authority", and "any Torah without work ends in futility and leads to sin". Such a person desecrates God's name, disgraces the Torah, extinguishes the light of the religion and brings harm on himself.
  • The Maharal adds that one paid by the community is constrained in what he can say, out of concern for his living.
  • Perhaps most later authorities disagreed. The Tashbes (Rabbi Shimon ben Semah Duran) rejects the Rambam's proofs: Hillel was a woodcutter only as a student.
  • The Beit Yoseph defends the established practice: even if the Rambam is right in theory, "et laasot laHashem heferu toratekha"; otherwise Torah would be forsaken.
  • He holds this is not relevant to rabbinic posts today, which carry many professional and pastoral duties, nor to teachers with preparation and marking. These are paid as regular jobs.
  • The Rambam meant those who sit and learn and expect a stipend, such as long-term Kollel; learning in Kollel is fine, but living off a stipend he would call wrong. Studying for a few years in order to serve the community he would agree to. The Beit Yoseph and others permitted even long-term study.

In one line

The Rambam opposed living off Torah study, but modern rabbinic and teaching posts are real jobs, and the Beit Yoseph permitted stipends.
