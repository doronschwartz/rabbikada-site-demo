Seif 9 — Cooked meat, raw meat

מותר לחתוך בשר מבושל או צלי דק דק בסכין.

You may cut cooked or roasted meat very finely with a knife, in the regular way: not with a shinui, and it was never ground before. Why? Two machlokot combine:

Machloket

One side

The other side

Halacha

Does tochen apply to food that is already edible?

Most Rishonim: yes, even edible food

The Ba'al HaTerumah (and possibly the Rambam, "though that's debatable"): tochen only applies to inedible food, which the crushing makes edible

We don't follow the lenient view: the Shulchan Aruch applies tochen even to vegetables

Are animals גידולי קרקע? (אין טוחן אלא בגידולי קרקע)

They don't grow from the ground; they are born from animals

They get all their nutrition from the ground

Debatable

Together, Maran permits cutting what is already edible and comes from an animal: cooked meat and cooked chicken.

The sushi example: a big piece of raw salmon isn't edible as it is, but cut into tiny pieces with something else, it is. That is the kind of "making edible" the Ba'al HaTerumah means.

אבל אסור לחתוך דק דק בשר חי לפני העופות, הואיל ואינם יכולים לאכלו בלא חיתוך, כמשוי ליה אוכל.

  • Raw meat (or raw fish in a big piece) may not be cut very fine, e.g. for birds, "or for anyone for that matter," because you are making it edible.

But even with cooked meat, no specialised instrument. You may crush cooked meat, fish or chicken, but not with a grinder:

  1. grinders are muktzeh;
  2. every heter we have learned works only without a specialised instrument.

Seif 10 — Grating cheese

אסור לגרור הגבינה בשבת במורג חרוץ בעל פיפיות שקורין ראליו.

Work it through:

  • (a) Cheese is edible before grating.
  • (b) Cheese doesn't come from something that grows from the ground. So cheese may be mashed, crushed and grated completely on Shabbat. You don't even need the combination of Seif 9.
  • However: you may not use an instrument designated for grating. That is the prohibition of this seif.

The same applies to eggs: boiled eggs may be mashed (cooked, and from an animal rather than directly from the ground), but not with a grater or a masher. That would be an איסור דרבנן: "Chachamim forbade all kinds of special instruments, and thus all of these instruments become muktzeh on Shabbos."

Robert: isn't cheese from the ground too?

Robert: The milk came from the cow, and the cow ate from the ground, and the milk made the cheese. Why isn't cheese גידולי קרקע?

The Rabbi: "No, because it's quite far, and it's not the milk itself. It's a whole procedure." Cheese has its own new status. Meat or an egg, directly from the animal, are still potentially גידולי קרקע. Cheese clearly isn't.

Seif 11 — Water for vegetables (a side point)

מותר להשקות התלוש כדי שלא יכמוש.

  • Not our topic: it relates to kotzer, which we touched on long ago.
  • Background: Chachamim forbade putting things into water on Shabbat because of tircha (bother). Drawing water used to be a long procedure. That is why the Mishnah in Sukkah says don't change the water for the lulav on Yom Tov.
  • But for food purposes it is permitted: you may put vegetables in water so they don't wither.

Seif 12 — Cutting very fine

המחתך הירק דק דק, חייב משום טוחן.

  • A fourth form of tochen beyond grinding, crushing and mashing: cutting into very small pieces.
  • How small? שיעור דקותן לא נודע לנו: "we don't know the measure of its fineness." (The Rabbi thinks it's the Sefer Mitzvot Gedolot who says this.) Already the early Rishonim didn't know.

The Rema: ויש אומרים דאסור לחתוך גרוגרות וחרובים לפני הזקנים… אבל מותר לפרר הלחם לפני התרנגולים, כיון דכבר נטחן… דאין טוחן אחר טוחן.

  • You may not cut figs, dates or carobs very small "in front of the elderly".
  • The Magen Avraham says it's davka (only for the elderly), but everyone disagrees: it's אורחא דמילתא, just the usual reason you'd cut so small.
  • But bread may be crumbled, for chickens or anyone, because of אין טוחן אחר טוחן.

Heter 4 — The Rashba: grinding סמוך לסעודה

The Rema: וכל זה לא מיירי אלא לחתוך ולהניח, אבל אם לאכלו מיד הכל שרי, מידי דהוי אבורר לאכול מיד דשרי. (תשובת הרשב"א)

The source:

  • In borer (learned in the summer) the Gemara itself permits selecting for immediate consumption, סמוך לסעודה.
  • "To the best of my knowledge, the Rashba is the first one" to say the same applies to tochen. It is not in the Gemara.

What it permits:

  • Even with none of the first three heterim (no shinui, never ground before, never cooked), you may mash a banana in the regular way if you're about to feed it to your baby now.

The Biur Halacha explains why it can even be done with a knife, unlike borer, where immediate selection is only permitted without a kli:

  • דכמו בבורר דרך אכילה הוא… והכא נמי… אפילו כשחותך בסכין לאכול לאלתר דרך אכילה היא, ומשום שאינה מיוחדת לטחינה.
  • Cutting with a knife to eat right away is the way of eating, and a knife isn't designated for grinding.

The Beit Yosef brings the Rashba but adds: if you can cut slightly bigger pieces, it's good, because it's the Rashba's chiddush, not the Gemara's.

Can we rely on it?

Ben Ish Chai ("as far as I remember")

Don't rely on it at all

The Shulchan Aruch doesn't codify everything in the Beit Yosef (mainly what is in the Gemara). The Beit Yosef never said he disagrees, only "if possible, cut bigger". And the Rema brings it as halacha

The practical upshot (Chacham Ovadia):

  • Any crushing, mashing or tochen is permitted סמוך לסעודה: for the meal you are about to eat, "certainly in the next 5–10 minutes" (as in Hilchot Borer).
  • If you can cut bigger pieces, תבוא עליו ברכה. But it's fine me'ikar hadin even if you don't.
  • "Not everyone agrees to this, but there's plenty to rely on."

"Major heter." You wake up on Shabbat and want to mash a banana for the baby: permitted. You can't find a knife to mash the avocado with the back of it, and you want to mix it with oil and eat it now with your crackers or challah: permitted. Preparing in the kitchen the meal you're about to eat in a few minutes: permitted.

The Rema — ממרח doesn't apply to food

There is a melacha of ממרח (spreading, smoothing), but it doesn't apply to foods:

  • you may spread avocado on bread (mash it in a permitted way, then spread);
  • chocolate spread on bread is fine.

The Rabbi's Tochen Summary Sheet

"A document which I composed some time ago, about eight, nine years ago… I just came across it this week, b'siyata dishmaya. It had been buried somewhere for a good few years." (He offered to put it on the group.)

Definition

  • In the Mishkan, after borer the seeds were ground into a fine powder, sieved, made into a paste, baked, and made into a dye.
  • Tochen = grinding, or cutting into minute pieces (דק דק, measure unclear, but it must be very small).
  • It applies to non-edibles too: the Rambam and the Gemara talk about crushing a clod of earth. "We'll talk in a moment about mud on your jacket."
  • Implements: a mincer, chopper, fork or knife is de'oraita when crushing completely.
  • By hand: it is rabbinic unless that is the usual manner.
  • Crushing a clod of soil by hand is the usual way, so de'oraita.
  • Food isn't usually crushed by hand, so mashing a banana by hand to leave for later is a shinui: not de'oraita, but still forbidden.

Practical examples

  • Mashing banana or avocado (for babies or anyone): permitted with the back of a knife. Spreading is fine: no ממרח with food.
  • A very small salad (tomato, cucumber, pepper):
  • peeling must be close to the meal because of borer, preferably with a knife, not a peeler. (Chacham Ovadia allows a peeler, since the peel is גוף אחד, one body with the vegetable, but a knife is better.)
  • You may take the stem off tomatoes.
  • Don't cut too small unless it's right before the meal.
  • A tiny "falafel" salad: cut very close to the meal, preferably a bit larger than usual.
  • Salting cucumbers (from last week): salt together with oil or lemon, or oil first, to avoid the pickling issue.
  • Egg and onion: shell the eggs close to the meal (borer). No tochen with egg, and an egg slicer may be used. Chopping the onion small must be right before the meal.

Seif 13 — Honey from the hive (before lash)

אסור לרדות דבש מן הכוורת… מפני שדומה לתולש.

  • You may not take honey from the honeycomb, because it resembles detaching (kotzer).
  • Kotzer normally means detaching something from its source of growth. Honey doesn't grow in the comb, it was made there, but Chachamim forbade it rabbinically.
  • Honey already out of the comb, or floating on top: permitted.

Part Two — מלאכת לש (Kneading): An Introduction

"One of the most misunderstood melachot"

  • "I think it's one of the most misunderstood melachot, if not the most misunderstood."
  • In the many contemporary books on the melachot, in Hebrew and English, "I have not come across a sefer which properly goes through לש in a really structured manner."
  • The Orchot Shabbat was very good, "probably the best," but it's for Ashkenazim.

What is lash?

  • The tenth melacha. In making the dye: plant, harvest, thresh, select (borer), grind (tochen), and now knead the powder with liquid, then cook it.
  • Definition: gathering together, through a liquid, things that were not gathered before, transforming separate particles into a paste-like, unified mass.
  • The liquid can be anything that binds: water, fruit juice, milk, oil.
  • Lash applies to non-edibles too.

The fundamental machloket: is putting the water in already kneading?

Making challah has two stages:

  1. מגבל / נתינה: putting the ingredients together (water onto flour);
  2. גיבול: kneading it with your hands into dough.

Kneading is clearly lash. Is stage one lash too?

Shabbat 18a: מאן תנא נתינת מים לדיו זו היא שרייתן? אמר רב יוסף: רבי היא, דתניא: אחד נותן את הקמח ואחד נותן את המים, האחרון חייב, דברי רבי. רבי יוסי בר יהודה אומר: אינו חייב עד שיגבל.

Why the halacha doesn't simply follow Rabbi

  • Normally הלכה כרבי מחבירו: Rabbi, the author of the Mishnah, usually prevails.
  • But the Rambam, the Rif and most Rishonim rule like Rabbi Yosi, because of a סתם משנה: נותנין מים למורסן, אבל לא גובלין. You may add water to bran, but not knead it.
  • The anonymous Mishnah is the default position, and הלכה כסתם משנה trumps הלכה כרבי מחבירו.
  • The Rambam: הלש כגרוגרת חייב… המגבל את עפרה זו היא לישה. The melacha is the kneading.
  • Other Rishonim, such as the Semag, the Ba'al HaTerumah and Rabbi Baruch of Mainz: halacha follows Rabbi, despite the stam Mishnah.

How the Shulchan Aruch rules

Ruling

Shulchan Aruch (here in 321 and in 324:3)

סתם like the Rambam and Rif: lash is the kneading. Then a יש אומרים: the Ba'al HaTerumah and Semag

Rema

We are machmir like the Ba'al HaTerumah and Semag. Ashkenazim: even the mingling is a problem

Ben Ish Chai, Kaf HaChaim, Or LeTzion (Rav Ben Zion Abba Shaul)

Since Maran brought the second opinion in both places, one should try to be machmir and treat the mingling as a problem

Chacham Ovadia (following the Chida)

A סתם ויש means the halacha is like the סתם. The יש אומרים is brought משום כבודו, so you know there's another opinion: "you don't need to follow it." The mingling is not the problem; the kneading is

On the "rules" of reading the Shulchan Aruch: "The honest truth is, I believe all of these rules are not really rules. For every rule… you have rules which go against it." The Shulchan Aruch's purpose was to summarise the Beit Yosef: "this is the main opinion, but you should know there is a second opinion."

The shiur proceeds like Chacham Ovadia, the Rambam and the Rif: the melacha is the kneading.

Crucial caveat: when mixing is the kneading

"Extremely important caveat. This is something people don't know. I've seen people do this on Shabbos, and I think it's a melacha d'oraita."

Some mixtures bind by themselves the moment you combine them. Then even Rabbi Yosi agrees you're chayav with the mixing itself. "Not begins: happens!"

Weetabix with a little milk ("Again, I haven't seen anyone who discusses this, but לעניות דעתי I think this is pashut"):

  • With lots of milk, "that's less of an issue. Let's talk about that later."
  • But some like their Weetabix "very stodgy" and pour in a bit of milk. The milk is completely absorbed and binds the crumbly Weetabix into one unified mass.
  • The pouring itself is the lash.

The source, Siman 340: הנותן זרע פשתן או שומשמין במים חייב משום לש, מפני שמתערבים ונתלים זה בזה.

  • Sesame or flax seeds put into water mix and cling together by themselves, so putting them in is chayav even according to Rabbi Yosi.

Summary

Tochen: five heterim

  1. A שינוי גדול: the back of a knife or the underside of a cup, onto a chopping board, never a mortar.
  2. אין טוחן אחר טוחן: bread, crackers, sugar cubes, tablets, snuff.
  3. Already edible and not from the ground:
  4. cooked meat and chicken;
  5. fish even raw;
  6. cheese, eggs and liver outright.
  7. סמוך לסעודה (the Rashba, via the Rema): Chacham Ovadia permits it me'ikar hadin, preferably cutting a little bigger; the Ben Ish Chai doesn't rely on it.
  8. Mostly crushed before Shabbat (only שחיקה lacking): finish it.

Tochen: always

  • No specialised utensils: graters, mincers, crushers are muktzeh. Slicers are fine.
  • Raw meat may not be cut very fine.
  • ממרח doesn't apply to food.
  • Mud may be brushed off the body, and off clothes through a non-Jew.

Lash

  • Gathering separate particles into one mass through a liquid.
  • Rabbi vs Rabbi Yosi bar Rabbi Yehuda:
  • Maran and Chacham Ovadia: only the kneading is lash.
  • Ashkenazim (the Rema) and some Sefardi poskim: even the mingling.
  • But if the mixing itself binds (Weetabix with a little milk, sesame in water), the pouring is lash according to all.
  • בלילה עבה = de'oraita; בלילה רכה = derabbanan; no mixture = mutar.
