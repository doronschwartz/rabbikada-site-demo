Topic: When Asarah BeTevet falls on Friday: may one make Kiddush before nightfall?

Sources: The Gemara ("mitaanin umashlimin"); the Rosh; the Maharam of Rothenburg; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Asarah BeTevet

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A fast begins at dawn and ends at nightfall; from sunset it is a doubt, so we fast until night. The Gemara says a fast not completed is not counted.
  • When the fast falls on Friday (the last time was three years earlier), may one eat once Shabbat is accepted, before nightfall (about 4:40 in London)?
  • The Gemara says "one fasts and completes". The Rosh, the Maharam of Rothenburg and other Ashkenazi Rishonim hold that once Shabbat is accepted the fast is over. The Maharam once prayed Minhah and Kabbalat Shabbat early and ate before sunset. On this view the Gemara only allows continuing the fast into Shabbat for one who wants to.
  • Other Rishonim read it as an obligation to complete the fast. The Shulhan Arukh rules this way: even after accepting Shabbat, wait until nightfall for Kiddush.
  • Since many Rishonim permit it, and after sunset it is already a doubt, anyone with a slight headache or weakness, even short of the usual grounds to break a fast, has much to rely on to eat after sunset once Shabbat is accepted.
  • This applies to a public fast. For a private fast on Friday, such as on a parent's anniversary, one may rely on the lenient view and eat once Shabbat is accepted.

In one line

On Asarah BeTevet on a Friday, wait for nightfall before Kiddush, but with any need one may eat after sunset once Shabbat is accepted; for a private fast this is permitted outright.
