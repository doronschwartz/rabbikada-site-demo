Where we are

The Rabbi opened Siman 6, which is about the women a Kohen may not marry and the law of a Kohen's wife (eighteen seifim). He said Seif 1 is a very important and practical seif, and it touches on civil marriage. He planned to go through the sources and then the later authorities. The class covered Seifim 1 to 4.

Seif 1: the women forbidden to a Kohen

A Kohen may not marry a gerushah, a zonah or a halalah (forbidden by the Torah), nor a halusah (forbidden derabbanan, as the Gemara in Yevamot says).

  • Gerushah: a divorcee, including a woman divorced from betrothal only (erusin).
  • Zonah: halachically, a woman who had relations with a man forbidden to her (nivlah lepasul), such as a non-Jew. A woman who has relations with single Jewish men outside marriage is not a halachic zonah, however the word is used today.
  • Halalah: a daughter born from a union a Kohen was not allowed to make, for example a Kohen who married a gerushah and had a girl.
  • Halusah: forbidden derabbanan.

Doubtful cases (safek). A safek gerushah, safek zonah or safek halalah is a doubt about a Torah prohibition, so if one married her he must divorce her. For a safek halusah, the Shulhan Arukh says one should not marry her in the first place, but if he did, he may stay with her. The Ramban's reason is that the halisah was done in Beit Din, an act of the Beit Din, so even a doubt there is treated with caution. The Rabbi noted that the children of a safek halusah are kosher, because we cannot say he may stay with her and then penalize the children (the Rambam). For a definite halusah, a gerushah or a zonah, the children are halalim.

Reah haget: a get that only releases her from him

This is the practical part. The Gemara in Yevamot teaches that a reah haget (a "whiff of a get") makes a woman forbidden to a Kohen. Two points:

  1. What it is. A reah haget is where a husband divorces a woman from himself only and not from other men. It is not a valid get and she cannot remarry with it, but it still creates the status of a gerushah, and she is disqualified from marrying a Kohen.
  2. A further step, from the Rishonim. The Rema quotes the Rashba: if there was a rumour (kol) that she had been betrothed, and the husband gave a get out of caution (humra), that also creates a reah haget and she is disqualified, even though in truth the child would not be a mamzer.

Get al tenai. If a husband gives a conditional get and she never fulfilled the condition, does that create a reah haget? Beit Shammai says yes and Beit Hillel says no. We rule like Beit Hillel, so a conditional get is not a reah haget. If the husband then dies she is a widow and not an agunah, and a Kohen may marry her. A Kohen may even give a conditional get to begin with.

What comes out of all this. One may not marry a definite gerushah, zonah or halalah, nor a doubtful one, and one must divorce in all these cases. A gerushah includes one divorced from erusin. A safek halusah may not be married at the outset but may be kept.

The question of civil marriage

The Rabbi explained why the issue of kol matters so much. The question is: what if a woman had only a civil marriage, never a halachic kiddushin, and afterwards received a get "as a humra"? Can she then marry a Kohen?

Based on what we have learned so far, it looks as though she should not be able to. The Rabbi said that by strict law no get was needed, and if she had married without one the children would be fully kosher. But since she received a get, prima facie there is a reah haget, and that should forbid her to a Kohen.

He then went through the answer in stages.

The Beit Shemuel and the Beit Yoseph. They resolve an apparent contradiction in the Rashba with a distinction: - If there is a real safek whether kiddushin took place, and a get was given out of caution, the get does create a reah haget. We are not sure what happened, so the get is treated as a get. - If we know what happened, and halachically and in fact there was no kiddushin (for example, one witness is dead and the other denies it, so there is no hashash), a get given anyway adds nothing. We know the reality, so it is only a precaution and does not create a reah haget.

Applying it. In a civil marriage, the Rabbi said, we know as a fact that no kiddushin happened, so no one suspects that a ring was given in a halachic way. The get is given only because of a separate caution (humrah) about whether it could look like an eshet ish matter. So it does not fall under the Rashba's reah haget. According to Rav Ovadia Yoseph, as the Rabbi presented his conclusion, a woman in that position may marry a Kohen.

A practical point. A student asked whether in such a case it would be better not to give a get, to keep options open for a Kohen. The Rabbi said he would not tell anyone to leave out a get for that reason. If there is a real humrah of eshet ish, the get is given. A case where she already has a Kohen lined up is a different question.

Pithei Teshuvah on Seif 1

  • A Kohen who says he is not really a Kohen. The Pithei Teshuvah deals with a man who has lived as a Kohen for many years (called up first, receiving the Kohen's privileges) and then, wanting to marry a gerushah, claims he was only pretending. The Pithei Teshuvah says the answer is simple: even thirty days of acting as a Kohen is a hazakah, so fifteen years certainly is. A claim after the fact does not help. Without clear evidence he remains a Kohen and may not marry a gerushah. This fits the lesson from Siman 3 on Kohanim today: we may add other evidence, but a person who has lived as a Kohen stays one.
  • The woman's own prohibition. The Radbaz and the Hida say that the prohibition applies equally to the woman: both she and the Kohen commit an offence each time, and both receive malkot.
  • No "safek Kohanim" today. The Rabbi told the class to put out of their minds the idea that the general doubt about Kohanim today helps a Kohen who marries a safek gerushah. He said we concluded in Siman 3 that it may be combined with other doubts, but by itself it is never a reason. The Pithei Teshuvah brings a case of a woman who may have been a captive (shevuyah), a doubt on top of a doubt, and still says it is not enough: Hazal fixed some prohibitions that began from a doubt and made them definite, like kosher cheese mixed with non-kosher cheese. So if one marries a gerushah or even a safek gerushah, he must divorce her, unless there is a specific separate doubt about him.

Seif 2: a young girl and miun

A girl whose father has died, under 12, may be married off by her mother or brother (a rabbinic marriage). She may refuse (miun) before age 12. - If she did miun, she is permitted to a Kohen, because miun shows there was never a real kiddushin. - If instead she waited for a get, the get shows she accepted the marriage, and she is forbidden to a Kohen by Torah law. - A woman who does miun to a second husband only in order to return to her first husband may not go back to him. The Rabbi said the Beit Yoseph limits this to returning to the first husband, not a new Kohen; the Bah is strict, but the Beit Shemuel permits, and if she married she may stay. The transcript of this section is hard to follow, so these notes give only the main point: miun generally frees her, a get is a problem.

Seifim 3 and 4: rumours about a get

Seif 3. A Kohen and his wife are married, and a rumour spreads that he divorced her. We do not ask them to divorce, as far as the marriage they are in. The concern is only for the future: if he dies, she may not marry a second Kohen. The Helkat Mehokek and the Beit Shemuel ask: if she must leave a second husband because of the rumour, are the children of the first marriage halalim (children of a gerushah)? They answer that we do not worry, because we say he divorced her just before he died, so the problem is only for the second husband.

Seif 4. A rumour that "Ploni the Kohen wrote a get for his wife". If in that place people use "wrote" to mean "gave", it is a concern for a future marriage. If that is rare, there is no problem. A concern needs both the rumour and the local usage. The Rambam does not make the distinction, but the Shulhan Arukh does not follow him, because the Gemara and the other Rishonim require both. In all cases the couple may stay together; it applies only to a second husband.

He said the next class would deal with the definition of a zonah and related questions.

Practical summary

  • A Kohen may not marry a gerushah, zonah or halalah, and may not marry a halusah (rabbinic). If he marries a doubtful gerushah, zonah or halalah he must divorce her; for a safek halusah he may stay.
  • A reah haget (a get releasing her from him only) disqualifies a woman for a Kohen; a conditional get (get al tenai) does not.
  • A precautionary get after a civil marriage, where we know no kiddushin took place, does not create a reah haget according to the Beit Shemuel's distinction, so she may marry a Kohen. The Rabbi did not suggest leaving out a get in order to keep that option open.
  • A man who has lived as a Kohen for years cannot cancel it by saying he was never a Kohen.
  • The general doubt about Kohanim today does not by itself permit marrying a doubtful gerushah.
  • A rumour that a Kohen divorced his wife does not force the couple to separate, and matters only for a future marriage after his death.
