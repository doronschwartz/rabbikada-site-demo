Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 294, seifim 1-5; 295 Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 14 March 2024

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

Why two Havdalot?

Siman 294 covers Havdalah in the Amidah; 295 and 296 cover Havdalah on the cup. Why do we have both?

The Gemara explains that the Sages first instituted Havdalah on wine, like Kiddush. When people became too poor to afford wine for both Friday and Saturday night, they moved Havdalah into the Amidah, as Atah Honantanu. When times improved they restored it to the cup, but people were already used to saying it in prayer, so both remained. The main misvah is one, ideally on the cup.

Atah Honantanu is still useful:

  • If someone has no wine or local beverage, Atah Honantanu fulfils Havdalah.
  • It permits melakhah before Havdalah on the cup, for example driving home from shul.

"Barukh HaMavdil bein kodesh lehol," said without Hashem's name, is a later, shortened Havdalah, which arose for women who had not prayed Arvit and wanted to do melakhah before their husbands came home. Men should rely on Atah Honantanu or Havdalah on the cup before doing melakhah, and use "Barukh HaMavdil" only if they have no choice. Someone who makes Havdalah on the cup at home before shul, so as to drive, has done the real Havdalah.

Besamim and fire are not Havdalah. Havdalah is "Barukh... HaMavdil bein kodesh lehol", on wine. The blessings on spices and fire were instituted for Saturday night and added to the same cup. If they are not at hand, there is no need to find them.

Is Havdalah from the Torah? The Rambam holds Havdalah has the same status as Kiddush: "Zakhor et yom HaShabbat" means remembering Shabbat as it comes in and as it goes out. This applies to the verbal distinction, such as Atah Honantanu, not to the cup. Others say it is rabbinic, but the consensus seems to follow the Rambam. People often take Kiddush more seriously than Havdalah, but they are equivalent.

Atah Honantanu in the Amidah

Seif 1: Havdalah is inserted in "Atah honen", the blessing of understanding. If forgotten, one does not repeat, since Havdalah will be made on the cup. But if one forgot it and also ate before Havdalah, one must repeat.

Why in "Atah honen"? The Gemara: distinguishing needs understanding. The Yerushalmi: it is the first request in the Amidah, and before asking for anything we must first separate from Shabbat.

The Mishnah Berurah adds that even someone who already made Havdalah on the cup must still say Atah Honantanu in Arvit.

If forgotten: once you have said "Barukh Atah HaShem" at the end of the blessing, continue. Before that, go back and add it.

Arvit missed altogether. Someone who missed Arvit prays Shaharit twice on Sunday morning. The poskim say he does not add Atah Honantanu in the second Amidah, since the Sages' original institution was only on Saturday night. But if he also did not make Havdalah on the cup on Saturday night, he should add it. Sefaradim make Havdalah on Sunday morning without Hashem's name in the closing blessing, since it is unclear that Havdalah still applies then. So in that case Atah Honantanu is the more certain Havdalah. "You've got till Tuesday" is not straightforward.

The double error

The Gemara: forgetting Atah Honantanu alone is not repeated, and eating before Havdalah alone is wrong but needs only Havdalah. If someone both forgot Atah Honantanu and ate before Havdalah, the Sages penalise him: he must repeat Arvit with Atah Honantanu. The Rabbi's explanation is that Atah Honantanu had become less important once Havdalah went back to the cup, and the Sages wanted to stop people neglecting it.

A question raised in class: if he said "Barukh HaMavdil" before eating, is it still a double error? The Rabbi's view was that it probably still counts, since "Barukh HaMavdil" has only a limited function.

The Biur Halacha brings the Rashba: forgetting Atah Honantanu and then doing melakhah before Havdalah is also a double error. In practice we do not go back for this, because of the doubts involved.

Asked whether repeating the Amidah might be a blessing in vain, he answered that the Sages' institution makes it a new, valid prayer.

No wine for the cup

Seif 2: if he forgot Atah Honantanu and has no wine or local beverage for the cup that night, and does not expect any the next day, he must repeat Arvit.

Seif 3: if Tishah BeAv falls on Sunday, he does not repeat, since he will make Havdalah on Sunday night.

The Rabbi asked: what does "tomorrow" mean? Havdalah on the cup works either until Sunday morning or until Tuesday. The Biur Halacha brings the Hida: expecting wine on Sunday can be relied on, but not a delivery on Tuesday. The Rabbi explained the contrast with Tishah BeAv. There, there is no choice, so we rely on Havdalah on Sunday night. Normally, we allow a delay of up to about 24 hours, but not until Sunday night.

If he remembers during the Amidah, before Shomea Tefillah, he may add it in Shomea Tefillah, where any request may be added.

Seifim 4-5: where he need not go back, if he has already said "Barukh Atah HaShem" he may not go back. He may repeat the Amidah afterwards as a voluntary prayer, a tefillat nedavah, if he wishes. He cannot turn half an obligatory prayer into a voluntary one, so he finishes and then prays again. The nedavah must be intended as a gift, not as an obligation, and should include something new.

The poskim advise that if he forgot Atah Honantanu and expects the cup only much later, after several hours of work, writing or cooking, it is better to pray Arvit again as a nedavah with Atah Honantanu than to rely all evening on "Barukh HaMavdil."

May he eat meanwhile? Asked about someone with no wine until the next afternoon, the Rabbi recalled that Rav Ovadia Yosef addresses this: if he will have it that night he should wait; if only the next day, he may eat that night. "You have to use a bit of common sense."

Siman 295: VeAtah Kadosh, Vihi Noam and Havdalah in shul

The Rema: after Arvit say Vihi Noam and VeAtah Kadosh slowly, because the wicked are returning to Gehinnom. The Rabbi noted that the souls seem to return at several stops: Vehu rahum, Barekhu and now VeAtah Kadosh. "We shouldn't speculate about these things." The Shulhan Arukh itself does not mention this. Vihi Noam is simply a good start to the working week.

When Yom Tov falls during the coming week, Ashkenazim and Western Sefaradim (Moroccans, Spanish and Portuguese) omit Vihi Noam, since it refers to six days of work. Eastern Sefaradim always say it, following the Kabbalah.

Havdalah is made in shul for those without wine. Since it is unclear whether women can make Havdalah for themselves, a man going home to make Havdalah for his family should listen in shul with the intention not to fulfil the misvah, so he can still make it at home.

The Rema: we mention Eliyahu HaNavi on Saturday night, since Mashiah does not come on Erev Shabbat or Shabbat; now we await him again.

Practical summary

  • Say Atah Honantanu in Arvit even if you already made Havdalah on the cup.
  • If you forgot it, do not repeat, unless you also ate before Havdalah, or you will have no cup until after Sunday.
  • Before Havdalah, rely on Atah Honantanu or Havdalah on the cup for melakhah; "Barukh HaMavdil" is a last resort.
  • If you want to repeat when not required, finish the Amidah and pray again as a nedavah.
  • A man who will make Havdalah at home for his family should not intend to fulfil it in shul.
