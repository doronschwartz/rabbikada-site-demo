Topic: Does staying silent when one's lineage is attacked count as an admission?

Sources: Gemara Ketubot; Shulhan Arukh; the Rambam; the Raavad

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 24 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A Gemara in Ketubot discusses someone accused of being a mamzer, a halal or not Jewish, who stays silent. Is silence an admission that requires the family to be checked before marriage?
  • Of three opinions, all the Rishonim follow the Tanna Kamma: we must be concerned that silence shows admission, and the family needs checking. This is codified in the Shulhan Arukh.
  • This sits in tension with the famous Gemara praising one who stays silent when insulted. Also, does it still apply today? The Rishonim limit it in five ways, so in practice it rarely applies.
  • First, the Rambam: silence in an argument is praised, but not when one's lineage is attacked, which is very important and must be objected to. Even so, an investigation going back about four generations that finds nothing resolves it.
  • Second, the Raavad, Rabbi Avraham ben David of Provence: this applied only when Batei Din took such allegations seriously, so failing to take the accuser to court implied admission. In his day, 800 to 900 years ago, silence in an argument is on the contrary a good sign.
  • But even for the Raavad, if someone says it outside an argument, one must stand up and object.
  • More limitations follow next time.

In one line

Silence in the face of an attack on one's lineage can raise concern, but the Rambam and Raavad greatly limit this.
