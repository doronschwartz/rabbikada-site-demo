Topic: How visitors keeping two days in Israel should conduct themselves in public

Sources: Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the Bet Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Friday 18 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Even Hakham Ovadiah, who allows asking an Israeli to do melakhah even for a non-misvah purpose, does not permit a visitor to use public transport or a taxi on his second day. Even if a Jewish driver takes him somewhere for a misvah, or the bus is prepaid, sitting in the car or bus is weekday activity and should be avoided.
  • Those keeping two days in Israel pray the Yom Tov prayers, do not wear tefillin, have the Yom Tov meals and do no melakhah all day.
  • The Bet Yoseph asks how visitors may make a minyan (for example ten people in a hotel) and take out a Sefer Torah while it is a weekday for everyone around, which seems to depart from the minhag of the place.
  • The answer: the Hakhamim of Eres Yisrael accepted and encouraged this, so that visitors could keep Yom Tov properly with public prayer and gatherings rather than praying alone. That became the accepted minhag.
  • If nine visitors cannot find a tenth, they should not ask an Israeli to complete the minyan for the Yom Tov prayers and Sefer Torah, since he is not obligated in them. Ten people actually keeping the second day are needed.

Sources quoted

  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: even on the lenient view, no taxis or public transport.
  • The Bet Yoseph: the question of a public Yom Tov minyan in Israel, resolved by the accepted practice of the Hakhamim of Eres Yisrael.

In one line

Visitors may keep the second day fully with their own minyan of ten who keep it, but should avoid taxis and public transport.
