Topic: Calling the Kohen and the Levi: a Kohen who is praying, a Kohen who walks in late, and the order when there is no Kohen or no Levi Source: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Siman 135:5-8, with the Rema and the Mishnah Berurah Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada at the Thursday Shulhan Arukh class, Tiferet Eyal, Thursday 7 July 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Where we are

The class continued Siman 135, the order of the aliyot. The Rabbi again stressed why this matters: these are questions "which you need to answer on the spur of the moment", while the whole congregation is waiting.

The previous week covered a synagogue that missed a Shabbat reading (it catches up the following week), the order of Kohen, Levi and Israel, and the custom that a Kohen is called before a talmid hakham, even though the Rambam and others put the hakham first.

A Kohen in the middle of prayer

You take out the Torah, look round, and see the only Kohen in the middle of Shema. The Shulhan Arukh says you do not call him. You call an Israel instead, and the Kohen does not need to leave the room. Normally, calling someone else while a Kohen is present makes people say he is not a real Kohen. Here everyone can see he is busy praying, so no one will think that.

The Mishnah Berurah adds that "Shema" includes its blessings, such as Yoser Or. The Rabbi summarised:

Someone asked about Shabbat, when a child may be one of the seven: if the adult Kohen is in Shema, should a young Kohen be called? The Rabbi said he could argue it both ways. Since our custom is not really to call children, the adult Kohen may have no real replacement; but one could also say that where a qualified Kohen is present, use him.

Do not keep the congregation waiting

What if the gabbai waits for the end of a paragraph and calls the Kohen, but the Kohen does not know the halacha and insists on finishing his Shema first? The Rabbi said it is not right to make everyone wait, but if insisting will cause a fight, let him finish. "Always better, shalom."

Why is a short delay such a big deal? "Other people's time is precious. You can play around as much as you want with your own time. You can't play around with other people's time." Someone compared it to waiting for an extra Kaddish after a shiur. The Rabbi answered that a person who is free to leave may leave, and that a Kaddish "comes nowhere near" learning Torah. "Be desperate to hear some words of Torah."

The Kohen who walks in late

There was no Kohen, so an Israel was called up for the first aliyah. Then the Kohen walks in.

  • If the Israel has already said "Barukh atah Hashem" of the first berakhah, it is too late. The Kohen has missed his chance and the Israel continues.
  • If he has only said Barekhu and the congregation answered, he has not really started. Barekhu is only an introduction. The Kohen is called by name, then the Levi, and the Israel stays at the tevah and takes the third aliyah, so that he is not embarrassed.

Saying Barekhu again is no problem, the Mishnah Berurah explains, because unlike Kedushah or Kaddish it is not tied to a particular place in the prayers.

Someone asked about hinting to a Kohen that he might leave the room so that more people can be called on a weekday. The Rabbi said he never tells a Kohen to leave, "has veshalom". When, for example, there are two yahrzeits, he simply mentions to the Kohen that there are two yahrzeits today, and the Kohen understands. Then the question becomes when he may come back in.

When there is no Kohen

When there is no Kohen at all, the first aliyah may go to a Levi or to an Israel. The Levi's right comes only after a Kohen. Without a Kohen he has no more right than an Israel, but nothing stops him from taking the first aliyah. Someone mentioned that a Levi had been called first by mistake a couple of weeks earlier. "Absolutely fine," said the Rabbi.

What you cannot do:

  • Israel first, then Levi second. People would think the first was a Kohen. An Israel second and a Levi third is fine.
  • Kohen after Kohen, or Levi after Levi. People would say the first one was not good enough. This is a law of the Gemara, and Ashkenazim do not do it either.

Why would people suspect that? A Levi loses his status only if his father married a woman forbidden to him, such as a mamzeret or a netinah. A Kohen loses it far more easily, for example if his father married a divorcee. "Half of the halachot in this chapter are all about what people say," said the Rabbi. "The rabbis took very seriously what people say."

Once the order of Kohen, Levi, Israel is complete, Sefaradim may call any combination afterwards, as long as no Kohen follows a Kohen and no Levi follows a Levi. The Ashkenazi practice is different, as the class would see.

Someone asked whether a father and son may be called one after the other. The Rabbi explained that this is not a law at all. It is a concern for the evil eye, and good practice not to do it. If the gabbai called the son by name, he must go up.

A Kohen called by mistake for the Levi

The Kohen had his aliyah. For the second aliyah the gabbai, not seeing a Levi, calls another Kohen, and he begins. Then the Levi says: "I'm here."

As with the late Kohen: if the second Kohen has only said Barekhu, stop him and call the Levi. If he has already begun the berakhah, he continues.

But there is a difference. When the Israel was stopped for the late Kohen, he had to be given the third aliyah so as not to be embarrassed. The Kohen stopped here gets nothing. "He did the first one already. This was a bonus." Taking away someone's bonus is not like taking away his salary, and the aliyah rightfully belongs to the Levi.

If there is no Levi at all, the same Kohen who had the first aliyah is called again for the second. Calling a different Kohen would suggest the first was flawed.

Big synagogues

Why such worry about what people think, when everyone in a small community knows who is who? Someone suggested the answer, and the Rabbi agreed: think of the huge synagogues of the Gemara's time, where a cloth was waved to show the crowd when to answer Amen, or of the readings held in the marketplaces. Nobody knew the people being called; they only saw the order, and assumed the first was a Kohen and the second a Levi. The same is true today when someone new comes to town.

Practical summary

  • Do not call a Kohen who is in the middle of the Amidah, or in the middle of a paragraph of Shema or its blessings; call an Israel instead. Between paragraphs, call him if there is no other Kohen.
  • A Kohen who walks in after the Israel said "Barukh atah Hashem" has lost the aliyah. If the Israel only said Barekhu, call the Kohen and Levi, and give that Israel the third aliyah.
  • With no Kohen, a Levi or an Israel may take the first aliyah, but never Israel first and Levi second.
  • Never call Kohen after Kohen or Levi after Levi. With no Levi, the first Kohen is called again.
  • Do not make the congregation wait, but where insisting would cause a fight, keep the peace.
