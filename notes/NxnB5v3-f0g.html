Topic: The berakhah on a rainbow, not gazing at it, and telling others

Sources: The Gemara; the Torah; Ibn Ezra; Rav Saadiah Gaon; Ramban; the Ben Ish Hai; Yehezkel; the Hayei Adam (Rabbi Avraham Danzig); the Mishnah Berurah

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Wednesday 12 February 2025

The halacha

  • On a rainbow the Gemara gives "Zokher HaBerit veneeman bivrito vekayam bemaamaro": Hashem remembers the covenant not to destroy the world, keeps it, and His word alone would suffice even without the sign.
  • Unlike the shared choice of berakhot on seas, storms and comets, the Hakhamim gave the rainbow its own berakhah, showing its importance.
  • Ibn Ezra held the rainbow was new after the flood; Rav Saadiah Gaon, Ramban and others held it always existed and Hashem made it the sign of the covenant.
  • Science explains it by refraction, but so too thunder, lightning and comets; we still thank Hashem, who stands behind the conditions that produce it.
  • One posek some 350 to 400 years ago claimed there are two kinds of rainbow, natural and a sign of intended destruction, without explaining how to tell them apart. The Ben Ish Hai considered saying the berakhah without Hashem's name because of this, but all the poskim reject it, and one says the full berakhah.
  • The Gemara says not to gaze at a rainbow, citing a verse in Yehezkel: it is a kind of manifestation of God's glory, so gazing lacks reverence. It is not a bad omen.
  • Sepharadim may and should tell others so they can bless. The Hayei Adam, about 250 years ago, said not to, as it spreads bad news; the Mishnah Berurah records this and some Ashkenazim follow it, but Sepharadim never accepted it.

In one line

On a rainbow say "Zokher HaBerit" in full, do not gaze at it, and Sepharadim may tell others so they too can bless.
