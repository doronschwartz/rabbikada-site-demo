Topic: How the enactment of Rabbenu Gershom affects a wife who refuses a get today

Sources: Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Wednesday 18 February 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • From the Torah a man can divorce his wife without her consent, but a separate enactment forbids divorcing a woman against her will. Today, among Ashkenazim and Sepharadim alike, a Beth Din will not facilitate a get without her consent.
  • Besides husbands who refuse to give a get, it is quite common, almost equally so by statistics in Israel, for a wife to refuse to receive one. He has divorced civilly and wants to move on, but halachically he is still married.
  • Ashkenazim, because of Rabbenu Gershom, allow him to remarry only through the long process of a Heter Meah Rabbanim.
  • The Shulhan Arukh never accepted the Takkanah in Sepharadi countries, and held its time frame has passed even for Ashkenazim. In practice we still say not to marry two wives.
  • Sepharadim may therefore rely on the basic law (ikkar hadin) without the 100 rabbis: once the husband and the Beth Din have exhausted every option and the couple are civilly divorced, the Beth Din can permit a second marriage. It can never sanction bigamy that is illegal in secular law.
  • First he must have the get written and appoint an agent (shaliah) to deliver it. The get waits in the Beth Din's files until she is ready, a month or years later, which is for her own benefit.

In one line

A thousand years on, Rabbenu Gershom still matters: Ashkenazim need a Heter Meah Rabbanim when a wife refuses a get, while Sepharadim can be permitted more easily once the get is deposited.
