Topic: Funerals on Hol HaMoed, and whether mourners tear keriah then

Sources: The Gemara; the Rishonim; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; the Kaf HaHayim

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Friday 14 November 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Anything for the needs and dignity of the deceased (kevod hamet) may be done on Hol HaMoed, even melekhet uman: digging a grave, taharah and the rest. Funerals take place on Hol HaMoed and should not be delayed.
  • The seven days of mourning begin only after the festival. One bereaved on the first day of Pesah or Sukkot has the funeral on Hol HaMoed, must somehow return to the simhah of the festival, and sits shivah only afterwards, which is very difficult.
  • Whether mourners tear keriah on Hol HaMoed is a dispute among the Rishonim, based on the Gemara. The Shulhan Arukh rules that keriah is done for all seven relatives.
  • The Rema writes that the Ashkenazi minhag is to tear on Hol HaMoed only for a father or mother, and for other relatives after the festival.
  • Mourning practice is largely minhag. Even among Sepharadim the minhag was not always like the Shulhan Arukh: in Yerushalayim it was only for father and mother, and the Kaf HaHayim writes the same of Izmir.
  • Where there is no known minhag, the default, as even the Rema writes, is keriah for all seven relatives. So: follow a known minhag; otherwise Sepharadim tear for all relatives, Ashkenazim for father and mother only.
  • This concludes the halachot of Yom Tov, Yom Tov Sheni, travellers and Hol HaMoed; a new topic follows.

In one line

Funerals proceed on Hol HaMoed with shivah afterwards, and keriah is torn then for all relatives by Sepharadim and for parents only by Ashkenazim, unless one's minhag differs.
