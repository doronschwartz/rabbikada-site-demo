Topic: Whether lighting Shabbat candles counts as accepting Shabbat

Sources: One of the Geonim; the Gemara; Shulhan Arukh (Hilchot Shabbat and Hilchot Hanukkah); Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Wednesday 2 June 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • One of the Geonim, over 1,200 years ago, held that lighting Shabbat candles is an explicit acceptance of Shabbat, since their purpose is to begin Shabbat, so the woman may no longer work even while the sun is out.
  • Most Rishonim disagreed, bringing proofs from the Gemara that people lit candles and still worked afterwards.
  • The Shulhan Arukh brings both opinions, and the rule is that the halacha follows the second. So for Sepharadim, strictly, lighting candles is not accepting Shabbat; she may still eat, drink and work until Shabbat begins.
  • Yet in Hilchot Hanukkah the Shulhan Arukh says to light Hanukkah candles before Shabbat candles, implying one cannot after. So the poskim say it is better to avoid work after lighting, relying on the main halacha only for something important.
  • Ashkenazim rule that lighting the candles is accepting Shabbat.
  • For both Ashkenazim and Sepharadim, a woman who stipulates (a tenai) that she lights without accepting Shabbat may continue working. Hakham Ovadiah rules she can stipulate once a year that this applies every time she lights.

In one line

For Sepharadim lighting candles does not strictly accept Shabbat, but a once-a-year stipulation lets any woman work freely after lighting.
