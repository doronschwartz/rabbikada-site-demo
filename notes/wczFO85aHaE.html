Topic: The berakhah "shenatan mehokhmato levasar vadam" on seeing an outstanding non-Jewish scholar

Sources: The Gemara; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Rishonim and Aharonim; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Tuesday 28 January 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The same Gemara says that on seeing an exceptionally wise person of the nations one blesses "shenatan mehokhmato levasar vadam", who gave of His wisdom to flesh and blood. The Rambam and Shulhan Arukh codify it.
  • Unlike the berakhah on Torah sages, "to those who fear Him", here wisdom alone suffices; character and fear of God are not part of it.
  • The Shulhan Arukh, following some Rishonim, adds that it is for proficiency in the sciences and disciplines of the world, not for the greatest theologian of another religion.
  • On that basis one might think of figures like Albert Einstein or Stephen Hawking, but there are problems.
  • Some Aharonim, brought by Hakham Ovadiah, say it is logical that he must at least keep the seven Noahide laws and believe in God; one who publicly rejects one of them does not qualify. So according to some poskim a blessing on an atheist such as Hawking is problematic and better avoided.
  • Some poskim, Hakham Ovadiah among them, hold this berakhah is only for non-Jews, since a Jew's wisdom is the wisdom of Torah, so it is not said on a Jewish scholar, however brilliant.
  • So today it is limited: a world-renowned pioneer in a science, a believer who keeps the seven Noahide laws, and not Jewish. If one met such a person, one would seemingly say it, as in the Gemara, Rambam and Shulhan Arukh.

In one line

The berakhah on a non-Jewish scholar applies to a world-renowned scientist who believes in God and keeps the Noahide laws, which makes it rare today.
