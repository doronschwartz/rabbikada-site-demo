Topic: Maaser on the profit from a sold asset, on inheritance, and on gifts for a purpose

Sources: Rav Moshe Feinstein; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Tuesday 8 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • An asset's rise in value needs no maaser until it is sold and a profit realised: bought for 1,000 pounds and sold for 2,000, the 1,000 profit would seemingly need 100.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein was asked 51 years ago by a rabbi in England about property that had doubled in value. He ruled that inflation must also be taken into account.
  • So if 2,000 pounds today equals 1,500 pounds when the asset was bought, the real profit is 500 and the maaser is 50, not 100. The calculation is difficult, but some assessment of inflation is needed.
  • Maaser applies to inheritance. It is not a hovat hefsa, an obligation on the object, so even if the father took maaser on it, the heir must tithe afresh.
  • Money given for a specific purpose needs no maaser: a parent paying for a flight to visit, or a grandparent giving money specifically for things for a new baby.

In one line

Maaser on a sale is taken from the real profit after inflation, inheritance is tithed afresh, and gifts for a set purpose are exempt.
