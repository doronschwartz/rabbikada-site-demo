Topic: The status of children of converts regarding marrying a mamzer

Sources: The Rambam; the Gemara; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 9 June 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The permission to marry a mamzer applies to one who converted. The halacha changes for children of converts.
  • A regular Jew who marries a giyoret: the child follows the father, as in any marriage, and is a regular Israel. He may not marry a mamzeret, and a daughter may marry a Kohen.
  • The Rambam rules, and this is the halacha, though it raises a question he does not go into, that the same applies when a male ger marries a regular Jewess: the child is a regular Israel.
  • When a ger marries a giyoret, which is common, the child is fully Jewish but has the lineage of a ger, and may marry a mamzer or mamzeret. This continues if that child also marries a convert or a child of converts.
  • Strictly it could continue for many generations, but eventually people forget the convert origin and would think a regular Jew is marrying a mamzer.
  • The Gemara gives two opinions on how long it lasts: up to 10 generations, or until it is generally forgotten that they are children of converts. The Rambam takes the second view, and the Shulhan Arukh brings both.

Sources quoted

  • The Rambam: a ger who marries a Jewess has a regular Israel child.
  • The Gemara: the permission for children of converts lasts ten generations, or until their origin is forgotten.
  • Shulhan Arukh: brings both opinions.

In one line

A child of two converts may marry a mamzer, for some generations, but a child with one Jewish-born parent is a regular Israel.
