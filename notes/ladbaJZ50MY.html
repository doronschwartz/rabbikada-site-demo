Overview

This shiur completes the section on muktzeh (Simanim 308-311), covering the positive aspects of Shabbat observance. The main focus is on tiltul min hatzad (indirect movement) and the halachot of dealing with a deceased person on Shabbat.

Three Categories of Moving Muktzeh Objects

Category 1: Tiltul Min HaTzad (Indirect Movement)

Definition: Moving something permissible with your hand, which causes a muktzeh item to move indirectly.

Example: Pulling a vegetable from the ground where earth (muktzeh) moves as a result.

Halachic Principle:

  • Tiltul min hatzad l'tzorech davar ha'mutar - MUTAR (indirect movement for a permitted purpose is allowed)
  • Tiltul min hatzad l'tzorech davar ha'assur - ASSUR (indirect movement for a forbidden purpose is prohibited)

Practical Examples:

Permitted:

  • Taking ice cream from the freezer where raw fish (muktzeh) moves indirectly - you want the ice cream (permitted item)
  • Pulling a vegetable from earth where soil moves - you want to eat the vegetable

Prohibited:

  • Shaking a table to remove money (muktzeh) so the cleaner won't take it - your purpose is for the muktzeh item itself
  • Moving muktzeh solely to preserve or protect the muktzeh item

Category 2: Tiltul B'Gufo (Movement with Body Parts)

Definition: Moving muktzeh with any body part OTHER than your hands (legs, elbows, blowing, etc.)

Halachic Principle:

  • ALWAYS MUTAR - permitted in all circumstances
  • Even l'tzorech davar ha'assur - even if done to preserve the muktzeh item itself
  • The Chachamim never made the gezeirah of muktzeh apply to movement with body parts other than hands

Practical Application:

  • Pushing muktzeh straw off a bed with your elbow - mutar
  • Kicking muktzeh items with your leg - mutar
  • No distinction between whether it's for a permitted or forbidden purpose

Category 3: Movement with Extension of Hand (Machlokat Acharonim)

Definition: Using a stick or other object to push away a muktzeh item (not picking it up, but moving it)

Machlokat:

  • Taz: Views this as another form of tiltul min hatzad (indirect movement)
  • Therefore: Mutar l'tzorech davar ha'mutar (when moving it for a permitted purpose)
  • Example: Using a stick to push muktzeh off a table because you need the table space
  • Shulchan Aruch HaRav (Baal HaTanya): Views this as an extension of your hand
  • Therefore: Treated as regular muktzeh carrying - generally assur

Practical Application:

  • Safek d'rabbanan l'hakeil - in cases of necessity, can be lenient
  • Better to use other options if available

Siman 311: Dinei Met B'Shabbat (Laws of Deceased on Shabbat)

Seif 1: Met in Danger of Desecration

מת שמוטל במקום שירא מפני הדליקה A deceased person lying in a place where there is concern for fire or intense heat that will cause desecration.

Halacha: אם יש כיכר או תינוק מטלטלו על ידיהם

  • Place a non-muktzeh item (loaf of bread or baby) on the deceased
  • Then move the body directly to another room or onto the floor
  • This is a form of shinui/heiker showing you're not moving muktzeh outright

Reason: Although a deceased person is muktzeh machmat gufo (completely muktzeh - nothing can be done with them), by placing something non-muktzeh on them, Chachamim permitted moving them.

Rolling Method (Tiltul Min HaTzad for Met)

ואם אין לו כיכר או תינוק, אם יש לו שתי מיטות If no non-muktzeh item is available and you have two beds:

Halacha: מטלטלו על ידו, שהופכו ממיטה למיטה עד שיגיע

  • Roll the deceased from one bed to another bed
  • Continue until removed from danger

This is tiltul min hatzad - moving the bed causes the body to move indirectly.

Special Exception:

  • Normally tiltul min hatzad l'tzorech davar ha'assur is forbidden
  • For a met specifically, Chachamim made an exception - you may do tiltul min hatzad even though it's for the deceased person (the assur item)
  • This is unique to met and doesn't apply to other muktzeh

Direct Movement When Necessary

ואם אין לו לא זה ולא זה If you have neither non-muktzeh items nor the ability to roll:

Halacha: מטלטלו במיטתו גופו וחוזר ותורשו

  • May move the deceased body outright (within same reshut)
  • Only where there is genuine concern for bizayon hamet (desecration due to heat/humidity)

Modern Application:

  • When someone dies on Shabbat in a hot room
  • Open all windows
  • Place something non-muktzeh on the deceased
  • Lift and place them on the floor (cooler, better preservation)
  • This prevents rapid deterioration and bizayon hamet

Seif 5: Moving Met for the Space He Occupies

אם צריך למקום המת או לדבר שהמת מונח עליו If you need the space where the deceased is located or the item he's lying on:

Halacha: מותר לטלטלו מן הצד, הוא הדין ממיטה למיטה, כיוון דלצורך דבר המותר הוא

  • Permitted to move the deceased through tiltul min hatzad (rolling from bed to bed)
  • Why? Because you need the space - this is l'tzorech davar ha'mutar
  • Even if there's no heat or concern for desecration

Example: Deceased is on the sofa, you need the sofa for guests - you may roll the body off.

Seif 7: Handling the Deceased Body

Permitted Actions:

מותר לסוך המת

  • May anoint the deceased with oil (as preservative)
  • Even though touching muktzeh, this is permitted (touching muktzeh is always mutar)

ולשמוט הכר מתחתיו

  • May pull the pillow from under the deceased
  • To prevent heat buildup around the body
  • כדי שלא יסריח - so it won't decompose quickly
  • Even though this indirectly moves the body

Prohibited Actions:

ובלבד שלא יזיז בו שום איבר

  • May NOT move any limbs
  • Cannot manipulate the body itself

The Mouth and Jaw

ומה שהפה נפתח והולך If the mouth is gradually opening:

Permitted: קושר את הלחי בעניין שלא יוסיף להיפתח

  • May tie the jaw to prevent it from opening further
  • This prevents additional movement

Prohibited: אבל לא כדי שיסגור מה שנפתח

  • May NOT tie the jaw to close what has already opened
  • This would involve moving the limb

The Eyes - Important Halacha

ומטעם זה אין מעצמים עיניו של מת בשבת

Clear Halacha (Mishnah, Gemara, Rambam, Shulchan Aruch):

  • ASSUR to close the eyes of a deceased person on Shabbat
  • Reason: Moving a limb of muktzeh

Contradiction with Zohar:

  • Zohar states it's terrible to leave the eyes open
  • Must close eyes immediately upon death (during the week)
  • Because of the severity in Zohar, a minhag developed to close eyes even on Shabbat
  • Mishnah Berurah: States this minhag is wrong, goes against explicit Mishnah
  • But notes it may not be worth objecting since it's based on Zohar
  • Certainly prohibited to move limbs to straighten the body

Practical Halacha:

  • Should NOT close the eyes on Shabbat despite minhag ta'ut
  • Close immediately after Shabbat ends

Seifim 8-9: General Principles of Tiltul Min HaTzad

Seif 8: The Radish in the Ground

טלטול מן הצד לצורך דבר המותר מותר Indirect movement for a permitted purpose is allowed.

Case: צנון שטמן בארץ A radish/vegetable preserved in the ground:

Conditions:

  1. Already detached before Shabbat (no tolesh/harvesting issue)
  2. מקצת עליו מגולין - Some leaves are exposed (not fully submerged)
  3. ולא השריש - Hasn't taken root
  4. ולא נתכוון לזרע - Not placed there for seeding (otherwise looks like harvesting)

Halacha: נוטלו אף על פי שבנטילתו מזיז עפר ממקומו

  • May take it directly with your hand
  • Even though taking it moves earth (muktzeh) from its place
  • Why? Tiltul min hatzad l'tzorech davar ha'mutar

ואף על פי שיוסיף מחמת לחוט הקרקע מותר

  • Even if the radish grew larger from ground moisture - permitted

Not a Basis:

Rama: ואף על פי שהניחו שם מבעוד יום להיות שם כל השבת Even though placed there before Shabbat to remain the entire Shabbat:

אינו יכולים משום בסיס דבר האסור

  • The food does NOT become a basis to the earth
  • Food never becomes a basis
  • Therefore may pull out the exposed portion and move the earth

Seif 9: Tiltul B'Gufo - Movement with Body

טלטול בגופו אפילו לצורך דבר האסור מותר Movement with body parts, even for a forbidden purpose, is permitted.

Case: קש על המיטה Straw (muktzeh - normally used for fire) on your bed:

Halacha: מנער בגופו

  • May push it away with your body
  • Even if purpose is to preserve the straw itself
  • Why? You're doing it with your body (not hands)

Alternative Method: מניח מבעוד יום כר וכסת על גביו

  • If you place a pillow or blanket on it before Shabbat
  • Shows your intention that it should be part of the bed
  • Then it's not muktzeh at all

Fruits in Straw:

פירות הטמונים בתבן או בקש המוקצים Fruits completely submerged in muktzeh straw:

Halacha: יכול לדחוק בהם מחט או קוש, ונוטל מהקש שעלה עליו

  • May use a needle or stick to make space
  • Then pull out the fruits
  • Even though the straw moves indirectly

Note: Taz sees this as proof that using an extension of your hand is not considered direct hand movement, but rather tiltul min hatzad.

Practical Applications and Summary

Muktzeh Item on Table (Not a Basis)

Scenario: Child left muktzeh on table before Shabbat, table had challah so not a basis.

Options:

  1. Best Option - Tiltul B'Gufo:
  • Push it off with elbow or leg
  • Always mutar, no conditions
  • Problem: If expensive item (phone), will get damaged
  1. Tiltul Min HaTzad (lifting table):
  • Assur if purpose is to protect the muktzeh item (prevent theft, damage to phone)
  • Mutar if need the table space - lift table and roll muktzeh onto sofa
  • Distinction is critical: What is your kavana (intention)?
  1. Using a Stick (Machlokat):
  • According to Taz: Like tiltul min hatzad - mutar if l'tzorech davar ha'mutar
  • According to others: Like direct hand movement - assur
  • In case of necessity, can be lenient (safek d'rabbanan)

Phone on Bed

Note: If phone is kli she'melachto l'issur, may move it l'tzorech mekomo (for its place) directly.

If phone is muktzeh machmat chesaron kis (expensive, valuable item):

  • Cannot move for its place directly

Options:

  1. Push off with body part (if won't get damaged)
  2. Lift bed and roll phone off onto safe surface
  • Mutar because you want to sleep on the bed (l'tzorech davar ha'mutar)
  1. Use stick to prod it off (safek, better to use other options)

Chiddush of the Magen Avraham (Major Innovation)

The Lamp on the Table Case

Scenario:

  • Muktzeh lamp on table
  • Table is not a basis (had challah on it)
  • Want to move table with lamp to another room to use the lamp there

Question: Is this permitted?

Initial Analysis:

  • Seems like tiltul min hatzad l'tzorech davar ha'assur
  • Moving it FOR the muktzeh item (to use the lamp)
  • Should be ASSUR

Magen Avraham's Chiddush:

  • MUTAR in this case
  • Distinction: Tiltul min hatzad l'tzorech davar ha'assur only applies when goal is shmirat davar hamuktzeh (preserving/protecting the muktzeh)
  • When moving it to USE the muktzeh and get benefit from it, this is considered l'tzorech davar ha'mutar
  • The benefit you're getting is mutar on Shabbat

Very Broad Definition:

  • Many disagreed with Magen Avraham
  • In case of need, can be lenient
  • Shows much broader understanding of what "l'tzorech davar ha'mutar" means

Siman 312: Kivod HaBriyot (Human Dignity)

Using Stones for Cleaning

Historical Context: Before toilet paper, people used stones and pebbles for cleaning - these are normally muktzeh.

Halacha: משום כבוד הבריות התירו לטלטל אבנים לקנח

  • Due to kivod habriyot, permitted to carry stones for cleaning
  • אפילו להעלותם לגג עמו דהוי טרחה יתירה מותר
  • Even permitted to carry them up to the roof (extra effort/burden)
  • All because of kivod habriyot

Creating Privacy Enclosure

אבנים גדולות שמסדרן אותן כמין מושב חלול Large stones arranged like a hollow seat or enclosure:

Halacha: ויושב עליהן בסודו במקום מיוחד לבית הכסא, מותר לצדדן

  • May arrange stones to create:
  • Privacy enclosure
  • Seat structure (like toilet seat)
  • Permitted to move muktzeh for this purpose

Critical Principle: ואף על גב דבנין עראי הוא, לא גזרו ביה רבנן משום כבוד הבריות

  • Even though this is binyan arai (temporary building structure)
  • Normally forbidden d'rabbanan (rabbinically)
  • Chachamim permitted it because of kivod habriyot

Scope of Kivod HaBriyot

Two Major Points:

  1. Extends Beyond Direct Necessity:
  • Not just for the cleaning act itself (obviously needed)
  • Even for dignity, privacy, comfort (making enclosure, seat)
  • All considered part of kivod habriyot
  1. Overrides Other D'Rabbanans:
  • Not just muktzeh (purely rabbinic)
  • Even other rabbinic prohibitions (like binyan arai)
  • Can override many melachot when d'rabbanan

Kivod HaBriyot vs. Kivod HaMet

Important Distinction:

Kivod HaBriyot:

  • Only for living people
  • Much wider heter
  • Can override d'rabbanans

Bizayon HaMet:

  • For deceased
  • Different concept
  • NOT called kivod habriyot
  • No concept of "kivod habriyot for dead people"

Practical Application: The Stain Case (Machlokat Poskim)

Scenario:

Person gets bad stain on suit/dress on Shabbat while in synagogue:

  • Cannot go home to change
  • Very embarrassing
  • Water and scrubbing would remove it

Question:

May one ask a non-Jew to remove the stain (involves melachah d'oraita)?

Two Approaches:

Approach 1 - Magen Avraham (Majority View):

  • ASSUR to ask non-Jew
  • "It's your kavod to show you're keeping Shabbat"
  • The stain shows honor for Shabbat
  • Your keeping halachot of Shabbat is the biggest kavod
  • When people ask, can explain: "This happened on Shabbat, it's forbidden to remove"

Approach 2 - Minority View:

  • MUTAR to ask non-Jew
  • Gadol kivod habriyot - human dignity is great
  • Embarrassing to walk around with huge stain
  • Asking non-Jew is only d'rabbanan (even though he does d'oraita)
  • For kivod habriyot, may do d'rabbanan

General Principle:

גדול כבוד הבריות שדוחה לא תעשה

  • Gemara: Kivod habriyot can even override a lo ta'aseh (negative Torah commandment)
  • Certainly on Shabbat for muktzeh and d'rabbanans
  • Can use kivod habriyot for necessary things

Met on Shabbat:

  1. With non-muktzeh item: Place bread/baby on body, move directly
  2. Rolling method: Roll from bed to bed (tiltul min hatzad)
  3. Direct movement: Only when necessary for bizayon hamet
  4. Never move limbs: Cannot manipulate body parts
  5. Do not close eyes: Against explicit Mishnah (despite minhag ta'ut)

Kivod HaBriyot:

  • Permits muktzeh movement and other d'rabbanans
  • Only for living people (not deceased)
  • Extends to dignity, privacy, comfort
  • Wide-ranging applications in Shabbat observance

Practical Guidelines to Remember

לעולם נקוט כללים בידך - Always keep the principles in hand:

  1. Ask yourself: What am I trying to accomplish?
  • If for permitted item → likely mutar
  • If for muktzeh item itself → likely assur
  1. Consider your method:
  • Body parts (not hands) → always best option
  • Indirect (tiltul min hatzad) → depends on purpose
  • Direct with hands → only if explicit heter
  1. Framework matters more than details:
  • Know when there's a question
  • Apply general principles
  • Consult when uncertain
