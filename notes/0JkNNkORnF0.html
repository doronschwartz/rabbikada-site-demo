Topic: Double knots on Shabbat: the Rema and the Shiltei HaGibborim, the Hida, the Ben Ish Hai, and the Sefaradi ruling Source: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Siman 317, seif 1, the Rema, the Mishnah Berurah and the Shaarei Teshuvah Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada in his Monday-night series on the Melakhot, Monday 4 March 2019 (Kosher and Mattir, Part 4)

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Where we stand

Ashkenazim: a professional knot is out of the question. A simple knot is permitted only if, when tying it, you intend to untie it within 24 hours; where there is a need, within seven days, but never more.

Sefaradim following the Shulhan Arukh: a professional knot is always forbidden. A simple knot is permitted if it is not permanent. Seven days is certainly fine, and there is room to be lenient for longer, up to about a month, if it is not tied for a long period. Followers of the Ben Ish Hai untie within 24 hours, following one opinion in the Rema.

A misvah. For a misvah, such as measuring a mikveh, you may tie a professional knot that is not permanent, or a simple knot that is permanent. Someone asked how we know the second. The Rabbi answered that the Aharonim derive it, since for the Shulhan Arukh, following the Rambam, both are equally rabbinic.

The Rema on double knots

At the end of seif 1 the Rema writes: some say one should not tie or untie a double knot, because we cannot be sure what a professional knot is. Even if it is not permanent, even for half a day, it is forbidden, since a professional knot is rabbinic even for a short time. "And this is the custom." The source is the Shiltei HaGibborim, the sixteenth-century Italian commentary on the Rif.

The Rema adds: where there is pain, for example someone tied his shoes with a double knot before Shabbat and cannot get them off to go to sleep, he may untie it, because in such a case the Hakhamim did not make this prohibition.

Two kinds of single knot. The first knot you make when tying shoelaces, two laces crossed and pulled, falls apart within seconds. It has no permanence and is permitted. But an overhand knot in a single thread, like a knot at the end of a sisit, pulled tight, has the status of a double knot. So it too may be a professional knot, and the Rema forbids it even for a short time.

The Mishnah Berurah explains: according to Rashi and the Rosh there would be no problem, since only time matters. But according to the Rif and the Rambam, a professional knot is forbidden even for a moment, and we do not know what one is. So any tight knot, a double knot or a single overhand knot at the end of a thread, is treated as possibly professional. For Ashkenazim, a double knot on shoelaces is forbidden even if you will untie it that evening.

The Hida

The Shaarei Teshuvah brings the Hida in the Birkei Yoseph: in our lands the custom is to tie a double knot in one's belt, not for a long time, only until one goes to sleep, and it is a simple knot. Those who are particular about double knots are a small minority, and their view is negligible.

The Rabbi said the plain meaning is that the Hida permits a double knot as long as it is not for a long time, so for Sefaradim, for up to seven days.

A double doubt. He added that this is logical. Even if a double knot is professional, it would only be rabbinic. And there are two doubts: perhaps the halacha follows Rashi and the Rosh, for whom a short-lived professional knot is permitted; and even following the Rambam, perhaps a double knot is not professional at all. Someone objected that a doubt from lack of knowledge does not count. The Rabbi answered that this applies where the answer can be found out. Here no one can know what counts as professional, so it is a real doubt.

A second knot on an existing one. Someone asked about adding a second knot on top of a single knot tied before Shabbat. The Rabbi said it is the same: making the second knot is making a double knot. So retying a loose sisit with a second knot would be forbidden, all the more since that knot is normally permanent.

The belt. There was a long discussion about what the Hida meant by two knots on a belt, and whether the first was even a proper knot. The Rabbi distinguished a true double knot from a bow with a second bow, and a single knot with a bow on it, which he left for the next class.

The Ben Ish Hai

The Ben Ish Hai, in Parashat Ki Tisa and in Rav Pealim, understood the Hida differently. "Heaven forbid" that the Hida should dismiss the Rema's opinion as negligible. The Hida was describing the people of his town: a few were not concerned, and there is no need to rebuke them, but he did not rule that it is permitted. So the Ben Ish Hai rules that a double knot is forbidden even for a short time.

The Rabbi disagreed. If the Hida had meant to forbid it, he would have said so, or said it is not the custom. "That's definitely the plain meaning" in the Hida: a double knot is permitted. Hazon Ovadia brings many Aharonim who read the Hida this way and rule like him, and Or LeSion rules the same. The Arukh HaShulhan also writes that the Rema was strict only for those who allow seven days, not for those who untie within 24 hours. With all of this together, there is good reason to be lenient.

A triple knot? For those who hold a double knot is not professional, a triple knot is no different. It is very strong, but it is still not a craftsman's knot like those of the camel driver or sailor.

When there is pain

Someone asked whether the Rema's leniency in a case of pain is a general rule for all rabbinic prohibitions. The Rabbi said the Aharonim conclude it is specific to here: the Hakhamim did not apply the prohibition where there is pain, because the whole concern is only a doubt. It is not a general principle, though Hazal permitted certain rabbinic acts for pain in specific cases, such as a woman expressing milk that is causing her pain.

The recording ends as the class turns to the Biur Halacha and seif 5, before the practical cases.

Practical summary

  • The Rema, following the Shiltei HaGibborim, forbids tying or untying a double knot, or a tight overhand knot in one thread, even for a short time.
  • If a double knot tied before Shabbat causes real discomfort, such as shoes you cannot take off, even Ashkenazim may untie it.
  • The Hida writes that the custom was to tie double knots and the strict view is a small minority.
  • The Ben Ish Hai reads the Hida as only tolerating the custom, and forbids double knots.
  • Hazon Ovadia, Or LeSion and many Aharonim follow the plain reading of the Hida: Sefaradim may tie a double knot with intention to untie it within seven days.
  • Adding a second knot on top of an existing one counts as making a double knot.
