Topic: The restrictions of the Nine Days from the law of the Gemara

Sources: The Gemara; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Sunday 27 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Rosh Hodesh Av has passed and the Nine Days have begun. By the Gemara, the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh, four things are forbidden.
  • Marriages: in the Gemara only for one who has already fulfilled Peru Urevu, but today no weddings at all are held in the Nine Days, as an extension.
  • Reducing simhah: going on holiday, vacations and entertainment are inappropriate and forbidden.
  • Binyan shel simhah, building for pleasure: a holiday home, a swimming pool, repainting a reasonable house, new curtains or cupboards, even planting new plants in the garden. What is needed for basic living, or to fix a danger, is permitted; a house that looks terrible and needs painting is a different story.
  • Reducing business: unnecessary purchases not needed now should be postponed until after Tisha BeAv, though there is debate over exactly what this means.
  • A custom recorded in the Shulhan Arukh, and the Sepharadi custom, is not to buy, make or fix new clothes. Everything else is permitted in the Nine Days.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara, the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh: weddings, reduced simhah, building for pleasure and reduced business in the Nine Days.
  • Shulhan Arukh: the custom not to buy or make new clothes.

In one line

In the Nine Days the Gemara forbids weddings, leisure, building for pleasure and unnecessary purchases, and Sepharadim also avoid new clothes.
