Topic: Why hames is uniquely strict, and what it symbolises

Sources: The Torah; Gemara Berakhot (the prayer of Rabbi Alexandri); the Gemara (the slaughter of the Yeser HaRa); the Hasidic masters; Pele Yoes (Rabbi Eliezer Papo)

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Thursday 14 April 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Most forbidden foods are either forbidden to eat (non-kosher meat may be fed to a pet) or forbidden also for benefit (meat cooked with milk). Hames alone is forbidden to eat, to benefit from and even to own.
  • It carries karet, and it is not nullified in 60: even a drop in Pesah food forbids the whole.
  • Gemara Berakhot: Rabbi Alexandri prayed after each Amidah, "Master of the Universe, it is known to You that we want to do Your will; what prevents us? Seor shebeisah", the yeast in the dough, the Yeser HaRa.
  • Hames and massah have the same ingredients and oven; massah must even be made from what can become hames. Only time separates them, and their letters differ only by het and hey. The difference is hot air, which makes hames look bigger and more enticing than it is.
  • The Gemara says that when God slaughters the Yeser HaRa, the righteous will weep at how they overcame it and the wicked at how they could not.
  • The Hasidic masters told of a charlatan who ran through a market shouting about what was in his clenched fist; when the whole town caught him, his hand was empty. Sin looks good but is empty.
  • At our birth as a nation we learn that serving HaShem begins with humility and seeing wrongdoing as empty, so hames must be wholly eradicated. The Pele Yoes wrote that a sin on Pesah is more severe than during the year.
  • We do not eat massah all year, since we live in the material world, but on Pesah we recall what our lives should strive for.

In one line

Hames is massah inflated with hot air, a symbol of the enticing emptiness of sin, which is why on Pesah it must be wholly removed.
