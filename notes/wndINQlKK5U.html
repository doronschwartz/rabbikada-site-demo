Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 284, seifim 1-7 Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 18 January 2024

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

Twenty-one verses and seven blessings

Siman 284 covers the reading of the Haftarah and its blessings, in seven seifim. The Rabbi began with a short review of last week. The Ashkenazi poskim write that ideally the Haftarah should be read from a scroll of that book of Neviim written on klaf, and wealthy communities do this. The Magen Avraham's next best is a printed text of the whole Tanakh. The third option is to read from the Humash. The Sefaradi custom of a printed Haftarah book has no special status: "it's purely ceremonial." If it falls, it is like a Humash falling.

Seif 1: the Haftarah is read from the Prophets, on a subject connected to the parashah, ideally 21 verses, unless the subject ends sooner. Sefaradim often read fewer than 21 for this reason. The Rema says 15 verses are enough.

The origin: when the Greeks forbade reading the Torah, the Sages instituted reading from the Prophets instead, seven people reading three verses each, making 21. When the decree ended, the custom stayed. The seven Haftarah blessings (two before, five after) correspond to the seven people called up. The person reading the Haftarah first reads the Maftir from the Torah, so that the Prophets are not put on the same level as the Torah.

The Rema adds that the Haftarah with its blessings is said only with a minyan and only together with a Torah reading. Without a Sefer Torah, one may read the Haftarah, but without the blessings.

A Sefer Torah found pasul. The Mishnah Berurah says that if, after the reading, the Sefer Torah is found to be pasul and there is no other, the Haftarah is still read with its blessings. The Rabbi explained: the congregation made blessings on a reading they believed was valid, so there was a real setting of Torah reading, even if mistaken. This seems to apply even if the problem was found during the first aliyah. But if the scroll was taken out knowing it was pasul, the Haftarah may not be read with blessings.

He discussed a case he called "very far fetched": reading the Haftarah from a klaf kept in someone's home after the service. It could work with the blessings only if the same congregation continues there, with a majority of the original minyan. A few people collecting others on the way home would be a new congregation.

Rosh Hodesh, listening and answering amen

Seif 2: on Shabbat Rosh Hodesh the Haftarah blessing ends only "Mekadesh HaShabbat", not with Rosh Hodesh as in Musaf. The reason: there is no Haftarah on a weekday Rosh Hodesh, so the Haftarah has no connection to Rosh Hodesh. Some say it may be mentioned in the middle of the blessing, but the Rabbi knows of no community that does so.

Seif 3: the congregation should listen to the Haftarah blessings and answer amen. The Rabbi wondered how many people know this. The Mishnah Berurah adds that the person called up must say the blessings loudly enough for everyone to answer: "no point whispering it, trying to be humble." He also stressed that talking during the Haftarah is forbidden, though the consequence is less severe than talking during the Torah reading.

A child as Maftir, and who reads the Haftarah

Seif 4: a child may be called up for Maftir and read the Haftarah, as is done all the time. The Rema: if the person called for Maftir cannot read the Haftarah, someone else may read it, but this is forbidden in the first instance.

The Rabbi explained what is forbidden: the Maftir says the Torah blessings, and then someone else reads the Haftarah with its own blessings before and after. That makes the Haftarah a separate act, like a ninth aliyah, and it is no longer clearly secondary to the Torah. After the fact, the Mishnah Berurah allows it when a child read the Maftir, since the Kaddish in between shows he was called for the Haftarah.

The levels:

  • Best: the Maftir reads the Haftarah himself with its blessings.
  • Acceptable: the Maftir says the Haftarah blessings and someone else reads the text for him, while he listens to every word, as his agent. The Mishnah Berurah suggests he should even say it word by word with the reader. The Rabbi said: "I don't like it," but it is fine.
  • Forbidden in the first instance: one person is Maftir and another reads the Haftarah with the blessings.

Seif 5: if the reader loses his voice in the middle, the next person starts again from the beginning of the Haftarah. Whether he repeats the blessings before is debated, and the conclusion is that he does not, since the first reader said them for everyone. He says the blessings after. Asked whether the first reader should stop three verses before the end, the Rabbi agreed that would be better. If he lost his voice only after finishing, the blessings after cannot be said by someone else; "what can you do."

Reading along: Sefaradim and Ashkenazim

The Rema: two people should not read the Haftarah together, nor alternate verses, because two voices are not heard.

The Rabbi described what one sees in many Ashkenazi shuls: the person called up reads aloud, and everyone else murmurs the Haftarah at his own pace. Everyone agrees that if the reader reads from a proper scroll of the Navi, it is wrong to read by yourself and not listen, just as during the Torah reading. The custom grew from the Magen Avraham, who wrote that when the reader uses a Humash each person should read for himself. Sefaradim never did this.

His practical guidance: listen to the reader and follow in the text. Following the Arizal, one may whisper the words quietly, as long as one is listening. Do not read on your own at your own pace. If you must leave, read it quickly yourself and go.

Waiting for the Sefer Torah to be rolled

Seif 6: the Maftir does not begin the Haftarah until the Sefer Torah has been fully rolled. This is from the Gemara, and Rashi explains that the person rolling must also hear the Haftarah.

For Middle Eastern Sefaradim this is simple: close the case. Ashkenazim fasten a short binder, which takes seconds. The Spanish and Portuguese custom of a long silk band, ribbons and a large crown takes about a minute. He called it a beautiful custom, but beginning the Haftarah while the binding continues goes against the Gemara. He tries to delay a little by announcing page numbers, and suggested the community find something to fill the gap, perhaps a short synopsis of the Haftarah.

Double parashiyot, Hashkavot and the sermon

Seif 7: when two parashiyot are read together, the Haftarah is that of the second, except for Aharei Mot and Kedoshim. The Rema adds that after the reading it became customary to say Hashkavot and Mi Sheberakh, each place by its custom.

The Hashkavah first appears in the Rishonim, with no Talmudic source. The Rabbi noted a benefit: people come to shul during the year for their parents' Hashkavah. Saying them between the aliyot came later and is not simple, as we learned about interruptions during the reading.

The Mishnah Berurah (15): after Ashrei nothing should be added before Musaf. Yimlokh, returning the Torah and the Kaddish are all part of it, but a Mi Sheberakh for the sick at that point is "not a good custom." Most communities say it after the reading, before Ashrei, or when the Torah is taken out.

This raised the question of the sermon. Old Spanish and Portuguese siddurim mark the sermon before Ashrei. Many shuls give it before Musaf and add verses so the Kaddish has something to follow. Some write it is better at the end of the service. "You can see the whole development of minhagim."

He ended by noting that 285 (Shenayim Mikra) was learned in the past, and next comes 286, Musaf on Shabbat.

Practical summary

  • Listen to the Haftarah blessings and answer amen; the reader should say them aloud.
  • Do not talk during the Haftarah.
  • Ideally the Maftir reads the Haftarah himself. If someone reads it for him, the Maftir says the blessings and listens to every word.
  • Follow along with the reader rather than reading at your own pace.
  • The Haftarah should not begin until the Sefer Torah has been rolled.
