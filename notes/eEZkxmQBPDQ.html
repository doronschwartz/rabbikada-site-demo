Topic: Defining Libbun, and the practical differences between Libbun and Hagalah

Sources: The Gemara; the poskim; Shulhan Arukh; Rashi and the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Thursday 27 March 2025

The halacha

  • The Gemara defines Libbun as heat so intense that sparks emerge, or until a layer almost peels off. The poskim put this at roughly 350 to 400 degrees.
  • Some opinions say Libbun need only match the temperature at which the taste was absorbed, but this is not generally followed. Some items simply cannot undergo Libbun because it would destroy them.
  • Hagalah is immersing or boiling in hot water. Libbun destroys whatever remains in the utensil, whereas Hagalah uses heat to expel the taste.
  • Therefore for Hagalah the Shulhan Arukh requires the utensil to be clean and free of rust, so the heat reaches every part; Libbun has no such requirement.
  • Also, the Shulhan Arukh rules that in the first instance one waits 24 hours after a utensil's last use before Hagalah.
  • The reason: why does the hot pot not reabsorb the expelled taste? Rashi says heat that is expelling cannot reabsorb, so no wait is needed. The other view is that the water is assumed to be 60 times the taste, nullifying it, but that is not always so. After 24 hours any reabsorbed taste is spoiled (Notein Taam LiFgam), which is no concern.
  • So unless one knows there is 60 times the water, one waits 24 hours before Hagalah; this is not needed for Libbun.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: Libbun means until sparks fly or a layer almost peels off.
  • Rashi: the expelling heat does not reabsorb taste.
  • Shulhan Arukh: Hagalah requires a clean, rust-free utensil and, in the first instance, a 24-hour wait.

In one line

Libbun burns out the taste at very high heat, while Hagalah expels it in boiling water, so Hagalah needs a clean utensil and ideally a 24-hour wait.
