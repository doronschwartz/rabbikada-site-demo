Topic: Delaying children at the start of a marriage

Sources: The Mishnah (sailors' onah once in six months); Rav Yehuda Herzl Henkin (Benei Vanim)

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 6 February 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • It is easier to be lenient about spacing once "Peru urevu" (a son and a daughter) is fulfilled; before that, the main misvah is at stake. Each case is individual.
  • The growing practice of marrying and then deciding to wait a couple of years before having children, for convenience, is against halacha. Having already postponed the misvah to 18, 20 or 24, a married couple may not delay without proper grounds.
  • Medical advice not to have a child, on physical or mental grounds, does permit delay even before the first child.
  • Where a couple marry with real uncertainty about whether the marriage will work, Rav Henkin (Benei Vanim) allows delaying up to six months. He draws on the Mishnah that some professions, such as sailors, fulfil the marital misvah only once in six months. This needs rabbinic guidance and is never for convenience.
  • A couple who are both studying, for example a wife in medical school with no help for a baby, is a complex case that needs a rabbi's advice.
  • Even so, it is better to marry and delay (even if the delay is not right) than to postpone marriage itself, because marriage is an independent misvah.

In one line

Delaying children after marriage for convenience is wrong; health grounds or genuine doubt about the marriage (up to six months, per Rav Henkin) are different and need rabbinic advice.
