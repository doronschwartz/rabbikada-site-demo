Topic: What Torah may be studied on Tisha BeAv, washing, brushing teeth, and a recent bride

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Tuesday 25 July 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Permitted study: the sad parts of Tanakh, the Hakhamim and the Gemara; the laws of Tisha BeAv and of mourning (long in the Shulhan Arukh and often unlearned); and the history of the Temples and our tragedies.
  • Hakham Ovadiah and others permit works of Musar, since the fast is a means to Teshuvah and Musar arouses a person to it.
  • The point is not to take one's mind off the mourning. Spending the day on newspapers just to pass the time is also not a good idea; it is a day to reflect, learn our history and think how to help rebuild the Beit HaMikdash.
  • Washing is forbidden as on Yom Kippur, but hygiene is permitted: washing hands after the bathroom or after changing a child's nappy.
  • The Shulhan Arukh permits an istenis, someone extremely sensitive who genuinely cannot function without it, to wash his face. This is a very high bar.
  • Likewise brushing teeth or rinsing the mouth is not allowed, unless one genuinely cannot function without it and there is no alternative; then brush with the head facing down, swallow nothing, and do only what is needed.
  • A bride married within the last 30 days may wash and anoint herself as she needs, since the Hakhamim recognised the importance of first impressions.

Sources quoted

In one line

On Tisha BeAv study only what saddens or brings Teshuvah, wash only for hygiene, and brush teeth only if one truly cannot function without it.
