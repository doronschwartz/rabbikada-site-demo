Sefer: Derech Hashem by Rabbi Moshe Hayim Luzzatto (the Ramhal) Section: Part 1, Chapter 4, paragraph 8 to the end of the chapter Shiur: From Dayan Daniel Kada's Derech Hashem class for the Hazak girls' group (Wednesday evenings), Wednesday 10 March 2021. It fills a gap in his Derech Hashem series at Tiferet Eyal, where these paragraphs were taught but not recorded.

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Review

The previous class showed two ways the world is raised up: things done for a misvah, and even ordinary things like eating, sleeping or going out, when they are done with the right intention. Tonight the Rabbi set out to finish Chapter 4, from paragraph 8 to the end, and then to review everything so far before the Pesah break.

Love of Hashem

Paragraph 8 says the main thing a person must work on is the love and fear of Hashem. We come to love Him by appreciating His creation. There are two ways to relate to nature: to see Hashem behind it, or to grow so used to it that we sink into it.

The Rabbi brought the question of the Hovot HaLevavot, about a thousand years ago: why do human beings take so long to become independent, when other creatures stand on their own within days or months? Because if we were born able to think like adults and saw the sunrise, the moon and the trees for the first time, we would see Hashem at once and there would be no free choice. Instead, we spend our early years getting used to nature until it becomes "second nature." The test is to see Hashem behind it.

Fear of Hashem

There are two kinds of fear. The basic one is fear of punishment, which is how children are first taught. The real fear, says the Rambam, comes after love: when you truly love someone, you are careful never to upset them. Think of a spouse: "Look how much she's done for me; I'm going to be so careful that I don't end up upsetting her." So too with Hashem.

The Ramhal asks us to contemplate Hashem's greatness and our own smallness. Looking down from a plane, you realise we are a speck of dust in the universe. Such thoughts humble a person, make him want to serve Hashem, refine the darkness of the personality and bring out the light of the soul.

Love and fear of Hashem are two of the six constant misvot, the only misvot that apply to every person, at every time and in every place.

Paragraph 9: the study of Torah

There is one means higher than all others for drawing near to Hashem: the study of Torah. The Ramhal names two ways. Higayon is reading, letting the words pass through the mind. Haskel is understanding, examining and analysing.

The Rabbi asked whether there is value in reading words you do not understand, as happens when people are handed something to recite. One participant answered that habit matters, as when children learn to bentsh before they understand it; another that it connects people to the community. He agreed these are real values. A third pointed out that the Ramhal speaks of reading the written Torah and of understanding the oral Torah. "Very good. Excellent."

The written Torah, the whole Tanakh, is the word of Hashem. The prophets each have their own style, as the same sunlight comes through lenses of different colours, but it is all the word of Hashem, and reading it, even without fully understanding, "has the power to connect a person to God."

The final paragraph: light and concealment

The chapter ends by explaining all the states we pass through, dark or bright, spiritually and also emotionally, the "good days and bad days" our literature speaks of. They are the illumination of Hashem's face or its concealment: hearat panim and hester panim.

The Rabbi's image: think of a beam of light. Hashem's light is always shining through the window; it is up to us whether we open the shutters or close them. When we do the misvot, we draw ourselves into the beam and feel close to Hashem. When we sin, we close the shutters and it grows dark, "not because of Hashem, but because you don't want to draw yourself near."

That is the meaning of Birkat Kohanim, "Ya'er Hashem panav elekha": may you be in the light of His face. In the time of the Beit HaMikdash there was more hearat panim; in exile there is more concealment. So the purpose of everything is to draw near to Hashem and enjoy His light, and to keep away from sins that take us out of it.

The whole picture so far

The group then reviewed Part 1:

  • Chapter 1: the six fundamentals: existence, perfection, necessary existence, independence, simplicity and unity.
  • Chapter 2: the purpose of creation, to bestow good, by letting us cleave to Hashem; we must choose it so that it becomes our own.
  • Chapter 3: body and soul, this world and the world to come; Olam HaBa is in this world, renewed, with body and soul together and a refined body, after the soul waits in the world of souls; all this follows from the sin of Adam.
  • Chapter 4: the misvot keep us from being dragged down by the physical world and let us raise it, together with love and fear of Hashem, the study of Torah, and drawing ourselves into Hashem's light.

Chapter 5, which is more Kabbalistic, explains how things come down from Hashem into this world. It completes Part 1, and Part 2 turns to Hashem's providence.

Summary

  • We come to love Hashem by seeing Him behind nature instead of getting used to it.
  • True fear of Hashem comes from love: not wanting to upset the One who gave us so much.
  • Torah study is the highest way to draw near: reading the written Torah, and understanding.
  • Hashem's light always shines; misvot open the shutters and sins close them.
