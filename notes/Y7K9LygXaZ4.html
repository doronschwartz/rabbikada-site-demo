Where we are

The Rabbi recalled the previous class: the Mamzer who comes from a forbidden union carrying karet (other than niddah), the status of a child of a niddah, the mahloket about births after 12 months or before nine months, and the idea of "months that cause" (hodashim gormim). Today he began Seif 15, which he said is in three parts, and went on through Seif 20.

Seif 15: a rumour about a married woman

The Shulhan Arukh says that when there is a kol (a public rumour) that a married woman has been unfaithful, there are three parts to the law.

Part 1: the children. We do not suspect that her children are mamzerim, even if there is a rumour or even if there were witnesses who testified to her adultery. The source is the Gemara in Sotah: "A man should marry the daughter of a woman with a reputation, but not the woman herself." Rashi learns that if there is no choice it is better to marry the daughter, but one still should not. The Rambam learns that it is simply fine. The Rabbi said the Shulhan Arukh is clearly like the Rambam, because it says "we do not suspect her children" and does not say it is better not to. Even a woman known to have been unfaithful a number of times, her children may marry others.

Part 2: a Kohen. If the husband is a Kohen, a rumour does cause concern. A woman who has relations with a forbidden man becomes a zonah, and a Kohen may not be with a zonah. If he lives with her afterwards and has children, they could become halalim. The Shulhan Arukh says the Kohen must suspect her "by Torah law". For an Israelite, it is a midat hasidut: he should distance himself from such a woman if there is a rumour, but is not obliged.

Part 3: a very promiscuous woman (prusah beyoter). Here the Shulhan Arukh says we suspect that even the children could be mamzerim. This is a question the Gemara leaves unresolved (teiku), so the Rambam and the Rishonim rule stringently. The Darkhei Moshe quotes one view that the concern is only for Kehunah (that the children should not marry a Kohen), not mamzerut. The Rema brings it, and the Rabbi said not everyone follows it. The Beit Shemuel leaves the question open.

Why the contradiction with Siman 4? The Shulhan Arukh adds the words "a rumour that is spread" (kol shaita). The Rabbi explained the simple answer, following the Beit Shemuel: there are different levels of rumour. A rumour heard once or twice, "perhaps she had an affair", is not enough even for a Kohen. A persistent rumour, heard again and again, is a worse kind of kol, and a Kohen must suspect.

In practice. The Rabbi said there are no clear definitions of prusah beyoter, and a dayan must use common sense: how strong is the rumour, is it about a specific woman, and is this a single episode or "her career"? A family history of serious rumours may matter when families consider a match.

Questions from the class

  • Why isn't she forbidden to her husband because of the rumour? The Rabbi said this is a very large and complicated subject, one of the most complicated simanim, and the concern is not as strong when it is only a kol. If there was a kol together with yihud or something physical, it is different, and there is a big mahloket. He said he did not want to give a wrong answer. If there is a kol but no evidence, Beit Din may still, if it believes the rumour, decline to authorize a marriage.

Seif 16: a woman who remarries while her husband is alive

A husband travels abroad. His wife is told he died and she marries a second man, "husband two". But husband one is alive. She is still married to husband one, and her relationship with husband two is not a halachic marriage. The Rabbi then went through four scenarios.

Scenario

The child

Why

1. Children born to husband two

A Torah mamzer

Straightforward: a child of a married woman

2. Husband one gives a real get, and she then has children with husband two

A rabbinic mamzer according to the Beit Shemuel (a mahloket among the Rishonim)

Her original act is still ongoing (maaseha kayamim)

3. Husband one comes back and has children with her before husband two gives a "performative" get

A rabbinic mamzer

Same reason: a penalty (kenas)

4. Husband two gives a performative get, then husband one returns and has children with her

Fully kosher

The act is no longer ongoing, so there is no penalty

The reasons. The Beit Shemuel's approach is that this is a kenas for not checking properly that husband one had died. While maaseha kayamim (her actions are still ongoing) there is a penalty. Once the situation is broken by a get, there is none. The Helkat Mehokek disagrees and holds that the reason is that it "looks like" a married woman (nireh keeshet ish) of husband two. He also understands Tosafot as holding that a prohibition of this kind (asurah labalah, asurah laboel) itself creates rabbinic mamzerut. The Rabbi said the Shulhan Arukh follows the Beit Shemuel's line.

Important caveat. In scenario 2, the Rabbi stressed that the mamzerut is not simply because she is now forbidden to husband two. If husband one gave a real get and husband two gave a performative one, and then she remarried husband two, everyone agrees the child is fine, because it is only a prohibition, which does not itself create mamzerut.

Questions from the class

  • Is scenario 4 common today? A student said yes: a couple separate, she lives with someone else, a get is given late, and children are born in the meantime. The Rabbi agreed this is common. The question is what "married to husband two" means: with huppah vekiddushin, as the Gemara in Yevamot discusses, or a civil marriage? That is a major point, and whether a non-halachic marriage counts is a difficult matter.

Seif 17: half slave, half free

A man who is half a slave and half free has relations with a married woman. The child is mixed: from the Israelite side, a mamzer; from the slave side, kosher. The child "cannot be fixed" (eino takanah). The Rambam says he cannot marry a Jewish woman (he would be half mamzer), cannot marry a shifhah, and cannot marry a convert (he is half Israelite), so he is stuck unless freed. Rashi says the whole child becomes a mamzer, so he may marry a mamzeret. The Helkat Mehokek prefers Rashi.

Seifim 18-19: a non-Jew and a mamzeret, and a mamzer and a non-Jewish woman

The Rabbi gave the principle that where there is kiddushin and a transgression, the child follows the one who has the fault. If a non-Jew has relations with a mamzeret, there is no kiddushin, so the child follows the mother and is a mamzer. If a non-Jew has relations with a Jewish woman, the child is kosher, but a daughter of such a union has a pegam for Kehunah because her mother is a zonah. The Shulhan Arukh rules like the Rosh here, as it did in Seif 5.

If a mamzer has relations with a non-Jewish woman, the child is not Jewish. If the child converts, he is a kosher Jew.

Seif 20: a mamzer and a shifhah

The Rabbi began this seif and said the main discussion would continue next time with contemporary teshuvot.

The Gemara has a dispute: Rabbi Tarfon says the child of a mamzer and a shifhah is a slave, and the other view says he is a mamzer. All the Rishonim follow Rabbi Tarfon. The slave, when freed, is a regular Jew. So a mamzer may take a shifhah, have as many children as he wishes, then free her and the children.

Why is this allowed? The Rambam holds that a Jew marrying a shifhah is only a rabbinic prohibition, and Hazal waived it for a mamzer because of takanat habanim (to repair the children). Other Rishonim hold it is a Torah prohibition from Lo yihyeh kedeshah, and read it as "do not have someone set aside" (kadesh leomed): a mamzer is already set aside, so the prohibition does not apply.

Limits. This only works for a male mamzer. A mamzeret's children would be mamzerim, as the Helkat Mehokek says. On the question whether a mamzeret could marry a slave, the Rabbi said that according to the Rambam no, since there is no takanat habanim when the children would still be mamzerim.

The questions for next time: What does it mean today to make someone a shifhah? Does it work in England or Australia, where slavery is illegal, or must it be done abroad? He said these questions appear in the contemporary teshuvot after the Holocaust, to be seen next time.

Practical summary

  • A rumour about a married woman does not make her children doubtful, but a Kohen must suspect her, and an Israelite should keep away from her only as a matter of piety.
  • Different levels of rumour matter: once or twice is not enough; persistent is worse.
  • If a woman remarries while her husband is alive, her children from the second man are Torah mamzerim; later children may be rabbinic mamzerim as a penalty while the situation is ongoing.
  • The Beit Shemuel's approach is that the penalty ends when the situation is broken by a get; the Helkat Mehokek differs in the reason.
  • A half-slave child is stuck between two statuses: Rashi and the Rambam differ.
  • A mamzer may use a shifhah to have kosher children, but only a male mamzer, and the practical questions today are left for the next class.
