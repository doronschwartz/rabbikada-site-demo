Topic: Why non-heat electricity (LEDs, fridges, phones) is forbidden on Shabbat, as background for Yom Tov

Sources: The poskim; the Hazon Ish; Rav Asher Weiss; the Talmud

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 10 June 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Incandescent bulbs on Yom Tov are, for most poskim, creating a new flame and forbidden. The question now is electricity without heat: an LED, a fridge, a freezer or a phone.
  • On Shabbat red heat is clearly bishul and mavir. For non-heat electricity everyone agrees it is forbidden, but over the last 120 to 150 years authorities have debated the source of the prohibition.
  • An early theory: molid, a rabbinic branch of makkeh bepatish, creating something new, as with putting perfume on a garment (molid reah). Completing a circuit would be molid zerem. The difficulty: something made to be switched on and off is hard to call creating something new.
  • The Hazon Ish: boneh, building, since completing the circuit builds it, though building normally means a physical structure. This theory too was questioned.
  • Another explanation: makkeh bepatish, the final hammer blow, by completing the circuit.
  • Rav Asher Weiss offers an original idea: an act clearly recognised as work, even without a precise melakhah, automatically falls under makkeh bepatish. His proof: the Talmud says the Hakhamim spent some three and a half years assigning every activity to its melakhah, and placed under makkeh bepatish whatever they could not assign.
  • Tomorrow: how this applies on Yom Tov.

Sources quoted

  • The Hazon Ish: completing a circuit is boneh.
  • Rav Asher Weiss: clear acts of work without a defined melakhah fall under makkeh bepatish.

In one line

All agree non-heat electricity is forbidden on Shabbat, with molid, boneh and makkeh bepatish offered as the source.
