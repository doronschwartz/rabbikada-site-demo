The halacha

  • Parashat Emor commands counting 49 days, seven weeks, from the second day of Pesah until the day before Shavuot. The Omer was a measure of grain offered and waved in the Temple on that day.
  • The Rambam and others hold the counting is an independent Torah misvah, so it still applies biblically without the Temple.
  • An opinion in the Gemara, followed by most Rishonim and seemingly the Shulhan Arukh, ties it to the offering, so today it is rabbinic.
  • So Hazon Ovadiah writes to omit "misvat aseh" from the prayers before counting, as it implies a Torah misvah.
  • The reason, per the Rambam and the Hinukh: Shavuot, "Weeks", completes Pesah. Freedom needs the Torah to teach us how to use it, so we count in eager anticipation of receiving it.
  • Why count up, not down? Counting down is just waiting for a day to arrive. Counting up means working towards a goal, like earning a bonus, looking back on days achieved. Each day counted is a day of preparing ourselves for Shavuot.

In one line

We count the Omer upwards because each day is a step of preparation towards receiving the Torah on Shavuot.
