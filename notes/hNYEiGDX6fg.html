Topic: The meaning of the Harahaman "yakim lanu et sukkat David hanofelet" added on Sukkot

Sources: Amos ("I will raise up the fallen sukkah of David"); Gemara Berakhot ("Nafelah velo tosif, kum betulat Yisrael")

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Sunday 20 October 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • On Sukkot we add to Birkat HaMazon "Harahaman hu yakim lanu et sukkat David hanofelet". Some also add "leshev besukkat oro shel livyatan", for another day.
  • It comes from Amos: in the future HaShem will raise up the falling sukkah of David. Sepharadim and Ashkenazim pronounce it slightly differently; the reading in the pasuk seems the more precise.
  • A house that falls and is rebuilt is a new house. A sukkah is temporary by nature and sometimes falls, but when put back up it is the same sukkah.
  • So Amos calls the kingdom of David, from which Mashiah comes, a sukkah: though it seems destroyed in exile, it only needs re-erecting, and the same kingdom will return.
  • The Gemara in Berakhot reads another pasuk in Amos, which simply means Israel will not rise again, as "it has fallen and will fall no more; rise, maiden of Israel".
  • Sitting in the temporary sukkah, we recall that the Beit HaMikdash, the kingdom of David and full sovereignty are only temporarily fallen, and we pray for their return.

Sources quoted

  • Amos: the source of the Harahaman.
  • Gemara Berakhot: Israel falls but always rises again.

In one line

The kingdom of David is called a sukkah because, though fallen, it will be raised again as the same kingdom.
