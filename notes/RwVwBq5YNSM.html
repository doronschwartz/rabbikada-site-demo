Daily Halacha: Yom Kippur, Wednesday 1 October 2025

The points

  • Yom Kippur is a day to return to our origins, to who we really are.
  • The Gemara in Avodah Zarah tells of Rabbi Hanina ben Teradyon, one of the ten Hakhamim martyred by the Romans after the destruction, sentenced for teaching Torah to be burnt wrapped in a Sefer Torah, with wet wool on his body to prolong his agony.
  • To his daughter he said that Hashem, who avenges the Torah, would avenge him too. To his students he said he saw the letters of the Torah flying upwards.
  • The executioner, used to victims who screamed and cursed, was moved by a man using his last painful moments to uplift others. He offered to remove the wool and hasten his death if promised a place in the World to Come. Rabbi Hanina agreed; the executioner did so, leapt into the flames, and a heavenly voice proclaimed that both had a portion in the World to Come.
  • Within every person, even a pagan executioner, is a Neshamah ready to surface. Unlike other religions, we hold that people are not inherently evil: when we sin we are lost and confused, but our essential goodness remains, though some cover it up greatly.
  • It took the executioner only moments, acting at once without despair, to change his entire identity. So the 25 hours of Yom Kippur can recentre us and shape a vision of who we want to be this year and for life.
