Topic: How the Torah's one "day of blowing" becomes 30 biblical blasts, plus 30 in Musaf

Sources: The Torah (Parashat Emor, Pinehas and Behar); the Gemara (Rabbi Abbahu); the Rishonim

The halacha

  • Most communities blow 100 times on each day of Rosh Hashanah, though the Torah seems to imply a single blowing. One must know which are biblical, which rabbinic and which custom.
  • The Torah mentions teruah in three places, Emor, Pinehas and Behar, so three teruot are required.
  • From "vehaavarta shofar teruah" in Behar, the Hakhamim learn that each teruah needs a proclamation, a long tekiah, before and after: three times three, nine blasts in all.
  • Already in the Gemara's time it was unclear what a teruah is: the very short blasts (today's teruah), the medium ones (shevarim), or both together.
  • So Rabbi Abbahu instituted all three: tekiah teruah tekiah three times (9), tekiah shevarim tekiah three times (9), and tekiah shevarim teruah tekiah three times (12), making 30, all required biblically.
  • The Gemara also requires blowing during the Musaf Amidah, so that "your remembrance rises before Me for good". In the Rishonim's time some blew nine there and some 30; today the custom is 30 in the repetition.
  • That makes 60; how we reach 100 follows tomorrow.

In one line

The doubt over what a teruah is turns the Torah's nine blasts into 30, and Musaf adds another 30.
