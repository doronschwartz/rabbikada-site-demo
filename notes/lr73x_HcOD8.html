These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Shehiyanu on building a sukkah

This was the final class on Hilchot Sukkah before Sukkot began the next evening.

Siman 641 rules that no berakhah is said on building a sukkah, since building is only a preparation for the misvah (hekhsher misvah), not the misvah itself. Shehiyanu, however, is said on momentous occasions, and the Gemara treats building the sukkah as one: in principle one could say Shehiyanu when it is finished. In practice we rely on the Shehiyanu said at Kiddush. Anyone who did say it when building did nothing wrong.

Why not say Shehiyanu on finishing a shofar, a Megillah or tefillin? The poskim explain that Shehiyanu, "who has brought us to this time", is tied to time. A shofar is made once and used for years. A sukkah is renewed each year: even if it stays standing, one is meant to renew the sekhakh, or at least shake it. Its building is bound up with the season.

Rain on the first night

The Rema rules that if you could not eat in the sukkah on the first night, because of rain or any other reason, and said Shehiyanu at Kiddush indoors, you say Shehiyanu again when you first eat in the sukkah the next day. The Shehiyanu said in the sukkah on the first night normally covers three things: the festival, the misvah of sitting in the sukkah, and its building. A Shehiyanu said indoors covers only the festival. If you had said Shehiyanu when building the sukkah, that would suffice.

Almost all the poskim agree with the Rema, and this is clearly the halacha for Ashkenazim. Two opinions disagreed: - The Bah (Bayit Hadash): one can have the sukkah in mind when saying Shehiyanu at home, just as one says Shehiyanu on a new suit and has in mind a tie that is elsewhere. The counter-argument is that the thanks is only fully felt when actually sitting in the sukkah. - The Dagul MeRevavah (the Noda BiYehudah): the Rosh explains that Shehiyanu is said again on the lulav, despite the Shehiyanu of the night before, because the lulav is taken only by day. The sukkah can be done day and night, so the night's Shehiyanu should cover it.

The majority of Sefaradi poskim, including the Kaf HaHayim and Rav Hayim Palagi, rule like the Rema: one says it again, and so a person can say Shehiyanu three times within twenty-four hours, on the festival, the arbaah minim and the sukkah. Rav Ovadia Yoseph, out of his great concern about a berakhah in vain, took the two minority opinions into account and ruled that one does not say it.

With the forecast showing rain in London from six in the evening until three in the morning, this was very practical. The Rabbi said that the dissenting views are two very minority opinions, and he believed there is enough to say Shehiyanu; it depends whom a person follows. He mentioned that the Gemara calls rain at the start of Sukkot a bad sign, and hoped it would not come.

The second night and the second day

He put a question to the class. If you sat in the sukkah on the first night and day, but it rained the whole second night, do you say Shehiyanu when you first sit in the sukkah on the second day? After all, we treat the second day as possibly the real first day.

His answer: no. Shehiyanu on the sukkah can be said even on a weekday, as it can be said when building. So even if the first day were an ordinary weekday, the Shehiyanu said in the sukkah then still covers the sukkah.

Someone asked: then why do we not say Shehiyanu on the lulav on the second day? He answered that Shehiyanu on the lulav may also be said when binding it, so the same principle applies.

A last case: if you ate the first night in someone else's sukkah, do you say Shehiyanu in your own? The vast majority of poskim say no; the berakhah is on the misvah, not on your particular sukkah. The simplest way to avoid doubt is to take the arbaah minim in the sukkah and have the sukkah in mind with that Shehiyanu as well. Many Sefaradim take the arbaah minim in the sukkah, a custom from the Zohar. The Spanish and Portuguese custom is to take them in the synagogue.

Kiddush on Sukkot

Siman 642: when the first night falls on Shabbat (not this year), the berakhah after the Amidah on Friday night ends only "Mekaddesh HaShabbat". It was instituted for latecomers on Shabbat specifically.

Siman 643, the order of Kiddush. We follow Beit Hillel that the berakhah on the wine comes before the Kiddush. On the first night the order is:

  1. Borei Peri HaGefen
  2. Mekaddesh Yisrael VeHaZemanim
  3. Leshev BaSukkah
  4. Shehiyanu

Leshev comes before Shehiyanu so that Shehiyanu covers both the festival and the sukkah. On the second night the order changes: Shehiyanu comes before Leshev BaSukkah, because now it is said only for the festival. The sukkah's Shehiyanu was already said the first night, and works even if that was a weekday.

Standing and sitting. Normally, when one person says a berakhah for others, everyone should sit, since sitting makes a group. We stand for Kiddush on Friday night only to honour the arrival of Shabbat. On Sukkot it is different. The Rambam holds that the misvah of sukkah is dwelling, and the berakhah must come before the misvah. Leshev means both "to dwell" and "to sit". So one stands for Kiddush, says Leshev BaSukkah, and only then sits. On the first night, everyone then sits in silence and says Shehiyanu together. The Rosh, who holds that the main misvah is eating, has everyone sit for the whole Kiddush, which is the Ashkenazi custom.

The Rabbi set out the practice for Sefaradim, following the Shulhan Arukh and the Rambam: - First night: stand for Borei Peri HaGefen, Mekaddesh and Leshev BaSukkah; sit; then Shehiyanu together. - Second night: stand for the whole Kiddush, including Shehiyanu and Leshev BaSukkah; then sit and drink. - Friday night and Shabbat morning: stand until after Leshev BaSukkah, even though on Shabbat morning we normally sit.

The Bah held that one who cannot sit at all may not say Leshev BaSukkah. The majority disagree, and so we hold: the misvah is to reside, and sitting is only its usual form.

Meals without Kiddush. On Hol HaMoed, or on Shabbat afternoon, the Shulhan Arukh's main ruling is Leshev BaSukkah first, then Hamosi, since dwelling is the misvah that faces you first. He also quotes a custom, from the Rosh, of Hamosi first. The Rabbi's view is to follow the Shulhan Arukh's first ruling, as the Ben Ish Hai does, to show that sukkah is about much more than bread.

Someone added that this only applies when eating a kebeisah of bread. He agreed, and repeated his own view: if you will not eat a kebeisah of bread in the sukkah all day, he finds it very difficult not to say Leshev BaSukkah, and would say it.

Should women answer Amen?

Sefaradi women do not say Leshev BaSukkah. Rav Ovadia Yoseph therefore ruled that when the husband says Kiddush, women should not answer Amen to Leshev BaSukkah: between Kiddush and drinking one may only say what belongs to the Kiddush, and for them it is an interruption. Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul disagreed: women still have a misvah to sit in the sukkah and are connected to it, so it is no interruption, and they should answer Amen. The Rabbi said he hears Hakham Ben Sion's argument strongly.

Practical summary

  • No berakhah on building a sukkah; its Shehiyanu is included in the Shehiyanu of the first night's Kiddush.
  • If rain kept you out of the sukkah the first night: most poskim say Shehiyanu when you first eat in it; Rav Ovadia Yoseph rules not to. The Rabbi felt there is enough to say it.
  • If you sat in the sukkah on the first day, do not repeat Shehiyanu for the sukkah on the second day.
  • First night: wine, Kiddush, Leshev, Shehiyanu. Second night: wine, Kiddush, Shehiyanu, Leshev.
  • Sefaradim stand for Kiddush on Sukkot and sit after Leshev BaSukkah.
  • At other meals, say Leshev BaSukkah before Hamosi.
