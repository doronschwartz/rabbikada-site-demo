Topic: Neutering female animals, in practice and in veterinary teaching

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Taz (Turei Zahav); Rav Moshe Feinstein; the Teshuvot Bemareh HaBazak

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Females are less difficult: most poskim hold that neutering a female, even directly on the organs, is only rabbinically forbidden. This is the ruling today and seemingly of the Shulhan Arukh.
  • A minority held it is Doraita for females too; the Taz held it permitted, avoided only because it pains the animal. Rav Moshe Feinstein notes these are minority opinions not relied upon.
  • So a Jewish vet or student may not neuter the organs of a female directly, though the prohibition is rabbinic.
  • Since direct neutering of a female is only rabbinic, indirect neutering (kos shel ikkarin) is a notch lower and is permitted. Radiation or chemotherapy that stops the organs reproducing is fine, as long as the organs themselves are not touched.
  • Direct neutering remains forbidden for any client, Jewish or not. Transferring ownership was discussed but seems rejected.
  • Teaching: a Jewish veterinary college teaching chemotherapy or radiation for neutering should use female animals, and can then teach it to Jewish and non-Jewish students.
  • Next: what non-Jews at a Jewish college may be asked to do.

Sources quoted

  • Taz: neutering a female is permitted in principle; rejected as a minority view.
  • Teshuvot Bemareh HaBazak: use female animals when teaching indirect neutering.

In one line

A Jewish vet may neuter a female animal indirectly, by radiation or chemotherapy, but not by operating on the organs.
