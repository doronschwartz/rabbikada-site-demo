Topic: The two opinions in the Gemara on the child of a non-Jewish man and a Jewish woman

Sources: Mishnah and Gemara Kiddushin

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 30 April 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The child of a non-Jewish man and a Jewish woman could in theory be a non-Jew, a Jew, a mamzer, or a Jew with certain disqualifications.
  • The Gemara has two main opinions: the child is a mamzer, or the child is kasher, a regular Yisrael. Both rest on the Mishnah in Kiddushin.
  • The Mishnah: where a woman cannot contract kiddushin with this man but can with others, the child is a mamzer. The classic example is brother and sister, where the kiddushin does not even take effect, unlike, for instance, a Kohen and a divorcee, whose marriage takes effect though forbidden.
  • Opinion one applies this to a non-Jewish man: she could marry other Jews, but there is no kiddushin with him at all, so the child is a Jewish mamzer, which is very severe.
  • The Mishnah also says: where she cannot contract kiddushin with him or anyone else, the child follows her. The example is a Jewish man with a non-Jewish woman, whose child is certainly not Jewish; that is explicit in the Mishnah and agreed by all.
  • Opinion two applies this principle: a non-Jewish man cannot contract kiddushin with any Jewish woman, so the child follows the mother and is fully Jewish and kasher.
  • A mamzer can marry very few people and is tainted for all generations. How the Rishonim rule follows next.

In one line

The Gemara debates whether the child of a non-Jewish father and Jewish mother is a mamzer or a kasher Jew, based on two principles of the Mishnah.
