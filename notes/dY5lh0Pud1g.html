Topic: Amalek as the philosophy of doubt and moral relativism

Sources: Mishlei 26; Shemuel (Shaul and Amalek)

Daily Halacha: Purim, Thursday 21 March 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Hebrew has several words for a fool, each distinct: tipesh, shoteh, kesil and evil. In Mishlei 26 Shelomo HaMelekh directs his anger at the evil, in some ways the worst.
  • The evil doubts everything as a matter of principle: constant scepticism is enlightenment, there are only questions, nothing is objectively true, any behaviour can be justified, and only believers in truth must be fought.
  • Evil (aleph, vav, yud, lamed) has the letters of ulai, "maybe".
  • We can no longer identify Amalek's descendants, but Amalek's philosophy of doubt, randomness and chance remains our archenemy. The gematria of Amalek, 240, equals safek, doubt.
  • Amalek attacked after the Exodus to show the nations we were not invincible, and later tried to confuse us by speaking another language, perhaps dressing as another nation.
  • Lack of conviction was the downfall of Shaul HaMelekh, who doubted Hashem's instruction and did not destroy Amalek, which later brought the danger of Haman.
  • In a world full of doubt, where even clear things are questioned, remembering Amalek means an unswerving commitment to pursuing truth and the right course of action.

In one line

Amalek is doubt itself, and remembering Amalek means living with conviction in the pursuit of truth.
