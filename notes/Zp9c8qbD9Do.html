Topic: What Bedikat Hames requires, and what it does not

Sources: The Torah and the Hakhamim; the Mishnah Berurah

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Thursday 11 March 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Misconceptions about Bedikat Hames leave people stressed and exhausted before Pesah; it should be done properly but not beyond what is required.
  • It is a rabbinic misvah. From the Torah, declaring oneself mafkir, abandoning ownership of one's hames, suffices.
  • The Hakhamim required searching and removal for two reasons: one may not nullify wholeheartedly (would someone with expensive whisky truly let another take it?), and one may be tempted to eat it on Pesah.
  • The main halacha, followed by Sepharadim and seemingly by the Mishnah Berurah for Ashkenazim, is that one searches for a kazayit, about 18 to 27 grams, roughly half a slice of bread. Crumbs are not part of the misvah; they are covered by the nullification on the night of the search and on Erev Pesah.
  • So in rooms where one will not eat on Pesah, look only for sizeable pieces; taking things apart for tiny crumbs is unnecessary. Spring cleaning is fine, but do not blame Pesah for it.
  • Places where hames is not usually brought, such as a bedroom where no one eats, need only a quick scan. Where children go, check whatever they can reach, again for pieces, not crumbs. The house outside the kitchen and dining room can take about an hour.
  • In the kitchen and where one will eat, be more meticulous, so that even crumbs do not fall into Pesah food.

Sources quoted

  • The Torah and the Hakhamim: nullification suffices biblically; searching is rabbinic.
  • The Mishnah Berurah: the search is for a kazayit.

In one line

Bedikat Hames is a search for pieces of hames the size of a kazayit, not crumbs, except where food will be prepared or eaten on Pesah.
