Topic: The secondary misvah of continuing to have children (Shevet)

Sources: Yeshayahu ("He did not create it to be empty, He formed it to be inhabited"); the Gemara; the Rambam; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 29 January 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Torah misvah is at least one son and one daughter. Hazal add a lower-level misvah, from Yeshayahu: HaShem formed the world to be inhabited. Even after a son and a daughter one should continue, "sow in the morning, and in the evening do not rest your hand".
  • The Gemara's reason: you never know what will happen with your children (whether they survive, are fertile, continue the line), especially when early death was common.
  • The Rambam gives a different reason: every child is a world, so the more children, the greater the misvah. This applies even today, when children rarely die before their parents.
  • Being rabbinic and lower in level, it could not be enforced as the main misvah could. The Rema rules that a man who has a son and a daughter and fears strife between children of two marriages may marry a woman who cannot have children.
  • Whether there is a limit, and whether it applies to women, comes next.

In one line

After a son and a daughter there is a further, rabbinic misvah to keep having children, for continuity (the Gemara) and because each child is a world (the Rambam).
