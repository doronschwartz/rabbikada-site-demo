Topic: Spending and borrowing for the festival, including the vulnerable, and the absence of sadness on the hag

Sources: The Gemara, Besah; Shulhan Arukh; the poskim; Pirkei Avot; the Rambam; the Zohar; the Hakhamim

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 20 May 2025

The halacha

  • In the Gemara's time the husband bought clothes for his wife; today she does so herself. The point is a joint venture: he invests in meat and wine if he likes them, she in jewellery or clothes, something special for the hag.
  • The Gemara in Besah says a person's income is fixed each year, but what he spends on Shabbat and Yom Tov is not counted. So one should not skimp but spend nicely on what one enjoys.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules, following the Hakhamim ("Lavu alai vaani porea"), that one may buy on credit or borrow for the festival. The poskim qualify this: only with a rational way to repay. Borrowing with no intention or means to repay is forbidden, as Pirkei Avot says.
  • The Rambam is crucial: the Torah always speaks of the widow, the convert and the stranger. One who feasts extravagantly with his own family but does not think of the lonely and the poor is not rejoicing in the misvah but "rejoicing in his stomach". The Zohar says strongly that HaKadosh Barukh Hu rejects such celebration.
  • So one should invite those in need to the festival meals, or if that is impossible, give them money to rejoice.
  • Simhah means avoiding sadness, so there is no avelut on the hag: if, God forbid, someone loses a relative, the funeral takes place but shivah begins after the hag.
  • The Hakhamim forbade weddings on Hol HaMoed, because we do not mix two celebrations and a wedding would take the focus from the hag. (On Shabbat and Yom Tov itself a wedding is impossible, lest one write the ketubah.)
  • Rosh Hashanah is among the Yamim Tovim and also has a misvah of simhah, though not at the same level.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara, Besah: festival spending is not counted against one's fixed income.
  • The Rambam: a festival meal that excludes the vulnerable is only rejoicing in one's stomach.

In one line

Spend generously on the hag, even on credit if you can repay, but true simhah includes the lonely and the poor.
