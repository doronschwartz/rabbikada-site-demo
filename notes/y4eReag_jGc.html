Topic: Whether owning shares in a company that holds Hames is a problem on Pesah

Sources: Rabbi David Hoffmann; the Gemara; Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Pesah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Owning Hames anywhere in the world is a Torah problem. The question is whether a shareholder in a company dealing in Hames is a partner in its assets, or more like a creditor.
  • Rabbi David Hoffmann, in Germany some 150 years ago, writes that this was set as an exam question, seemingly for Semikhah, and the answer was to be lenient.
  • The basis is the Gemara's case of Hames in a snake's mouth: technically still one's own, but with no practical access one need not retrieve it.
  • So too a shareholder with no say in voting or management has no practical access to the Hames itself. Most poskim conclude he may be lenient.
  • Some were concerned and included shares in the sale of Hames. Strictly it is permitted anyway, so including them is fine.
  • A significant shareholding, which Rav Moshe Feinstein put at 30% and above, is a more serious issue. Selling shares is more complicated than selling food, so one should seek rabbinic advice.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: Hames in a snake's mouth.

In one line

An ordinary shareholder need not worry about Hames over Pesah, but a major shareholder should seek rabbinic guidance.
