Topic: A child whose Kohen status is doubtful because children were mixed up

Sources: The Gemara; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 21 April 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Since today we require no proof from one who says he is a Kohen, a major question follows: to what extent is a Kohen today a real Kohen? That begins tomorrow.
  • First, the Gemara discusses Jewish children who were mixed up, so it is unknown whether a child's father is a Kohen or a Yisrael.
  • The Gemara, Rambam and Shulhan Arukh rule that he takes on the stringencies of both. As a Yisrael he gets none of a Kohen's privileges: no Terumah, no aliyah as a Kohen.
  • As a Kohen he keeps a Kohen's restrictions: he may not marry a divorcee or become impure through a dead body.
  • If two children were mixed up and both fathers are certainly Kohanim, in most cases both are treated as full Kohanim, even though they will not know who their father is.
  • The exception is children conceived outside marriage: there a Kohen must know not only that he is a Kohen but who his father is.

In one line

A child of doubtful Kohen status keeps the stringencies of both a Kohen and a Yisrael, while mixed-up children of two Kohanim are generally full Kohanim.
