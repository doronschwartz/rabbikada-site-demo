Topic: The first night obligation to eat in the sukkah, and women and the Berakhah Leshev BaSukkah

Sources: Shulhan Arukh (laws of shofar); the Rama

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Sunday 9 October 2022

The halacha

  • Eating in the sukkah on the first night (and outside Israel the second) is on par with eating Massah on the first nights of Pesah.
  • As with Massah, the rest of the festival carries no obligation: one could eat fruit and other foods that do not require a sukkah. Only bread, cake and the like require it.
  • On the first night there is a Torah obligation to eat at least a kezayit of bread in a sukkah, wherever one is and even if one has no sukkah of one's own.
  • The difference from Pesah: this applies only to men. Sukkah is a positive time-bound misvah, so women are exempt; on Pesah women are obligated because they too were part of the miracle of the Exodus.
  • May a woman who sits in the sukkah say the berakhah? Shulhan Arukh (laws of shofar) rules she may do the misvah and is rewarded, but may not say "asher kiddeshanu bemisvotav vesivanu", nor may others say it for her, since she was not commanded.
  • The Rama, for Ashkenazim, holds that by wanting to do the misvah she obligates herself and may say the berakhah, which Ashkenazi women do.
  • Among Sepharadim, despite the Shulhan Arukh, women in Iraq, the Spanish and Portuguese, and many Moroccan communities say the berakhah. A woman with a clearly established family custom may follow it; one unaware of her custom should follow the Shulhan Arukh and not say it.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: a woman may not say a berakhah on a time-bound misvah she is exempt from.
  • Rama: she may, since she takes the obligation on herself.

In one line

Men must eat a kezayit of bread in the sukkah on the first night; Sepharadi women without an established custom sit in the sukkah without the berakhah.
