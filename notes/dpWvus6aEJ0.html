Topic: The duties of parents to their children, including the limits of punishment

Sources: The Talmud; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; "Makkeh aviv veimo mot yumat"

Daily Halacha: Honouring Parents, Thursday 4 February 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The last note of the series turns to what parents owe children. The Talmud warns against instilling too much fear in a home: home must feel safe and calm.
  • Do not ask children for things you know they will refuse, which only sets them up to fail. If unsure, ask indirectly and let them choose; usually they will do it.
  • In principle a parent may physically discipline a child, but only for hinukh (education). Striking another Jew is otherwise forbidden.
  • Hitting in anger has no educational value and is strictly forbidden. It is allowed only from love, with minimal force; the Rambam speaks of a small, gentle strap. Not straight after the act, but later, sitting the child down and explaining why it was serious.
  • The Shulhan Arukh forbids hitting a child old enough to retaliate (certainly from about 10 or 11), so as not to put a stumbling block before him: a child who wounds a parent is liable to death ("makkeh aviv veimo"). At that age any force is forbidden.
  • Only where the law of the land allows it (for example in England; Scotland differs).

In one line

Parents must keep a calm home, avoid commands they know will be refused, and never punish in anger; any physical discipline must be rare, gentle, loving, lawful, and never once a child can hit back.
