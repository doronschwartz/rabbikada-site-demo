Topic: Is hames absorbed during the year a permitted absorption (Hetera Bala) or a forbidden one?

Sources: The Gemara (spits of the sacrifices); the Rambam; Rabbenu Tam; the Rif; Shulhan Arukh; the Mishnah Berurah

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Thursday 27 March 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Normally a utensil that absorbed forbidden taste in a dry setting needs Libbun. The Gemara makes an exception for the spits used with the sacrifices in the Beit HaMikdash: Hagalah suffices, though they absorbed dry and the taste later became forbidden.
  • The reason is Hetera Bala: at the time of absorption the taste was permitted. Libbun is needed only when the taste was already forbidden when absorbed, such as non-kosher meat or meat and milk.
  • The question for Pesah is whether hames absorbed during the year counts as Hetera Bala, since it was not forbidden then.
  • The Rambam (and Rabbenu Tam) held it is Hetera Bala, so Hagalah would always suffice for Pesah and Libbun would never be needed.
  • Other Rishonim, the Rif included, held it is Issura Bala: it already bears the name hames. The Shulhan Arukh rules strictly like this, hence the practical distinction between Libbun and Hagalah.
  • Where Libbun is difficult and there are other reasons to be lenient, many poskim, as seen in the Mishnah Berurah, combine the view of the Rambam and Rabbenu Tam with other principles to permit. This will come up with practical appliances.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: spits of the sacrifices need only Hagalah because of Hetera Bala.
  • The Rambam and Rabbenu Tam: hames absorbed during the year is Hetera Bala.
  • The Rif: it is Issura Bala.
  • Shulhan Arukh: rules strictly, requiring Libbun for dry absorption of hames.

In one line

The Shulhan Arukh treats year-round hames as a forbidden absorption needing Libbun when dry, but the Rambam's lenient view is used together with other reasons where needed.
