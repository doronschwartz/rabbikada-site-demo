Topic: The status of maaser kesafim and how it relates to sedakah

Sources: Tosafot; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the Torah (Avraham, Yishak, Yaakov); Shulhan Arukh; Tosafot in Gemara Taanit

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Wednesday 2 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The third concept, after the yearly minimum and giving when asked, is maaser: a tenth of one's income.
  • Its origin is debated: Tosafot hold it is from the Torah; others say it is rabbinic; Hakham Ovadiah and most poskim hold it is a minhag, an ancient custom going back to the Avot.
  • Avraham gave maaser to Shem son of Noah after the war with the four kings; Yishak found "mea shearim", a hundredfold, in Parashat Toledot, from which the Hakhamim learn he gave maaser; Yaakov in Vayese vowed to give a tenth of all he would be given.
  • That it is a minhag is evident from the Shulhan Arukh not codifying it anywhere. Still, it is ancient and was always taken very seriously.
  • Tosafot in Taanit record a story: a man always gave maaser of a field yielding 1,000 and asked his son to continue. The son gave 100 the first year, then only 10, and the field's yield fell to 100; his relatives explained it was because he had abandoned the minhag.
  • There are two reasons to give a tenth: it is recommended under the misvah of sedakah itself, beyond the bare minimum, and there is the ancient minhag of maaser.
  • Because it is a minhag, in a very difficult year one fulfils sedakah by giving something smaller, provided one gives when asked.

In one line

Maaser is an ancient and important minhag rather than an obligation, so one should strive for a tenth but may give less in genuine hardship.
