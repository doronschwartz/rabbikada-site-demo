Topic: Whether to count the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy on one's fingers

Sources: The Ben Ish Hai; the Arizal; the Rambam; Rav Ovadia Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Elul, Thursday 8 September 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Thirteen Attributes are the key part of Selihot; the Gemara says they never return empty-handed.
  • The Ben Ish Hai writes that it is a fine custom to count them on one's fingers, one by one, as many Sefaradim do. It shows love for the misvah and helps concentration on each word. He writes the same for the thirteen expressions of praise in Yishtabah and the eleven spices of the Ketoret.
  • Rav Ovadia Yoseph agrees for Yishtabah and the Ketoret, but for the Thirteen Attributes he says it is better not to count on the fingers.
  • His reason: it is not clear which words are the thirteen. The Ben Ish Hai follows the Arizal, counting from "El", with "Erekh" and "Apayim" as two. For the Rambam the first "HaShem" is an introduction and the second is the first attribute. Others count each "HaShem" separately. Counting on the fingers decides between the opinions.
  • Those who have the custom of counting should continue it; those who do not should simply say the words with kavanah.

Sources quoted

  • Ben Ish Hai: counting on the fingers shows love of the misvah and aids focus.
  • Rav Ovadia Yoseph: better not to count the Thirteen Attributes, since their division is disputed.

In one line

Counting the Thirteen Attributes on one's fingers is a fine custom for those who have it, but since their count is disputed, Rav Ovadia Yoseph advises others not to.
