Topic: "Aser teaser", the promise that maaser brings wealth, and why it does not always seem so

Sources: Gemara (Rabbi Yohanan and the son of Resh Lakish); Parashat Reeh; Mishnah, Pirkei Avot

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Thursday 10 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Rabbi Yohanan and Resh Lakish, third century Amoraim, were brothers-in-law and study partners. Rabbi Yohanan asked Resh Lakish's young son what he had learnt, and the boy asked why the verse in Reeh doubles "aser teaser".
  • Rabbi Yohanan answered with a play on words: tithe so that you become wealthy (ashir). Asked how he knew, he said "try it". The boy objected that one may not test Hashem, and Rabbi Yohanan replied that this is the one misvah where one may. Some suggest this applies even today.
  • Yet many meticulous givers of maaser are not wealthy. His most straightforward answer: maaser does bring wealth, but one cannot build a philosophy on a single statement. The Gemara lists things that bring poverty (treading on bread crumbs, lending with interest, vowing sedakah and not giving), which can cancel it out. "Words of Torah are poor in one place and rich in another": Torah is understood only when it is all put together.
  • Others say the promise applied to tithing produce, not maaser kesafim, which today is a custom.
  • A third view: the wealth is that of Olam HaBa.
  • A fourth, which he also prefers: wealth is mostly financial here, but it also means much more, as the Mishnah in Avot says the truly wealthy person is content with his lot.

In one line

Maaser does bring wealth, but other factors can cancel it out, and true wealth is not only financial.
