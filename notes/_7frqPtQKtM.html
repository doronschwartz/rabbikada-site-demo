Topic: Washing clothes for children, bathing, showering and swimming in the week of Tisha BeAv

Sources: The Gemara; Hazon Ovadiah; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Tuesday 18 July 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • By the din of the Gemara, haircuts, shaving and laundry are forbidden only in the week of Tisha BeAv, which is the minhag of the Sepharadim; Ashkenazim are stricter (haircuts for the three weeks, laundry from Rosh Hodesh).
  • Children who regularly dirty themselves may have their clothes washed and wear fresh clothes. Hazon Ovadiah says up to three, other poskim six, some eight; it depends on the child. Over eight one should refrain.
  • Washing oneself is forbidden by the Gemara only on Tisha BeAv itself. Already in the times of the Rishonim this was extended to the week of Tisha BeAv (Sepharadim), and by Ashkenazim to the nine days. Shulhan Arukh stresses care with each community's accepted minhagim here.
  • The context has changed: the bathhouse was a whole day's treat (one may not go before Minhah), whereas today a quick daily shower is basic hygiene. A relaxing bath is forbidden unless there is a medical need.
  • One who showers daily or every second day and cannot function without it may do so, since it is not washing for pleasure: briefly, with lukewarm water, mindful of the period. Human dignity, which can override certain restrictions, requires one not to have body odour.
  • Swimming purely for leisure is forbidden all nine days. For health it seems permitted for Ashkenazim until the week of Tisha BeAv, but in that week he does not think it permitted, unless medically required (physical or mental health).

In one line

In the week of Tisha BeAv one avoids bathing for pleasure, but a short lukewarm shower for hygiene, or swimming on medical advice, is permitted.
