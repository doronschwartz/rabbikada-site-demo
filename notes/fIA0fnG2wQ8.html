Topic: Rav Moshe Feinstein's ruling on the child of a woman who stayed with her second husband after the Holocaust

Sources: Rav Moshe Feinstein; the Rambam; Tosafot

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 28 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The 1973 case is exactly the dispute between the Rambam and Tosafot: after her first husband gave her a get, she stayed with man two and had children. The question arose when a child, some 20 to 25 years old, came to marry.
  • The Rambam: the child is kasher. Staying with man two was forbidden, since the Hakhamim required her to divorce both men, but that sin has no effect on the child, neither from the Torah nor rabbinically.
  • Tosafot: the child is a mamzer miderabbanan, a penalty (knas) for remarrying on the lenient evidence.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein's first reason: this is a doubt in a rabbinic matter, so one may be lenient and rely on the Rambam. The child may marry a regular Jewish woman.
  • His second, more creative reason: from the details of the story he argued she could not have received a proper heter, so marrying man two was wrong from the start, an adulterous relationship.
  • Tosafot's penalty applies only where the Beit Din permitted her to remarry and it later proved wrong. In plain adultery there is no such penalty, so after the get the child is kasher even according to Tosafot. Paradoxically, the penalty exists only where we permitted her.
  • In this true case the child was allowed to marry.

Sources quoted
