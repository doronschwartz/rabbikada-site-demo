Topic: The berakhah "shekakhah lo beolamo" on seeing beautiful creatures

Sources: The Talmud (Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel); Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the later poskim

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Thursday 30 January 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • One who sees beautiful creatures blesses "shekakhah lo beolamo", that such things exist in His world. The Gemara includes beautiful people, animals and even trees.
  • Beauty is highly subjective, so this berakhah is very personal: for something truly striking that takes one aback with its splendour. The Gemara speaks of "beriyot tovot"; the Rambam describes creatures that are beautiful and exceptionally well formed.
  • It is said only the first time one sees the creature.
  • For example, one who sees a peacock in person for the first time, having known it only from pictures or video, and is struck by its beauty, says the berakhah.
  • The same applies to a person who strikes one as exceptionally beautiful on first sight. This is codified in the Rambam, Shulhan Arukh and later poskim.
  • It is uncommon because it is hard to define, and people fear saying an unwarranted berakhah.
  • The Gemara tells that Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel said it on a beautiful woman he passed by chance, without intending to look. Gazing at a woman is strictly forbidden, but when one sees in a permissible way and is struck, it is justified, indeed a misvah, to bless.

In one line

When truly struck by the beauty of a tree, animal or person seen for the first time, in a permissible way, one says "shekakhah lo beolamo".
