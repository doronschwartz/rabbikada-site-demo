Topic: Whom each category of mamzer may marry, and why there is no mamzerut among non-Jews

Sources: The Mishnah; the Seven Misvot of the Children of Noah; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Wednesday 10 June 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A mamzer from the Torah may marry another mamzer from the Torah.
  • A rabbinic mamzer is not a mamzer from the Torah, so may not marry a Torah mamzer; only another rabbinic mamzer, or a ger or giyoret.
  • A safek mamzer may not marry a Torah mamzer, a rabbinic mamzer, or even another safek mamzer, since each may in fact be kasher. As the Mishnah says, and this is the halacha, a safek mamzer may marry only a convert.
  • There is no mamzerut among non-Jews. They are bound by the Seven Misvot of the Children of Noah, including forbidden relations; and though they have no kiddushin, a couple committed to living together are forbidden to commit adultery.
  • Yet a non-Jewish child born of such adultery is not a mamzer. The reason given, brought also in the Shulhan Arukh: the Torah placed the blemish of mamzerut only on Jews. If the child converts, he is a full ger and not a mamzer.
  • A second reason: a convert is considered a new entity, so even if he were a mamzer as a non-Jew, once converted he is kasher and may marry a regular Jew.

In one line

A safek mamzer may marry only a convert, and since there is no mamzerut among non-Jews, a convert from an adulterous union may marry a regular Jew.
