Topic: May an Israeli visiting abroad have an aliyah on the second day of Yom Tov, or be Hatan Torah or Hatan Bereshit?

Sources: The Havot Yair; the Hida

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Wednesday 9 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A visitor from Israel keeps one day, does no melakhah in public, puts on tefillin and then goes to the synagogue. The question arises when a community wants to honour such a guest as Hatan Torah or Hatan Bereshit, which outside Israel falls on the second day, Simhat Torah.
  • If the second day is Hol HaMoed for him (second day of Sukkot or Pesah), there is a Torah reading in Israel too, so he may be called up.
  • On a final second day (eighth day of Pesah, second day of Shavuot) that is not a Monday, Thursday or Shabbat, there is no reading for him. He should make every effort not to be called up; the Havot Yair writes he should decline and explain. Some poskim say that if called he may go up.
  • If that day is a Monday, Thursday or Shabbat it is a reading day for him anyway, so he may be called up, and there is more to rely on for being Hatan Torah or Hatan Bereshit.
  • The Hida, about 250 years ago, wrote that on a non-reading day he certainly may not be Hatan Torah or Hatan Bereshit, just as one not fasting on a fast day, even for medical reasons, is not called up, since the reading comes from the fast.
  • Some poskim permitted it because of the joy of Simhat Torah, when many communities call everyone up. He calls this a very weak argument; the consensus is that one with the status of a ben Eres Yisrael should not be given these honours.

Sources quoted

  • The Havot Yair: one called up on a non-reading day should decline the aliyah.
  • The Hida: no Hatan Torah or Hatan Bereshit on a day that is not a reading day for him, compared to one not fasting on a fast day.

In one line

A visiting Israeli may be called up on the second day only if it is a Torah reading day for him anyway, and should generally not be Hatan Torah or Hatan Bereshit.
