Topic: How one who cannot fast for medical reasons should eat and drink on Yom Kippur

Sources: A story of Rav Hayyim of Brisk

Daily Halacha: Yom Kippur, Thursday 29 September 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Rav Hayyim of Brisk, strict in most matters, was lenient on Yom Kippur for anyone who needed to break the fast. He explained he was being strict about the Misvah of guarding one's health. One told medically to eat should not feel he is doing wrong: just as Hashem wants the healthy to fast, He wants those who medically cannot to eat.
  • We rely on a doctor, even a non-Jewish one. If one doctor says the person must eat and another says not, we follow the one who says to eat.
  • Even if all doctors say there is no danger, if the person genuinely feels it would be dangerous, he may eat and drink, since only he knows how he feels.
  • In immediate danger to life one eats as much as needed. But one who knows from the start that he cannot fast should spread out small amounts, not three meals.
  • The severe punishment of Karet applies only to eating food the size of a date (over about 30 grams), or drinking 44 ml or more, within about five minutes. Less, or the same over a longer span, is still a Torah prohibition without Karet.
  • So such a person should eat under about 30 grams of food and under 40 ml of liquid, then wait about 10 minutes before eating and drinking again.
  • Measuring is generally not allowed on Shabbat, Yom Tov or Yom Kippur, so measure beforehand. If that was not possible, one may measure with a scale or measuring cylinder on Yom Kippur.

In one line

A person who medically must eat on Yom Kippur is doing Hashem's will, and unless in immediate danger should eat in small measured amounts spread through the day.
