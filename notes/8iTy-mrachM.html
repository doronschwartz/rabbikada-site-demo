Topic: Music and dancing during the Three Weeks, and what remains permitted for Sepharadim

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rama; the later poskim

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Tuesday 6 July 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Recap: the Shulhan Arukh records two things for the Three Weeks: one must avoid danger, and it is good to refrain from Shehiyanu if it can be said after Tisha BeAv.
  • A third, recorded much later in the poskim, is music and dancing. The Shulhan Arukh omits it because elsewhere he rules music questionable all year round.
  • The Rama is more lenient, so Ashkenazi poskim write that at least in the Three Weeks one should refrain. The later poskim write that Sepharadim, though lenient during the year, should at least follow this Ashkenazi custom now.
  • One whose livelihood is teaching or performing music has what to rely on to continue, even until the week of Tisha BeAv itself if there is great need, though preferably he stops from Rosh Hodesh.
  • Where there is a misvah, such as a Brit Milah, Bar Misvah or wedding, music and dancing are fully permitted; the custom covers only music that is not for a misvah.
  • Everything else until Rosh Hodesh Av is permitted for Sepharadim, including haircuts and weddings, especially when delay would cause problems such as finding a venue. Refraining became the Ashkenazi custom, never the Sepharadi one.
  • The Nine Days begin on Rosh Hodesh Av (this year on Shabbat) and end on Tisha BeAv; here the Gemara itself restricts activities of simhah.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: music is questionable all year round.
  • The Rama: more lenient on music during the year.
  • The later poskim: Sepharadim should refrain from music in the Three Weeks.

In one line

Sepharadim refrain from music and dancing in the Three Weeks unless it is for a misvah or livelihood, while haircuts and weddings stay permitted until Rosh Hodesh Av.
