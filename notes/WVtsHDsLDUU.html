Topic: The significance of Birkat HaLevanah and whether women say it

Sources: The Gemara; Rambam; the Meiri; Shulhan Arukh; the Magen Avraham

Daily Halacha: Birkat Kohanim & Prayer, Monday 16 October 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Hakhamim gave berakhot on parts of nature (the sea, certain mountains, the 28 year solar cycle) and also on the moon, which we see renewed each month.
  • The Hakhamim say one who blesses the moon is as if receiving the Shekhinah; in the Gemara and in Kabbalah the moon relates to the Shekhinah, which receives, as the moon receives light from the sun. So it is said with great joy and, as the Gemara and Rambam stress, standing.
  • The simple reading of the Gemara is that women are obligated; the Meiri says so clearly and the Rambam implies it. The Shulhan Arukh is silent.
  • Why is it not a time-bound misvah? Because it has no set time of its own; something external (the moon waning) prevents saying it later. Contrast the sukkah, which is tied to a set time.
  • The Magen Avraham, about 400 years ago, wrote that women should not say it. Later Aharonim explain that in Kabbalah the woman is compared to the moon, and since Havah caused the sin, she caused the moon to be diminished. The custom has become widespread that women do not say it.
  • A woman who wishes to say it has what to rely on, since the simple understanding is that women may.

In one line

Birkat HaLevanah is said standing and with joy by every male, and while women customarily do not say it, one who wishes to has what to rely on.
