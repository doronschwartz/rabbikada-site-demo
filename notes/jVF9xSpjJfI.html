Topic: Saying Birkat HaLevanah when clouds cover the moon, and going outside to say it

Sources: The Radbaz; the Hida; the Ben Ish Hai; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Birkat Kohanim & Prayer, Wednesday 18 October 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Since the berakhah needs benefit from the moon's light, it is not said when thick clouds conceal the moon.
  • The Radbaz adds that a thin cloud is permitted, since one still benefits from the light. This is the main halacha and the ruling and custom of Ashkenazim, as long as the moon is not completely concealed.
  • The Hida and the Ben Ish Hai wrote to be stringent and say it only when the moon is completely clear. This is a humrah; strictly speaking a thin cloud is enough.
  • We Sepharadim are stringent in the first instance: early in the month, wait a few more days. But by the 11th or 12th, if one fears missing it before the 15th, say it even through a thin cloud.
  • The Rema brings that we go outside to say it. The moon, which waxes and wanes, is compared to the Shekhinah in exile and to human life; just as we turn to receive Shabbat at the end of Lekha Dodi, we go out to receive the Shekhinah.
  • If going out is not possible (cold, old age, a block of flats), it may be said indoors, but one should see the moon directly with the window open, not through the glass.

In one line

Sepharadim ideally wait for a clear moon but may bless through a thin cloud late in the month, and one should go outside or at least see the moon through an open window.
