Topic: The misvah of simhah on the festival: each person in what suits them, meat and wine, and fine clothes

The halacha

  • Beyond Torah and oneg, the festivals have a din not found on Shabbat: simhah, rejoicing with family and others. Since "be happy" is vague, the Gemara gives guidance.
  • Rabbi Yehudah ben Beteira: each rejoices in what befits them, men, women and even children, since the Torah says "you, your son and your daughter". Men with certain foods, women with colourful clothes (bigdei sivonim), children with sweets and nuts.
  • When the Beit HaMikdash stood, the main simhah was the meat of the korban hagigah. Now, says the Gemara, "ein simhah ela beyayin": for men the primary simhah is wine, which lifts the spirit, and there is an obligation of wine on the hag. It was taken so seriously that one Amora, asked a question on Yom Tov about an egg laid that day, said he had drunk for the festival and to come back tomorrow.
  • The Rambam also writes to have meat. The commentators explain wine is primary but meat is still a misvah, so given a choice between expensive meat and expensive wine, the wine comes first.
  • All this applies only if one enjoys it: there is no need to force oneself, as the purpose is to be happy. A woman who enjoys meat and wine also fulfils the misvah with them.
  • Men also have a misvah of fine clothes: the Shulhan Arukh rules Yom Tov clothes should be nicer than Shabbat clothes, so a new suit is best kept to wear first on the hag. The categories are interchangeable.
  • Some poskim say meat and wine apply at both the night and day meals, especially relevant on Shavuot. A full milky meal on Shavuot is then not necessarily correct practice: the original minhag was a milky dish, with the meals still full, festive meat meals. More tomorrow.

In one line

Rejoice on the festival in what truly gladdens you, chiefly wine and meat for men and fine clothes for women, interchangeably, and treats for children.
