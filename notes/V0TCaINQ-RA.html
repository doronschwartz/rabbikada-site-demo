Topic: Why the people could climb Har Sinai straight after the revelation: holiness is not intrinsic

Sources: The Torah, Maamad Har Sinai; the Meshekh Hokhmah

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Sunday 1 June 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • On the first day of Shavuot we read of the revelation at Har Sinai. A border kept anyone from the mountain, on pain of death, but once the long blast sounded and the revelation ended, they could go up.
  • The Meshekh Hokhmah (Rabbi Meir Simhah HaKohen, Latvia, about 100 years ago) asks why the permission came so abruptly; one would expect time to pass before approaching a former death trap.
  • His answer is a protest against the pagan idea of holiness, which sees Kedushah as a magical, inherent quality of an object, a taboo remote from people.
  • For us nothing is holy in itself. Holiness comes from an encounter between a person and HaKadosh Barukh Hu. The mountain was holy only during the revelation; afterwards it was like any other mountain, not a place to worship or seek blessings.
  • There is no Kedushah without someone who sanctifies, whether HaKadosh Barukh Hu or a person.
  • So too the Torah: it gains its Kedushah for us only when we commit to it and spend time with it. Shavuot is a time to renew that commitment.

Sources quoted

  • The Meshekh Hokhmah: the sudden permission to climb Sinai teaches that holiness is not intrinsic.

In one line

Holiness is created by encounter and commitment, which is why Sinai became an ordinary mountain once the revelation ended.
