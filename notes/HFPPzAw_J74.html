Topic: Praying, or serving as hazan, in shorts or short sleeves

Sources: The Gemara; the Mishnah; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Monday 14 June 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara shows the Hakhamim honoured tefillah with special clothes, based on verses such as "Hishtahavu LaShem behadrat kodesh" and "Hikon likrat Elokekha Yisrael".
  • The Rambam writes that one must prepare one's clothes for prayer, and the Shulhan Arukh includes covering the legs. The right way to pray is properly dressed, with trousers.
  • The Mishnah says one whose arms are uncovered may not be shaliah sibbur. Hakham Ovadiah limits this to above the elbow: with sleeves reaching the elbow one may pray and even lead. He writes likewise that one in shorts should not be hazan.
  • Whether one may pray in shorts is subjective and depends on local norms; the Shulhan Arukh itself ties covering the legs to the local custom. Hakham Ovadiah permits it on kibbusim in Israel and at summer camps, where people dress casually all day, though not as hazan.
  • In some places in Israel that may be the norm. In England, where one would not attend a business meeting in shorts even in hot weather, coming to tefillah so dressed is highly questionable and he believes it should be avoided.
  • If the norm changes, so would the approach to tefillah.

In one line

Where casual dress is the norm in formal settings one may pray in shorts, but in England one should come to tefillah properly dressed.
