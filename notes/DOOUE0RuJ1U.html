Topic: May one who shaved before Yom Tov shave again on Hol HaMoed today?

Sources: Rabbenu Tam; the Rishonim; Shulhan Arukh; the Mishnah (laundry); Rav Moshe Feinstein; Rav Hayim David HaLevi; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 21 October 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Rishonim discussed one who had a haircut just before Yom Tov. Rabbenu Tam allowed another on Hol HaMoed, since the decree does not apply.
  • Most Rishonim disagreed, and the Shulhan Arukh rules clearly that it is forbidden even after a haircut on Erev Yom Tov, because onlookers will not know.
  • That made sense when haircuts were infrequent. Today, with people shaving every day or two, there are strong grounds to allow one who shaved before Yom Tov to shave on Hol HaMoed. The Hakhamim wanted people to look good for Yom Tov, and he came in looking good.
  • The Mishnah permits washing hand towels and children's things on Hol HaMoed because they are used regularly; shaving, done daily by some, is similar.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein, some 50 to 60 years ago, held there are grounds to allow it, as did Rav Hayim David HaLevi, Chief Rabbi of Tel Aviv. Many forbid it in all circumstances, as the plain Shulhan Arukh writes.
  • Hakham Ovadiah writes that one cannot object to those who are lenient, while a yerei shamayim should be strict.
  • In practice: one who would feel unlike himself for the rest of the festival has very strong grounds to shave, provided he shaved before the moed; one who does not mind should perhaps wait until after.

Sources quoted

  • Rabbenu Tam: permits a haircut on Hol HaMoed after one before Yom Tov.
  • Shulhan Arukh: forbids it even then.
  • The Mishnah: items used daily may be washed on Hol HaMoed.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein and Rav Hayim David HaLevi: grounds to permit shaving.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: do not object to the lenient; the God-fearing should be strict.

In one line

One who shaved before the festival has grounds today to shave again on Hol HaMoed if it affects his simhah, though many forbid it.
