Topic: Why the Misvah to wage war against Amalek no longer applies, while remembering Amalek still does

Sources: The Rishonim; the Hafes Hayim; Rambam; the Hida; the Gemara (descendants of Haman in Benei Berak)

Daily Halacha: Purim, Thursday 14 March 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • War against Amalek is one of the 613 Misvot, but for halachic and practical reasons it no longer applies.
  • Most Rishonim say it applies only in the times of Mashiah. Shaul was commanded by Shemuel when Amalek was identifiable; after his failure they scattered and can no longer be identified. Practically, too, no one knows who descends from Amalek.
  • The Hafes Hayim: it is an all-or-nothing Misvah, to destroy the nation as a whole. Once scattered, there is no Misvah regarding individuals.
  • The Rambam holds, though some dispute it, that a descendant of Amalek may convert. The Hida explains the Gemara this way: descendants of Haman taught Torah in Benei Berak.
  • If they come in peace, even only as a ger toshav keeping the seven Noahide laws, they must be accepted, and there is a Misvah to look after them. The Misvah against Amalek is to wipe out its evil.
  • The negative commandment not to forget Amalek in our hearts, and the positive one to recall it verbally, still apply.
  • Some say the remembrance serves the war, which no longer applies; others link it to the principles of faith in wiping out evil, which still apply. Either way it remains a Misvah from the Torah. How it is done comes tomorrow.

In one line

The war against Amalek is not applicable today, but the Misvot not to forget and to remember Amalek remain.
