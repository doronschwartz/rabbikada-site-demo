Topic: The significance of Neilah and when it should be prayed

Sources: Gemara Keritot; Rashi; Talmud Yerushalmi; the Ramban; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Mishnah Berurah

Daily Halacha: Yom Kippur, Wednesday 20 September 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Neilah, the fifth prayer, is unique to Yom Kippur today, but it was once also said on public fasts the Hakhamim declared for communal troubles such as drought.
  • The Gemara in Keritot says every moment of Yom Kippur atones, and that atonement is by day, not night. Rashi explains from the verse "on this day" that atonement comes by day, since at Arvit the fast is not yet felt.
  • The Talmud Yerushalmi, and Rishonim such as the Ramban, say the main atonement is at the end of the day, at Neilah. These are levels: atonement begins at night, grows through the day, and reaches its crescendo at Neilah.
  • The Rambam and Shulhan Arukh rule that Neilah begins when the sun is on the treetops, about 45 minutes to an hour before sunset, and must be completed by sunset.
  • The Mishnah Berurah writes that the custom became to rely on those who allow until nightfall, especially in Europe, where the time between sunset and nightfall is long.
  • At the very least the silent Amidah must be finished before sunset, and preferably the repetition too. It is wrong to spend that time selling Aliyot or the opening of the Hekhal, or lengthening El Nora Alilah.
  • The Eastern Sepharadim added Selihot to Neilah to fill the time after sunset; the Spanish and Portuguese do not.
  • Birkat Kohanim should be before sunset, but if the repetition runs late, it may be said until nightfall.
  • In Israel most places finish the repetition before sunset and add the Selihot in the few minutes after.

In one line

Neilah is the crescendo of atonement, and at least its silent Amidah must be finished before sunset.
