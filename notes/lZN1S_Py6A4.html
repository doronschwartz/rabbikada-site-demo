Topic: The berakhot on lightning and thunder, and whether to say them with Hashem's name

Sources: The Gemara; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Taz; the Ben Ish Hai; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Tuesday 11 February 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • It is commonly thought that lightning and thunder have two different berakhot; that is not correct. The Gemara, as most Rishonim read it, gives a choice of Oseh Maaseh Bereshit or "shekoho ugevurato malei olam" on either, as the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh rule.
  • About 400 years ago a custom arose, first recorded he believes by the Taz, to say Oseh Maaseh Bereshit on lightning and shekoho ugevurato on thunder, which is louder, like an earthquake, and shows Hashem's might more. This is our practice, but one unsure or forgetful may say either on either.
  • It must be said within toh kedei dibbur, three or four seconds.
  • The Ben Ish Hai writes that the custom in Baghdad, and a colleague told him also in Jerusalem, was never to say these with Hashem's name. Reasons given: it is hard to be sure one is within the time; and one blesses again only on a new storm after the clouds disperse, which is hard to judge.
  • The prevalent Ashkenazi custom is to say them in full, and many poskim, Sepharadi too, say it is a shame to lose a berakhah instituted by the Hakhamim and recorded in all the great works over a doubt avoided by care with timing.
  • So one with an established custom may keep it, but the default is to say the full berakhah within seconds: on seeing lightning, and again on the thunder a few seconds later.
  • Woken by it at night before washing, one may rub one's hands and bless at once, rather than lose the berakhah.

In one line

Say the full berakhah within seconds of lightning and of thunder, customarily Oseh Maaseh Bereshit on lightning and shekoho ugevurato on thunder.
