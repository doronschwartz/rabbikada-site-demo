Recap

Nine sessions: the Oral Torah given to Moshe and taught four times; each generation deriving new laws; prophecy vs the Sages' authority; laws added each generation; Rebbi gathering them into the Mishnah; its structure in detail; the Talmud's four purposes, including derashot, intentionally cryptic; and "the four cubits of halachah": everything on earth is for the human, whose purpose is perfecting intellect and character, above all knowing Hashem and His unity. Last week: why so many people, when so few strive for perfection? To make the Sage's work possible, and to give him a society. "The four cubits" = walking with God in that pursuit.

Tonight: the last part of the introduction, then ten appendices that show the Rambam's encyclopedic knowledge.

Part 1: The Two Talmuds

וכאשר רב אשי חיבר התלמוד: one of the greatest of his generation, clearly with heavenly help.

Rav Ashi died; the Bavli closed around 500 CE, about 1,500 years ago.

The Yerushalmi

Meanwhile, the Sages of the Land of Israel compiled the Yerushalmi, associated with Rabbi Yochanan, earlier, around 400.

"It's called the Jerusalem Talmud, but it isn't specifically Jerusalem: it's the Talmud of Eretz Yisrael. In English academia it used to be called the Palestinian Talmud, from before 1948."

  • It covers the first five orders; in Taharot, only Niddah. So nothing in Taharot except Niddah has any Gemara, Bavli or Yerushalmi.
  • "Understood only with great difficulty," with help from the Tosefta and baraitot. That's why Jewish learning has always focused on the Bavli: "Even people who finish the Bavli don't necessarily go on to the Yerushalmi. Learn the Bavli more. The Yerushalmi is of course Torah, and we need to study it one day."

Part 2: After the Talmud: No Adding, No Subtracting

הרי כל מי שעמד אחריו, להבין דבריהם ולא להוסיף ולא לגרוע.

Everyone after Ravina and Rav Ashi can only understand and interpret, never add or subtract.

Why? The Talmud is the final Sanhedrin, so it can't be argued with. Everyone later is explaining.

The Geonim

וחיברו הגאונים פירושים רבים, ולא עלה ביד אחד מהם להשלים פירוש כל התלמוד.

The Geonim wrote many commentaries, but to the Rambam's knowledge none completed the whole Talmud: some died young, some were caught up in disputes.

The Rif supersedes them all

וההלכות אשר עשה רבנו הגדול רבי יצחק… הספיקו במקום אלו כולם.

Rabbi Yitzchak Alfasi, the Rif (North Africa, 1000s), abridged the whole Talmud and extracted every practical halachah: "no one before him managed that."

ואין לי תפיסה עליו בהן… לא יגיעו לעשרה בשום פנים.

I disagree with him in fewer than ten places.

"Outstanding. Between the Rishonim there are thousands of disputes across the Talmud. The Rambam went through the Rif and says perhaps ten places are wrong."

The commentary we lost

The Rambam says he also wrote commentaries on the Talmud: on Mo'ed, Nashim and Nezikin, plus four tractates elsewhere.

"Unfortunately we don't have it. We have no direct commentary of the Rambam on the Talmud. It would have shed so much light on his Mishneh Torah, which all the other commentators had to do in his stead."

Part 4: Why a Commentary on the Mishnah?

The Mishnah is fully explained only by the Gemara, and can't always be understood from itself. "Today we have the Rambam, Bartenura, many commentaries, recently Kehati, and English ones. In those days it was very hard."

"Basically: I'm not writing word by word for people who aren't clever. Self-explanatory mishnayot get no comment."

A trailblazer: no one wrote another full Mishnah commentary until Rabbi Ovadiah of Bartenura, Italy, 15th century, just as no one before or since has done a Mishneh Torah.

Part 5: The Ten Appendices

"Now you'll be exposed to the Rambam's encyclopedic knowledge. Remember: this is before ChatGPT, before the internet." He says they're "not essential", but they help anyone who wants to be complete in Mishnah.

1. The Sages who taught halachah: 91

The class's guesses: "20"; "lower than 20." (Philip laughed throughout: "Please, can we stick to the topic? Thank you.")

The Mishnah names Sages only from Shimon HaTzaddik onward (before him: prophets, back to Moshe). From Shimon HaTzaddik to Rebbi, 91 Sages taught at least one halachah in their own name. "He's 100% right. It's all been verified since. No one has argued."

Familiar and unfamiliar names: Rabbi Hyrkanos, Shemaya and Avtalyon, Yehoshua ben Perachyah, Yehoshua ben Beteira, Rabbi Elazar ben Azaryah, Rabbi Tzadok, Yehudah ben Tabbai, Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel… and some mentioned only once: Pappias, Rabbi Meyasha, Rabbi Yosei ben HaChotef Efrati.

2. Sages named for ethics or stories: 37

Not halachah: an ethical teaching (often only in Avot) or a story:

- Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi, only in Avot

- Yehudah ben Teima: הוי עז כנמר, "we say it every morning in Shacharit," yet he appears once in the whole Mishnah

- Ben Azzai, Ben Zoma, Rabbi Chananyah ben Akashya, Ben Bag Bag, Ben Hei Hei, Antigonos

- Elyehoeinai ben HaKof, a Kohen Gadol mentioned once, in Parah; Chanamel HaMitzri

91 + 37 = 128.

Mark's AI: "between 120 and 130." "It can't give you an exact number. The Rambam gives 91. Unbelievable."

Plus two not counted, making 130

Why left out

Elisha ben Avuyah

Quoted in Avot (teaching a child is like ink on new paper; an old man, on erased paper), but called אחר, "the other": he "entered the orchard"** and became a heretic

Menachem

Mentioned only to say Shammai replaced him as av beit din. Teaches nothing, not even through a story

4. Generations

Groupings of the leading Sages by generation: before the destruction, e.g. Shimon HaTzaddik and Dosa ben Hyrkanos; Antigonos; the Zugot (Yosei ben Yoezer and Yosei ben Yochanan, Yehoshua ben Perachyah and Nittai HaArbeli… Hillel and Shammai): "these seven pairs were in the Second Temple and didn't see the destruction". Then those who saw it, and onward.

5. Teachers and students

Who learned from whom (e.g. Rabban Yochanan ben Zakkai's students). "Rabbi X says in the name of Y" means X was Y's student.

Accepted, undisputed, usually transmitted from Rabbi Meir (סתם משנה רבי מאיר), though not always the halachah

Why "others" and "some say"? (Horayot 13b.) Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, the Nasi, sat between Rabbi Meir (the chacham) and Rabbi Natan (av beit din), with equal honour to all three. Wanting more respect for the Nasi's office, he changed the protocol so that only he received the full honour. Insulted, Rabbi Meir and Rabbi Natan spent the day asking him questions he couldn't answer. The consequence: their teachings would be cited without their names. "Others say" = Rabbi Meir; "some say" = Rabbi Natan.

10. Sages with a single halachah: 37

Some taught a great deal (Rabbi Meir, Rabbi Yehudah); some sparingly. Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov taught little, so the Gemara calls his Mishnah קב ונקי, "a small measure, but clean," and we often follow him.

Of the 91, 37 taught exactly one halachah ("37 again, but a different 37"), each listed: Nachum HaLavlar and Rabbi Meyasha in Pe'ah; Chananyah ben Chachinai and Rabbi Yosei ben HaChotef Efrati in Kilayim; Elazar Chisma and Rabbi Yosei ben Meshullam in Terumot; Rabbi Chutzpit in Shevi'it; Nachum HaMadi; Rabbi Ila'i and Rabbi Dostai in Eruvin; Rabbi Shimon ben Bechri in Shekalim; and the rest.
