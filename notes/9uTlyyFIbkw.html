Topic: The Bene Israel of India: their history and Jewish identity

Sources: Rabbi Yaakov Sapir, his travel writings

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 12 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • This concerns the Bene Israel, a third group of Indian Jews, distinct from the Jews of Baghdad origin and of Cochin, who had established communities, Rabbanim and long traditions.
  • It seems that some 1,700 to 1,900 years ago they were exiled from Israel or Yemen to the outskirts of India and cut off from Am Yisrael. They remained devoted but lost much of the misvot and the masorah, keeping only basic knowledge.
  • About 200 years ago some reached Baghdad and their status began to be discussed.
  • Their Jewish identity is accepted almost by all. Rabbi Yaakov Sapir, born around 1820, who went to Eres Yisrael young and travelled as an emissary collecting sedakah (to Egypt, Yemen, Singapore and even Australia), met them about 160 years ago and wrote that they are clearly Jews: they keep apart and have some traditions.
  • The bigger issue he noted: they perform some form of kiddushin, but have forgotten how to give a get and simply send a wife out of the house without the written document the Torah requires.
  • A woman not properly divorced who remarries would have children who are mamzerim, so the question is whether one may marry them. The Rabbanut dealt with this extensively, as the next class explains.

Sources quoted

  • Rabbi Yaakov Sapir: the Bene Israel are Jews, but they do not divorce with a get.

In one line

The Bene Israel are accepted as Jews, but their lack of proper divorce raises the question of mamzerut and marriage.
