Prophecy is a relationship

The seventh essay is on prophecy and the unique prophecy of Moshe. The Rabbi connected it to the parashah read a few weeks earlier, where God rebukes Aharon and Miriam for speaking about Moshe and comparing him to themselves.

The Ramhal writes that God wished to create a way to reveal Himself to people while they are still living in this world. When He connects with them, He also reveals to them things they need to know: secrets of how He runs the world and what will happen in the future. This is prophecy.

The Rabbi noted that the true place for a relationship with God is when we are free of the physical body, in the world of souls or the World to Come. Yet God made it possible to reach something of that in our earthly bodies. And he stressed a point he felt had not been emphasised enough when they learned it in Part 3: prophecy does not mean knowing the future. Prophecy is an intense relationship with God. Knowledge, whether about God, about how He runs the world, or about the future, is a by-product of that relationship. "Just because someone knows the future doesn't always mean he's a prophet." There are other ways to get there.

Certainty and riddles

Because the prophet's connection is spiritual, the way to prepare for it is to become more spiritual. A participant described prophets of other traditions who sing their way into a trance, and suggested that Moshe needed nothing of the kind. The Rabbi agreed. Another participant compared ordinary prophecy to switching on a router to make a connection, which sometimes works and sometimes does not, while Moshe's router "is always on and always steady".

The principle of every prophet, says the Ramhal, is that he is completely certain that it is God revealing Himself and he understands what is revealed, without doubt. This does not mean he understands every detail of the message. The Torah says God speaks to Moshe "mouth to mouth, and not in riddles", which implies other prophets receive riddles, such as a vision of a boiling pot, which they must interpret. The interpretation can even go wrong without the right training. But the experience itself is unmistakable. "You know it when you have it." A participant recalled the Gemara in Yoma about the letters lighting up on the High Priest's breastplate, which also had to be read correctly.

The prophet's body gives way

When prophecy comes, the prophet trembles, his limbs shake and his physical senses fail. Then he receives the vision and understands God's will. The physical body is a block to the supernatural, so it must be put out of action for the spirit to connect. "It is a seizure," the Rabbi agreed. That is why people sometimes thought the prophets were mad.

He asked what we would make of a real prophet today. There will not be one, but if there were, people would probably think him unbalanced until he proved himself. He mentioned the "Jerusalem syndrome" he saw when living in Israel, where people walking the streets of the Old City believe they are Mashiah or feel all kinds of things because they are in such a holy place.

Through his close connection a prophet may also be able to perform miracles, according to the level he has reached.

Moshe's prophecy

There are many levels of prophecy, but none comes anywhere near Moshe's. Any other level can be reached by someone who works very hard. Moshe's level was designated for him alone, and no one will ever reach it. Why not, if a person works hard enough? The Rabbi's answer: because the Torah tells us so. "That's the only reason."

Why is this so important theologically? Because it rules out anyone who claims to have received a new Torah or to have reached a higher level than Moshe. "This is why the notion of another prophet coming and taking even Torah from Moshe is impossible." Within the Jewish framework, Moshe was the greatest prophet, and no one can ever reach his level.

Ruah HaKodesh

Below prophecy is a lower level, Ruah HaKodesh. A person receives an energy into his mind through which he understands something, the reasons for things, without trembling or falling to the ground. The Rabbi distinguished it from mere intuition: it is something very clear. "Each one of us has the ability to reach that."

With it a person may understand things that are normally very hard for human beings, and at its highest level even things beyond human reach, such as knowledge of the future. A participant compared it to a "eureka moment" in science, when many pieces suddenly fall into place. The Rabbi said that in a sense this is an element of Ruah HaKodesh. But Ruah HaKodesh comes without effort and without doubt, while scientific insight usually comes through hard work and can be mistaken. "Ruah HaKodesh means understanding things."

The last paragraph of the essay was left for the next class.

Summary

  • Prophecy is an intense relationship with God; knowing the future is only a possible by-product.
  • A prophet is always certain of the experience, even if he must interpret a message given in riddles.
  • During prophecy the body fails, because the physical is a block to the spiritual.
  • Moshe's prophecy is unique and unreachable, which rules out any later "prophet" who claims to replace his Torah.
  • Ruah HaKodesh, a clear understanding received without effort, is a lower level within reach of each of us.
