Topic: Why Birkat Kohanim is said at Minhah on a fast day, and not at Minhah on Yom Kippur

Sources: The Gemara and the rulings of Hazal on Birkat Kohanim

Daily Halacha: Asarah BeTevet

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Birkat Kohanim really belongs in every daytime prayer: Shaharit, Musaf and Minhah. We do it at Shaharit, and at Musaf on Shabbat and Rosh Hodesh.
  • Hazal stopped it at Minhah on ordinary days, since people may have drunk at their meal and a Kohen may not bless when drunk.
  • They extended this to fast days too, so that people would not confuse a fast-day Minhah with an ordinary one.
  • That decree applies only where confusion is possible. On a fast day Minhah is normally prayed late, close to sunset, together with Arvit, so it cannot be confused, and Birkat Kohanim is said: only when Minhah is within about half an hour of sunset.
  • On Yom Kippur Minhah is prayed early to leave time for Neilah, so it could be confused and there is no Birkat Kohanim at Minhah; it is said at Neilah instead. Neilah also existed on the public fasts for rain, with Birkat Kohanim there.
  • On a fast day when Minhah is prayed early in the afternoon, Birkat Kohanim is not said.

In one line

On a fast day Kohanim bless at Minhah only when it is prayed close to sunset; Yom Kippur's early Minhah has no Birkat Kohanim, which moves to Neilah.
