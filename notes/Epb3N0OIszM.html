Sefer: Derech Hashem by Rabbi Moshe Hayim Luzzatto (the Ramhal) Section: Part 3, Chapter 2, paragraph 7 Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Derech Hashem series at Tiferet Eyal, Monday 22 November 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The first way, summed up

Chapter 1 dealt with dreams and the soul. Chapter 2 so far has dealt with using God's names to change the course of nature by connecting to the root. Paragraph 7 sums this up: a person can use these spiritual forces to bring about great changes beyond what physical means allow. All the systems of existence are tied one to another, and all of them go back to God. When you reach the source directly through His name, the effect runs all the way down without being diluted.

But it is still not automatic. "You're not pressing buttons." It has to come with meditation and a real connection, and God may still say no. It is easier only because you are closer to the source.

The second way: the angels

Now comes a second way to bypass nature (black magic is still to come). "This is what Kabbalists do the most today": they use the names of angels.

The Rabbi's example: many siddurim now print, at the end of Hallel on Rosh Hodesh, a verse to say three times for protection during the coming month, followed by an appeal to an angel (whose name begins with a zayin) to guard and protect, and then "may it be the will of God, in whose hand is everything". "You're praying to the malakh. You understand what you're doing?" You are invoking him.

The Ramhal explains that angels are given abilities, but they do not run continuously. Each one does its task, and its activity varies with its level. Yet if invoked correctly, they can work beyond their usual measure. The Rabbi likened them to robots: if you know the engineering, you can switch one on when it is off, or make it work faster and more powerfully. Through this, people have done all kinds of wonders, when God agrees.

Why does it work? God gave honour to His name, and every angel's name carries God's name within it: Mikhael, Gavriel, Refael. When a person mentions the angel's name together with God's name, the angel is bound to act according to the one who invoked it. In Kabbalah this is called making the angels take an oath.

Which way is easier?

A participant answered: the angels. Speaking to God directly is spiritual; invoking angels is more mechanical. The Ramhal makes the same point. The first way requires great closeness to God; the more righteous you are, the more successful you will be. The second way needs much less. Righteousness helps, but since acting is part of the angels' nature, mentioning their names works like any other natural means. "You want to cut something? Use a knife."

The Rabbi gave another example: a prayer printed in some siddurim before Havdalah, adjuring the angel in charge of forgetfulness to take away a "stupid heart" and all forgetting. When he was younger and wanted to remember everything, he was very fond of it. But essentially you are making an angel swear.

Two warnings

First, even with angels, you must be in the right frame of mind. You cannot be a rasha and use these names.

Second, be careful not to ascribe power to the angels themselves, which can come close to avodah zarah. The Rosh Hodesh text at least ends "may it be the will of God". The Rabbi noted that some do not say the line in Shalom Aleikhem asking the angels to bless us, for this reason. If one speaks to angels, one must understand that they are emanations of the Kadosh Barukh Hu and that all their power is His.

He also cautioned that some names circulating in siddurim are recited without any understanding of what they are or where they came from.

Paragraph 7, continued: the backdoor

The Ramhal summarises: two fundamentals. One is mentioning God's name, which draws new energy. The other is compelling the angels through God's name. The difference is that in the first you go all the way up yourself; in the second you stay below and send someone else up. That is why most people who engage in these things today use angels.

Even so, it is not certain. The angels have limits, and God can prevent the effect, just as He could stop a knife from cutting. With angels it is easier for Him to intervene, since that is less openly miraculous.

And the Ramhal concludes: it is not fitting for an ordinary person to use the king's sceptre. Both ways are permitted only to the holy, who cleave to God and act to sanctify His name and fulfil His will. Otherwise, even if it works, you will be punished. The Rabbi's image: the angels are the back doors of the palace. "You've got protexia. You know the king's son." But the king has not invited you, and using his son to get in is wrong. "It may work. You'll end up losing out from it."

A question about memory

A participant asked: if someone used the angel of forgetfulness to help a person with Alzheimer's, would that be wrong, since God gave the illness? The Rabbi answered that such reasoning would mean never praying for anyone who is ill, and the class ended on that discussion. Next time: part three of the chapter, black magic.

Summary

  • Going to God directly through His name is the higher way, and it requires great holiness.
  • The second way is invoking angels, whose names contain God's name; it is more mechanical and so more common today.
  • Never ascribe independent power to angels; they are emanations of God.
  • Neither way is guaranteed, and God can always prevent the effect.
  • Using these "back doors" without being on the level is wrong and brings punishment even when it works.
