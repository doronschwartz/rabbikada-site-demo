Topic: Three principles of davar haaved: proportion, who may do the work, and not postponing

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Wednesday 5 November 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • First: permission is not unlimited. The greater the loss, the more effort and tirha may be spent; a trivial loss does not justify great exertion. One should always take the easier, less bothersome option.
  • For example, a stain that will be hard to remove after the moed is davar haaved and may be washed out. Where the garment can go in the washing machine, that is less tirha than scrubbing by hand, and so better.
  • Second: when it is permitted, one may do it oneself, or if necessary ask another Jew, even if he must be paid, though doing it oneself is better.
  • Third: one may not intentionally push something off so that it becomes davar haaved on Hol HaMoed. Car tax in the UK, if unpaid, can lead to clamping. If renewal falls on Hol HaMoed and could have been done a month earlier, one may not deliberately leave it. If one forgot or did not manage, one may do everything involved on Hol HaMoed.

In one line

Under davar haaved the effort must match the loss, a paid Jew may be employed, and the situation may not be deliberately left for the moed.
