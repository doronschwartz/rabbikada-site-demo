The halacha

  • Siman 2 deals mainly with yihus, lineage in marriage. Its importance is clear in Parashat Pinehas, which lists the families as untarnished, and in the Gemara, which says the Shekhinah rests on a union with proper lineage.
  • The Shulhan Arukh opens: one should not marry someone with a pesul. For a regular Yisrael this mainly means a mamzeret; a Kohen has more restrictions, chiefly a divorcee or a halalah.
  • The Gemara says one who marries a woman for money will have children who are not the best (einan mehuganim).
  • The Rivash: this applies only where there is a pesul and one overlooks it for financial gain. Marrying a permitted woman mainly because she is well off is fine.
  • The other view in the Rishonim, which we follow: even with a permitted woman, if money is the main reason, the focus is beyond the misvah and bonding with one's spouse, and it affects the children.
  • Everyone agrees that if one would marry the person anyway and wealth is a bonus, that is fine. Marrying mainly for money or politics is not a prohibition if she is kasher, but the Hakhamim advise against it.
  • The Rema writes that if in-laws retract a promised financial package before the wedding because they cannot pay, it is wrong to back out for that reason alone. A lack of integrity on their part is a different question.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: one who marries for money has children who are not the best.
  • The Rivash: the problem is overlooking a pesul for money.
  • The Rema: do not break off a match only because promised money did not come.

In one line

Marrying a permitted partner mainly for money is not forbidden but is discouraged, and a match should never be broken off for financial reasons alone.
