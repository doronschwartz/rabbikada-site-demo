Topic: Four exceptions to the ruling against teaching women Torah, and the approach today

The halacha

  • The Rambam and Shulhan Arukh rule that a father should not teach his daughter the Oral Torah, and preferably not the Written Torah either. There are four caveats.
  • First, a woman who wants to learn by herself may. The Rambam himself writes in the same paragraph that a woman who learns receives reward. History has many learned women: Bruriah, wife of Rabbi Meir and daughter of Rabbi Hananiah, learned 300 laws a day from 300 rabbis according to the Gemara, corrected her husband, and the Tosefta follows her in a dispute. The Rashbas quotes a responsum of a Rabbanit with approval, and the Hida writes of his wife's unique wisdom. The Hida holds girls must stand for a woman who teaches them Torah.
  • Second, the Rema: the limit is on theoretical Torah. Practical halacha (Shabbat, kashrut, festivals, family purity) women must learn. This, says the Beit Yoseph, is why women recite Birkat HaTorah.
  • Third, the Hida, brought by Hakham Ovadiah: we need not force a daughter to learn as we must a son, but if she wants to, her father may teach her.
  • Fourth, the Hafes Hayim, about 100 years ago: the ruling fitted times of a strong home tradition. Today, when women learn so much else, they should be taught all the Written Torah and books of Musar, or they will have secular knowledge without basic Torah.
  • He spoke of the Written Torah. Some still do not recommend women learning Gemara, because of the Mishnah in Sotah. Rav Soloveitchik held it imperative today and taught Gemara to women. Rav Yehuda Herzl Henkin, who passed away this year, writes in Benei Banim that women who learn only simple Torah alongside deep secular knowledge may come to belittle the Torah, so one who wishes may learn even Gemara. Not everyone agrees.
  • Today women and girls must be taught Torah so they appreciate its beauty and depth.

In one line

A woman may learn on her own, must learn practical halacha, may be taught if she wishes, and today should be taught Torah to see its beauty.
