Topic: Who is obligated to teach Torah: grandparents, every Jew, oneself, and whether it extends to daughters

Sources: The Torah, Parashat Vaethanan and the Shema; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; Neviim (Elisha and Eliyahu); Gemara Kiddushin

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Monday 3 May 2021

The halacha

  • "Vehodatam levanekha velivnei vanekha": the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh rule that a grandfather, and even a great-grandfather, must also teach the child Torah, stepping in if the father cannot.
  • Every Jewish male must teach Torah to others, from "veshinantam levanekha", since sons can mean disciples: Elisha's disciples are called Bnei HaNeviim, and Elisha called his teacher Eliyahu "Avi avi".
  • Unlike a father, one need not pay for this, but whoever has the knowledge and time must teach as much as he can. Sons and grandsons simply take precedence.
  • One whose father did not teach him must teach himself, as the Shulhan Arukh rules, just as one not circumcised by his father must see to it at bar misvah. With books in English and online classes, there is no excuse not to learn morning and evening.
  • The Gemara in Kiddushin derives "your sons", not daughters: there is no formal obligation to teach a daughter Torah, nor for a girl to learn by herself. Whether women may learn, and how much, comes tomorrow.

Sources quoted

  • Parashat Vaethanan: "vehodatam levanekha velivnei vanekha", the grandfather's obligation.
  • The Shema: "veshinantam levanekha", read as disciples.
  • Neviim: Bnei HaNeviim; Elisha calling Eliyahu "Avi avi".
  • Gemara Kiddushin: the obligation is to sons, not daughters.

In one line

Grandfathers, every knowledgeable Jew and even oneself must teach Torah, while the formal duty covers sons and not daughters.
