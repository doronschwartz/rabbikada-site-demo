Topic: Why one asks Mah Nishtanah even when alone: Pesah as freedom to ask questions

Sources: The Gemara; the Torah (Vehayah ki yishalekha binkha); the Arizal

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Monday 11 April 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Maggid opens with Ha Lahma Anya as an introduction; the main part begins with Mah Nishtanah, in essence one question with four reasons.
  • The Gemara says that even a person alone at the Seder asks himself the questions. If the question were only a means to an answer, this would make no sense.
  • Questions run through the Exodus: the Torah says three times "when your son asks you".
  • Pesah is freedom from back-breaking labour, which we show by reclining and the four cups. It is also freedom of thought and speech. When Moshe asked for a three-day holiday, Pharaoh said they clearly had time to think and made the work harder, enslaving them mentally as well.
  • Asking is therefore an end in itself: just as reclining shows physical freedom, asking shows intellectual freedom.
  • The Arizal reads Pesah as peh sah, the mouth speaks. An answer needs a question as much as a question needs an answer.
  • A genuine, appropriately asked question, unlike the cynical question of the Rasha, brings us closer to HaKadosh Barukh Hu, which is why so much at the Seder is meant to arouse curiosity.

Sources quoted

In one line

We ask Mah Nishtanah even alone because asking questions is itself the freedom Pesah celebrates.
