Topic: The berakhot Meshanneh HaBeriyot and Dayan HaEmet on seeing people of unusual form

Sources: The Gemara; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Rishonim; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Thursday 23 January 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The same Gemara speaks of people formed differently from the norm. Cases still relevant, listed in the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh, include one blind in both eyes or born without a leg or an arm; the Rambam adds anyone whose form differs from the usual. Great Hakhamim are recorded saying it on conjoined twins, and it is said with Hashem's name on a dwarf too.
  • Great sensitivity is needed: the person must never be made a vehicle for a berakhah in a derogatory or patronising way. It expresses that Hashem made His creatures diverse.
  • For one born that way the berakhah is Meshanneh HaBeriyot. If it happened later in life, such as losing a hand or one's sight, one says Dayan HaEmet, affirming that all God does is just even when we do not understand.
  • First caveat: one says it only where one has empathy and some connection with the person; saying it on a stranger in the street would arguably be wrong.
  • Second: the Rishonim debate whether it applies to all people or only to Jews. The Rambam does not limit it, but the Shulhan Arukh brings the view limiting it to Jews, so because of safek berakhot lehakel it is not said on non-Jews in practice.
  • Third: it is said once a lifetime for each category; the Rema rules every 30 days for Ashkenazim, but for Sepharadim it is once.

In one line

The berakhah on people of unusual form is said only with real empathy, in practice on Jews, and for Sepharadim once a lifetime per category.
