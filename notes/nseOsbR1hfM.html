The halacha

  • A major theme of this Siman, to come, is how we know someone is Jewish when they say so. First, the Gemara, Rambam and Shulhan Arukh give general advice on marriage.
  • Good middot and character come first. After that the Gemara gives a hierarchy of whose daughter to marry: a Talmid Hakham; then the Gedolei HaDor, here meaning righteous and kind people; then heads of synagogues and community leaders; then those who run the community's charity; then teachers.
  • Most important is not to marry the daughter of an Am HaAres. Here that means not someone unlearned, but someone who does not care about the misvot and treats them as unimportant.
  • The most beautiful union is a Talmid Hakham marrying the daughter of a Kohen, joining Torah and priesthood, since in earlier times the Kohen was the teacher whose role was to instruct.
  • An Am HaAres marrying a Kohen's daughter should be avoided. Someone on a journey of learning is different from someone who simply does not care, and marrying a Kohen's daughter comes with responsibilities.

In one line

After good character, prefer the daughter of a Talmid Hakham and avoid one whose family disregards the misvot.
