Topic: Purim Katan restrictions, and in which Adar to keep the nahalah of someone who died in a regular Adar

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rama; the Gemara

Daily Halacha: Purim Katan & Adar Rishon, Thursday 22 February 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Purim Katan has a celebratory aspect, so one may not fast or give hespedim, and Tahanun is omitted at Shaharit.
  • For someone who died in Adar of a regular year, the Shulhan Arukh rules that the nahalah is kept in Adar Sheni. First, Purim in Adar Sheni shows it is the main month. Second, unpleasant matters are not brought forward: a fast that falls on Shabbat is postponed to Sunday, not advanced.
  • So Kaddish, candles, and the fast some keep on a parent's nahalah are all in Adar Sheni. This is the Sepharadi custom.
  • The Rama rules the opposite for Ashkenazim. The Gemara puts Purim in Adar Sheni only to juxtapose it to Pesah; otherwise Adar Rishon is the first. Honouring a parent's memory is a misvah, done as early as possible. He also proves from vows that Adar Rishon is the main one.
  • Someone who died in Adar Rishon or Adar Sheni of a leap year is remembered in that same month by all.
  • Nothing prevents keeping both, since Kaddish may be said for a parent whenever needed. People who belong to both Ashkenazi and Sepharadi synagogues may do so.

In one line

For a death in a regular Adar, Sepharadim keep the nahalah in Adar Sheni, Ashkenazim in Adar Rishon, and keeping both is fine.
