These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Introduction

Siman 141 is the longest siman in Hilchot Gittin, and the Rabbi said the class would move fast. Its core is Seifim 1-13, which deal with the two kinds of agent in a get: the shaliah holakhah, who carries the get from the husband to the wife, and the shaliah kabbalah, who receives it on her behalf.

Seif 1: Can a shaliah kabbalah receive from a shaliah holakhah?

Before asking whether the same person can be both agents, there is a prior question: can a shaliah kabbalah receive a get from a shaliah holakhah at all? The Gemara (Gittin 63) records a dispute between Rav and Rabbi Hanina. Rav says she cannot, Rabbi Hanina says she can.

Normally one would expect the halachah to follow Rav. But the Rif, the Rambam, the Rosh and others rule like Rabbi Hanina. The reason is the flow of the Gemara: it goes on to ask whether the same shaliah can serve in both roles, and that question only makes sense according to Rabbi Hanina.

The Beit Yoseph says plainly that since the Rif, Rambam and Rosh agree, this is the ruling. The Shulhan Arukh therefore rules like Rabbi Hanina in the plain text, yet adds "some say". The Rabbi explained that this does not always mean a stringency to follow. According to the Kaf HaHayim, the Hida and Rav Ovadia, it is sometimes only reporting another view. Here, though, the Beit Yoseph clearly means that where you can be strict, you should: the shaliah holakhah should hand the get directly to the woman, and not to her agent.

Why Rav was strict

The Gemara gives two reasons. The first is the husband's dignity (bizayon): he is divorcing his wife and expects her to be present, even if he does so through an agent. The second is a worry that it resembles a get thrown into a courtyard that only afterwards became hers: when he appointed his agent, she had no agent yet.

The two reasons differ in practice. If she appointed her agent before he appointed his, the second worry disappears. And the husband can waive his dignity, as the Gemara in Havdalah shows. So one can satisfy Rav on both counts: the husband says he does not consider it a bizayon, and she appoints her agent first.

The Rashba and our wording

The Rashba says that if she told her agent to receive the get "from my husband's hand," then the husband must give it to the agent himself, even though a shaliah holakhah normally cannot. The Pithei Teshuvah applies this today: our harshaah (the authorisation text) says to give the get "into her hand," so unless the wording is changed, or it was agreed in advance, handing it to her agent would be a problem. Not out of concern for Rav, but because of the wording itself.

One student asked why the old wording was dropped. The Rabbi's view: it was probably removed to be strict for Rav, and over time became the established text. If there is a real need, such as a woman in hospital, the old wording can be used.

Seif 1: The same agent in both roles

Can the shaliah holakhah also become the shaliah kabbalah? The Gemara leaves it as a doubt, and we rule that it is a safek, a doubtful get. Two questions follow: what is the problem, and if the attempt was made, does it help if he then hands it to her directly?

Rashi's view. An agent must be able to go back to the husband and say "I have completed my task." If he becomes her agent mid-way, he leaves the first role without that possibility. He is "uprooting" the agency. The Rabbi said he could not claim to understand Rashi fully here. The result: once he tried, the get is spoiled and a new get is needed.

The Rambam's view. The agent simply cannot change hats while the task is unfinished, so he remains a shaliah holakhah. If the next day he hands her the get, it works.

The ruling. The Shulhan Arukh follows the Rambam: even after the doubtful attempt, if the shaliah then gives it to her hand, it works. The Taz follows Rashi, but the Pithei Teshuvah follows the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh. For the case to be a real safek, he must make clear that it is for her, for example by saying "receive."

Seifim 2-7: A girl and the receiving of a get

  • A na'arah (12 to 12 and a half): her father can receive her get, and she can receive it herself, but her hand is weaker than his. So she cannot appoint an agent, unless her father has died or she has married.
  • A ketanah (under 12) can never appoint an agent. If her father is alive and she is only betrothed, he can receive for her. Once she is married, or her father has died, she cannot appoint an agent but must wait until age 12.
  • A betrothed ketanah receiving herself: Rashi in Kiddushin says she can, Rashi in Gittin says she cannot while her father is alive. The Rif and the Rambam follow Gittin, and the Shulhan Arukh rules that she cannot, with "some say" that she can. The Rashba adds that she can with her father's consent, and the Shulhan Arukh follows him.
  • The minimum age: the Gemara requires that she understand why a get is given, tested by whether she can tell nuts from stones. The Rabbi said this is a very young age, and that the Rishonim had different texts of the Gemara; on one reading a very young child can be divorced.

Seif 8: Appointing the shaliah kabbalah

A shaliah kabbalah must be appointed before two witnesses, whether the same pair who witness the receiving of the get or a different pair. The Rema says one should give the get only once one knows the appointment was made before witnesses, though after the fact one may assume it was.

Why does a shaliah kabbalah need witnesses, while a shaliah holakhah does not? The Rambam's principle: "when you hold the get, you have power." A shaliah kabbalah arrives empty handed and says "I was appointed"; we have no way of knowing. A shaliah holakhah arrives with a proper get in his hand, and we assume that all is in order.

Seifim 9-13: Does the shaliah holakhah need witnesses?

  • The Rambam: no witnesses are needed to confirm the appointment of the shaliah holakhah. He is believed because he holds a proper, validated get.
  • The Rosh: the Yerushalmi requires the appointment to be done before two. Even so, the shaliah is believed to say that it was. The Beit Yoseph reads the Yerushalmi differently, so the Rambam too may be concerned.

The Shulhan Arukh brings both views. In the first seif he gives the Rambam as the plain view; in the later one he gives the Rambam first and the Rosh second, which seems to go against the usual rule that the last view is the one to follow. The Rabbi said the general rules about how Maran rules have too many exceptions, and that one has to follow the Beit Yoseph's flow. Either way, we should be strict like the Rosh.

Today we go beyond the Rosh: the agent brings a full harshaah, which is read and checked. So in practice there is little difference between the two kinds of agent. In both you must prove the appointment.

All of this applies only where there is no challenge (irur). If there is a challenge, that is a separate subject later in the siman. And it applies only with a properly validated get. The Beit Shemuel adds that if the get is unsigned, it does not help even if the husband confirms it.

Edei mesirah and edei hatimah

If the shaliah gave the get without witnesses to the handing (edei mesirah), relying only on the witnesses who signed (edei hatimah), the Rambam says it is a get, after the fact, and the Rosh says it is not. The Shulhan Arukh adds a new point: if you can, give the get again. The Rabbi noted that the earlier discussions never said this, so it is a new point.

Seif 10: Can the shaliah be one of the witnesses?

This question applies to both agents, though it matters only according to the Rosh, since the Rambam needs no witnesses for the shaliah holakhah.

The Yerushalmi seems to contradict itself, once saying that an agent can be a witness and once that he does not count. The Gemara is clear that when two agents receive a get together, they can be both agents and witnesses.

The Rambam's plain meaning, as the Bah understands him, is that the agent cannot be a witness. The Beit Yoseph reads him as allowing it. The reason for the strict view, according to the Bah: with two agents there is no concern, but with one, it might look as if the whole thing happened before one person, since the agent stands in the husband's place. It is a rabbinic decree.

The Shulhan Arukh rules that what is needed is two witnesses for the receiving: a kosher shaliah may join one more witness. But it appears the Rosh disagrees, and our custom, which poskim follow, is to have two separate witnesses, not the agent. So we use an agent in addition to the witnesses, not instead of them.

Practical summary

  • A shaliah kabbalah may receive a get from a shaliah holakhah (like Rabbi Hanina), but where possible the husband's agent should hand it to the woman directly.
  • Our harshaah says to give the get into her hand, so the husband's agent should not give it to her agent unless the wording is deliberately changed.
  • The same person acting as both agents is a doubt. If it was done, the shaliah should still hand the get to her afterwards, as the Shulhan Arukh follows the Rambam.
  • A ketanah cannot appoint an agent, and a na'arah only after her father's death or marriage. We prefer to avoid a shaliah kabbalah altogether.
  • Today we do not rely on the Rambam alone: a written, checked harshaah is required for the shaliah holakhah, and we use two separate witnesses, not the agent.
