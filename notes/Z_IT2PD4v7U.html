Topic: Honouring parents for a convert, an adopted child, a child born through IVF, and a parent with dementia

Sources: The principle "a convert is like a newborn child"; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Talmud on raising an orphan

Daily Halacha: Honouring Parents, Monday 18 January 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A convert is a new halachic identity, so one could argue he has no obligation to his biological parents. Yet the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh require him to give them some honour (miksat kavod).
  • The misvah has two parts: basic gratitude to someone who did us good, and the special status of a parent. A convert's parents lose the second, but gratitude remains. How much depends on how they raised him; to an abusive parent he owes nothing.
  • A convert may pray for his non-Jewish parents, say a Hashkavah, and even say Kaddish for them.
  • An adopted child: the Talmud says one who raises an orphan is as if he gave birth to him. Some say this means a full obligation; more likely it is a similar obligation of gratitude.
  • A child born through artificial insemination (IVF) has the full, regular misvah towards the biological parent.
  • A parent with dementia who no longer recognises the child must still be honoured: the obligation rests on us, whether or not the parent knows.

In one line

Converts and adopted children honour their parents out of gratitude, IVF children have the full misvah, and dementia does not remove it.
