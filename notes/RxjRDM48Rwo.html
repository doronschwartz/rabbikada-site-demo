Topic: The meaning of "Basukkot teshevu" and when the Berakhah Leshev BaSukkah is said

Sources: Vayikra ("Basukkot teshevu shivat yamim"); the Rosh; the Rambam; the Rif; Rabbenu Tam; Shulhan Arukh; the Gemara

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Sunday 19 September 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Torah says "Basukkot teshevu shivat yamim": everyone must dwell in a sukkah for the seven days.
  • Most Rishonim read "teshevu" as to sit or dwell: the Rosh says to hang around in the sukkah, the Rambam says to sit. Either way it has nothing to do with food; whatever one does at home is done in the sukkah.
  • On this view one says Leshev BaSukkah each time one enters, as ruled by the Rambam, the Rif and most Rishonim.
  • Rabbenu Tam (France, 12th century) held the main dwelling is eating and drinking, so the berakhah is said only when eating or drinking.
  • Shulhan Arukh writes that the custom has become to say it only when eating. The Temanim, following the Rambam, still say it on every entry, the simple reading of the Gemara and the pesukim; most Sepharadim and Ashkenazim follow the custom.
  • The berakhah is said only for a proper meal (keviut seudah): more than a beisah of bread, about 56 grams. So it is quite rare, often only on Yom Tov and Shabbat; one should have in mind all other eating, drinking and activity.
  • In cold countries we associate the sukkah with eating, but it is where we should eat, drink, sleep and work all seven days. It is not wrong to do these things there; weather permitting, do as much as possible in it.

In one line

The misvah is to live in the sukkah, but by custom Leshev BaSukkah is said only over more than 56 grams of bread.
