Topic: Two further approaches to Shabbat in the polar regions: a day from sunset to sunset, and no halachic time at all

Sources: The Minhat Elazar of Munkacs; Rav Bleich; Rav Yoseph Mashash, Mayim Hayim; the Gemara and the Ritva (Yom Kippur not observed)

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Wednesday 10 July 2024

The halacha

  • The first two approaches both assume that days there are still 24-hour periods.
  • The Minhat Elazar of Munkacs argues that a day may be defined as sunset to sunset, however long. At the North Pole, six months of darkness and six of light make one day a year, so six years would be weekdays and the seventh year Shabbat.
  • Since one cannot count from creation, one would count from arrival. This sounds reasonable but comes out very strange; it has not been accepted and is not the practice.
  • Rav Bleich, a contemporary posek, discusses a view also quoted by Rav Yoseph Mashash: halachic time is defined by sunset and sunrise, so where there are none there is no halachic day, and misvot tied to day or night, including Shabbat, do not apply.
  • The proof: the Gemara reports a message from Israel to Bavel, in a time of persecution, not to observe Yom Kippur. The Ritva explains the Beit Din did not sanctify Tishrei, so there was no Yom Kippur; time can be transcended.
  • Rav Yoseph Mashash, who himself concludes like Rabbi Yaakov Emden, quotes this from an unnamed sage and rejects it: there would be no festivals, Hames could be eaten on Pesah and food on Yom Kippur, and no tefillin, tallit or prayer. He calls it a disgrace even to consider.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Yoseph Mashash, Mayim Hayim: such a view is embarrassing and unacceptable.

In one line

Neither a year-long polar "day" nor exemption from all time-bound misvot has been accepted.
