Topic: Why Purim has two dates, which cities keep the 15th, and how one can become obligated in both days

Sources: The Megillah; the Hakhamim

Daily Halacha: Purim, Tuesday 28 February 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Purim is the only time in the calendar when it is theoretically possible to keep two days, or none.
  • The Jews fought on the 13th and rested on the 14th, hence Taanit Esther on the 13th and Purim on the 14th. In Shushan they fought an extra day and rested on the 15th, so the celebration reflects the two stages: Shushan on the 15th, everywhere else on the 14th.
  • The Hakhamim extended the 15th to any walled city like Shushan. But Yerushalayim lay desolate and unwalled at the time of the miracle, and it would be inappropriate for it to keep the 14th, so the test is whether the city was walled at the time of Yehoshua's conquest of the land.
  • So Yerushalayim keeps the 15th, as does Shushan itself. York, a walled city in England, keeps the 14th, since it was not walled in Yehoshua's time. The day after Purim is called Shushan Purim.
  • One can be obligated in two days: a person who kept the whole of Purim on the 14th in Ashdod and enters Yerushalayim on the night of the 15th, and will be there at dawn, must read the Megillah that night and the next day and do all the misvot again.
  • Dawn is the decisive factor. Even if he leaves Yerushalayim at 10 in the morning and returns to Ashdod, he must complete the Megillah, Mishloah Manot and Matanot LaEvyonim a second time.

In one line

Purim is on the 14th, but on the 15th in cities walled since Yehoshua such as Yerushalayim, and where you are at dawn decides your obligation, even for a second day.
