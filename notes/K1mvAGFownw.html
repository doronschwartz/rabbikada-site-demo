Topic: Four reasons Batei Din today require proof of Jewish identity

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; Tosafot

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • If most Rishonim, the Rambam and the Aharonim on the Shulhan Arukh believe one who says he is Jewish, why are Batei Din so particular today?
  • First, there used to be no interest in becoming Jewish. The Gemara says we remind a would-be convert how hard it is to be Jewish and how much persecution there is. Today being Jewish can bring benefits, for marriage, money or social standing.
  • Second, many people think they are Jewish and are not: their father's line was Jewish but they married out generations ago, or a mother or grandmother had a Reform conversion, which is not valid. Such people may grow up in traditional homes with Jewish values and still not be halachically Jewish.
  • Third, the Shulhan Arukh says that where there is a reason for suspicion (irur), one must check. Some poskim hold that the fact many who come to marry or join a community are not religious weakens the presumption.
  • Fourth, Tosafot's reason relies on the majority who come being Jewish. There is a complex discussion whether one may rely on a majority when it is possible to check, and today checking is easy: documents, community membership, a phone call or email to the previous rabbi.
  • All these reasons together explain why the claim alone is not accepted today.

In one line

Benefits of being Jewish, invalid conversions, grounds for suspicion and the ease of checking all lead Batei Din to verify Jewish identity today.
