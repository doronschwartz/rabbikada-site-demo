Topic: The minyan needed for the Torah reading, what "starting" and "finishing" mean, and what to do when a mistake is found in the Sefer Torah Source: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Siman 143 Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada at the Thursday Shulhan Arukh class, Tiferet Eyal, Thursday 24 November 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Ten men for the reading

Siman 143 has only five paragraphs, but the Rabbi said it would take the whole evening. After a quick review of the previous week, he began with the first paragraph: the Torah is not read with its berakhot with fewer than ten adult men. Reading the Torah with berakhot is a davar shebikdushah, like Kaddish and Kedushah, so it needs a minyan.

A child under thirteen does not count towards the ten. However, Sefaradim hold that a child who understands to whom he is making the berakhah may be called up as one of the seven aliyot, and he said he has relied on this. The Mishnah Berurah notes the distinction: a child can be one of the seven, but still cannot make up the ten. "You have to have ten men. That's non-negotiable."

What counts as "starting"

If they began with ten and some left, they may finish. What counts as beginning? The Rabbi asked the class for a proof. If the person called up has only said "Barekhu et Hashem hamevorakh," that is not yet the start. It begins once he has said "Barukh Atah Hashem" of the berakhah. He linked this to an earlier class: if there is no Kohen and a Yisrael has said "Barekhu" when a Kohen walks in, he steps aside, but once he has said Hashem's name the Kohen no longer displaces him.

The same principle applies to the repetition of the Amidah. If it began with ten, it may be finished, together with the Kaddish that belongs to it.

How many must remain? A majority, which is six. The Rabbi said seven is needed only for a zimun, where the majority must be recognisable at a glance.

He added a strong warning. This is not permission to leave. It is very severe to leave while the Sefer Torah is open. If you must leave, leave before it is taken out. If you need to step out, do it when it is closed or covered between aliyot. "It's very serious."

What counts as "finishing"

The Mishnah Berurah explains that on a weekday they may finish all three aliyot, and on Shabbat all seven, with the Kaddish that follows. They may not add extra aliyot or call Maftir, and may not read the Haftarah. If they began the Haftarah with ten, they may finish it, because it is one unit.

If ten were present at the start and some left

Mishnah Berurah (Ashkenazim)

Finish all the aliyot of the day (three on a weekday, seven on Shabbat), with the Kaddish after

Rav Ovadia Yosef (Sefaradim)

Finish only the aliyah in progress

The Rabbi noted that in the Beur Halacha the Mishnah Berurah himself sounds unsure, but the Mishnah Berurah is his ruling. Rav Ovadia Yosef is concerned about that view, and rules that one finishes only the current aliyah.

Someone asked: if the tenth man goes to the toilet, should everyone wait for him or carry on? According to Rav Ovadia Yosef the reading cannot continue to a new aliyah with nine. Do something else meanwhile, such as Ashrei or a short shiur, and continue when he is back.

Others who have already heard

What if some of the ten have already heard the reading? The Shulhan Arukh brings a view that all ten must be obligated, and a view that it is enough if the majority have not yet heard. The halacha follows the second: as long as six have not heard it, the reading may be done.

The Rabbi mentioned that Ashkenazim have a lenient opinion allowing nine men and a child holding a Humash to make a minyan in extreme need. Sefaradim do not rely on it, and the Shulhan Arukh was against it.

Scrolls that are not a full Sefer Torah

  • A book of the Torah written separately, not sewn together with all five books, has the status of a Humash, and no berakhah is made on it.
  • Where there is no Sefer Torah, one person should still read the portion aloud, without berakhot, "so as not to forget" the reading. This also applies on a Monday or Thursday with only nine. He recalled doing this during COVID.
  • Even in a village with no other scroll, a Sefer Torah that is not kosher does not permit a berakhah.

A mistake in the Sefer Torah

This was the main topic. The Rabbi first set out the two positions brought by the Mishnah Berurah:

  • The majority of authorities: a Sefer Torah with even one missing or wrong letter is invalid. If a mistake is found, even after all seven aliyot, nothing has been read. A new scroll is taken out and all seven are called again from the beginning of the parashah, with new berakhot.
  • The Rambam and a minority: a scroll with a mistake, even with verses missing, is not a Sefer Torah for the misvah of writing one, but it may be read from with a berakhah if there is no other scroll.

The Shulhan Arukh (143:3) follows the majority from the outset: one may not knowingly read from a scroll with a mistake with a berakhah, even where there is no other. But in 143:4, following Mahari Beirav of Safed, once it has already happened one relies on the Rambam for what has been read. As the Rabbi put it, the Shulhan Arukh intertwines the two opinions.

The practical result:

  1. The mistake is found after the reading is over. The reading is valid. Next time, use a different scroll.
  2. The mistake is found between aliyot. Those aliyot count. Take out another Sefer Torah and continue from where they stopped with the next aliyah, counting towards the seven. If three were read, call the fourth from the new scroll.
  3. The mistake is found in the middle of an aliyah. Stop, take out another Sefer Torah, and continue from the place where the mistake was found, reading at least three verses. Only then does that person say the closing berakhah. No new opening berakhah is said, because it is one continuous reading.

So starting again from the beginning of the parashah is unnecessary, and calling seven new people would be wrong: according to the Rambam the reading already counted, so new berakhot would be in vain. "Anyone who says you have to go back to the beginning of the Sefer is wrong. 143:4."

The Rabbi mentioned that a few weeks earlier a mistake had been found in a scroll during a reading and they started again, which was not necessary.

If there is no other scroll, they finish reading from the same one, without the closing berakhah. Someone asked whether it would be better to read from a Humash. The Rabbi first said there was no difference, then corrected himself: it is better to read from the scroll, since according to the Rambam it is still a reading from a Sefer Torah.

The Rema agrees with all this, with one difference: in the middle of an aliyah he holds that if three verses have been read, the person stops and says the closing berakhah on the first scroll, since he began with a berakhah, and the reading then continues on the second scroll with a new berakhah. "It's a very small difference."

Extra and missing letters

The Rema adds that a new scroll is taken out only for a real mistake. A letter that is haser or yater, a vav or yud spelled defectively or in full, does not require a new scroll. Our scrolls are not so precise in these spellings that the next one would be any better. The Rabbi gave Yaakov, usually written without a vav and in five places with one, and Efron, written with a vav except once.

Someone asked why we are so sure of pronunciation but not of spelling. The Rabbi explained that pronunciation is preserved because it changes how the word sounds. Whether Efron has a vav, "we're always going to say Efron." That is why the tradition of full and defective spellings could more easily be forgotten.

Practical summary

  • The Torah is read with berakhot only with ten adult men; a child may be one of the seven aliyot but not one of the ten.
  • The reading has started once the person called up says "Barukh Atah Hashem." If people leave after that, Sefaradim finish only that aliyah (Rav Ovadia Yosef); the Mishnah Berurah allows finishing all the aliyot.
  • Do not leave while the Sefer Torah is open, and do not plan to leave in the middle.
  • If a real mistake is found in the scroll, what has been read stands. Take out another scroll and continue from where you are, without restarting the parashah.
  • A defective or full spelling (an extra or missing vav or yud) is not a reason to change scrolls.
