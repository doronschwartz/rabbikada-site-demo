The halacha

  • Erev Pesah on Shabbat last happened in 2021 and before that in 2008, so the details need refreshing.
  • The firstborns' fast is not in the Talmud Bavli and the Rambam does not codify it. Masekhet Soferim (perhaps 7th or 8th century) writes that firstborns fast to recall that HaKadosh Barukh Hu spared the Israelite firstborns; it is discussed whether the Yerushalmi holds this. It became customary in the time of the Rishonim.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules firstborns fast. Because of its obscure origin, the custom is to be exempted by a Seudat Misvah, such as a Berit Milah or a siyum.
  • One must actually take part: hear the siyum and eat from it. Having food brought home from a siyum is a common but wrong practice.
  • The Shulhan Arukh cites, but does not follow, a view that female firstborns fast. The Rema brings an Ashkenazi custom that a father fasts for a firstborn son under Bar Misvah; this is not the Sepharadi custom.
  • This year the fast cannot be on Shabbat, nor on Friday (we avoid fasting on Erev Shabbat), so it would move to Thursday, as Taanit Esther does. The Shulhan Arukh brings two opinions, "yesh omrim" that one fasts and "yesh omrim" that one does not; when he brings two such opinions the halacha follows the second.
  • So most Sepharadi poskim hold a firstborn need not fast at all this year. Hakham Ovadiah writes that attending a siyum is praiseworthy, but strictly one need not. Ashkenazim fast on Thursday, the 12th of Nisan, or attend a siyum.

Sources quoted

  • Masekhet Soferim: the origin of the fast.
  • Shulhan Arukh: two opinions when Erev Pesah is on Shabbat; the halacha follows the second.
  • The Rema: a father fasts for a young firstborn son.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: going to a siyum is "tavo alav berakhah".

In one line

This year Sepharadi firstborns strictly need not fast, though attending a siyum is praiseworthy, while Ashkenazim fast or attend a siyum on Thursday.
