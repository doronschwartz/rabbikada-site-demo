Topic: Drying, soap, combing and braiding after a permitted shower on Shabbat and Yom Tov

Sources: The Hakhamim; the poskim; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 17 June 2025

The halacha

  • Wherever showering is permitted on Shabbat or Yom Tov, in cold, lukewarm or hot water, four further points apply.
  • Drying: hairless parts of the body may be dried normally with a towel. Hair, of men or women, may not be dried vigorously, because of the rabbinic prohibition of sehitah, squeezing, a form of dash. From the Torah it is not, since the water sits between the hairs rather than absorbed in them, but the Hakhamim forbade it. One may only place the towel gently and pat, removing the water on the surface.
  • Soap: liquid shampoo or shower gel is fine. Most poskim forbid a bar of soap, and even Hakham Ovadiah, who finds a basis for those who use it, says it is better not to. So use liquid forms.
  • Combing: combing with a regular brush is forbidden, because it inevitably pulls out hair, the prohibition of gozez, shearing. One may use a brush with very soft, widely spaced bristles, since pulling hair is then not inevitable, and if it happens that is fine. Some poskim require a special Shabbat comb; Sepharadim are generally not particular, the main thing being soft bristles.
  • Braiding hair is forbidden, a rabbinic prohibition because it resembles boneh, building.

Sources quoted

  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: though there is a basis to allow bar soap, it is better not to use it.

In one line

After a permitted shower pat the hair dry, use liquid soap, comb only with a soft brush and do not braid.
