Topic: Which injuries make a man a pesua dakka or kerut shofkhah, and the case of prostate surgery

Sources: Rashi; the Gemara; the Hazon Ish; the Sis Eliezer (Rav Eliezer Waldenberg); Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The status requires that he can no longer have children. Rashi explains pesua dakka as an injury to the testicles (besim) and kerut shofkhah as an injury to the organ (gid). If such an injury prevents reproduction, all the marriage laws learned apply.
  • The Gemara adds a third: the hutei besim, the "threads" of the testicles, which Rashi describes as the tubes from which the sac hangs.
  • This mattered for a question asked many times, from the Hazon Ish to Rav Moshe Feinstein: prostate surgery on older men, where part of the enlarged prostate was cut and the sperm tubes were also cut. He does not know if this is still done; certainly it was common 30 to 50 years ago.
  • A Jewish doctor asked whether he could perform it, and whether it makes the patient a pesua dakka. All the poskim ruled it permitted, and that it does not make him a pesua dakka.
  • The Hazon Ish led with the hiddush that the Torah and Gemara speak only of external, exposed parts. Internal tubes cut in surgery do not create the status, even if he can no longer reproduce. Some disagreed, but Rav Moshe Feinstein and Rav Eliezer Waldenberg confirmed it, and that seems to have been the practice.
  • So the status comes only from external injury to the testicles, the organ or the hutei besim. Internal surgery that affects reproduction, common today, does not create it.

Sources quoted

  • Rashi: pesua dakka is the testicles, kerut shofkhah the organ; the hutei besim are the tubes of the sac.
  • The Hazon Ish: only external injuries count.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein and the Sis Eliezer: followed the Hazon Ish on prostate surgery.

In one line

Only an external injury creates the status of pesua dakka, so internal surgery such as prostate surgery does not.
