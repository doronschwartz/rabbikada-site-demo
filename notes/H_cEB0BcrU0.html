Topic: Giving a male animal hormones that make its semen non-viable

Sources: The Vilna Gaon; Rav Moshe Feinstein; the Teshuvot Bemareh HaBazak

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Recap: damaging a male animal's reproductive organs is forbidden, directly from the Torah and indirectly rabbinically. Reducing the animal's desire to reproduce is permitted, since nothing happens to the organs.
  • The new question: hormones that leave the organs untouched but make the semen non-viable.
  • One view: this affects the seed itself and may be forbidden; the Vilna Gaon could hold that anything affecting the seed is a problem.
  • The other view: the prohibition concerns the organs, and here only their product is affected, internally. Rav Moshe Feinstein wanted to permit it on this ground.
  • He added that one should try to make the effect temporary. Some hold that temporary infertility is not sirus at all, since the Torah forbade irreversible castration; others forbid it for the period it lasts.
  • With both points together (only the semen, and only temporarily) one can certainly be lenient, as Rav Moshe Feinstein wrote explicitly.
  • For irreversible hormones Rav Moshe Feinstein was strict, but Bemareh HaBazak is lenient. Reversible hormones are better; if that is not possible there are grounds to be lenient, provided only the fluid is affected and not the organs.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: permits affecting the semen rather than the organs, certainly when temporary; strict on irreversible hormones.
  • Teshuvot Bemareh HaBazak: lenient if necessary, even for irreversible hormones.

In one line

Hormones that temporarily make a male animal's semen non-viable may be given, and there are grounds to be lenient even when the effect is permanent, as long as the organs are not touched.
