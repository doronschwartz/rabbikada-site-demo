Topic: Whether one may work on Purim

Sources: The Megillah, chapter 9; the Gemara; the Rishonim; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Purim, Thursday 13 March 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara notes a discrepancy in Megillah chapter 9: first Purim is instituted as days of mishteh, simhah "veyom tov", and yom tov would forbid work; later it says only mishteh and simhah, without yom tov.
  • The Gemara explains that perhaps Mordekhai and Esther first intended full yamim tovim, but the yom tov element, which would forbid work, was never accepted. So we may drive, write and so on.
  • Yet the Gemara tells of a Hakham who was upset with someone working on Purim, and explains: though melakhah is permitted, where the local minhag is not to work it is forbidden.
  • So rule the Rishonim, the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh: strictly work is permitted, but forbidden where the custom is not to work. The Rishonim already wrote, and it is the accepted practice of the poskim today, that the minhag now is not to work on Purim.
  • So one who can take the day off, or who runs a business, should refrain from regular work on Purim day. If there is a great loss (hefsed gadol), or one has already taken enough days off for the Yamim Tovim, one may work. Business connected with Purim, such as food, drink or Purim merchandise, is permitted as part of the simhah.
  • The Rambam, brought by the Shulhan Arukh, writes that even where one may work, one sees no blessing from work done on Purim.
  • "Work" means one's regular, fixed work, not small things like writing. This applies to the day; at night there is a debate, but there is no minhag to forbid it, so it is permitted.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: yom tov was not accepted for Purim, but local custom not to work binds.
  • The Rambam and Shulhan Arukh: no success comes from work done on Purim.

In one line

Work on Purim is strictly permitted, but the custom now is to refrain from regular work by day unless there is real need.
