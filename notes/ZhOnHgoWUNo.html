Topic: When the Omer restrictions end for Sepharadim and Ashkenazim, Lag BaOmer on a Friday, and refraining from work after sunset

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Midrash; the Torah ("sheva shabbatot"); Hazon Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Sefirat HaOmer, Thursday 15 April 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Even those who wait for Shabbat to say Shehiyanu may buy new clothes during the Omer, unlike the Three Weeks and the Nine Days.
  • Shulhan Arukh: for Sepharadim the restrictions continue until the 34th day, since the Midrash says Rabbi Akiva's students stopped dying half a month before Shavuot. So Sepharadim do not marry or cut hair on Lag BaOmer.
  • Ashkenazim hold the dying stopped on the 33rd, so they allow haircuts and weddings on Lag BaOmer itself.
  • This year Lag BaOmer falls on a Friday and the 34th on Shabbat, so all agree one may cut hair and shave on the Friday. Weddings on Friday are still not permitted for Sepharadim.
  • A lesser-known custom in the Shulhan Arukh: not working after sunset each evening of the Omer. Reasons: the students were buried after sunset; or "sheva shabbatot" suggests rest until one has counted.
  • It applies mainly to women, some say men too, and only to laborious work such as knitting and sewing, for half an hour to an hour.
  • Hazon Ovadiah writes that where it is not the custom one need not worry; most places do not keep it.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: restrictions until the 34th day; the custom of no work after sunset.
  • Hazon Ovadiah: no need to keep it where it is not customary.

In one line

Sepharadim keep the Omer restrictions until the 34th day, but this year all may cut hair on Friday Lag BaOmer.
