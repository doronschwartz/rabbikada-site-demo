Topic: Further examples of how far the Pesah stringency on nullification extends, Shulhan Arukh against the Rema

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Tuesday 29 March 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • On Pesah nullification in 60 does not work. Some Rishonim extended this stringency to other areas of the laws of Pesah; others limited it to nullification alone. The first example, given yesterday, was noten taam lifgam, permitted by the Shulhan Arukh even on Pesah and forbidden by the Rema.
  • We do not assume something is prohibited without a reason to suspect it. Meat, fish or cheese salted before Pesah and not guarded from Hames may be eaten on Pesah according to the Shulhan Arukh, unless there is a reason for suspicion (someone seen nearby with a sandwich). The Rema requires that it was guarded.
  • A drop of Hames that falls into food before Pesah is nullified in 60 as usual. The Shulhan Arukh holds it does not "reawaken" on Pesah. The Rema holds that in most cases it is hozer veneor, and becomes forbidden once Pesah begins, though the food may be eaten until then, even on Erev Pesah afternoon.
  • Kashering utensils: the Shulhan Arukh goes after the majority use, so a utensil used almost always for cold and only once or twice for hot does not need kashering. The Rema requires hagalah if it was used for Hames even once.
  • The Sepharadi approach: the Pesah stringency is limited to nullification on Pesah itself, and the regular laws of kashrut otherwise apply. The Rema extends it, though even for him it is a stringency, so in some cases he is lenient.
  • Sepharadim with a custom to be strict should continue it, but from the letter of the law the rulings above stand.

In one line

For the Shulhan Arukh the only Pesah stringency is no nullification on Pesah itself; the Rema extends it to salted foods, reawakened Hames and kashering.
