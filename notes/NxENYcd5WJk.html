Topic: Seeing or eating the simanim, why these foods, apples and honey, pomegranates, and when to eat them

Sources: The Gemara; the Rishonim; Shulhan Arukh; Rashi; the Bet Yoseph; the Geonim; the Tur and the Maharil; the Pri Hadash; Shir HaShirim

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Tuesday 31 August 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Many Rishonim had a text of the Gemara saying to see the simanim, not eat them; having them on the table was the original custom. The Shulhan Arukh writes to eat them, but one who cannot prepare them, such as checking spinach for insects, still gains by placing them on the table. The Yehi Rason prayers came later, in the time of the Rishonim.
  • Why dates, spinach, leeks, beet and squash? Rashi says because they grow quickly (the Bet Yoseph notes dates do not). The Geonim say their names suggest prayers, which led to many customs of other foods.
  • Apples and honey, now the symbol of Rosh Hashanah, are not in the Gemara. They appear in the Tur and the Maharil, about the 14th century, among European Rishonim who probably lacked the other foods: one of the most recent simanim.
  • The pomegranate is also later. The Pri Hadash asks why we pray to be full of misvot like a pomegranate when even the sinners of Israel are, as the Gemara reads Shir HaShirim. He answers that they become so over a lifetime; we pray to be so within the year.
  • Those whose custom is to do the simanim between Kiddush and HaMosi may continue. Otherwise do them after HaMosi and a kazayit of bread, to avoid a dispute over whether Birkat HaMazon covers them or an after-berakhah is needed.

In one line

The simanim were first meant to be seen, apples and honey came late, and they are best eaten after HaMosi.
