Topic: Two textual differences in Al HaNissim: Matityah or Matityahu, and how to read "Hashmonai"

Sources: Shulhan Arukh, Hilchot Gittin; Terumat HaDeshen; Ezra; Divrei HaYamim; Bet Yoseph; the Hida; the Ben Ish Hai; old Spanish and Portuguese siddurim; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

The halacha

  • After last week's two differences in the Menorah blessings, Al HaNissim has two more. The first is the name of the man who began the revolt: Matityah or Matityahu.
  • In a get names must be precise. The Shulhan Arukh, in Hilchot Gittin, brings the Terumat HaDeshen (Rabbi Yisrael Isserlein, Austria, an Ashkenazi): although Tanakh writes Matityah (in Ezra and Divrei HaYamim), people commonly say Matityahu, so a child was presumably named that and the get says Matityahu.
  • The Bet Yoseph disagrees: Tanakh says Matityah, and our liturgy calls him Matityah, so the get is written Matityah. That is the halacha for Sepharadim, and our siddurim all have Matityah.
  • Later Ashkenazi poskim write two gittin, one with each name; Ashkenazi siddurim have Matityahu.
  • The second difference is the word after "Yohanan Kohen Gadol": is "Hashmonai" a description ("the Hasmonean") or a second name of Yohanan? Sources go both ways.
  • Ashkenazim read it as a description, and among Sepharadim the Hida and the Ben Ish Hai agree. The old Spanish and Portuguese siddurim treat it as Yohanan's second name ("Yohanan, also called Hashmonai"), and so Hakham Ovadiah rules.

In one line

Sepharadim say Matityah, Ashkenazim Matityahu; and the Spanish and Portuguese read Hashmonai as Yohanan's second name, not a description.
