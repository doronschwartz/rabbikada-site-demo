Topic: Chemotherapy, temporary castration and surgical neutering of male animals, and the Jewish vet's livelihood

Sources: Rav Uziel

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Physically damaging a male animal's reproductive organs is Doraita; doing so indirectly is deRabbanan.
  • Contemporary poskim write that chemotherapy or radiation which leaves the organs unable to reproduce has the status of kos shel ikkarin: indirect, and rabbinically forbidden for males. It is not a permitted method.
  • The permitted ways are reducing the animal's desire to reproduce or, according to some poskim, making the semen non-viable.
  • Temporary castration (sirus zemani), where the organs are disabled but a second operation could restore them: Rav Uziel wrote that this too is forbidden, probably on a Torah level, even though it is reversible.
  • Cutting off the organs of a male animal is certainly a Torah prohibition, with no heter, whether the animal belongs to a Jew or a non-Jew.
  • A Jewish vet asked to do this must decline the job, even at a loss. We never permit an Issur Doraita, nor an Issur deRabbanan, for loss of parnasah.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Uziel: temporary, reversible castration is still forbidden.

In one line

A Jewish vet may not neuter a male animal by surgery, chemotherapy or reversible castration, even if it costs him work.
