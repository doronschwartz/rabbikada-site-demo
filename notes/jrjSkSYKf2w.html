Where we are in the Siman

Siman 144 deals with conditions in a get. Last week's main topic was a condition made for her benefit (tenai letovatah), such as "if I do not come back within 12 months, you are divorced", as opposed to a condition where the husband wants something from her. The Rabbi said this class would finish the Siman: Seifim 3 to 6 are fairly straightforward, and Seif 7 is the important one.

Seif 3: He dies before the 12 months end

The husband says: "If I do not come back within 12 months, this is a get from now" (me-achshav). He dies after six months. The get took effect from the start, so she is divorced. If she has children, there is no question.

The question is when she has no children and there is a brother-in-law (yavam). He is dead and cannot return, so perhaps she may marry at once. But perhaps she must wait until the 12 months actually pass. The Gemara leaves this as a safek, so the Rishonim rule strictly: halitzah, or wait the 12 months.

Why might she need to wait? There are three reasons:

  • Tosafot: a rabbinic decree, since people will not know he died.
  • The Rambam, as the Beit Shemuel reads him: this is the Rambam the class has met before, about a debt of 200 zuz. If a set time has not yet arrived, the matter is not complete, even if the time is certain to come.
  • The Ran: perhaps he meant to hold on to her for the whole 12 months, and wanted no get to take effect before then. Then she is still married to a dead man and needs yibum or halitzah.

The Shulhan Arukh says that if she married anyway, she must leave. The Beit Shemuel leans to this at least according to the Ran, and perhaps even according to the Rambam, but he is not fully sure.

Seif 4: "If the sun rises"

The husband says the get is valid "if" or "on condition" that the sun rises tomorrow. The sun will certainly rise, so one expects a get from now. Yet the Shulhan Arukh calls it a safek, and she needs halitzah, not yibum.

This is the doubt from last week, following Rabbi Yose: is a condition tied to a time that must come really a condition, or does the get only take effect when the time arrives? The Beit Shemuel adds a second doubt. The wording "when" the sun rises sounds like the moment of sunrise itself, so even Rabbi Yose might agree the get only works then.

The Shulhan Arukh adds a view that even a plain al menat is only a safek, since al menat is not treated as a get from now. The Rabbi asked who holds this view, and why it appears only here. The Beit Shemuel asks the same, and the Gra goes further and says there is no such opinion. In practice we treat al menat as a get from now, which is the view of almost everyone.

Seif 5: This is not a condition at all

The Rabbi asked why this Seif is different. The husband says: "If I do not come back within 12 months, write a get and give it to my wife." Nothing takes effect now. He is simply instructing his agent (shaliah) when to act. So none of the rules of conditions apply.

  • If the get is written or given within the 12 months, it is invalid, because it goes against his words.
  • If it is given after 12 months, even long afterwards, it is valid, as long as the husband is alive. He set no end date.
  • If he died, and the get was given before the death, it is a get. If it is unknown which came first, it is a safek.

Seif 6: "After a week", "after a year"

The Gemara says: if he said "after the Shemitah", it means after a year; "after a year" means after a month; "after a month" means after a week. There are two readings.

Rashi and the Rosh: a condition. "If I do not come back after a year, you are divorced." The word l'achar adds a further period. If he had meant the end of the year, he would have said "within". So the get takes effect after a year and a month. After a month, a month and a week.

The Rambam: an instruction to the agent. Here the word sets the time frame for writing. After the Shemitah, the get may be written only in the following year. After a year, only in the following month. After a month, only in the following week. After Shabbat, only Sunday to Tuesday. If he said "before Shabbat", only Wednesday to Friday. As the Rabbi put it, Rashi is learning "from when" and the Rambam "until when".

The Rabbi took questions from the class:

  • Does the Rambam agree with Rashi's din? Yes, and the Acharonim say there is no reason Rashi's din is not true in practice. Whether Rashi would accept the Rambam's din is less clear. Either way, both are followed.
  • What if the get was written outside the Rambam's frame? The Rambam says it is invalid. The Shulhan Arukh follows the Rambam's reading but treats the result as a safek, not simply invalid.

Why is Seif 5 different from Seif 6?

In Seif 5 he may give the get at any time after 12 months, but in Seif 6 there is a strict frame. Why? A student answered that in Seif 5 the 12 months is only a starting point. The Beit Shemuel makes this distinction: "until 12 months" means he cannot act before then, while l'achar brings in a specific unit of time.

The Rabbi also noted a detail in the Rema (after "30 days" only one extra day, after "a month" a week) and said honestly that he has never fully understood the distinction. A student suggested that "30 days" already names the smallest unit, a day, so there is nothing smaller to add. It works as an explanation, but remains a difficult point.

Seif 7: Shema piyes, perhaps they reconciled

The Gemara has two cases of a condition for her benefit: "if I do not see you for 30 days, you are divorced", and "if I do not come within 12 months".

First, the background. If there was yihud in the 30 days, it is a get yashan and invalid. A get yashan is a get that is old by the time it takes effect. If the couple were alone together after it was written, she may become pregnant, and the child would be conceived after the date of the get. People will say a child was conceived after the divorce. The Rabbi called this a rabbinic and rather remote concern, but a real one.

In our Seif there was no yihud. He visited, they came and went, but never were alone together. After 30 days without seeing each other, the get takes effect and is valid. Yet the Gemara asks: perhaps they were reconciled (shema piyes)? So the husband must give a ne'emanut, a statement that they were not reconciled. Without it, the get is rabbinically invalid.

Why does this worry appear only here? In the earlier Seifim the husband wanted something from her. In a condition made for her benefit, there is a special concern about reconciliation. The Rishonim give four explanations of the concern:

  • Rashi: they fell in love again, had yihud, and it becomes a get yashan. Tosafot are unhappy with this.
  • Rabbenu Tam: perhaps he cancelled the get. If it is an im condition, he can cancel it at any time. If it is a get from now, he cannot cancel by words, but perhaps he came to her in order to cancel it, even though nobody saw.
  • The Ri: only a worry that people will think he cancelled, and they will carry on as a married couple.
  • The Rif, the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh: perhaps she waived the condition. She might have said, "I do not mind if you stay away longer than 30 days." It was for her benefit, so she can forgive it, and then the get does not take effect.

The Rif and Rambam's reading is easy if the condition is im, which can be changed. But the Ramban understood the Gemara to speak about al menat, and asked how she could waive it. This is the source of his well-known chiddush: even with al menat, when both of them agree, they can cancel the condition.

The Shulhan Arukh follows the Rif and the Rambam, not Rashi or Rabbenu Tam. If we knew she had waived the condition, the get would be invalid by Torah law. Since we only suspect it, the get is rabbinically invalid unless he gives the ne'emanut.

The 12-month case

Here the worry is that he came secretly. A woman might marry someone else, and he was in fact home. So he gives a ne'emanut that he did not come during the 12 months. The Shulhan Arukh adds that the minhag is that when making such conditions at the outset, he states that there was no shema piyes.

The way to avoid it all

The Rabbi gave a simple practical solution. When writing such a condition, say that if he comes, the get is cancelled only if he comes with a Beit Din. Then no ne'emanut is needed, because without a Beit Din there is no cancellation.

Practical summary

  • A get from now, conditioned on his not returning within 12 months: if he dies earlier, a childless widow with a yavam still needs halitzah or must wait the 12 months, because the Gemara is in doubt.
  • A condition tied to something sure to happen, such as sunrise, is a safek in the Shulhan Arukh. In practice we treat al menat as a get from now.
  • "Write and give a get after 12 months" is an instruction to the agent, not a condition. It may not be given early, but is valid however late, while the husband lives.
  • With "after a week, month or year", both Rashi's reading (a condition with an extra period) and the Rambam's (a frame for writing) are followed. Outside the frame, the get is a safek.
  • For a condition in her favour, the husband gives a ne'emanut that they did not reconcile (and, for 12 months, that he did not come). Without it, the get is rabbinically invalid.
  • Better still, make any cancellation depend on his coming with a Beit Din, and the problem does not arise.
