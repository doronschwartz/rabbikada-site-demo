Topic: The first six melakhot of food preparation on Yom Tov: ploughing through winnowing

Sources: The Hakhamim; Shulhan Arukh; the Rambam; the poskim; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Moadim

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Melakhot unrelated to food, such as writing or building, are forbidden on Yom Tov as on Shabbat. The discussion concerns the first 11 melakhot, which all relate to preparing food.
  • Ploughing and planting (horesh and zorea) are identical to Shabbat: they take time to yield food and are not for okhel nefesh.
  • Koser, harvesting, such as picking an apple to eat on Yom Tov, is forbidden; it is debated whether from the Torah or the Hakhamim. Harvesting is normally done in bulk for the long term, to store or sell, so even picking one apple is forbidden. Likewise meamer, gathering fallen fruit, even fruit that fell before Yom Tov.
  • Dash, extracting the edible from the inedible, such as shelling peas, is generally forbidden on Yom Tov, since it is normally done in bulk, melekhet avodah. But the Shulhan Arukh and the Rambam rule that by hand, where it cannot be done in bulk, it is permitted in the regular way. On Shabbat it requires a shinui; on Yom Tov one may shell peas or anything else from its pod by hand normally.
  • Squeezing olives and grapes, a toladah of dash, is forbidden on Yom Tov. A minority of poskim allowed squeezing other fruits such as oranges, but the vast majority, including Hakham Ovadiah, forbid it as on Shabbat.
  • Zoreh, winnowing, is done long term and is forbidden.
  • So the first six melakhot are basically identical to Shabbat, except shelling by hand. The next melakhot show differences.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh and the Rambam: shelling by hand is permitted on Yom Tov in the regular way.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: squeezing any fruit is forbidden on Yom Tov.

In one line

Ploughing, planting, harvesting, gathering, squeezing and winnowing are forbidden on Yom Tov as on Shabbat, but pods may be shelled by hand normally.
