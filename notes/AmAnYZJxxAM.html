Topic: The fast of the firstborn when Erev Pesah is on Shabbat

Sources: Masekhet Soferim; the Talmud Yerushalmi; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Pesah

The halacha

  • The earliest source for firstborns fasting on Erev Pesah is Masekhet Soferim (7th or 8th century), recalling that the Jewish firstborns were spared in Egypt. The Rishonim debate whether the Yerushalmi agrees; the Rambam does not codify it.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules that firstborns fast, and this is the universal custom. Some say female firstborns fast, but the Shulhan Arukh says that is not the custom. The Rema writes that a father fasts for a firstborn son, but that is not the Sepharadi custom.
  • When Erev Pesah is Shabbat, the Shulhan Arukh brings two opinions. One: fast on Thursday the 12th of Nisan, since one cannot fast on Shabbat or enter Shabbat fasting from Friday. Two: the fast is pushed off and not kept at all.
  • The Rema rules that Ashkenazim fast on Thursday. For Sepharadim the halacha generally follows the second of two opinions in the Shulhan Arukh, so strictly there is no need to fast or attend a siyum.
  • Hakham Ovadiah writes that if possible one should attend a siyum on Thursday. The custom is to break the fast at a Seudat Misvah, such as a Berit Milah or a siyum.
  • Normally one must hear the siyum and also eat from the meal, not just listen or have food brought home. This year, since the fast is only a stringency, hearing a siyum over Zoom suffices even without eating.

In one line

When Erev Pesah is Shabbat, Sepharadi firstborns strictly need not fast on Thursday, but it is good to attend and eat at a siyum, and this year even joining by Zoom suffices.
