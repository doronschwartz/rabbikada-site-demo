Sefer: Derech Hashem by Rabbi Moshe Hayim Luzzatto (the Ramhal) Section: Part 2, Chapter 7, paragraphs 2-4 (the end of Part 2) Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Derech Hashem series at Tiferet Eyal, Thursday 2 September 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Where we are

The previous class finished Chapter 8, on the four periods of human history: the worst was before the Torah; then the time of the Beit HaMikdash, with Torah and prophets; then our time; and the best is eternity, when all humanity will have a direct connection with Hashem. This class finishes Chapter 7, the last part of Part 2.

The Rabbi set out the plan of the book. Part 1 was about Hashem, Part 2 about His providence and how He relates to us. After Sukkot, Part 3 begins a completely new subject: ruah hakodesh, the different souls, prophecy and Moshe. Part 4, the most practical, is about our daily service and Shabbat.

On Monday they learned that every physical thing has a spiritual counterpart, its koah, which gives it existence, and that this comes down through the angels. Chapter 7 adds that before it reaches this world, it also passes through the mazalot, the stars and constellations.

Paragraph 2: what happens to things comes through the stars

It is not only the existence of things that comes down this way, but everything that happens to them: whether a person lives or dies, is healthy or ill, rich or poor. All of it is prepared above in the spiritual roots and then "copied" down, through the cycles and groupings of the stars.

Paragraph 3: mikreh, chance

Every event that happens to physical things is bound to the stars, and depends on what it receives from that system. That is a mikreh, a chance occurrence. So, the Rabbi said, "if you stop here, the world is random." The first energy comes from Hashem, since nothing exists without Him, but once it passes into the stars, what happens depends on which star a thing or a person is under, good luck or bad. His example: one born under Maadim, Mars, may happen to be drawn to bloodshed, by chance.

A participant compared this to the philosophers who held that Hashem created the world and left it alone. The Rabbi explained the difference: they held that Hashem left the world altogether. Here, nature runs by the stars, but Hashem has not left: "God's there waiting for us." Whoever seeks a relationship with Him is not left to chance.

Paragraph 4: rising above the stars

Human beings, too, are under this system, and what happens to them depends on the constellation they are under. But then comes the key line: the effect of the stars can be cancelled by a stronger power above them. That is what Hazal meant when they said "Ein mazal leYisrael", Israel is not under the stars. The power of Hashem overrides the power of the stars, so what happens to such a person comes directly from Hashem.

So by default the world, and many people's lives, are left to chance. But a person who places his trust in Hashem and has a direct relationship with Him rises above all of it. "If you're truly an eved Hashem," the Rabbi said, you do not need to worry about mazal. The Rabbi added that "Israel" here means whoever has that relationship: a non-Jew can have it, and a Jew can lack it.

Astrologers, the Ramhal adds, can know only part of how the stars work, and so can foresee only a little of the future. And for one who is directly with Hashem, it can be cancelled altogether.

Ending Part 2

To summarise the chapter: the world is under the force of the stars and constellations, but we can bring ourselves above it through a direct relationship with Hashem. "Your whole life should be like that." With this the class finished Part 2, halfway through the book.

Summary

  • Not only the existence of things but everything that happens to them comes down through the stars and constellations.
  • So nature, and much of human life, is left to chance, mikreh.
  • Hashem has not left the world; whoever turns to Him is with Him.
  • "Ein mazal leYisrael": one who trusts Hashem and has a direct relationship with Him rises above the stars.
  • This chapter completes Part 2; Part 3, on the soul and prophecy, begins after Sukkot.
