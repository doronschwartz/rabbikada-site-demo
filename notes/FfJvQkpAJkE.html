Topic: The significance of Neilah and the time by which it must be prayed

Sources: Gemara Keritot; Rashi; Talmud Yerushalmi; the Ramban; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Mishnah Berurah

Daily Halacha: Yom Kippur, Wednesday 20 September 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Neilah is the fifth prayer of Yom Kippur. It was once also said on public fasts declared for communal troubles such as drought, which we no longer have.
  • The Gemara in Keritot says every moment of Yom Kippur atones, by day and not at night. Rashi explains from "on this day" that atonement is by day, as at Arvit the fast is not yet felt.
  • The Talmud Yerushalmi, and the Ramban and others, place the main atonement at the end of the day. So there are levels: it begins at night, grows through the day, and reaches its crescendo at Neilah.
  • The Rambam and Shulhan Arukh rule that Neilah begins when the sun is on the treetops, about 45 minutes to an hour before sunset, and ends by sunset.
  • The Mishnah Berurah writes the custom became to rely on those who allow until nightfall, as in Europe there is a long gap of up to about an hour between sunset and nightfall.
  • At the very least the silent Amidah must be completed before sunset, preferably the repetition too. It is wrong to use that time selling Aliyot or the opening of the Hekhal, or drawing out El Nora Alilah.
  • To fill the time after sunset, the Eastern Sepharadim added Selihot to Neilah, which the Spanish and Portuguese do not.
  • Birkat Kohanim should be before sunset, but if the repetition runs on, it may be said until nightfall.
  • In Israel most places finish the repetition before sunset and add the extras afterwards.

In one line

Neilah is the climax of atonement, so at least its silent Amidah should be finished before sunset.
