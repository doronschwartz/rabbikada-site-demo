Topic: The three Haftarot of rebuke read in the Three Weeks and their source

Sources: Yirmiyahu chapters 1 and 2; Yeshayahu chapter 1; the Pesikta DeRav Kahana; the Tur and Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Wednesday 23 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • All of Israel reads three special Haftarot on the three Shabbatot between the 17th of Tammuz and Tisha BeAv, the "three of rebuke" (Telata DePuranuta).
  • First, Yirmiyahu chapter 1: like the quickly blossoming almond, the bad will come fast; the boiling pot and danger from the north allude to Bavel and Nevukhadnessar. It ends positively: "I remember the kindness of your youth", when we followed Hashem in the desert for 40 years.
  • Second, Yirmiyahu chapter 2: the people did two evils, leaving Hashem and going to what gives nothing, which is the greater insult. It ends with comfort: if we return sincerely our glory will be restored.
  • Third, Yeshayahu chapter 1, perhaps the most damning Haftarah of the year, always read the Shabbat before Tisha BeAv (this year Erev Tisha BeAv, as the fast is on Sunday). The leaders are compared to Sedom, and Hashem rejects sacrifices from those who oppress the poor and the widow: "who asked you to trample My courts?"
  • It contains "Eikhah", how the faithful city has become a harlot, hence its place before Tisha BeAv; some communities read that word in a special tune. It too ends happily: "Siyon will be redeemed with justice".
  • The Talmud does not mention these three; Haftarot were not yet fixed for every Shabbat. The source is the Pesikta DeRav Kahana, named for Rav Kahana's opening teachings but a compilation of many Amoraim, from somewhere between the fifth and seventh centuries.
  • It was adopted toward the end of the Geonic period. The Rambam does not mention it; the Tur and Shulhan Arukh do. It is a custom of all Israel and a distinctive mark of the Three Weeks.

Sources quoted

  • The Pesikta DeRav Kahana: the earliest source for the three Haftarot.
  • The Tur and Shulhan Arukh: record the practice.

In one line

The three Haftarot of rebuke, each ending in comfort, are a custom of all Israel going back to the Pesikta DeRav Kahana.
