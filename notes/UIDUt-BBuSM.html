"You know the saying: two Jews, three opinions. How did this come about? Is it correct practice? What can't be argued about, and what can?"

The chain

Yehoshua → the elders → the prophets, prophet to prophet → the Men of the Great Assembly, 120 of the greatest Sages of their time, who also derived new laws.

The last of them, Shimon HaTzaddik, also Kohen Gadol, about 2,100 years ago, counts as the first of the Sages. Prophecy ends; the dispute with the Sadducees begins; the era of the Chachamim starts. Then, generation after generation, to…

"To do a major project, it's not enough to be wise and have great language. You also need prominence and authority. His wealth helped him do it." (At least, that's the Rambam's implication.)

He gathered everything received from Moshe until his day: the accepted and the disputed.

"Isn't that incredible?"

The Rambam traces his chain: Rebbi ← Shimon his father ← Gamliel ← Shimon ← Gamliel ← Shimon ← Hillel ← Shemaya and Avtalyon ← Yehudah ben Tabbai and Shimon ben Shetach ← Yehoshua ben Perachyah and Nittai HaArbeli ← Yosei ben Yoezer and Yosei ben Yochanan ← Antigonos ← Shimon HaTzaddik ← Ezra ← Baruch ben Neriyah ← Yirmeyahu.

Here it stops. In the Introduction to the Mishneh Torah he continues: Yirmeyahu ← Tzefanyah ← Chabakuk ← Nachum ← Yoel ← Michah ← Yeshayahu ← Amos ← Hoshea ← Zecharyah ← Yehoyada ← Elisha ← Eliyahu ← Achiyah ← David ← Shmuel ← Eli ← Pinchas ← Yehoshua ← Moshe ← Hashem.

Rav Kook, opening schools as guest speaker, would line up about a hundred students, each one a generation: you're Moshe, you're Yehoshua, you're Pinchas… and this is our generation. "It's absolutely incredible that we can literally trace back, generation to generation."

Then why does the Gemara search for sources?

Bava Kamma (start of chapter 8) spends two full pages on eight or nine derivations that an eye for an eye means money. Sukkah gives about four sources that pri etz hadar means etrog. Why, if it's all from Moshe?

To hang the law on the verse. "From an educational point of view, the best way to remember something is to connect it to a verse we know by heart."

  • Hadar = הדר, "that dwells" on its tree from year to year, as the etrog does.
  • Or הדר על פני מים, "that grows by water."

"Many fruits live from year to year. They know 100% it's etrog. They're arguing only about which verse to hang it on. I say this mnemonic, you say that."

Part 4: Then What Is Halachah leMoshe miSinai?

The question: Tefillin must be black. No source in the Torah. (It isn't rabbinic either: wear non-black tefillin every day and you've never fulfilled the mitzvah. Rabbinic law like muktzeh, of course, has no verse.) The Sages call it הלכה למשה מסיני. But by the Rambam's account, everything received is from Moshe at Sinai. So why label only some?

The answer: Over time the Sages found some anchor in the verse for most received laws (an eye for an eye = money). For a few they couldn't: not even a hint. For black tefillin, the only authority is Moshe. Those are called halachah leMoshe miSinai.

The Rambam lists them, "before there was an encyclopedia, before you could Google": about 25 across all of Shas. "I think it's all of them, but perhaps I forgot one." Examples:

  • Tefillin are black.
  • Every Torah "eating" means a kezayit.
  • Sukkah walls: halachically you may "bend" a wall, or extend it up or down.
  • Mikveh: the rules of interpositions (chatzitzot).

On category 3: "If it's received, I must accept it. If you derived it, I can argue back." That's why the Gemara always asks what are they arguing about? What's the reason? "I want to understand your rationale, because then I have the right to disagree."

On category 4: Rabbi Yosei HaGelili's town ate poultry with milk: he didn't feel the fence was needed. "You feel it needs a fence; I'm the rabbi in my city, I feel it doesn't." Some fences were later lifted: there was a time gentile oil was forbidden, then abolished.

Gezerot vs takkanot: a gezerah is stronger, a decree or fence (don't, or X may happen). A takkanah is good practice instituted for our benefit, which became law once accepted.

Part 6: Why Disputes Multiplied

The Gemara (Sotah 47b): משרבו תלמידי שמאי והלל שלא שמשו כל צרכן, רבו מחלוקות בישראל ונעשית תורה כשתי תורות.

When the students of Shammai and Hillel multiplied who had not served their teachers enough, disputes multiplied, and the Torah became like two Torahs.

The wrong reading

Many people read this as applying to categories 1 and 2: students didn't listen properly, so what was transmitted got garbled, "Chinese whispers." Moshe said black tefillin; the students half-heard; "black, grey, something…"

The Rambam: people who say this don't understand. "One of the worst statements I've ever heard." It kills the whole transmission. Categories 1 and 2 are intact and factual, even when students didn't fully learn from their teachers.

The right reading: method, not memory

Disputes belong only to category 3. Why did they increase?

Two people with full intellectual ability, the same rigorous training from their teachers, given the same text to derive from: 99 times out of 100, they'll reach the same answer. There's human diversity, so some minor disagreements, which existed even between Beit Hillel and Beit Shammai and before, but the core derivations come out the same.

When that skill isn't properly honed, the derivations diverge more and more.

So there was far less dispute before Hillel and Shammai and their students (about 1,900 years ago), and the further we go, the more.

"Contrary to what we're told to believe: yes, difference of opinion can be good. But we'll agree to disagree, I've got my opinion: an opinion reached without proper intellectual rigour means nothing to me."

A correction about today

"The amount of dispute in contemporary halachah increases each generation…" The Rabbi retracted that wording:

Today's Sages, even the greatest, aren't doing category 3 at all. Category 3 ended with the Gemara. Nor categories 1 and 2 ("they weren't with Moshe"), nor 4 and 5 (no authority for new gezerot and takkanot). No rabbi can stand up and derive a new law from the verse.

All they can do is apply the Talmud's derivations to new situations. Electricity on Shabbat or Yom Tov: "I can't go to the Torah and derive what it says about electricity. I don't have the authority. All I can do is look in the Talmud and extrapolate from its extrapolations."

Part 7: Then Why Does the Mishnah Record Minority Views?

If the law follows the majority, why did Rebbi, whom the Rambam praises so highly, write the Sages say X, Rabbi Meir says Y? Why not just the final law?

"We touched on this in the letter to Rav Pinchas HaDayan." Pinchas asked why the Mishneh Torah gives no dissenting views, when Rebbi did.

The answer: it took time for the halachah to crystallise.

Poultry and milk. Most Sages made the fence; Rabbi Yosei HaGelili didn't. Rebbi writes it's forbidden. A grandson of someone from Rabbi Yosei HaGelili's town walks around eating chicken with milk, following his town's custom (minhag was much stronger then). Is he doing wrong? No: he's under Rabbi Yosei HaGelili's authority. So the Mishnah tells you: there is such an opinion; we don't follow it; but if you meet such a person, he has a tradition and can't be criticised.**

Today, no community traces back to Rabbi Yosei HaGelili: "someone who says I eat chicken and milk because I follow Rabbi Yosei HaGelili: you throw him out." Already in the Rambam's time no one could claim that chain, "so there's no point in my Mishneh Torah writing opposing views."

Summary

  1. Each generation passed on three bodies: what came from Moshe; what was derived and agreed; what was derived, disputed and decided by majority.
  2. The chain: Moshe → Yehoshua → elders → prophets → Great Assembly → Shimon HaTzaddik (first of the Sages) → … → Rabbeinu HaKadosh, uniquely wise, wealthy, pious and eloquent, who gathered it all. Rav Kook's line of a hundred students.
  3. Received laws are never disputed (eye for eye = money; etrog; hadas). The Gemara's many "sources" just anchor them to verses, for memory.
  4. Halachah leMoshe miSinai = received laws with no anchor at all (black tefillin, kezayit): about 25, all listed by the Rambam.
  5. Five categories: received with anchor; received without; derived; gezerot; takkanot. Only 3–5 can be disputed, decided by majority.
  6. Disputes multiplied not from "Chinese whispers" ("one of the worst statements") but because the skill of derivation weakened. Same method → same answer, 99 times in 100.
  7. No one derives new law today; we apply the Talmud's derivations (electricity).
  8. The Mishnah records minority views because communities still followed them; that's long over, so the Mishneh Torah doesn't.
