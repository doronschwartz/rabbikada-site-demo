Topic: The source of Avinu Malkenu and whether it is said on Shabbat

Sources: Gemara Taanit; the Meiri; the Rivash (Rabbi Yishak bar Sheshet); the Rema; the Arizal

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Monday 11 September 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The source is the Gemara in Taanit: in a drought one of the Tannaim prayed, even 24 blessings, and was not answered. Rabbi Akiva (second century, just after the destruction of the Temple) came forward, said that there is none to rely on but "our Father, our King, have mercy on us", and was answered immediately.
  • In the times of the Geonim a long list of Avinu Malkenu prayers developed, and by the time of the Rishonim the custom was widespread in Ashkenaz and Sepharad on Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur and the Ten Days of Teshuvah.
  • Some Rishonim forbade it on Shabbat: it is a prayer of requests, and on Shabbat we do not ask for our needs. This is why the Hakhamim shortened the Amidah on Shabbat.
  • Others permitted it. The Meiri argued that the prohibition is only on personal requests, not public needs (Sorkhei Rabbim); the Rabbi says it is not clear this is accepted, and it seems even public requests are prohibited on Shabbat.
  • A second reason: a set text used all week may be kept on Shabbat, as in Birkat HaMazon we say "Reenu, zunenu, parnesenu" even on Shabbat.
  • Conflicting customs already existed: the Rivash records that Saragossa said it on Shabbat and Barcelona did not.
  • The Rema rules it is not said on Shabbat, and this is the custom of all Ashkenazim. The Shulhan Arukh does not rule; the Arizal said to say it, which is the custom of most Middle Eastern Sepharadim. The Spanish and Portuguese, some Moroccan and some other communities do not say it on Shabbat.

Sources quoted

  • Gemara Taanit: Rabbi Akiva's two lines of Avinu Malkenu, answered immediately.
  • The Meiri: public needs may be prayed for on Shabbat.
  • The Rivash: records the differing customs of Saragossa and Barcelona.
  • The Rema: Avinu Malkenu is not said on Shabbat.
  • The Arizal: it should be said on Shabbat.

In one line

Ashkenazim and Western Sepharadim omit Avinu Malkenu on Shabbat, while most Eastern Sepharadim, following the Arizal, say it.
