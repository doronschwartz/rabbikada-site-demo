Introduction to Siman 314

Siman 314 deals with דברים האסורים משום בנין וסתירה בשבת - things that are forbidden because of building or demolishing on Shabbat. The siman contains 12 sections (se'ifim).

This siman provides the foundational principles for understanding contemporary questions about opening packages, cans, bottles, and various containers on Shabbat.

Background Gemarot - The Apparent Contradictions

Gemara 1: Shabbat 122

Principle stated: אין בנין בכלים ואין סתירה בכלים - There is no concept of building or demolishing when it comes to utensils.

Implication: Unlike building structures or working with the ground (which is the av melakhah from the Mishkan), building or demolishing utensils does not fall under the melakhah of bonneh.

Important caveat: One still cannot make something entirely new, as that would violate makeh b'patish (the finishing hammer blow - completing an item).

Gemara 2: Shabbat 146

Statement: שובר אדם את החבית לאכול ממנה גרוגרות - A person is allowed to break a barrel in order to eat food from it.

Condition: ובלבד שלא יתכוון לעשות כלי - provided one does not intend to make a utensil (i.e., one cannot deliberately create an opening that will serve as an entrance).

Implication: It appears one may break through any packaging on Shabbat to access the contents.

The Problem: Gemara in Eruvin

Mishna: נתנו במגדל ואבד המפתח הרי זה עירוב - If one placed eruv food in a tower (migdal - a large utensil) and lost the key, it is still considered a valid eruv.

Gemara's question: How can this be a valid eruv when the person cannot access it? (The halacha requires that eruv food must be accessible.)

The Critical Question: Why doesn't the Gemara simply answer that one can break open the migdal to access the eruv? We have two gemarot that seem to permit this:

  1. אין בנין וסתירה בכלים - no building/demolishing with utensils
  2. שובר אדם את החבית - one may break a barrel open

Why does the Gemara treat this as inaccessible?

Three Approaches of the Rishonim

Approach #1: Tosfot and Rosh (Position of Shulhan Arukh)

Core principle: There IS biniyan u'stira b'kelim with regular utensils. The Gemara's lenient statements apply only to specific types of utensils.

The distinction - Musteki utensils:

  • A musteki utensil is a כלי רעוע - a shaky, substandard utensil
  • Examples: utensils that are patched up, broken and repaired, temporary in nature
  • These are not meant for long-term use

Application of gemarot:

  • When the Gemara says שובר אדם את החבית, it refers ONLY to a musteki barrel (one that's already broken/patched)
  • When the Gemara says אין בנין וסתירה בכלים, it refers ONLY to musteki utensils
  • Regular, complete utensils (כלי שלם/chavita shleima) CANNOT be broken or built on Shabbat

Explanation of Eruvin case:

  • The migdal is a normal, complete utensil
  • Therefore, one cannot break it open
  • Thus the Gemara correctly asks how the eruv is accessible

Key point: According to this approach, the nature/quality of the utensil determines the halacha, NOT the size.

Approach #2: Ran, Rabbeinu Nissim, and Trumat HaDeshen

Core principle: The distinction is based on SIZE, not on the quality/nature of the utensil.

The distinction:

  • Small utensils: אין בנין וסתירה בכלים - no building/demolishing restrictions
  • Large utensils: There IS biniyan u'stira b'kelim

Size threshold: 40 se'ah (approximately 275-350 liters, the volume of a mikveh)

Application of gemarot:

  • שובר אדם את החבית - This refers to a barrel, which is a relatively small utensil (under 40 se'ah)
  • Therefore it may be broken
  • The migdal in Eruvin is a large utensil (over 40 se'ah)
  • Therefore it cannot be broken, and the Gemara correctly questions accessibility

Key point: According to this approach, size determines the halacha, NOT the quality of the utensil.

Approach #3: Rashi and Vilna Gaon

Core principle: Take the Gemara literally - אין בנין וסתירה בכלים applies to ALL utensils.

No distinctions:

  • Does not matter if it's shaky or complete
  • Does not matter if it's large or small
  • If it's a utensil, there is NO biniyan u'stira b'kelim

Explanation of Eruvin: They have a different way to answer the Gemara's question (not detailed in the shiur).

Key point: This is the most lenient approach - no restrictions on breaking or building any utensils.

The Rambam's Position - Unclear

Apparent contradiction in Rambam:

Source 1 (Chapter 23): שובר אדם את החבית לאכול ממנה גרוגרות ובלבד שלא יתכוון לעשות כלי

  • States simply that one may break a barrel
  • Makes no mention of it being specifically a musteki utensil
  • This suggests the Rambam follows Rashi's approach

Source 2: וכן המפרק את התנור והחבית כל שצריך אומן לתקנן הרי זה גמר מלאכה

  • Discusses barrels in a context that implies there IS biniyan u'stira b'kelim
  • Also: וכן העושה כלי אדמה כגון תנור וחבית כל שהוא פוסתן חייב

The problem: These sources seem contradictory. Therefore, the Shulhan Arukh does not simply follow the Rambam, as his position is unclear.

Shulhan Arukh - Siman 314, Se'if Aleph

Opening Statement

אין בנין וסתירה בכלים - There is no building or demolishing with utensils.

Qualification: והני מילי שאינו בנין ממש כגון חבית שנשברה - This specifically refers to something that is not a real building, such as a barrel that has broken.

Continuation: ודבק שבריה בזפת - and its broken pieces were stuck together with tar (i.e., it's a patched-up utensil).

Conclusion: יכל לשברה ליקח מה שבתוכה - Then one may break it to take what is inside.

Position: The Shulhan Arukh follows Tosfot and Rosh - the distinction is based on the nature/quality of the utensil (musteki vs. complete).

Rema's Addition

Rema adds: שאינה מחזקת ארבעים סאה - which does not hold 40 se'ah.

Effect: The Rema adds the Ran's condition as well, creating a DOUBLE requirement:

  1. It must be a musteki/shaky utensil (Shulhan Arukh's requirement)
  2. It must be less than 40 se'ah (Ran's requirement)

Result: The Rema is MORE STRINGENT - taking on both opinions l'chumra.

Contrast with Shulhan Arukh:

  • According to Shulhan Arukh alone: A huge but musteki utensil may be broken (size doesn't matter)
  • According to Rema: Even a musteki utensil over 40 se'ah cannot be broken

Prohibition Against Making Holes

ובלבד שלא יכוין לנקוב נקב יפה להיות לו לפתח - Provided one does not intend to bore a nice hole to serve as an opening.

Reason: דאם כן הוי ליה מתקן מנא - because then it would be makeh b'patish (completing/fixing a utensil).

Background on making holes:

  • A hole that is עשוי להכניס ולהוציא (made for bringing things in AND out) = Torah prohibition (toledah of makeh b'patish)
  • A hole that can only bring in OR only take out = Rabbinic prohibition

Application: Even when breaking a musteki utensil (which is permitted), one cannot intentionally create a proper opening.

Complete Utensils - Strict Prohibition

אבל אם היא שלימה אסור לשוברה - But if it is a complete/normal utensil, it is forbidden to break it.

Even more: אפילו בענין שאינו עושה כלי - Even in a manner where one is not making a utensil.

Further prohibitions:

  • ואפילו נקב בעלמא אסור לנקוב מחדש - Even a simple hole is forbidden to make anew
  • ואפילו יש בו נקב מחדש אם להרחיבו אסור - And even if there is already a hole, widening it is forbidden

Clear position: According to Shulhan Arukh (following Rosh and Tosfot), one CANNOT break, puncture, or widen holes in complete/standard utensils, regardless of size.

Mishna Berurah - Citation of Vilna Gaon

Vilna Gaon's position: ובביאור הגר"א הסכים להפוסקים דסבירא ליה דאין סתירה בכלים

  • The Vilna Gaon follows the poskim who hold there is no building/demolishing with utensils
  • אפילו כשהיא בתשמישתא - Even when it's a normal, complete utensil in use
  • אם לא כשהוא עושה אותה כלי חדש או פתח לקבל ולהוציא - UNLESS one is making a new utensil or an opening for bringing in and out

Important note: The Mishna Berurah cites this but does NOT rule according to it (as it contradicts Shulhan Arukh and Rema).

Significance: Shows all three approaches in the Rishonim are recorded in the halachic literature.

Summary of Three Positions in Halacha

Position 1: Shulhan Arukh (Tosfot/Rosh)

  • Distinction based on QUALITY/NATURE of utensil
  • Musteki (shaky/temporary) = permitted
  • Complete utensil = forbidden
  • Size is irrelevant

Position 2: Rema (adding Ran)

  • Requires BOTH conditions
  • Must be musteki AND less than 40 se'ah
  • Most stringent position

Position 3: Vilna Gaon (Rashi)

  • NO restrictions on biniyan u'stira b'kelim for any utensils
  • Most lenient position
  • Not the accepted halacha but recorded as a legitimate opinion

Contemporary Applications

Case 1: Opening Packets (Crisps, Sweets, Snacks)

Question: May one tear open a packet of crisps or sweets on Shabbat?

Analysis:

  • The packet is a utensil holding the food
  • Tearing it open appears to be an act of stira (destruction)

Halacha - PERMITTED according to majority of poskim:

  • Hacham Ovadia Yoseph
  • Hacham Ben Tzion Abba Shaul
  • Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein (also permits, based on additional reasoning found later in the siman)
  • Most Sefardi poskim
  • Many Ashkenazi poskim

Reasoning:

  1. These packets have the status of musteki (shaky/temporary utensils)
  2. They are inherently temporary in nature - not meant for long-term use
  3. They are like the patched-up barrel mentioned in the Gemara
  4. Size: Clearly less than 40 se'ah
  5. Therefore permitted according to ALL THREE opinions:
  • Shulhan Arukh: It's musteki ✓
  • Rema: It's musteki AND small ✓
  • Vilna Gaon: אין בנין וסתירה בכלים anyway ✓

Stricter opinion:

  • Rav Falk (Ashkenazi posek) ruled packets must be opened before Shabbat
  • Reasoning: While temporary in nature, they are complete utensils, not actually broken/patched
  • This is a minority stringent position

IMPORTANT CAVEAT - Makeh B'Patish concern:

  • If one opens a packet with INTENT to reuse it afterwards (e.g., to fill with water, use for a game), this is FORBIDDEN
  • This would be making a keli (utensil), which is makeh b'patish - potentially d'oraita
  • The permissibility applies ONLY when:
  • One tears it open destructively, OR
  • One opens it normally but WITHOUT intention to reuse it
  • Normal scenario: open, consume contents, dispose of packaging = PERMITTED

Case 2: Opening Cans (Tuna, Pickles, Ring-Pull Cans)

Question: May one open a can of tuna or pickles on Shabbat?

Types of cans:

Type A: Pull-Tab/Ring-Pull Cans (Modern soda/beer cans)

  • The tab/lid becomes useless after opening
  • PERMITTED according to everyone
  • Not creating a reusable utensil
  • Simply opening to access contents

Type B: Cans Requiring Can Opener

  • Creates an opening in the can
  • After consuming contents, can is typically discarded

Halacha - PERMITTED with same conditions as packets:

Reasoning:

  1. Same musteki status as packets - temporary utensils
  2. Small size (under 40 se'ah)
  3. Permitted according to Shulhan Arukh, Rema, and Vilna Gaon

CRITICAL CONDITION - Makeh B'Patish:

  • If opening with SPECIFIC INTENT to reuse the can afterwards (some people reuse pickle/tuna cans)
  • This would be FORBIDDEN - making a keli
  • Must open before Shabbat if intending to reuse

Timing of intent matters:

  • If one decides AFTER opening and consuming to reuse the can = permitted
  • The prohibition only applies if one had specific intent AT THE TIME of opening
  • Normal scenario: open, consume, discard = PERMITTED

Beer bottles with flip-off caps:

  • These are definitely permitted - the cap is completely unusable afterwards
  • Everyone agrees this is fine

Case 3: Wine and Grape Juice Bottles - THE COMPLEX CASE

Question: May one open wine or grape juice bottles on Shabbat?

Why this is more problematic:

Difference from packets/cans:

  • When opening a bottle, one creates a reusable cap/cork
  • The cap/cork will be used repeatedly over weeks
  • Not temporary - the bottle and cap are kept long-term
  • Not disposable like packets or tuna cans

Two distinct halachic issues:

Issue #1: Makeh B'Patish - Creating a Utensil

  • By twisting/opening the cap, one is creating a functional cap
  • This cap will be used to open and close the bottle many times
  • This appears to be making a keli

Issue #2: Biniyan U'Stira B'Kelim - Destroying the Utensil

  • The bottle appears to be a complete utensil (chavita shleima), not musteki
  • It's made of glass, nice appearance, meant to be kept
  • How can this be considered a shaky/temporary utensil?

Hacham Ovadia Yoseph's Position on Wine/Grape Juice Bottles

Position: PERMITTED, but better to open before Shabbat (meheyot tov)

Response to Issue #1: Makeh B'Patish

Argument: The cap has ALREADY been made as a keli before Shabbat.

Manufacturing process:

  • The bottle is made
  • The cap is made separately
  • They are then attached/stuck together
  • Opening does not CREATE a keli - the keli already exists
  • One is merely detaching two items that were stuck together

Additional reasoning:

  • The cap was designed with the status of being attached TO BE DETACHED
  • It was always meant to be separated
  • It always had the status of a keli
  • One is not manufacturing anything new

Response to Issue #2: Biniyan U'Stira B'Kelim

Multiple arguments:

Argument A - According to lenient opinions:

  • According to Rashi and Vilna Gaon: אין בנין וסתירה בכלים at all - no problem

Argument B - The Rambam's position:

  • Hacham Ovadia argues the Rambam really holds like Rashi
  • Therefore support for leniency from Rambam

Argument C - Level of prohibition:

  • Even according to Shulhan Arukh's strict approach, this would only be issur d'Rabanan
  • Multiple opinions permit it

Argument D - Asui L'Hipared (Made to be Separated):

  • This is the key argument
  • The very nature of this utensil is that it was made to be taken apart
  • When someone makes a regular barrel, it's made as a complete unit to remain intact
  • That's a chavita shleima (complete utensil)
  • HERE: The bottle and cap are manufactured specifically TO BE SEPARATED
  • The cap is עשוי להיפרד - designed for separation
  • Therefore, even Rosh, Tosfot, and Shulhan Arukh would agree
  • This is different from a normal utensil because its essential nature is temporary attachment

Conclusion on both issues:

  • From makeh b'patish perspective: permitted (keli already made)
  • From biniyan u'stira b'kelim perspective: permitted (asui l'hipared)

Practical Halacha for Wine/Grape Juice Bottles

Hacham Ovadia's ruling:

  • Permitted to open on Shabbat
  • However: מהיות טוב (meheyot tov) - better to open before Shabbat

What is meheyot tov?

  • NOT l'chatchila/b'diavad (first instance/second instance)
  • Different concept
  • When it's easy to avoid entering a dispute, one should do so
  • If one forgets or cannot open before Shabbat: FULLY PERMITTED to open on Shabbat
  • If one can remember: better to open beforehand and avoid the entire sugya/dispute

Other poskim - FORBIDDEN:

  • Many poskim prohibit opening wine/grape juice bottles on Shabbat
  • Must be opened before Shabbat
  • Reasons:
  1. Makeh b'patish concern (making the cap/cork as a utensil)
  2. Biniyan u'stira concern (destroying a complete utensil)

Corks specifically:

  • Some argue corks should be less problematic
  • Corks are often temporary - they break, crack, cannot be replaced properly
  • Could have musteki status
  • Still, many poskim require opening before Shabbat

Screw-top wine bottles:

  • Similar concerns as grape juice bottles
  • More of an issue than corks (more durable, clearly reusable)

General practice:

  • Try to remember to open wine and grape juice bottles before Shabbat
  • If one forgot or guest brings bottle on Shabbat:
  • Sefardim following Hacham Ovadia: may open
  • Others: should ask non-Jew (see below)

Asking Others to Open Bottles/Packages

Asking a Non-Jew (Amira L'Akum)

General principle: Even matters that are mahloket Rishonim - one may ask a non-Jew.

Source: The Chida writes this regarding bishul (cooking) - applies broadly.

Reasoning:

  • Where there is a legitimate dispute among Rishonim
  • Even if halacha follows the strict opinion
  • One may ask a non-Jew because of the lenient opinion (Rashi/Vilna Gaon)

Application:

  • Everyone agrees one may ask a non-Jew to open bottles, cans, packages on Shabbat
  • This relies on Rashi and Vilna Gaon's opinion that אין בנין וסתירה בכלים
  • This is true even according to strict Ashkenazi positions

Wine bottles and non-Jews:

  • Issue: If wine is not mevushal, non-Jew touching it creates yayin nesech concern
  • Solution depends on type of wine
  • If mevushal: no issue with non-Jew opening
  • If not mevushal: more complex (would need separate analysis)

Asking a Child

General practice: Can ask a child to open bottles/packages.

Reasoning:

  • Chinuch concerns are different
  • Less stringent than having adult violate prohibition

Ashkenazi Asking Sefardi

Halachic status: Not fully clear from shiur how this works technically.

Practical consideration: If Sefardi holds it's fully mutar, can do for others who are uncertain.

Psik Reisha B'D'Rabanan - A Critical Principle

Background from End of Se'if Aleph

Case presented in Shulhan Arukh: ואם היה סכין תקועה מערב שבת בחבית - If a knife was inserted into a barrel before Shabbat

Scenario:

  • Knife is stuck in barrel from before Shabbat
  • One wants to remove and reinsert it on Shabbat
  • Problem: Removing and reinserting will inevitably WIDEN the hole
  • We just learned: widening a hole is forbidden (אפילו יש בו נקב מחדש אם להרחיבו אסור)

Shulhan Arukh's ruling: מותר להוציאו ולהכניסו שאינו מתכוין להוסיף - Permitted to take it out and put it in because one does not intend to widen

Question: This is PSIK REISHA (inevitable consequence) - how is it permitted?

The Dispute: Psik Reisha B'D'Rabanan

Classic Psik Reisha Case from Gemara

Scenario: Child wants to play with ball. In those times, no footballs - would use chicken head as ball.

Question: May one cut off chicken's head to use as ball, saying "I don't intend to kill the chicken, I just want the head for the ball"?

Gemara's answer: NO - פסיק רישא ולא ימות

  • "Cut off the head and it won't die?!" - impossible!
  • It's inevitable that cutting the head will kill the chicken
  • Cannot claim lack of intent when result is inevitable
  • This is FORBIDDEN

Mordechai's Principle (Rabbi Hillel Mordechai ben Hillel of Rothenberg)

Key distinction:

  • Psik reisha with D'ORAITA prohibition = FORBIDDEN
  • Psik reisha with D'RABANAN prohibition = PERMITTED

Rationale:

  • Torah-level prohibitions: even inevitable unintended consequences are forbidden
  • Rabbinic-level prohibitions: if unintended and inevitable, permitted

Application to our case:

  • Making/widening a small hole (not large enough for bringing in AND out) = D'RABANAN
  • It's inevitable (psik reisha) that removing/inserting knife will widen hole slightly
  • But it's unintended (eino mitkaven)
  • Therefore: פסיק רישא בדרבנן מותר - psik reisha with d'Rabanan is permitted

Rema's Position - More Stringent

Rema's condition: ודוקא שהוציאו גם כן פעם אחת מבעוד יום

  • Only if one removed it once before Shabbat as well

Reasoning:

  • If removed before Shabbat, hole is already established
  • Won't necessarily widen further on Shabbat
  • Without this, it's definite psik reisha

Rema's principle: דהוי פסיק רישא דעושה נקב בפתח החבית

  • Psik reisha b'd'Rabanan is FORBIDDEN
  • Disagrees with Mordechai and Shulhan Arukh

Halacha L'Maaseh: Psik Reisha B'D'Rabanan

Basic halacha: Following Shulhan Arukh - פסיק רישא בדרבנן מותר

Disagreement: Magen Avraham and others disagreed (brought in Mishna Berurah).

Consensus position:

  • When it's פסיק רישא בדרבנן בדלא ניחא ליה (psik reisha with d'Rabanan where one has NO BENEFIT) = 100% MUTAR

Key terms:

  • B'de'lo nicha lei = where one has no benefit, doesn't want the consequence
  • This is universally accepted as permitted

B'De'lo Nicha Lei vs. Nicha Lei

B'De'lo Nicha Lei (No Benefit)

Definition: The inevitable consequence provides NO benefit to the person.

Halacha:

  • 100% permitted according to everyone (when it's d'Rabanan)
  • Universal agreement among poskim following Shulhan Arukh

Example:

  • Widening hole in barrel when removing/replacing knife
  • One has no benefit from wider hole
  • Just wants to remove knife

Nicha Lei (With Benefit)

Definition: The inevitable consequence provides benefit to the person.

Halacha - More complex:

  • Shulhan Arukh in Se'if Aleph seems to permit
  • BUT Shulhan Arukh contradicts himself elsewhere in his writings
  • Therefore: Only permit when there's a big need (tzorech gadol)
  • Cannot rely on this routinely for nicha lei cases

Practical ruling:

  • Psik reisha d'Rabanan de'lo nicha lei = definitely mutar
  • Psik reisha d'Rabanan nicha lei = only when big need

Major Application: Refrigerator Light on Shabbat

The Common Problem

Scenario:

  • Arrive at vacation home 20 minutes before Shabbat
  • Discover refrigerator light turns on/off when door opens
  • All Shabbat food is in refrigerator
  • Cannot access food without opening door
  • Opening door will activate light

Common desperate questions:

  • "Can I break the light bulb?"
  • "What should I do?"
  • "Do I have to not eat all Shabbat?"

The Halachic Analysis

Factor 1: Level of prohibition

  • Electricity is d'Rabanan (Rabbinic prohibition)
  • This is the accepted halacha we follow

Factor 2: Inevitability

  • Opening fridge door INEVITABLY turns light on/off
  • This is classic psik reisha

Factor 3: Benefit analysis - CRITICAL

  • Question: Is there benefit from the light?
  • Key consideration: Is there OTHER LIGHT in the room?

If there IS other light in the room:

  • No benefit from fridge light (de'lo nicha lei)
  • Room is already illuminated
  • Fridge light adds nothing

If there is NO other light in the room:

  • There IS benefit from fridge light (nicha lei)
  • More complex - would need strong need to permit

The Halacha: Refrigerator Light

When room HAS adequate lighting:

  • PERMITTED to open refrigerator repeatedly on Shabbat
  • Even without magnets or other preparations
  • Why? פסיק רישא דרבנן דלא ניחא ליה
  1. Electricity = d'Rabanan
  2. Inevitable (psik reisha)
  3. No benefit (de'lo nicha lei) - room already has light

Everyone would agree:

  • At minimum, Shulhan Arukh clearly permits this
  • Even stricter opinions would likely agree given all three factors

Preferred solution (l'chatchila):

  • Better to use magnets or disable light if possible
  • But if cannot: fully permitted to open fridge

Ashkenazim and Refrigerator Lights

Position: Ashkenazim following Rema have a problem with this.

Why:

  • Rema holds psik reisha b'd'Rabanan is FORBIDDEN
  • Even with de'lo nicha lei, would be more stringent

Practical difference:

  • Ashkenazim more careful about refrigerator lights
  • Must use magnets or other solutions
  • Cannot rely on leniency of psik reisha b'd'Rabanan

Sefardim:

  • Following Shulhan Arukh
  • Permitted to open when room has adequate light
  • Even without any special preparation

Summary of Practical Applications

Permitted for All:

  1. Opening packets of crisps/sweets (assuming no intent to reuse)
  2. Opening tuna/pickle cans (assuming no intent to reuse)
  3. Ring-pull/flip-top beverage cans (cap becomes useless)
  4. Beer bottles with flip-off caps (cap becomes useless)
  5. Asking non-Jew to open any package/bottle (even where halacha is strict)

Disputed/Should Try to Avoid:

  1. Wine bottles with corks
  • Sefardim (Hacham Ovadia): permitted but better before Shabbat
  • Many others: must open before Shabbat
  1. Grape juice bottles with screw caps
  • Sefardim (Hacham Ovadia): permitted but meheyot tov before Shabbat
  • Many others: must open before Shabbat
  1. Refrigerator light (with room lighting)
  • Sefardim following Shulhan Arukh: permitted
  • Ashkenazim following Rema: problem, need magnets/solution

Always Forbidden:

  1. Opening any package with INTENT to reuse it as utensil (makeh b'patish)
  2. Deliberately making an opening/hole while breaking utensil
  3. Breaking the light bulb in refrigerator (destructive act)

Key Principles Summary

Three Approaches to Biniyan U'Stira B'Kelim:

  1. Shulhan Arukh (Tosfot/Rosh): Nature of utensil (musteki vs. complete)
  2. Rema (adding Ran): BOTH nature AND size (under 40 se'ah)
  3. Vilna Gaon (Rashi): No restrictions at all on utensils

Critical Concepts:

  1. Musteki: Temporary, shaky, patched-up, or substandard utensil
  2. Chavita Shleima: Complete, standard, permanent utensil
  3. Makeh B'Patish: Completing/finishing a utensil
  4. Asui L'Hipared: Made to be separated/disassembled
  5. Psik Reisha B'd'Rabanan De'lo Nicha Lei: Inevitable unintended rabbinic consequence with no benefit - PERMITTED
  6. Meheyot Tov: Better to do something (not l'chatchila/b'diavad, but avoiding dispute)

Mahloket Status:

  • When matter is mahloket Rishonim with legitimate lenient opinion
  • May ask non-Jew to perform action even if halacha follows strict opinion
  • This allows asking non-Jew to open bottles/packages on Shabbat
