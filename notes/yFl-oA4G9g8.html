Topic: When Shehiyanu is said on a new fruit, in what order, and on fruit available all year

Sources: The Mishnah; the Rambam; the Rosh; Shulhan Arukh; the Mishnah Berurah; the Kaf HaHayim

Daily Halacha: Tu BiShvat, Tuesday 11 January 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Mishnah says one who sees a new fruit says Shehiyanu, thanking HaKadosh Barukh Hu for reaching a new milestone. A new fruit is one that has come out in its new season.
  • Originally, as the Mishnah and the Rambam write, it was said on seeing the new fruit on the tree. The Rosh wrote that seeing without eating does not make everyone happy, so it should be said when eating.
  • The Shulhan Arukh records that strictly one may say it on seeing, but the custom is to say it when eating. One who buys a fruit to eat days later would not be wrong to say it then, but customarily waits.
  • Order: the Mishnah Berurah, for Ashkenazim, says Shehiyanu first, so it does not interrupt between Borei Peri HaEs and eating. The Kaf HaHayim, for Sepharadim, applies "the frequent precedes the infrequent": Borei Peri HaEs first, then Shehiyanu, which is not an interruption. Sepharadim do likewise with a new tallit.
  • It is a misconception that Shehiyanu is said on any fruit not eaten for 30 days. It is only for a seasonal fruit newly available.
  • In England most fruits are available all year, so it is hard to find a Shehiyanu fruit; in Israel seasonal fruits such as grapes, peaches and mangoes are clear. A fruit available all year in regular shops (not just expensive speciality shops) gets no Shehiyanu, even if never eaten before.

Sources quoted

  • The Mishnah: "HaRoeh Peri Hadash", one who sees a new fruit says Shehiyanu.
  • The Rosh and Shulhan Arukh: the custom is to say it when eating.
  • The Mishnah Berurah and the Kaf HaHayim: the order of the two Berakhot.

In one line

Shehiyanu is said when eating a genuinely seasonal new fruit, for Sepharadim after Borei Peri HaEs, and not on fruit sold all year round.
