Topic: Fulfilling the misvah of Shabbat candles with electric light

Sources: Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Thursday 10 June 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The question arises in a hotel that forbids candles, or when one has none.
  • There must be an act of lighting: one cannot make a berakhah on a light on since the morning, but must switch it off and on again for Shabbat.
  • Hakham Ovadiah holds that a flame is required, not just light, but that an electric bulb counts as a flame because it gives off heat, so one may even make the berakhah on it.
  • He does not distinguish between bulbs, but it seems this applies only to bulbs that give off heat. LED lights seemingly would not count, and one would not make a berakhah on them.
  • He holds this so strongly that he advises switching off the electric lights every Friday night before the berakhah, then lighting the candles and switching the lights back on. Sepharadim do not make a berakhah where someone has already lit in the room, and electric light already on counts as such a lighting.
  • Ideally one lights real candles, which clearly mark Shabbat and give it its special feeling. Where that is impossible, one may make the berakhah and switch on the lights.

In one line

Where candles cannot be lit, one may make the berakhah and switch on a heat-giving electric light, though real candles are preferred.
