Class Notes — Rabbi Daniel Kada

Sefer: HaMaspik L'Ovdei Hashem (Kifayat al-Abidin) — Rabbi Avraham ben HaRambam Chapter: Perek 6 (אריכות אפים — Slow to Anger / Calmness) — Conclusion Edition: Feldheim

Recap: Chapters 1–6 (Part 1)

  • Chapter 1: Two paths — the Tzaddik (basics) and the Chasid (beyond). No point being a Chasid without the basics.
  • Chapter 2: All middot are interrelated — working on one affects others.
  • Chapter 3: Purity of motive — four levels, from social pressure (lowest) to serving because Hashem commanded (highest).
  • Chapter 4: Rachamanut (compassion) — first of the 13 Attributes. Important, but sometimes cruelty is necessary. When in doubt → choose mercy.
  • Chapter 5: Nedivut (generosity) — giving with no obligation. Four kinds: money, influence, intellect, faith. Avraham modeled all four.
  • Chapter 6 (Part 1): Calmness ≠ apathy. Intellect must control emotion. Anger = drunkenness. Anger + arrogance go together; patience + humility go together. Training Method 1: stories from Torah (negative examples — Kayin, Eisav, the brothers, Moshe at Mei Merivah, Shaul, David). Training Method 2: Torah's mitzvot designed to prevent anger.

Training Through Stories: Positive Examples of Calmness

1. Reuven — Calming the Brothers

  • The brothers wanted to kill Yosef
  • Reuven intervened: "Throw him into this pit instead"
  • Reuven's plan: calm them down first, then bring Yosef home
  • Result: Yosef wasn't killed — only sold
  • Reuven used calmness to de-escalate

2. Yosef — Forgiving His Brothers

  • The first place in Torah where someone forgives
  • After Yaakov dies → the brothers are afraid → they make up a story that Yaakov told them to ask for forgiveness
  • Yosef says: don't worry, it was all from Hashem
  • He could have used his power and anger → but he recognized the yichud Hashem (everything comes from God)
  • He was willing to overlook what they did

3. Shaul — Forgiving Those Who Disrespected Him

ויאמר שאול לא יומת איש ביום הזה כי היום עשה ה' תשועה בישראל

  • People of Yavesh Gilad wanted to surrender to Nachash king of Ammon
  • Hashem made a miracle and they were saved
  • Some people demanded execution: "They were willing to surrender!"
  • Shaul said: no one will be killed today — Hashem has given us victory
  • Willing to forgo personal honour

4. David — Not Killing Shaul

  • Shaul tried to kill David multiple times
  • David had the opportunity to kill Shaul — didn't do it
  • Just cut his coat as a hint: "I could have killed you but didn't"
  • Shaul's response: צדיק אתה ממני — "You are more righteous than I"

5. David — Not Punishing Shim'i ben Gera

  • Shim'i ben Gera publicly cursed and denigrated David HaMelech
  • David said: הניחו לו ויקלל — "Leave him, let him curse"
  • Later, when Shim'i came to apologize at the Jordan → David forgave him and swore not to kill him
  • But: At the end of his life, David commanded Shlomo to execute Shim'i

Why the contradiction?

  • This was not personal revenge — it was halachic obligation
  • A Talmid Chacham who is publicly disgraced — the Gemara debates whether he can forgive
  • But a king, nasi, or leader — the Torah does NOT allow them to forgo their honour
  • David felt the timing wasn't right during his lifetime → left it to Shlomo
  • Proof: David writes in Tehillim that he didn't take personal revenge → the command to Shlomo was din, not nekama

The Chachamim on Calmness

לעולם יהא אדם ענוותן כהלל ואל יהא קפדן כשמאי

  • Always be humble like Hillel, don't be strict like Shammai

עלובין ואינן עולבין שומעין חרפתן ואינן משיבין

  • Those who sanctify Hashem's name: hear their disgrace and do not respond
  • עלובין = they don't even feel it inside — such a high level that it doesn't affect them
  • Hashem loves such people: ואוהביו כצאת השמש בגבורתו

לעולם יהא אדם רך כקנה ואל יהא קשה כארז

  • Be soft like a reed, not hard like a cedar
  • That's why we write a Sefer Torah with a quill made from a reed (kaneh) — it's soft, flexible, not stubborn

כל המעביר על מידותיו מעבירין לו על כל פשעיו

  • A person who overlooks the wrongdoing of others → Hashem overlooks their wrongdoing
  • "Who does He forgive? The one who forgives others"

Calmness Is on the Path to Prophecy

אריכות אפיים היא מתכונות הנבואה

  • A person who is truly calm is on the way to reaching a high level of prophecy
  • It is also one of the attributes of Hashem Himself

Training Through Mitzvot: Commandments Designed to Prevent Anger

1. Arei Miklat — Cities of Refuge

פן ירדוף גואל הדם אחרי הרוצח כי יחם לבבו

  • Purpose: let everyone calm down
  • The relative of the accidentally killed person may be so angry they can't control themselves
  • Torah says: go to the city of refuge, a safe space — let emotions settle
  • The Rambam in Moreh Nevuchim says the same: arei miklat = a designated place to allow people to cool down
  • Stays there until the Kohen Gadol dies → only then can he come out

2. Mitzvat Tochachah — Rebuking Your Fellow

הוכח תוכיח את עמיתך ולא תשא עליו חטא

Two understandings of this pasuk:

a) The Gemara's reading: When you rebuke someone, don't do it publicly (that would be bearing a sin — embarrassing them). Take them aside privately.

b) The second reading (Rishonim + Rambam in Hilchot De'ot): Tell someone when they've wronged you — don't harbor the upset within yourself.

  • ולא תשא עליו חטא = don't carry the sin/wrongdoing inside you
  • Many people harbor displeasure within themselves → the Torah says: speak it out
  • Rambam: מצוה עליו להודיעו — "It is a mitzvah to tell him: why did you do this to me?"
  • When you speak it out → you release the negativity from within yourself

3. Lo Tikom V'Lo Titor — Don't Take Revenge, Don't Harbor

  • The Torah prohibits revenge because revenge = exercising emotional anger
  • Working on calmness → you won't come to revenge
  • Netira (harboring) also prohibited — even if you don't act, the simmering is destructive

4. Don't Curse a Judge, Leader, or Deaf Person

  • Aside from the terrible act of cursing → these prohibitions help a person not lose control of emotions
  • The Rambam in Sefer HaMitzvot: the prohibition stems from the Torah wanting us to control our emotions
  • When a person curses → they've lost control

5. Don't Hit Anyone

  • We learn from Ki Tetzei: it's forbidden to hit someone beyond what's required
  • Whether another person or a parent (even worse punishment for parents)
  • Rabbi Avraham: the reason = you're losing control of your emotions, getting angry, not being calm

6. Don't Blaspheme

  • A person who curses Hashem → stems from lack of emotional control

7. Helping Your Enemy's Donkey

כי תראה חמור שונאך רובץ תחת משאו... עזוב תעזוב עמו

  • Simple meaning: "You must help him" (the verb can mean "to help")
  • Targum Onkelos reads differently: עזוב = leave — leave the anger within you, leave the negativity in your heart, and go help him
  • The Gemara learns: if you have two people to help and can only help one — prefer the person you don't get along with → to work on removing animosity

When Anger IS Appropriate — Spiritual Matters

The Principle

אף שתכונה הכרחית היא בעניינים התלויים ברצונותינו... אין הדבר כן בעניין הדת

  • When it comes to personal matters → always be calm
  • When it comes to spiritual matters → sometimes you must show anger and displeasure

כל תלמיד חכם שאינו נוקם ונוטר כנחש אינו תלמיד חכם

  • Any Talmid Chacham who doesn't take revenge like a snake = not a Talmid Chacham
  • Resolution: לא קשיא — הא במילי דעלמא הא במילי דשמיא
  • Personal matters → don't take revenge
  • Spiritual matters → a person must stand up

Moshe Rabbeinu — The Model

When Moshe did NOT get angry (personal matters):

  • The leaders said "Hashem should punish you" after conditions worsened in Egypt → Moshe didn't get angry, went back to Hashem and said: "Help them, they're right"
  • Eldad and Medad prophesied outside the plan → Yehoshua ran to Moshe upset → Moshe said: המקנא אתה לי ומי יתן כל עם ה' נביאים — "If only everyone had such enthusiasm"
  • Miriam spoke derogatorily about Moshe → she was punished with tzara'at → Moshe still prayed for her: אל נא רפא נא לה

When Moshe DID get angry (spiritual matters):

  • The Golden Calf → ויחר אף משה וישלך מידיו את הלוחות — threw the tablets
  • The sons of Elazar and Itamar didn't eat the chatat properly → Moshe got angry (even though they were later proven right)
  • The war against Midian → soldiers kept the girls alive against Hashem's command → Moshe got angry

The pattern: Personal → forgo. Spiritual → stand up.

Other Examples of Righteous Anger

  • Pinchas — killed Zimri and Cozbi for public immorality → rewarded with priesthood
  • Shmuel — cursed Shaul for not killing Agag/Amalek as commanded → executed Agag himself
  • Shaul — decreed death on Yonatan for eating during the fast (spiritual matter, even his own son)
  • David — ordered the death of the man who claimed to kill Shaul (even though David benefited) and Rechab and Baanah who killed Ish-Boshet
  • Shlomo — killed his brother Adoniyahu for rebelling against the monarchy
  • Eliyahu — killed 450 false prophets of Baal → קנא קנאתי לה' — "I was zealous for Hashem"
  • Elisha — spoke harshly to King Yehoram for being an idol worshipper

Note on Eliyahu and Pinchas: Some Midrashim say they're the same person. Rabbi Avraham says either way the point stands — if the same person, it's another example of Pinchas' zealotry; if different, you have two examples.

The Chachamim on Righteous Anger

  • Shammai — was very strict in religious matters. Chachamim say don't be like Shammai — but you see he stood up for what was right
  • Rav Kahana — killed an informer who was going to endanger Jewish lives by reporting to the authorities. Rav told him: flee to Bavel
  • The Gemara says: האי צורבא מרבנן דרתח אורייתא הוא דקמרתח ליה — "When a Chacham seems angry, it's the Torah burning within him"
  • The Mishnah: הוי עז כנמר... וגבור כארי לעשות רצון אביך שבשמים — sometimes you need to be bold like a leopard and strong like a lion

Critical Qualifications

1. Not Everyone Should Be Telling People Off

מי שרחוק מלכפות חובות התורה על זולתו... אסור לצאת מגדר סבלנות

  • If you don't know how to do it properly → stay out of it
  • If it's not your responsibility → stay out of it
  • If you don't have a relationship with the person → stay out of it
  • The Chatam Sofer on הוכח תוכיח את עמיתך: "amitecha" = your friend. You can't rebuke someone unless you have a relationship first. Create the relationship → only then can you influence.

2. Even When Appropriate — Show Anger, Don't BE Angry

  • The Rambam in Mishneh Torah and Moreh Nevuchim: when you need to discipline or rebuke → show yourself as angry, but remain fully in control internally
  • Your intellect still runs the show
  • You're acting angry — not being angry

3. On Balance — Calmness Should Far Outweigh Anger

אפילו בענייני הדת לא ימהר למקום להעניש אלא לאחר בירור אורך רוח וחקירה ודרישה

  • Even in spiritual matters → don't rush to punish
  • Investigate thoroughly first
  • Even with avodah zarah (the worst sin): ודרשת וחקרת ושאלת היטב — investigate, interrogate, ask well
  • Hashem's attributes: more are about mercy than judgment → so should ours be
  • A person who gets angry at everything → it's not genuine
  • A person who never gets angry → also not good
  • True character: doesn't get angry about personal matters, but stands up for spiritual matters

Does Hashem Get Angry?

The Answer: No — It's a Borrowed Expression

כי לא תחול בו מידת הכעס ולא מידה אחרת

  • Hashem is above feelings — any mature understanding of God doesn't allow for God to "get angry"
  • Whenever Torah says ויחר אף ה' → it means Hashem is about to punish
  • The Radak (Rabbi David Kimchi) on the pasuk: "They think they're making Me angry — but they're only punishing themselves"

Proof: הניחה לי ויחר אפי בהם

  • After the Golden Calf, Hashem says to Moshe: "Allow Me to get angry and destroy them"
  • If Hashem were truly angry → He wouldn't ask permission
  • When you're angry, you don't say "please allow me to get angry" — you just get angry
  • Therefore: הניחה לי ויחר אפי = הניחה לי ואכלה אותם = "Allow Me to punish them"
  • Proof from Devarim (Parshat Eikev): When Moshe retells the story, he says הרף ממני ואשמידם — "Leave Me alone and I will destroy them" — no mention of anger at all
  • This confirms: "anger" was never the point — punishment was

The Principle

דיברה תורה כלשון בני אדם — The Torah speaks in human language

  • Every time Torah uses "anger" about Hashem → it means punishment/retribution
  • The Rambam explains this at length in the Moreh Nevuchim and Mishneh Torah

Key Takeaways — Full Chapter 6 Summary

  1. Calmness ≠ apathy. It means having feelings but being in control of them.
  2. Training Method 1: Meditate on stories — both negative (anger → destruction) and positive (calmness → reward).
  3. Training Method 2: Think about the mitzvot designed to prevent anger — arei miklat, tochachah, lo tikom, not cursing, not hitting, helping enemies.
  4. Personal matters → always be calm. Spiritual matters → sometimes you must stand up.
  5. The Moshe model: Never angry for himself. Always angry for Hashem's honour.
  6. Not everyone should rebuke. Only if you have the ability, responsibility, AND relationship.
  7. Even when showing anger → remain in full control. Show anger, don't be angry.
  8. On balance → calmness should far outweigh anger. Investigate thoroughly before acting.
  9. Hashem doesn't "get angry" — it's a borrowed expression meaning punishment. We should aspire to the same: when we "get angry" for spiritual matters, it's really about standing up for what's right.
