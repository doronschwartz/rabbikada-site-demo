Topic: An Israeli who moves or travels outside Israel for Yom Tov

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; the Bet Yoseph; the Mishnah Berurah; the Hida; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the Peri Hadash

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 8 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara is clear that one who leaves Israel to live outside Israel keeps two days, being part of that community now. "I am Israeli, I only keep one day" does not work once one has a residence and a life abroad.
  • One only visiting (family, a holiday) who intends to return to Israel soon keeps the status of a ben Eres Yisrael and needs only one day.
  • In a place with no Jewish community, such as a holiday island, such a visitor may do melakhah on the second day.
  • Within a Jewish community, however small, melakhah in public is forbidden, as the Shulhan Arukh rules, lest people come to disregard the second day.
  • The Bet Yoseph permitted melakhah in private, where no one sees. The vast majority disagree: the Mishnah Berurah forbids it both publicly and privately, the Hida and other Sepharadi poskim disagree strongly, and Hakham Ovadiah concludes one should be stringent in the first instance.
  • For prayer he says the weekday tefillah. The Peri Hadash, who was in Egypt for Yom Tov, put on tefillin at home and said Shema, then went in Yom Tov clothes to the synagogue and joined the Shaharit. One may not say Hallel or pray Musaf, having no obligation, but may join the congregation as they sing.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: one who moves abroad to live keeps two days.
  • Shulhan Arukh: melakhah in public is forbidden to the visitor.
  • The Bet Yoseph: private melakhah permitted; the Mishnah Berurah, the Hida and Hakham Ovadiah: forbidden or to be avoided.
  • The Peri Hadash: his own conduct in Egypt.

In one line

An Israeli visiting a Jewish community abroad keeps one day but does no melakhah, publicly or privately, and joins the community in prayer.
