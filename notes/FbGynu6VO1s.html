Topic: The limit of a fifth on giving sedakah, and its exceptions

Sources: The Torah (Yaakov); the Gemara (the takkanah of Usha); the Hafes Hayim

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Wednesday 9 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Giving 10% of income after tax and work expenses is recommended. One who can give up to 20% fulfils a praiseworthy misvah; some read Yaakov's double expression of tithing as a fifth.
  • The Hakhamim in Usha instituted that one should not give away more than a fifth, and this is codified as forbidden, so that one does not end up dependent on others after giving too much.
  • There are five exceptions. First, pikuah nefesh, such as an operation, because of "do not stand by your neighbour's blood".
  • Second, before death, since the concern of later dependence no longer applies; one should not give more than a third, some say half, if there are heirs.
  • Third, the Hafes Hayim argues that one who has given 20% and is then specifically asked may give more.
  • Fourth, the Hafes Hayim argues one may give more for Torah study, since supporting it is a partnership in the learning and so an investment in oneself.
  • Fifth, the Hafes Hayim reads the word "hamevazbez" as forbidding much more than 20%, so very small amounts beyond it are permitted.

In one line

Give 10%, up to 20% is praiseworthy, and beyond that is forbidden except for danger to life, a deathbed, a direct request, Torah study, or small amounts.
