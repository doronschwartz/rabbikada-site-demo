Topic: Seeking forgiveness from those one has wronged, and why not to leave it to Erev Yom Kippur

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rambam, based on the Gemara

Daily Halacha: Yom Kippur, Tuesday 19 September 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Wronging another, financially, physically or even verbally, is a Torah prohibition; the Shulhan Arukh writes that even for hurtful words one must ask Mehilah and do Teshuvah, all year round.
  • Such a wrong is against the person and also against Hashem. Yom Kippur can atone for the latter only after the person has been asked and has forgiven.
  • Hence these laws appear in the context of Yom Kippur, and the custom to ask forgiveness beforehand, so that on Yom Kippur we can ask Hashem's forgiveness for wrongs against others too.
  • Asking only on Erev Yom Kippur, to come in with a clean slate, may not be genuine. A sincere request is made whenever one feels sorry, without waiting.
  • The Rambam, based on the Gemara, and the Shulhan Arukh rule that if refused, one returns later, not immediately, with three people and a gift or amends, up to three times, each before three people. Three requests in a row do not count. After doing this properly, one has done one's best.
  • The one asked should not be cruel and should forgive a genuine request, except one who was slandered (Mosi Shem Ra): reputation cannot be restored, so he has no duty to forgive.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: forgiveness must be sought even for hurtful words.
  • The Rambam, based on the Gemara, and Shulhan Arukh: asking three times before three people.

In one line

Ask forgiveness sincerely and promptly all year, not just before Yom Kippur, and beware of slander, which need not be forgiven.
