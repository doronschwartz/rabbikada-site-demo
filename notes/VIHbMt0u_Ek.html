Topic: The second approach to Shabbat where there is no sunrise or sunset: counting days as in the desert

Sources: The Tiferet Yisrael; Rabbi Yaakov Emden; the Hida; the Gemara (one lost in the desert)

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Tuesday 9 July 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Tiferet Yisrael's approach, keeping the times of one's place of departure, means two people in the same place keep Shabbat at different times, itself a problem.
  • Rabbi Yaakov Emden seems to apply the Gemara's case of one lost in the desert: count six days and make the seventh Shabbat.
  • That would put travellers who arrive on different days on different Shabbatot, and neither on the day Jews keep everywhere else.
  • The Hida understands Rabbi Yaakov Emden to mean this only when one does not know what day one arrived. If one arrives on a Tuesday, one counts 24-hour periods from then to Shabbat; even so, Shabbat depends on arrival, which is problematic.
  • The desert differs: there is a day and night there, just unknown, while here one assumes 24-hour periods are days, which is not clear; day and night may be defined by sunrise and nightfall.
  • In the desert, even on the six days one may do only the melakhah needed to survive. Seemingly, then, on the other days one could not freely carry on vacationing or drive.
  • A very different third approach follows tomorrow.

In one line

Rabbi Yaakov Emden, as the Hida reads him, has one in the polar regions count 24-hour days from arrival, an approach with real difficulties.
