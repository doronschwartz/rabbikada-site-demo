Topic: The status today of those claiming descent from the conversos

Sources: Rabbi Hayyim David HaLevi; Rav Mordekhai Eliyahu, letter of 1994

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 19 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Accepted as Jews in the 1500s and perhaps the early 1600s, the conversos who stayed behind later intermarried and merged into Christian society; those who moved to Jewish communities in the 1600s were accepted.
  • The Rabbanut today does not accept those claiming converso descent as Jews; they need a full giyur. This makes sense, since there is no way to trace mother after mother back 500 years.
  • Rabbi Hayyim David HaLevi, Chief Rabbi of Tel Aviv, wrote that the return of the Anusim wanting to embrace Judaism is a sign of the Mashiah and of the ingathering of the exiles.
  • Rav Mordekhai Eliyahu, the former Rishon LeSion, wrote in 1994 to an institution for the Anusim that they cannot be accepted as Jews without tracing the lineage.
  • But those who wish to return should be treated with kindness and love and perhaps encouraged, unlike usual converts who are pushed away, though the full process must still be gone through.
  • Once converted, with mikveh and milah, their certificate should be called a certificate of return to the ways of their forefathers rather than a certificate of conversion. This changes nothing in halacha, as it is a full conversion, but recognises they are returning to their roots.

In one line

Descendants of the conversos today need a full conversion, but should be welcomed warmly as returning to their roots.
