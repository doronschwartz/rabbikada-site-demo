Topic: How someone with no one at home can light Shabbat candles when synagogue starts before Plag HaMinhah

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Monday 7 June 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Candles may be lit only from Plag HaMinhah, about 7:30 in London now. Usually one goes to synagogue and one's spouse lights at home at the right time.
  • A single person, or a woman who wants to attend synagogue, may have to leave before Plag HaMinhah. This has been explored recently, especially in northern countries such as Scandinavia, where Plag HaMinhah can be at 9 o'clock. There are four options.
  • First, accepted by all: appoint a Jewish agent (shaliah) to come in after Plag HaMinhah, light and make the berakhah on your behalf.
  • Second, in extenuating circumstances: ask a non-Jew to light after Plag HaMinhah, without a berakhah, and make the berakhah yourself on coming home. Many objected, since a non-Jew is not part of the misvah, but some opinions allow it.
  • Third: set the electric lights on a timer to come on after Plag HaMinhah; they count as Shabbat candles and one makes the berakhah on returning.
  • Fourth: some poskim today hold one may accept Shabbat and light, even with a berakhah, before Plag HaMinhah in these circumstances, and then go to synagogue.
  • Each option is debated, but where there is no alternative one may rely on them.

In one line

One who must leave for synagogue before Plag HaMinhah may use a Jewish agent, a non-Jew, a light timer, or in need light early with a berakhah.
