Topic: Why Hillel instituted Prozbol, and the idea behind the release of loans

Sources: Parashat Reeh ("Hishamer lekha"); the Gemara; Mishlei ("eved loveh leish malveh"); Rambam

Daily Halacha: Shemitah & Prozbol, Thursday 12 May 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Loans made in the cycle may not be claimed once Rosh Hashanah after the seventh year begins; today this is rabbinic, since Yovel no longer operates.
  • The Torah warns "hishamer lekha": do not refuse to lend as the seventh year approaches for fear of losing the money. There is a misvah to lend when one can.
  • About 2,000 years ago Hillel HaZaken saw that people were nevertheless not lending, so the poor could not get on with their lives. He instituted Prozbol, which the Gemara explains as an enactment for the wealthy and for the poor.
  • Before the end of the seventh year the lender hands his debts over to the Bet Din, so the claim is no longer personal and he may still collect afterwards.
  • The idea of the release: a borrower is in a sense a slave to the lender ("eved loveh leish malveh"), and Shemitah, like the freeing of slaves, rebalances power and gives a fresh start. When the debt belongs to the Bet Din, the borrower is no longer subordinate to the lender.
  • Rambam's final understanding of the Gemara is that Prozbol works only because the release is rabbinic today.
  • Prozbol is still done today, in the months up to Rosh Hashanah.

In one line

Hillel's Prozbol transfers one's debts to the Bet Din so that lending continues despite the Shemitah release.
