Topic: Asking someone who has not yet accepted Shabbat to do melakhah for you, and annulling an early acceptance

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; Rav Moshe Feinstein; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Tuesday 1 June 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Shulhan Arukh rules that one who has accepted Shabbat early may still ask another Jew who has not yet accepted it to do work for him, such as switching on a forgotten light.
  • This differs from asking a non-Jew, which the Hakhamim forbade because on Shabbat itself no Jew may work. Here one's early acceptance was voluntary and the other person has not accepted, so one may ask explicitly, even knocking on a neighbour's door.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein ruled that a wife is not bound by her husband's acceptance of Shabbat. She may continue preparing until Shabbat begins, and he may ask her to switch on a light or hot plate needed for Shabbat.
  • Likewise on Mosei Shabbat, one who has not yet made Havdalah or prayed Arvit may ask someone who has ended Shabbat to do something for him.
  • If there is no one to ask, Hakham Ovadiah rules that accepting Shabbat works like a neder, which can be annulled before three. One who accepted Shabbat only verbally may annul it before three Jewish men and do the work.
  • This does not apply if one accepted Shabbat through prayer or Kiddush.

In one line

After accepting Shabbat early one may ask someone who has not to do work for him, and a merely verbal acceptance can be annulled before three.
