Topic: How to keep Shabbat where the sun never sets: the problem and the first approach

Sources: The Gemara (one lost in the desert); the Tiferet Yisrael (Rabbi Yisrael Lipschitz)

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Monday 8 July 2024

The halacha

  • The question arose only about 200 years ago, when Jews began living in the far north, so there is little in the Talmud or early Rishonim.
  • Many misvot depend on day and night: Shaharit, Arvit, and Shabbat from sunset on Friday to nightfall on Saturday, then Havdalah. Where there is no sunrise or sunset, when is Shabbat?
  • The closest case is the Gemara's traveller lost in the desert who loses track of the days. The conclusion: count six days and keep the seventh, so Shabbat can be set by the information one has rather than the objective day.
  • The Tiferet Yisrael, among the first to discuss it, distinguishes places like Copenhagen, which never get properly dark but do have sunset. There, as in parts of northern England in summer, Shabbat runs from sunset on Friday to nightfall on Saturday.
  • Where the sun stays above the horizon, there are three opinions. His is that one keeps the Shabbat times of the place one came from.
  • So travellers from England and America in northern Scandinavia would keep Shabbat five or more hours apart. Nor does he say whether one counts from one's hometown or from the last stop, such as Copenhagen.

In one line

Where the sun never sets, the Tiferet Yisrael has each traveller keep the Shabbat times of the place he came from.
