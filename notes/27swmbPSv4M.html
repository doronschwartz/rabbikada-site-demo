Topic: Were marriages between conversos valid, given their witnesses?

Sources: The Rambam, Iggeret HaShemad; the Maharival (Rabbi Yoseph ibn Lev)

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 19 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • If two conversos married and the wife later escaped to a new country, does she need a get, which may be impossible, before remarrying?
  • Kiddushin takes effect only before two kasher male witnesses. A witness who blatantly neglects Torah and misvot is not kasher, so perhaps their kiddushin with converso witnesses was invalid and she needs no get.
  • Some held the marriages valid. Halacha requires giving up one's life rather than converting, but what is done under duress is not punishable. The Rambam wrote in Iggeret HaShemad that one who did not give up his life, even if he served Avodah Zarah, did wrong under duress but remains a full kasher Jew; one must still love him and treat him well, and he must do teshuvah.
  • On that basis some in the 1500s held the conversos were still valid witnesses, so their marriages stand and a get is needed.
  • The Maharival, Rabbi Yoseph ibn Lev of Turkey at the end of the 1500s, held that many conversos, who began only under duress, came over time to abandon the misvot even privately. His conclusion: their kiddushin is presumed invalid, as the witnesses were most likely not kasher, and the woman may remarry.
  • There is a tension: still Jews, but not necessarily kasher to witness a marriage, and over time the view that their witnesses were invalid prevailed.

In one line

Whether converso marriages need a get depends on whether their witnesses were kasher, and the later view, led by the Maharival, held they were not.
