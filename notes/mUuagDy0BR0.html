These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The last class on the Sefer Torah

This was the final class on the laws of the Sefer Torah, which the series had begun the previous summer. The Rabbi announced that the following week, close to Tu BiShvat, they would begin the laws of Purim for four or five classes, and then Pesah.

He reminded the class of the previous week's conclusion: the Shulhan Arukh's own Sefer Torah was clearly a rolled scroll like the Ashkenazi ones, which is why the laws of Gelilah fit them so well.

Do not start the Haftarah during Gelilah (147:7)

The Shulhan Arukh rules: "ein hamaftir matkhil," the person reading the Haftarah does not begin until the Sefer Torah has been rolled, so that the one doing Gelilah can listen. The Mishnah Berurah adds that this includes even the berakhot before the Haftarah. It is enough that the scroll is rolled and closed; one need not wait for it to be fully dressed.

Someone asked: why should everyone wait for one person? Is that not tirha deSibura? The Rabbi said it was a fair question and he had no complete answer, but this ruling comes from the Gemara itself. He pointed to the language: a phrase like "ein hamaftir matkhil" is the Gemara's instruction, not advice from a later authority. And kevod haSibur does not override another person's misvah. In places he had been, the Haftarah began straight away, and he now realised this was not correct.

For Sefaradim with a case, the practical point is simple: do not begin the Haftarah, even the berakhah, while the Sefer Torah is still being closed.

Two Sifrei Torah (147:8)

When two scrolls are read, the second is not opened until the first has been rolled up. This comes from the Gemara's principle of not doing misvot in bundles, "hafilot hafilot," in a rush, as if to get them out of the way. The Rema adds that the first scroll is not lifted off the Tevah until the second has been placed there, so that the congregation is never left without a Sefer Torah in front of them.

For Sefaradim, with the scrolls standing in their cases on the Tevah, most of this does not apply. The Rabbi said he has seen many places that do not wait for the first scroll to be rolled before opening the second.

The Rema also allows taking out both scrolls together, with someone holding the second. The Shaar HaSiyun and Rav Ovadia Yoseph note that the original practice was to take out one, read it, return it, and then take out the second. If on a day with two readings only one was taken out by mistake, do not go back to bring the other out. Follow the original order and bring it after the first has been read.

The Rabbi admitted that for Sefaradim it makes more sense to take all the scrolls out together and leave them on the Tevah. In many Ashkenazi shuls, with Hagbahah and Kaddish between each scroll, "it's like musical chairs."

Not dismantling the Tevah while the Torah is there (Siman 148)

In the times of the Gemara there was no Ark in the synagogue. The Sefer Torah was brought from where it was kept, and the Tevah was decorated with cloths for the reading. The hazan should not take down the decorations while the Sefer Torah is still there, because the congregation would have to wait for him before they could leave. Take the Sefer Torah back first, and remove the decorations afterwards.

The Rabbi said the case is not relevant today, but the principle keeps coming back. "You see again and again kevod haSibur, how important it is." Necessary things are necessary, but never lengthen a service unnecessarily.

Not leaving before the Sefer Torah is put away (Siman 149)

The congregation may not leave the synagogue until the Sefer Torah has been returned. One or two may leave, but not the majority. The Rabbi pictured it: Ashrei after the reading, twenty-five people present, and fifteen rush out leaving a bare minyan. That is disrespectful. If you must leave early, it is best to go after the Torah is back in the Ark.

In the times of the Gemara, if the Sefer Torah was carried to another building and there was only one exit, everyone waited and followed it out.

Someone pointed out that if each person thinks "two or three may leave," the whole shul empties. The Rabbi agreed: one person is not noticed, but when you see everyone heading for the door, stay.

Accompanying the Sefer Torah

The Rema writes that it is a misvah for everyone the Sefer Torah passes to accompany it to the Ark, along with the one doing Gelilah and the one lifting it. Some bring children to kiss the Sefer Torah to educate them, "a beautiful minhag," though children of an appropriate age.

The Mishnah Berurah gives the source, "aharei Hashem Elokeikhem telekhu," "you shall walk after Hashem your God." The Rema adds that this applies also when it is taken out of the Heikhal.

The Rabbi described the Spanish and Portuguese custom: one person is honoured with accompanying the Sefer Torah on behalf of the congregation. The scroll is carried in procession by the one holding it, the person accompanying, the rabbi and the Parnas, in the same order going back. "If you've never seen it, you have to go and see it once."

But the Kaf HaHayim writes that the Rema's ruling was said for a time when the scroll was being carried somewhere else. In a synagogue where everyone has a place, there is no need for the whole congregation to follow it. Rav Ovadia Yoseph writes clearly that the custom is not for everyone to accompany it: we stay in our places, and show honour as it passes.

The Rabbi said he does not like the crowding sometimes seen. He suggested it comes from the Arizal's practice, at Hagbahah, of looking for a word that begins and ends with the letters of one's name. "Everyone wants to see their letter." But "the Arizal was the Arizal. Not everyone should be doing Arizal things." If you are nearby, lovely; if you are the rabbi or president, fine. But not everyone needs to push forward.

He added that when visiting a community you must know its custom. People sometimes come to the Spanish and Portuguese synagogue and follow the scroll, when there one person does it for everyone. "You have to use your common sense."

The class closed the laws of the Sefer Torah with "Hazak Barukh," and the Rabbi promised a short test the following week.

Practical summary

  • Do not begin the Haftarah, even its berakhah, until the Sefer Torah has been rolled or closed.
  • With two scrolls, do not open the second before the first is rolled, and do not lift the first off the Tevah before the second is there.
  • Never lengthen the service with things that can be done afterwards; kevod haSibur keeps returning.
  • The majority must not leave until the Sefer Torah is back in the Ark. If you must go early, go after it is returned.
  • Sefaradim, following the Kaf HaHayim and Rav Ovadia Yoseph, stay in their places rather than all following the Sefer Torah; follow the custom of the community you are in.
