Topic: Whether women recline at the Seder, for Sepharadim and Ashkenazim

Sources: The Gemara; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Mordekhai (Rabbi Mordekhai ben Hillel); the Rema; the Raavyah (Rabbi Eliezer ben Yoel HaLevi); Knesset HaGedolah (Rabbi Hayyim Benveniste)

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Thursday 7 April 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • We recline for the four cups and the massah, which the Gemara calls derekh herut, the way of the aristocracy.
  • The Gemara says a woman does not recline, either because it was not the norm for women, or because women were then apprehensive in their husbands' presence. But an Ishah Hashuvah, a prominent woman, must recline. The Rambam and Shulhan Arukh rule this way.
  • The Mordekhai (13th century, Germany) said all our women today are hashuvot and must recline, and the Rema codifies this for Ashkenazim.
  • The Raavyah (12th century, Germany) held that reclining no longer applies to anyone, since people no longer eat reclining. The Rema rules that women may rely on this, so Ashkenazi women generally do not recline, while men still do.
  • About 300 years ago the Knesset HaGedolah wrote that for Sepharadim too our women are considered important, and today husband and wife sit and eat together without that apprehension, so women should recline. This became the practice in most Sepharadi communities.
  • So in practice it is the reverse of the written rulings: Sepharadi women recline, Ashkenazi women generally do not.
  • Sepharadi women should recline like their husbands for the four cups and the massah, to the extent that one who did not lean must perform the misvah again.
