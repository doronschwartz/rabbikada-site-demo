Topic: Why Pidyon HaBen applies only to a natural birth, and what this teaches about Pesah

Sources: The Gemara and the halacha; the Torah, end of Parashat Bo; the Haggadah

Daily Halacha: Pesah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Pidyon HaBen applies to a firstborn boy whose parents are not Kohanim or Leviim. The Gemara and halacha limit it to a natural birth; a baby born by caesarean has no Pidyon HaBen. There are scriptural sources, but he explains the rationale.
  • Childbirth sounds like death, and historically a woman giving birth was close to death, yet it brings life. History is the same: what looks like ruin often generates life.
  • Egypt, the "iron crucible", seemed hopeless, yet from it came redemption, nationhood and Har Sinai. Purim turned annihilation into influence overnight. After 1945 Jewish continuity seemed lost, yet three years later we returned to Israel. A seed must rot before it sprouts.
  • Pidyon HaBen commemorates, as the end of Parashat Bo says, the striking of the Egyptian firstborn and the sparing of ours, done, as the Haggadah says, directly by HaKadosh Barukh Hu, not through any angel or messenger.
  • A natural birth, without human intervention, mirrors that: God directly bringing life out of near death. A birth requiring human intervention is no less special, but it is a different kind, belonging more to Shavuot, which commemorates human development.
  • Pesah gives hope: in every generation there are those who try to destroy us, yet from the darkest moments redemption sprouts.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara and halacha: no Pidyon HaBen after a caesarean birth.
  • End of Parashat Bo: Pidyon HaBen recalls the plague of the firstborn.
  • The Haggadah: the plague came not through an angel or messenger but from God Himself.

In one line

Pidyon HaBen follows only a natural birth because, like the Exodus, it marks God Himself bringing life out of what looked like death.
