Topic: Cross-dressing and other leniencies on Purim, and Rav Yoseph Messas on the origins of costumes

Sources: The Mahari Mintz; the Rama; a teshuvah of the Rambam; the Hida; Rav Yoseph Messas, Mayim Hayyim; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Purim, Wednesday 20 March 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Mahari Mintz, the first to record dressing up, says that for the joy of Purim it is even permitted, as a one-off, for men and women to wear each other's clothes. The Rama records this as the minhag.
  • The vast majority of poskim came out strongly against this. Much earlier, in a teshuvah, the Rambam forbade cross-dressing even as a one-off: in Egypt a hatan would cross-dress at weddings, excused as simhat hatan vekallah, and he writes, thank God, that the minhag was abolished. The Hida and many others agree, and this is the consensus.
  • So what the Rama records, that some things forbidden all year are permitted on Purim, including cross-dressing and rabbinic shaatnez, is not correct; the regular halachot apply on Purim.
  • Rav Yoseph Messas, from Morocco and later Chief Rabbi of Haifa, writes in Mayim Hayyim something shocking: the minhag likely comes from the pagan Roman carnival before Lent, with its masks, parades and costumed street parties, which originated in Italy and was held around February.
  • He concludes it has a semekh (a root) in avodah zarah and should be abolished, or allowed at most for children. There seems some truth to this, since its first mention is by the Mahari Mintz in Italy and it has no traditional source.
  • Still, it is a widespread minhag and he is not suggesting a ban; Hakham Ovadiah says there is no prohibition. But costumes are a minor part of Purim, and anyone who dresses up must keep the usual laws of modesty and cross-dressing.

Sources quoted

  • The Rambam: cross-dressing is forbidden even as a one-off celebration.
  • Rav Yoseph Messas, Mayim Hayyim: Purim costumes likely derive from the Roman carnival.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: dressing up is not prohibited.

In one line

Dressing up is a widespread but minor custom of doubtful origin, and Purim never permits cross-dressing or other things forbidden all year.
