Topic: Does the maidservant solution work today? The exchange between Rav Breisch and Rav Weiss after the Holocaust

Sources: The Gemara; Rav Mordekhai Yaakov Breisch; Rav Weiss of Manchester

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 5 June 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The method: a male mamzer acquires a non-Jewish woman as a shifhah, with immersion for slavery (far simpler than conversion), marries her, has children, then frees her and them, and they become regular Jews. The Gemara says it does not work for a mamzeret.
  • The issues, discussed in the last 70 years: slavery is abolished and dina demalkhuta dina requires following the law of the land; and is it real slavery when she will act as his wife?
  • After the Holocaust, Rav Weiss, Rosh Beit Din in Manchester, wrote of about ten cases of women who believed their husbands killed, remarried and had children, then found the husbands alive. He asked Rav Mordekhai Yaakov Breisch, Rav in Zurich for over 40 years.
  • Rav Breisch: use the slavery method. Dina demalkhuta dina, by many opinions, applies mainly to financial matters, and though questionable today, the plight of mamzerim justifies leniency.
  • Rav Weiss objected: it is not real slavery if she can call the authorities, and it would undermine real conversion. He proposed immersion for both slavery and conversion; either way the mamzer may marry her, and the children are a safek mamzer, so one may be lenient.
  • Rav Breisch replied that the doubt is better left on the marriage than on the children: with immersion for slavery alone, even if it failed, children of a non-Jewish woman are not mamzerim.
  • There are rumours of this being done quietly today; it remains very controversial.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Breisch: permits the slavery method because of the plight of mamzerim.
  • Rav Weiss: proposes a double immersion, leaving the children a safek mamzer.

In one line

Rav Breisch permitted the maidservant method for Holocaust survivors' children, Rav Weiss questioned it, and it remains very controversial.
