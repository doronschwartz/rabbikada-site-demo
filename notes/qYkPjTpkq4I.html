Topic: Who counts as an ani for sedakah and matanot laevyonim

Sources: The Mishnah; the Tur and Shulhan Arukh; the Rashbash (Rabbi Shelomo Duran); contemporary poskim

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Wednesday 23 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • We must know who is an ani both for the duty to give when asked and for matanot laevyonim on Purim, in a few weeks.
  • The Mishnah says one with less than 200 zuz, and no fixed income, is an ani who may ask and must be given. His home and the utensils he uses, which produce no income, are not counted.
  • The commentators explain 200 zuz as roughly enough to live on for a year.
  • The Tur and Shulhan Arukh cite the Rashbash, early 15th century, that today one may receive sedakah to start a new business and generate income; 200 zuz belonged to its time, and there is no fixed sum now.
  • In practice the poskim of this generation define an ani as one who cannot complete the month: who will struggle with rent or mortgage, tuition, food or utility bills. This applies to sedakah and Purim.
  • This concerns regular living. A sudden expense beyond one's means, such as medical bills, also makes one an ani.
  • An ani who receives from several people at once may keep it even if it exceeds what he needs; he may not go on asking once he has reached the threshold.

In one line

Today an ani is anyone who cannot get to the end of the month, or who faces a sudden expense beyond his means.
