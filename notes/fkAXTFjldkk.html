Topic: A brief Purim thought: kavod as the theme running through the Megillah

Sources: The Megillah; the Gemara; Rabbi Jonathan Sacks

Daily Halacha: Purim, Thursday 13 March 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • One theme stands out in the whole Megillah: kavod, people wanting their way.
  • Ahashverosh wants Vashti to come and display her beauty; she refuses to be objectified and stands up for her rights. He feels his authority tested and, on his officers' advice, has her executed: a dispute about authority and kavod.
  • Haman has everything: all bow to him, he is second in command, with great wealth and many children. Yet one person who withholds kavod is more than he can bear.
  • For Ahashverosh the value is dominance; for Vashti, refusing to be objectified; for Haman, everyone recognising his power.
  • Mordekhai is the opposite. He reports Bigtan and Teresh, which makes little difference to him, because he cares. And "Mordekhai did as Esther commanded him": though the Gemara calls him the leader of the generation, and he was her elder relative (in one reading her uncle), he does all she tells him.
  • As Rabbi Sacks notes in another context, physical things such as power, money and dominance are a zero-sum game and cannot be shared, so Vashti and Ahashverosh, and Haman and Mordekhai, end up fighting. The pursuit of truth and of what is right can be shared, so Mordekhai and Esther work together.

In one line

The Megillah's conflicts all stem from kavod, which cannot be shared, while Mordekhai and Esther, pursuing what is right, can work as one.
