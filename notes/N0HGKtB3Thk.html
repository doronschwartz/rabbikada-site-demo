Topic: Sitting on the ground, working, and preparing food on Tisha BeAv

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Gemara; Hakham Ovadiah; the Hida

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Wednesday 26 July 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Some practices are custom, not prohibition. The Shulhan Arukh records sitting on the ground in the synagogue; the Spanish and Portuguese instead change their place, as a mourner does.
  • The original custom was only in the synagogue and only until midday. One who finds it hard may sit low just in the synagogue; a very old person need not at all.
  • The Gemara says one who works on Tisha BeAv sees no blessing from it, without forbidding it outright, so the Shulhan Arukh makes it depend on local custom.
  • Hakham Ovadiah says most places do not work, so a self-employed person who can pause without loss should refrain.
  • A davar haaved, avoiding a loss, is permitted; what is not allowed is gaining. In his view an employee for whom a day off would use up limited holiday has a davar haaved and may work, preferably only after midday, though strictly all day.
  • Shopping for after the fast is permitted. The Hida records the custom to prepare food only after midday. This year, with Tisha BeAv on Thursday, one may start cooking for Shabbat on Thursday after midday.

Avoid work on Tisha BeAv where you can without loss; an employee may work, preferably after midday, and cooking starts after midday.
