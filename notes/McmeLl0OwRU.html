Part 1: The Principle Itself

היסוד הששי: הנבואה. והוא שידע האדם שזה מין האדם ימצאו בהם בעלי טבעים ומדות מעולות מאד ושלמות גדולה, ונפשותיהם נכונות עד שיקבלו צורת השכל. אחר כן ידבק אותו השכל האנושי בשכל הפועל, ונאצל ממנו עליו אצילות נכבד. ואלה הם הנביאים, וזו היא הנבואה, וזה ענינה.

Translation:

- Within the human race there are people of exceptional nature and character and great perfection, whose souls are so prepared that they fully realise their intellect.

- That human intellect then cleaves to the Active Intellect.

- From it, a great atzilut flows down upon them. ("How would we translate atzilut? A great flow. A great divesting down.")

- These are the prophets. This is prophecy.

וביאור יסוד זה על בוריו ארוך מאד, ואין כוונתנו להביא מופת על כל יסוד מהם… לפי שזהו כלל החכמה כולה, אבל הזכרתה דרך סיפור בלבד. ומקראי התורה מעידים על נבואת נביאים הרבה.

To explain this fully would take very long. My purpose isn't to prove each principle, since this is a whole science. I mention it only briefly. The Torah's verses attest to many prophets.

What the principle is, and isn't

"The sixth principle is not that the words of the Nevi'im are true. That belongs to principles 8 and 9, that the Torah is true and divine. The point here is that there is a concept of prophecy: Hashem speaks to perfected humans."

Formulation

Says

Accurate?

Ani Ma'amin (late 1400s/early 1500s, author unclear): אני מאמין באמונה שלמה שכל דברי נביאים אמת

The prophets' words are true

"That's not the point of the principle. That's not correct."

Yigdal: שפע נבואתו נתנו אל אנשי סגולתו ותפארתו

Hashem gave His flow of prophecy to His chosen, special people

"Much more precise."

Why it's a principle in its own right

Hashem is involved in the world. The Ramban, end of Parashat Bo: the whole purpose of Mitzrayim and the ten plagues. Pharaoh believed in a divine power, but not one that communicates with and cares about humanity. Hence כי אני ה׳ בקרב הארץ: I am Hashem in the midst of the land.

"God didn't create the world and leave us. He gave us a Torah (later principles) and also constant instruction, at least until the destruction of the Beit HaMikdash."

Part 2: Dreams, Ruach HaKodesh and Prophecy

People often confuse prophecy with dreams and with Ruach HaKodesh. Their only common ground: each reveals information from a lofty spiritual source. (This three-level framework is the Ramchal's; see the caveat below.)

1. Dreams: "one-sixtieth of prophecy" (Gemara)

Why we wash our hands in the morning:

Reason

Source

Our hands moved and touched parts of the body overnight; wash before prayer

The Rambam (al derech hapeshat)

Like the Kohanim washing before the Temple service: preparing to serve Hashem

The Rashba

At night soul and body part; the soul goes heavenward to recharge. The vacuum draws tumah, so we wash the extremities

The Zohar (hence אלהי נשמה שנתת בי טהורה: "You've given me back my soul". "That's not the simple meaning.")

The Ben Ish Chai: really we should wash the whole body daily; Chazal limited it to the hands, the body's "tips" (and "really the toes too: a different discussion").

The Ramchal on dreams: in the body the soul is suppressed. Freed at night, it meets spiritual entities, which may tell it about the future, and that is transmitted down: a dream is a fuzzy transmission. (The Rambam: "To the best of my knowledge he doesn't discuss how dreams work.")

But not every heavenly force is good: malachei chabalah convey false, fabricated messages. Hence the contradiction:

Zechariah: חלומות השוא ידברו: dreams speak falsehood. Yet the end of Bereishit is all dreams: Yosef's, Pharaoh's, everyone's.

Answer: it depends on the source.

Rule

Why

A dream follows its interpretation

We can't know whether its source is true

A repeated dream is taken seriously

ויישן ויחלם שנית: why does the Torah stress that Pharaoh slept and dreamt again?

Interpret positively

Even a true, bad dream can be changed by reframing it

"I remember one person who every week would call us together as a beit din to annul his dreams. Zichrono livrachah. Some people get very afraid of dreams."

2. Ruach HaKodesh: divine intuition

Talmai's 72 Sages (from Asarah b'Tevet last week): each translating separately, they were all divinely inspired to make the same changes. "That's not prophecy. They weren't having a prophetic experience. It was Ruach HaKodesh: divine intuition."

The Ramchal, Derech Hashem:

יקרה שיושפע בלב האדם שפע שיעמידהו על תוכן ענין מהענינים… a flow suddenly gives you understanding of something. Sometimes you won't notice it: אלא כמי שנופלת מחשבה בלבבו: like a thought just fell into your heart.

"A brilliant idea in Yiddish is an einfall: it fell from above. You're confused, and suddenly it all falls into place, and you feel divinely inspired. I think humans experience that. But it's completely different from prophecy."

"It's a big mistake to think prophecy is Hashem giving you extra information. That's a possible side effect. The essence is a Godly experience."

Proof: the Gemara (Megillah) implies at least 1.2 million prophets (below). "How many books of prophets do we have? We can count them on our fingers. We don't have their messages, because the message isn't the essence."

And it doesn't endure: "Prophecy for half an hour today doesn't mean tomorrow. You may have it once in your life." Hence the Bnei HaNevi'im: "like medical school or law school, there was literally a school for prophets, where they practised receiving prophecy."

The Rambam, Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah 7:1: בעת שתנוח עליו הרוח תתערב נפשו במעלת המלאכים הנקראים אישים, ויהפך לאיש אחר, ויבין בדעתו שאינו כמו שהיה אלא שנתעלה על מעלת שאר בני אדם החכמים, כמו שנאמר בשאול: והתנבית עמם ונהפכת לאיש אחר.

When the spirit rests on him, his soul joins the level of the angels called ishim; he becomes a different person, and knows he is not as he was: as said to Shaul: you will prophesy with them and become another man.

The Ramchal, Derech Hashem: higher than both is prophecy: שיגיע האדם ויתקשר בבורא יתברך שמו, ויתדבק בו דביקות ממש in a way he feels the cleaving, with no doubt. והנה עיקר הנבואה הוא השג הדבקות והקשר הזה עודו בחיים: its essence is attaining this cleaving and connection while still alive.

What is it like?

"I've got no idea, and it's very hard to describe. The best analogy: you're led blindfolded into a room. For hours you grope around, feeling this and that, trying to work out where you are. Then the blindfold comes off and you see everything. Everything falls into place. That's nevuah."

The Rambam (Moreh): comparing prophecy to philosophy is like trying to pour all the water in the world into one jug. "I could spend hours describing this room to you with your eyes closed, the colours, the books, the furniture; it can't compare to opening your eyes."

"All the questions, the philosophy, the investigation we go through in life trying to understand Torah and God: suddenly your eyes are opened. Not to everything, it's limited. But in whatever you're shown, you have a whole different understanding of what life is about."

Part 3: Who Can Become a Prophet? (Moreh 2:32)

The Moreh has three sections: God, prophecy, reward and punishment, "really the thirteen principles in their three categories." Section two is mostly about prophecy.

Among those who believe in prophecy there are three views:

View 1: the masses (da'at hamon)

שהאל יתעלה בוחר מי שירצה מבני אדם… ומשלחו

Hashem simply chooses whom He wants. "You wake up one morning and Hashem says: you're a prophet."

אין לדעתם הפרש בין היות האיש ההוא חכם או סכל, זקן או נער: wisdom, age, experience make no difference. (He must be a good, decent person: "Hashem isn't giving prophecy to the wicked.")

The Rambam: the view of the ignorant. It is also Rabbi Yehudah HaLevi's view in the Kuzari.

View 2: the philosophers (incl. Aristotle, "who believed in prophecy")

הנבואה היא שלמות מסוימת בטבע האדם: prophecy is a natural perfection, reached only after great training.

The person perfect in intellect and character, with a developed imaginative faculty: הכרח שיתנבא: he must prophesy.

View 3: the Torah's view (the Rambam's)

היא כדעת הפילוסופית בדיוק, חוץ מדבר אחד: exactly the philosophers' view, except one thing: one who is fit and prepared may still not prophesy, לפי החפץ האלהי: according to the Divine will. (He then works through the verses that seem to say otherwise.)

Requirements

"The miracle is…"

2. Philosophers

"Views one and three are miktzeh el katzeh: from one end to the other." For the Rambam, prophecy doesn't just fall on a person; you constantly train and perfect yourself.

The most consistent thread in all the Rambam's writing: the purpose of humanity is perfecting the intellect and the middot. Mitzvot train the character; study of Torah and the sciences refines the intellect.

Question (class): View 1 seems to equate prophecy with getting a message, which we said isn't prophecy.

"Not necessarily. Hashem may choose you just to give you the experience: you now understand what other humans don't, without telling anyone. Sometimes you'll be sent with a message. The dispute isn't about what prophecy is for; it's about who gets it."

Part 4: The Three Ingredients

Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah 7:1: אין הנבואה חלה אלא על חכם גדול בחכמה, גבור במדותיו, ולא יהא יצרו מתגבר עליו בדבר בעולם, אלא הוא מתגבר בדעתו על יצרו תמיד, בעל דעה רחבה נכונה עד מאד.

Moreh 2:36: prophecy is reached only through השלמות בחכמות העיוניות (the theoretical sciences), perfecting the middot to their best possible state, together with the perfection of the imaginative faculty (koach hamedameh) by nature.

Ingredient

What it means

The Rabbi

1. Intellect

Torah inside out, a huge talmid chacham, and the sciences of the world (not just Torah: "otherwise he'd say Torah")

"To understand God, study what He's given us: the Torah, and the world He made. From His handiwork you understand how He operates." Basic sciences, "not technology"

2. Character

Never ruled by the yetzer: no anger, no lust of the eyes, no pull of financial gain

"We all have dips. Even such a person fluctuates, which is why sometimes he has prophecy and sometimes not"

3. Imagination (ba'al de'ah rechavah)

The ability to think broadly: you see a boat on a river and a bird flying, and imagine a boat flying

"I've never understood why this is essential, but the Rambam reiterates it in many places. Whether that means reading lots of sci-fi, I'm not sure he means that."

A caveat from the Rabbi: "I've probably shoehorned the Ramchal into the Rambam. Here the Rambam seems to equate Ruach HaKodesh with nevuah. The dreams / Ruach HaKodesh / prophecy distinction was straight from the Ramchal. I'm not sure the Rambam agrees. I need to look into that properly."

The 1.2 million prophets

Megillah 14a: הרבה נביאים עמדו להם לישראל, כפלים כיוצאי מצרים: double those who left Egypt. 600,000 × 2 = 1.2 million.

"Really? 1.2 million people reached the perfection the Rambam describes? Perhaps. Perhaps we don't know them. I think it's difficult; the Rambam may not take it literally. But it does show prophecy isn't bearing a message: we don't have 1.2 million books."

Part 5: Must a Prophet Be Jewish?

The Kuzari: only a Jew, only in Israel

Five levels: inanimate, plant, animal, human, which all the Rishonim accept, and a fifth: the Jew. The Jew is inherently different.

So prophecy is a gift, only for a Jew, and only in Eretz Yisrael. Not even a convert: "a full-fledged Jew for everything, but an inferior kind of soul" for this purpose.

Question (class): How does the Kuzari explain Bilaam?

The Gemara: Hashem gave the nations prophets so they couldn't complain you had prophets and we didn't. The Kuzari would need a special exception. "I can't see him saying it here. I'll check and post it on the group."

The Rambam: any human

"I don't know anywhere the Rambam says the Jew is inherently different, and I don't believe he would." Prophecy isn't a gift: reach the perfection and you receive it, Jew and non-Jew equally. He always speaks of min ha'enoshi, the human race.

But: Jews are more aligned to perfection, because more mitzvot means more refinement. "More susceptible, not exclusive."

The difficulty: Har Sinai

At Matan Torah, the whole people heard Hashem; the Midrash says they fainted, which many read as prophecy. How did a nation of slaves suddenly reach that perfection?

"According to the Rambam it wasn't full-blown prophecy; it was a kind of philosophical revelation. Obviously Har Sinai fits better with the Kuzari's view."

The prophetic experience itself

The Rambam and the Ramchal: the prophet is stripped of his physical senses; it feels as if the body is turned inside out; he can't do anything else and must lie flat.

Part 6: Is There Prophecy Today?

The Gemara: with the death of Chaggai, Zechariah and Malachi, early in the Second Temple, the spirit of prophecy departed.

For the Kuzari, simple: we're in exile; Hashem no longer gives it. For the Rambam, if someone reaches the three levels, why not?

Moreh 2:36: the imaginative faculty can't be complete if a person isn't happy.

- Yaakov had no prophecy for 22 years, mourning Yosef.

- Music was essential to inducing prophecy: והיה כנגן המנגן ותהי עליו יד ה׳ (Elisha).

The Rabbi's connection: the Gemara (Gittin; Sotah) forbids music after the Churban. By the strict letter (Gemara, Rambam, Shulchan Aruch) live music is forbidden all year, except perhaps at a wedding. "Nobody keeps this halachah, we go to concerts; why is a different discussion. But I think there's a link: music was an ingredient of prophecy, bringing happiness."

"My understanding of the Rambam: not impossible today, but very, very hard. How can we be in exile, in servitude to the nations, without the Beit HaMikdash, and be fully happy?"

Part 7: A First Look at Principle 7

היסוד השביעי: נבואת משה רבנו עליו השלום. והוא שנאמין כי הוא אביהם של כל הנביאים אשר היו לפניו ואשר קמו אחריו, כולם הם תחתיו במעלה, והוא היה נבחר מכל מין האדם, אשר השיג מאמתתו יתברך יותר מכל מה שהשיגו ושישיגו שום אדם שנמצא או שימצא.

Moshe is the father of all prophets before and after; all are beneath him. He grasped more of Hashem's truth than anyone who ever was or will be.

וכי הוא עליו השלום הגיע התעלותו מן האנושית אל המעלה המלאכותית ונכלל במעלת המלאכים, ולא נשאר מסך שלא קרעו… ולא מנעו ממנו מונע גופני, ולא נתערב לו שום חסרון בין רב בין מעט, ונתבטלו ממנו כוחות הדמיונות והחושים… ונשאר שכל בלבד. ועל הענין הזה נאמר עליו שהוא מדבר עם השם יתברך בלא אמצעות המלאכים.

A shocking text to leave with

The Arizal, quoted by the Ba'al HaTanya (the first of the seven Chabad Rebbes, ~250 years ago): השגת משה רבינו עליו השלום לא היתה בבחינת פנימיות חכמה עילאה: Moshe's grasp did not reach the inner dimension of the highest sefirot (Chochmah, Keter); למעלה מנצח הוד יסוד מלכות לא היתה לו שום השגה בפנימיות: above Netzach, Hod, Yesod, Malchut, only external understanding.

The Tanya asks: ולכאורה יפלא, הרי נאמר ולא קם נביא עוד בישראל כמשה, ואיך השיג האריז״ל יותר ממנו?

Answers: there's a great difference between the grasp of חכמי האמת, the Rashbi and the Arizal (wisdom and knowledge), and Moshe's, which was prophecy: seeing, as in וראית את אחורי ופני לא יראו.

"On its face: Kabbalistic knowledge and prophetic knowledge are two disciplines. Moshe was a prophet, not a Kabbalist. The theological consequences are far-reaching. I think the Rambam would view this as completely heretical. I find it shocking. We have to be very careful with these things."

Question at the End

Who were the prophets before Moshe, and how did they learn without books?

Avraham, Yitzchak and Yaakov certainly: "Hashem spoke to Avraham" is prophecy.

"Knowledge doesn't just come from books. Avraham gazed at the world and understood it." And Chazal speak of some learning even before the Torah.

Avraham kept the Torah before it was given: "some take it literally. The simple understanding: he attuned himself to what the Torah would demand a thousand years later, because the Torah is the guide to perfecting middot and intellect, and he reached that himself."

Summary

  1. Principle 6 opens the second category (6–9): Hashem relates to humanity. It is not "the prophets' words are true" (that's 8–9; the Ani Ma'amin is imprecise, Yigdal precise). It establishes Hashem's involvement in the world (Ramban: the plagues answered Pharaoh).
  2. Dreams (true or false, follow the interpretation), Ruach HaKodesh (intuition, an einfall) and prophecy (a clear, transforming experience of cleaving to Hashem), per the Ramchal. The Rabbi is unsure the Rambam draws the same line.
  3. Prophecy is an experience, not information, and doesn't endure (Bnei HaNevi'im, 1.2 million prophets).
  4. Who prophesies: the masses / Kuzari (Hashem chooses), philosophers (automatic), the Torah / Rambam (prepared, then Hashem's will). The miracle is not getting it.
  5. Three ingredients: intellect (Torah and the sciences), character, imagination.
  6. Kuzari: Jews only, in Israel. Rambam: any human, though mitzvot help. Har Sinai fits the Kuzari better.
  7. No prophecy today: for the Rambam, without joy the imagination can't be complete: exile, no Temple, no music.
  8. Principle 7: Moshe above all prophets, pure intellect, no intermediary; the promised books became the Moreh; the Arizal/Tanya claim is "shocking."
