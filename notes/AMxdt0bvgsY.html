Topic: The best and least suitable times of day to recite Selihot

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rambam; the verse "Lehagid baboker hasdekha"; Rav Moshe Feinstein; Rav Ovadia Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Elul, Tuesday 6 September 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The original and best time is just before sunrise, at the end of the night before Shaharit, as the Shulhan Arukh and the Rambam write. Night is a time of concealment and day a time of hesed; the turning of night into day is an especially fitting time to ask for mercy.
  • Second best is any time from midnight (Hasot HaLailah), which already belongs to the coming day. This is why many places in Israel say Selihot at midnight and then sleep.
  • The common practice, Selihot in the morning shortly before Shaharit once it is already day, is also acceptable, though not the same as before dawn or after midnight.
  • Selihot before Minhah in the afternoon is halachically fine but not the best option: from midday, in Kabbalistic terms, it is already a time of din.
  • The least suitable time is the first half of the night, from dark until midnight. Those who view halacha through the Kabbalah forbid Selihot then, and most poskim say it is better not to say them at all than then.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein writes that there is no halachic prohibition: the objection is Kabbalistic only. The Spanish and Portuguese communities of London and Amsterdam for at least a century said the next day's Selihot after Minhah and Arvit; the practice was stopped on the advice of Rav Ovadia Yoseph, and today they say them before Shaharit.

Sources quoted

  • "Lehagid baboker hasdekha veemunatekha baleilot": by day we see HaShem's hesed, at night we need emunah.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: no halachic prohibition on Selihot in the first part of the night.

In one line

Best is just before dawn, then from midnight, then the morning before Shaharit, then the afternoon; the first half of the night is least suitable, for Kabbalistic rather than halachic reasons.
