Topic: Does owning property in Israel, or spending the festivals there, allow a visitor to keep one day?

Sources: Rav Shelomoh Zalman Auerbach; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; Rav Melamed

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Wednesday 16 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A visitor keeps two days by the majority view, unlike the Hakham Svi. But Rav Shelomoh Zalman Auerbach and Hakham Ovadiah give a further leniency, which must be understood correctly.
  • Rav Shelomoh Zalman: one who has a property in Israel and customarily spends the three festivals (Pesah, Shavuot and Sukkot) there keeps one day while there. Missing one festival in a particular year (for example when travel was impossible during Covid) does not change this. Hakham Ovadiah rules the same.
  • Rav Shelomoh Zalman added that owning the property is not the main point; it is a means that makes it easy to spend the festivals there. So one who generally spends the three festivals there in a hotel or rental may also keep one day, since owning property is not magic.
  • Others disagree: since most of the year is spent abroad, even the three festivals are insufficient.
  • Owning property alone, while coming only for Pesah or only for Sukkot each year, seems insufficient by their view; one still keeps two days. The second day is about the three festivals.
  • Some poskim, such as Rav Melamed, hold that owning property is enough, as it shows intent to settle in Israel eventually, but only if it was bought genuinely to live there in the near future, not as an investment. No one would agree that an investment property helps.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Shelomoh Zalman Auerbach and Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: spending the three festivals in Israel, with a property there, allows one day.
  • Rav Melamed: a property bought with genuine intent to live in Israel soon is sufficient.

In one line

One who regularly spends all three festivals in Israel keeps one day there, but merely owning property, especially as an investment, does not suffice.
