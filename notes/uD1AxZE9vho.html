Topic: How the treatment of Ethiopian Jews became more lenient, and practice in Israel and London

Sources: Rav Ovadiah Yoseph; the Radbaz; the Erekh Lehem (Rabbi Yaakov Castro); Rav Herzog

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 9 March 2026

The halacha

  • The Rabbanut under Chief Rabbi Herzog, and most poskim, required a conversion out of doubt.
  • Rav Ovadiah Yoseph held strongly that Ethiopian Jews are Jewish and need no conversion: why doubt the Radbaz, the first to discuss it properly? The Erekh Lehem after him said similarly. One should not doubt the great Radbaz because of modern researchers.
  • Unlike Soviet Jewry, here practice grew more lenient. In the 1970s and 1980s they were still converted, but without hatafat dam berit, just acceptance of misvot.
  • As the community grew, and rabbis were accused of racism, from about the 2000s the Batei Din in Israel began following Rav Ovadiah Yoseph. A conversion is welcome, but not required to be considered Jewish and to marry.
  • This is now the Rabbanut's official position for the main group, Beta Israel, with people in the community checking that those coming belong to it. He notes he is not an expert on this.
  • The Falash Mura, who apparently converted to Christianity, are still required to convert today.
  • The London Ashkenazi Beth Din does not follow Rav Ovadiah Yoseph and does not accept Ethiopian Jews without conversion.
  • At the Sepharadi Beth Din, to ensure its marriages and conversions are accepted by all Batei Din in the UK, a conversion as a stringency is asked for: just the mikveh and acceptance of the misvot.

In one line

Israel's Rabbanut now accepts Beta Israel without conversion following Rav Ovadiah Yoseph, while in London a conversion, at least out of stringency, is still required.
