Introduction

This class finished Siman 154. After last week's difficult Seifim, the Rabbi said "nothing too complex here", though the section on ten years without children raised many questions.

Seifim 8-9: a husband who is leaving

If we know a husband is about to leave the country, Beit Din may force him to swear that he will not leave. If he refuses, he must give a get conditional on his not returning within a set time, such as twelve months, as we learned in the laws of conditions (Hilchot Tenayim).

The question. A wrongly forced get is void and the children may be mamzerim. Here he has not yet done anything wrong. On what grounds may we force?

  • The Rivash (the source is the Rosh): it is not really force, because he has a choice. Either he swears not to go, or he gives the get. Since the choice is in his hands, it is not called kefiyah.
  • The Beit Meir gives a different reason: the get is conditional, so it only takes effect if he stays away. He can always come back, and the choice is his. It is not a get meuseh even if he has no ability to swear.
  • The Gra gives the same reason as the Rivash. The Pithei Teshuvah brings the Beit Meir.

What turns on it. A man who is not believed on an oath, so he has no real second option. According to the Rivash and the Gra, we cannot force him. According to the Beit Meir, we can. The Rabbi said this is the whole mahloket in Seif 8.

Seif 9 is similar: a man who cannot stay in his town, for example because of trouble there, and his wife is not obliged to follow him. Then we force him to divorce.

Seif 10: ten years without children

The Shulhan Arukh: a man who married and lived with his wife for ten years and had no children is forced to divorce her and pay the ketubah, or to marry another woman who can have children. If he says "I will not have relations with her, I just want to stay in the same house, and I will even appoint someone to watch me", it makes no difference. We still force him, since he must fulfil the misvah of peru urevu.

The Rema: this applies only if he has no child at all. If he has one child, we do not force him.

Source: Avraham and Sarah. After Avraham lived ten years in the land of Canaan, Sarah gave him Hagar. The Gemara learns the ten years from this.

Nowadays we do not force. The Rivash writes, and the Shulhan Arukh says in Siman 1, that we do not force this, because if Beit Din got involved in marriage arrangements (yihus zivugim) there would be no end. Batei Din had ignored this for a long time before him. But if a man asks his rabbi after ten years what to do, the rabbi must still consider what to advise.

One child: the Ramban and the Ritva

  • The Ramban: if there is any child at all, we do not force. A child shows there is no inherent problem, and it may happen again.
  • The Ritva: the misvah of peru urevu is a son and a daughter, so we force until he has a son and a daughter (zakhar unekevah).
  • If the wife can now no longer have children (age, illness), the Ritva says even the Ramban would agree that he must divorce, since the reason for leniency (that she can still have more) is gone.
  • Others say that once there is one child we do not break up a marriage.
  • The Beit Shemuel rules like the Ritva. The Pithei Teshuvah, in contrast, says that God-fearing people did not divorce when they had one child, and we should not invent anything. Hazal speak of "no children at all". So the Pithei Teshuvah concludes there is no need to divorce after one child.

The Rabbi said he was not entirely clear on the reason for leniency after one child. Perhaps Hazal enacted divorce only for those with no children, and a person with one has already "started".

Questions from the class. If the wife cannot have children but the man could with a surrogate or another wife, does this change the question? The Rabbi said the obligation remains and he may choose how to fulfil it, but the poskim hardly discuss this. He also noted that no one tells a couple with ten children, all boys or all girls, to remarry. Once there is one child, there is no obligation.

If there are no children at all: nowadays doctors can tell who has the problem. If the problem is with her, no Beit Din or rabbi will intervene, but it may be incumbent on him to think about it, as it is an obligation and not only a misvah. If the problem is with him, divorcing her would achieve nothing.

Does it apply outside Israel? The Rishonim disagree.

  • Some say it does not apply in Hus LaAres, as the misvah and the ten years come from Eres Kenaan.
  • The Ramban says it does apply abroad. The words "in the land of Canaan" teach that if you move to Eres Yisrael, you start a new ten years, because of the merit of the land. The Beit Shemuel follows this.

A Pithei Teshuvah to consider. A man who is ill and cared for by a wife who cannot have children: Hazal say a person is not required to spend more than a fifth of his money on a misvah. He argues that to ask this man to divorce, when he may never find another woman to care for him so well, is more than a fifth (a yosef mehomesh). The Rabbi said this is probably the main answer in practice. This is why couples stay together after ten years: leaving would be too damaging emotionally.

Rabbenu Gershom. An Ashkenazi man may not marry a second wife. Can the ten-year rule override this? The Pithei Teshuvah says no, it is not enough. But if he wants to divorce, and she refuses to accept the get, there is room to allow it. Sefaradim never accepted Rabbenu Gershom at all.

Breaks, miscarriage and the third husband

Breaks. If during the ten years he was away on business, or one of them was in prison or ill, that time is not counted, even if they occasionally had relations while one was ill. They did not have the same ability to conceive.

Miscarriage. If the wife miscarried, the ten years count from the day of the miscarriage. If she miscarried three times, she is presumed (hazakah) to miscarry, and we do not count more.

  • The Rabbi asked two questions: why must he divorce her at all, and why is three the number, when a hazakah is usually established by two? The Beit Shemuel says three, since miscarriage in early pregnancy is common.
  • The Shulhan Arukh says he divorces and pays the ketubah. The Rif and the Rosh explain: perhaps he is not meant to have children from her. It is the combination that fails. She is presumed to miscarry with him, so she may marry someone else, and does not lose her ketubah.
  • If he says she miscarried and she says she did not, she is believed, since she knows her own body. The Rishonim ask: perhaps she is lying to leave him (shema einah nosnah beaher). The Ramban says a woman would not give herself the reputation of a woman who cannot carry. The Beit Yoseph says the key is that she is not demanding a get, she is only contradicting him.

The third husband. A woman who waited ten years with one husband, remarried, and again had no child with a second husband, now has a hazakah (twice), and may not marry a third. She still gets the ketubah from the second, since a hazakah for money needs three times. If she does marry a third and he divorces her, she gets no ketubah, unless he knew when he married her. If the third husband already had children or another wife, he need not divorce her.

After thirty years and three husbands, she cannot claim a ketubah from the first two. But if she has already collected, she does not have to return it, since perhaps the problem arose only later.

Seif 20: a forbidden marriage

The last Seif is about a man who married a woman forbidden to him, even by a rabbinic prohibition (sheniyah), such as a great-grandmother. The Shulhan Arukh says yosi, he shall divorce.

What does yosi mean? The Rishonim argue.

  • Tosafot: wherever the Gemara says yosi, we may force.
  • Another Rishon: yosi means it is the right thing to do, but we do not force. Only the word yikfu, "they shall force", permits force. The Shulhan Arukh rules like Tosafot as the main view and brings this as "some say".
  • Where the woman is clearly forbidden to him, everyone agrees force may be used.

Today. Physical force (kefiyah beshotim) is not used, so we are left with the Rema's sanctions: telling the community not to help the man, not to do business with him, not to let a mohel circumcise his sons, not to bury him. These, the Rabbi said, are "the strongest thing which we have nowadays".

Practical summary

  • A man who is about to leave may be forced to give a conditional get. The Rivash and the Gra say the reason is that he can swear instead, and the Beit Meir says the get is conditional and he can return.
  • After ten years with no child, a man must divorce or remarry (Seif 10). Nowadays we do not force this, but the rabbi may still advise.
  • With one child, the Ramban and the Pithei Teshuvah say there is no obligation. The Ritva and Beit Shemuel would still require divorce where the wife can no longer have children.
  • A break through illness, business or prison is not counted, and a miscarriage restarts the count.
  • If the wife is presumed to miscarry, she is still paid her ketubah and may marry someone else, but not a third husband after two cases.
  • Where the law says yosi, force is disputed. Today we mainly use sanctions.
