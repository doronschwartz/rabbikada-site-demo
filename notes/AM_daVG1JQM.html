Topic: The status of a child born to a single woman (shetuki)

Sources: The Gemara

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 29 June 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A married woman's child is kasher even if she acted promiscuously, since it is assumed to be her husband's. The question now is a single woman, and a betrothed woman (arusah).
  • A child of a single woman whose father is unknown is what the Gemara calls a shetuki, from "to be silent": when he calls someone "father", the mother silences him, as we do not know who the father is.
  • From the Torah the child should be kasher, since most men are not forbidden to her and would not make a mamzer. But to preserve the lineage of the Jewish people, the Hakhamim made a stringency that a shetuki is a safek mamzer.
  • This applies where she says she does not know who the man was, or cannot communicate.
  • If she names the man, even if he denies it, she is believed and the child is kasher: from the Torah the child is kasher anyway, and she has a presumption of kashrut, so we assume the man was kasher too. This holds even if she names a non-Jew.
  • She is not believed to make the child that man's for financial matters such as inheritance.
  • If she says the father was a mamzer, she is not believed to disqualify the child, who remains a safek mamzer.
  • So it is important to know from whom she conceived, so that the child is kasher and not a safek mamzer.

In one line

A single woman's child is a safek mamzer if the father is unknown, but kasher if she names a kasher man.
