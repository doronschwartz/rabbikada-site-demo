Topic: Forgiving others as the key to the Thirteen Attributes and to our own forgiveness

Sources: Gemara Rosh Hashanah; Tehillim 121

Daily Halacha: Yom Kippur, Wednesday 15 September 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Yom Kippur does not atone for sins between people, so before it one must seek forgiveness from anyone one has hurt. The one asked should not be cruel or hard to appease, but try his best to forgive, difficult as it can be.
  • The Gemara in Rosh Hashanah says that after the golden calf Hashem taught Moshe the Thirteen Attributes, said 26 times on Yom Kippur, with a covenant that they do not return empty-handed. Yet we often do not see this.
  • The Gemara does not speak of reciting them: Hashem says that when Israel sin they should "do before Me" this order. We say them to remind ourselves that if we ask Hashem to be compassionate, gracious and slow to anger, we must be so with others. Emulating them is the guarantee.
  • This ties to the two ways Hashem deals with the world: Hanhagat HaMishpat, strict justice, and Hanhagat HaYihud, overlooking. One who deals with others by strict justice and will not forgive draws strict justice on himself; one who overlooks brings himself under Hashem's overlooking.
  • The Gemara reads "Nose avon veover al pesha": whom does He forgive? One who overlooks wrongs done to him.
  • "Hashem is your shadow": as we treat others, Hashem treats us. Before Yom Kippur we should let go of animosity, resentment and pent-up anger, and come to Hashem clean.

Sources quoted

  • Gemara Rosh Hashanah: the covenant of the Thirteen Attributes, "they shall do before Me", and forgiveness for one who overlooks wrongs.

In one line

When we forgive others, Hashem forgives us, which is one of the most effective ways to enter Yom Kippur.
