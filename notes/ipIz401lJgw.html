Sefer: Derech Hashem by Rabbi Moshe Hayim Luzzatto (the Ramhal) Section: Part 1, Chapter 5, paragraphs 2-4 (with a short look at paragraph 5) Shiur: From Dayan Daniel Kada's Derech Hashem class for the Hazak girls' group (Wednesday evenings), Wednesday 21 April 2021. It fills a gap in his Derech Hashem series at Tiferet Eyal, where these paragraphs were taught but not recorded.

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Review

The previous class divided all existence into the physical, heavenly and earthly, and the spiritual, which is either souls or separate beings (nivdalim). The separate beings are of two kinds, forces (kohot) and angels (malakhim), and this class explains what they are. Man is unique, joining a spiritual neshamah to a physical body; an animal's nefesh, its instincts, is the finest part of the physical world.

Paragraph 2: a tradition, not a proof

The physical world and its laws are well known to us; that is science. The spiritual world we cannot picture, so we can speak of it only according to our tradition. This chapter, the Rabbi said, is the basis of Kabbalah. It is not something to prove philosophically: "This is our tradition. This is what we believe."

One of the great principles is that above everything in this world, every chair, table, book or tree, there is a force. "Above" does not mean higher in space; it means beyond. A koah is a potential, and every thing exists because there is a potential above it, which comes down into the physical world. The forces are the roots, and the things of this world are only the branches, tied to one another "like the rings of a chain." This is the Kabbalistic idea of the Sefirot and of Hishtalshelut, the chain.

Why does Hashem not create a table directly? Because it would be too intense. His energy must be diluted, coming down link by link until it reaches the physical world. As the Rabbi explained before, if you dream up a world, it lasts only while you keep thinking of it; everything exists only because Hashem wills it, but through these forces between.

Paragraph 3: an angel over everything

"There is another tradition": over every occurrence in the world there are angels. The force allows a thing to exist; the angel brings about everything that happens to it, growing, withering, health or illness. The Midrash says there is no blade of grass without an angel above it telling it to grow. That is why, whenever the Torah speaks of angels, something is changing.

Asked about the Rambam, who does not speak of Kabbalah, the Rabbi pointed out that the Rambam himself counts ten levels of angels, each higher than the other, so he too holds that there must be some gap between Hashem and the physical world.

So the real existence of everything, and every change in it, is in the forces above; this world follows from them. The Ramhal's word is "ne'etak", copied: everything here is a reflection of what is above. Hashem's providence works on the forces, and the change comes down to the physical. If Hashem wanted a table to become a triangle, He would change its force, and the table would follow. "We have to do our best just to understand these things."

Paragraph 4: the exception is man

All of existence works from above downwards, with one exception: human choice. Because Hashem wanted us to choose between good and bad, He made us independent of these forces. If we were only a reflection of what was decided above, there would be no free will, and the purpose of creation, the yeser tov and yeser hara and Olam HaBa would all fall away.

More than that, we are not only free of their influence; we influence them. When I choose to do good, for example to give extra sedakah, that choice ripples upward and changes things above, which then come back down. Only man can do this.

Isn't a man-made table something new? A participant asked. The Rabbi answered that a table is made from parts that each already have a force; when we put them together, the forces of the parts come together on the whole.

From above and from below. Kabbalah speaks of an awakening from above, a gift we did not earn, and an awakening from below, which we work for. On the first night of Pesah there is a great spiritual energy given from above; then it is taken away, and through the 49 days of the Omer we work to reach it again ourselves. A child is taught the whole Torah in the womb and forgets it, so that he knows where he can reach. The gift shows us the level; the work brings us back to it.

The Ramhal concludes that there are two opposite movements in the world: one natural and necessary, from above downwards, and one by choice, from below upwards. How can a physical action affect the spiritual? Because of the chain: when the lowest link is moved, the movement reaches the forces above it.

Paragraph 5: not everything is free

The next paragraph, which the Rabbi only summarised, says that not all our actions are free. Some things we simply do because of how we were raised: most of us do not think twice about not eating non-kosher food. But a hungry person on holiday who walks past a restaurant and still says no, that is a real choice, from below upwards.

This, he said, is the sense in which we are not under the influence of the mazalot: the zodiac describes only the passive part of a person. Participants asked about upbringing and character and whether everything can be traced to what we are born with. The Rabbi agreed that some parts of us are predisposed and said he would think further about the question.

Summary

  • The spiritual world is known through tradition; this chapter is the basis of Kabbalah.
  • Every thing exists through a force above it, and these forces come down like links of a chain.
  • Angels bring about every change in the world; the Rambam too counts ten levels of angels.
  • Man alone is free of the forces and, through his choices, moves the worlds above.
  • Not every act is free; real choice is where we act against what we are used to.
