Topic: Other ways to establish Kohen status, and the self-declared Kohen who is not accepted

Sources: The Gemara; the Rambam

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 20 April 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • In practice we accept those who say they are Kohanim, but where there is doubt there are other ways to establish Kohen status.
  • First, the Gemara says one witness (ed ehad) suffices here, even a relative, and even when two people testify for each other, A that B is a Kohen and B that A is, despite the suspicion of collusion.
  • Second, as the Gemara and Rambam write, one who mentions in innocent conversation (mesiah lefi tumo) something showing he was a Kohen is believed. The Gemara's example: a man recalled that as a child he was taken out of school early to immerse in the mikveh so he could eat Terumah in the evening.
  • So whenever the rabbi or Beth Din sees that someone mentioned it innocently, not to make a claim, he is accepted as a Kohen.
  • Third, where we genuinely doubt someone and do not give him a Kohen's privileges, by saying he is a Kohen he has made it forbidden for himself (shavya anafsheih hatikha deissura). He must keep all the restrictions of a Kohen, such as not going to a cemetery or becoming impure.
  • So if we refused him the aliyah and Birkat Kohanim and he later came to marry a divorcee, one may not marry them, since in his own eyes he is a Kohen.

In one line

Kohen status can be shown by one witness or an innocent remark, and one whose claim is rejected must still keep a Kohen's restrictions.
