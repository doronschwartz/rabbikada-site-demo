Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 301, seifim 33-42 (seif 35 only mentioned) Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 4 July 2024

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

Seif 33: Money sewn into a garment

One may not go out on Shabbat in a garment with money sewn into it. This is only rabbinic (asur, not hayav), since it is not the normal way of carrying money. Why is the money not batel to the garment, like the belt of a coat? Because money is far more important than the garment.

The Rema: some permit going out in such a garment if leaving it at home risks theft, and the custom is to be lenient. The reason is a principle of the rabbis: adam bahul al mamono, a person panics about his money. If we do not let him protect it, he may do something worse, such as burying it, which is a Torah prohibition, or carrying it normally.

The Rabbi set limits. This applies to your own money, which you worked for, not money you found in the street. And it only applies when you need to go out; if you can stay home, you should. The Mishnah Berurah adds that someone who always stays in may draw thieves' suspicion. A bus ticket sewn into a garment, as was done in India and Iraq, is fine, since the ticket is secondary to the garment.

Seifim 34-36: Jackets on the shoulders and double layers

Seif 34: one may go out with a garment folded over the shoulders, such as a sweater tied around them once you have warmed up, since that is an accepted way of wearing clothes.

A jacket over the shoulders. On a hot day you take off your jacket and wear it draped on your shoulders, arms out of the sleeves. Rav Ben Sion Abba Shaul and Rav Ovadia Yosef both permit it, since people do wear jackets that way. Some poskim are strict because it may fall and you will carry it. Strictly it is permitted; on a very windy day there is more reason to be careful. "Many a time when I was walking, I relied on this."

A towel around the neck after the mikveh, common in Israel: Rav Ben Sion is lenient here too, since it is worn like a scarf.

Seif 36: one may wear two or three of the same garment: two shirts, two coats, two pairs of socks or trousers, whether for warmth or to bring a coat to a friend. But the Rema says not to wear two belts, one over the other: once you have one belt, the second has no function. Although the Shulhan Arukh is lenient, one should avoid it.

A gartel. Some poskim forbid walking to shul in a gartel over a belt as two belts. Strictly, the Rabbi explained, we hold that a belt, or even the waistband of trousers, already separates the heart from the lower body for prayer; the Gemara's requirement is for someone in a single robe. The Minhat Yishak (Rav Yishak Yaakov Weiss of Manchester) argues that Hasidim wear the gartel as a kind of uniform, even in the street, so it is a takhshit. The Rabbi liked this and concluded a gartel is permitted.

Seif 37: Gloves

The Shulhan Arukh permits gloves (quoting the Shibbolei HaLeket), then brings a stricter view, which one should follow in the first instance, that they must be tied to the coat, as we do for children, since you will take them off to scratch or when your hands warm up, and carry them. Mishnah Berurah 141: the custom is to be lenient, relying on there being no true Reshut HaRabbim today, but a baal nefesh should be careful.

Why is this concern greater than with jewellery? Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach explains that gloves are much more commonly taken off. So: - Gloves may be worn on Shabbat; they are clothing. - Without an eruv it is better to have them tied to the coat. If not, it is still permitted, since we have no Torah Reshut HaRabbim. - In a true Reshut HaRabbim such as Oxford Street, wear them only if tied. - The same applies to fashion gloves worn as a takhshit, and to earmuffs: ideally attached to the coat or hat.

Seifim 38-39: Sisit and straps

Seif 38: going out in a four-cornered garment whose sisit are invalid, for example one corner's strings have come off, is a Torah prohibition. The remaining strings are not batel to the garment, because you intend to fix them after Shabbat, yet they currently serve no misvah. If you have decided to replace all four, they become ordinary strings and are batel. Sisit without tekhelet are valid and fine.

The Mishnah Berurah suggests checking one's sisit before going out on Shabbat. The Rabbi said one may rely on the presumption (hazakah) that they are still fine, though checking is a nice practice.

Seif 39: straps and laces on a garment are batel to it, even if untied, like shoelaces or a coat belt with one end loose. Only an expensive silk cord is not batel unless tied.

Seifim 40-41: The black hat

"It's really surprising how many people don't know about this halacha which is explicit in the Shulhan Arukh."

Seif 40: ohel. A hat whose brim sticks out a tefah (we hold about 8 cm) may not be worn even at home, because the brim acts as an ohel, a tent sheltering you. A tent needs at least a tefah. The usual black Borsalino brim is about 10-11 cm. Rav Mordekhai Eliyahu had a hat made with a brim under a tefah, which his students wore. The new fashion for narrow brims has no problem.

Why are people lenient? The Mishnah Berurah brings reasons: one opinion holds it is not an ohel if you do not intend it as shelter, though sometimes you do wear it against the rain; and when the front of the brim is bent down, the ohel is not uniform. That second reason does not help a hat with an upturned brim (a "Hamburg"), which the Rabbi said is hard to justify above a tefah. His summary: strictly it is a problem and there are various justifications, but it is certainly better to wear a hat whose brim is bent down, or whose brim is less than a tefah.

Seif 41: blowing off. Some forbid a hat that is not firmly on your head, deep, or strapped under the chin, since the wind may lift it and you will carry it. People holding their hats on a windy day show the concern is real. People rely on there being no true Reshut HaRabbim, but strictly one should make sure the hat is firm or tied.

Why not a kippah too? Because if a kippah blows off you will put it back, since you will not walk bareheaded; a hat on top of a kippah you may give up on and carry. A baseball cap is fine. Wide-brimmed sun hats need a brim under a tefah.

Seif 42: Tefillin found in the street

If you find tefillin lying in the street on Shabbat, in a time of persecution you cover them and leave. Otherwise, if they have straps, put them on and walk home wearing them, a pair at a time. If there are too many pairs, stay with them until nightfall. If you fear staying, carry them less than four amot at a time, or pass them from person to person.

A woman who finds tefillin? The Magen Avraham says she may not wear them home, since for women it is not a way of wearing. The Rabbi said today some women do wear tefillin and a tallit, "whether we like it or not", and halacha follows reality; the question is open. If she can call a man, she should; otherwise she may arguably wear them home. Rabbenu Tam tefillin: the Kaf HaHayim permits wearing them home, since the Arizal held both are true.

Practical summary

  • Do not go out with money sewn into your clothes unless you must go out and fear theft.
  • A jacket draped over the shoulders, a sweater tied round them, or two layers of the same garment are fine; avoid two belts. A gartel is fine.
  • Gloves and earmuffs are best attached to your coat without an eruv; it is permitted otherwise.
  • Check your sisit are intact; do not go out in a garment with an invalid corner.
  • A hat brim wider than about 8 cm, especially upturned, is a problem; make sure your hat sits firmly.
