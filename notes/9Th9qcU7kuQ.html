The halacha

  • Recap: the five grains planted after Pesah are Hadash, forbidden by the Torah until the next Pesah; planted before Pesah and harvested after, they are Yashan and permitted.
  • In Israel it is far less of a concern, since Hakham Ovadiah ensured imported produce is only Yashan, and that is generally what the kashrut organisations there do.
  • In some Eastern European countries spring wheat was planted after Pesah and harvested in early winter, which would have meant storing flour for months, so justifications were put forward.
  • The Bayit Hadash argued Hadash applies only to grain grown by Jews. The Shulhan Arukh rejects this: from the Torah it applies to a non-Jew's produce too, so we cannot rely on it. The Rama's leniency of a double doubt (unknown origin) is very questionable and many disagreed.
  • Ashkenazim were generally more lax, but in the last 20 to 30 years the Ashkenazi kashrut authorities in England have become more particular. Sepharadim always were, as for us it is required by the letter of the law; the SKA ensures its licensees use only Yashan flour.
  • The risk is products such as bread from a non-Jewish factory with a hekhsher only on the ingredients. It is no issue in spring and summer; from about October or November until Pesah, when spring wheat reaches the market, one should follow public notices and consult rabbinic authorities about grain products without a reliable hekhsher.
