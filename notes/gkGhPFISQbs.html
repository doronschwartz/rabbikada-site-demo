Topic: The Jewish status of Ethiopian Jews: the Radbaz and the early approach of the Rabbanut

Sources: Eldad HaDani, mentioned by Rav Saadiah Gaon; the Radbaz, two Teshuvot; Rav Herzog; Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 6 March 2026

The halacha

  • Ethiopian Jews have much in common with us but seem not to have had the Oral Torah, and there is little documentation in Africa to verify anything.
  • Over a thousand years ago Eldad HaDani, mentioned by Rav Saadiah Gaon, described Jewish tribes in Ethiopia; whether they are the same people is unclear.
  • The halachic starting point is the Radbaz, Rabbi David ben Zimra of Egypt, 500 years ago. He wrote that the Jews of Kush are like the Sadducees, without the Oral Torah, but definitely Jewish, from the tribe of Dan. The case was redeeming a captive.
  • In another Teshuvah he raised a concern of mamzerut: their kiddushin is likely valid, since kiddushin is simple, but their divorces are certainly not done according to Hazal, so remarriage after them would produce mamzerim.
  • With aliyah to the State of Israel both questions arose. Chief Rabbi Herzog held one cannot rely on the Radbaz, as circumstances differed and researchers said they were not the same people.
  • So in the 1950s to 1970s they underwent a giyur lehumra, a conversion out of stringency: acceptance of misvot, immersion in the mikveh, and for men already circumcised hatafat dam berit.
  • One posek insisted on calling it a giyur lekullah: if they were not Jewish before, there is no mamzerut, since mamzerut exists only among Jews.
  • This was the view of most poskim, Rav Moshe Feinstein too, and of the early Rabbanut, but it changed, as the next class explains.

In one line

The Radbaz held Ethiopian Jews to be Jewish but raised a mamzerut concern, and the early Rabbanut required a conversion out of stringency.
