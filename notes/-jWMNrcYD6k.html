Introduction and General Principles (Review)

Three Main Categories of Muktzeh

  1. Kli Shemelachto L'Issur - A utensil whose primary use is for prohibited activities on Shabbat
  2. Muktzeh Machmat Gufo - Something that has no use on Shabbat and is not classified as a utensil
  3. Muktzeh Machmat Chisaron Kis - Something forbidden to use on Shabbat due to its valuable status, where people are particular not to use it for other purposes

Note: There are actually nine different categories of muktzeh in total, but these are the three primary ones.

General Rules for Doubts in Muktzeh

  • Doubt about what happened: We are strict (machmir)
  • Doubt about whether we can be lenient: Generally lenient because muktzeh is d'rabbanan

Movement Categories

  1. Tiltul B'Shinui (Moving unusually/peculiarly) - Always permissible for ALL muktzeh in ALL circumstances
  2. Tiltul Min HaTzad (Indirect moving) - More complex, depends on various factors

Seif Aleph: Muktzeh Machmat Chisaron Kis

The Basic Rule (Shulchan Aruch)

Text: "כל הכלים ניטלים בשבת חוץ ממוקצה מחמת חסרון כיס"

  • Generally, utensils can be moved on Shabbat
  • Exception: Muktzeh machmat chisaron kis (expensive articles)

Classical Examples Given

  1. Sakin shel shechita - Shechita knife
  2. Sakin shel milah - Circumcision knife
  3. Izmel shel saparim - Barber's razor
  4. Sakin shel sofrim - Scribe's knife (for smoothing parchment and quills)
  5. Koranos shel bsamim - Spice grinder's hammer

Key Characteristic

"כיוון שמקפידים שלא לעשות בהם תשמיש אחר" - Since people are particular not to use them for any other purpose

Prohibition Level

  • Forbidden even לצורך מקומו או לצורך גופו (for its place or for its secondary use)
  • This is the highest level of muktzeh
  • Can only be moved b'shinui (in an unusual manner)

Contrast with Kli Shemelachto L'Issur

  • Kli shemelachto l'issur (like a hammer): Can be moved לצורך גופו (secondary permissible use) or לצורך מקומו (need its place)
  • Chisaron kis: Cannot be moved even for these purposes

Detailed Analysis from Mishnah Berurah

Mishnah Berurah #2: Definition of "Expensive"

Key principle: "מחמת חשש הפסד" - Due to concern of loss/damage

  • Person is particular not to move them unnecessarily
  • "ומקצה אותו מדעתו" - Therefore sets it aside in their mind

Mishnah Berurah #6: Subjective Nature

Critical principle: "ואם הוא אינו מקפיד לא הווה לדידי מוקצה מחמת חסרון כיס"

  • If you don't care, it's not chisaron kis
  • Muktzeh is extremely subjective

Practical Application: iPhone Example

Analysis: An iPhone is expensive, but:

  • Do we move it around needlessly? Yes, constantly
  • We put cases and screen protectors, but still handle it frequently
  • Conclusion: May be kli shemelachto l'issur rather than chisaron kis
  • Primary use: phone calls, texting (forbidden on Shabbat)
  • But we're not particular about moving it around

Credit/Debit Card Example

Two scenarios:

  1. For someone who uses it as a ruler: Not chisaron kis (can move לצורך מקומו)
  2. For someone particular about it: Would be chisaron kis (cannot move)

Demonstrates: Same object, different halachic status based on individual behavior

Rema's Addition: Merchandise

Rule: "כלים המיוחדין לסחורה מקפיד עליהם"

  • Items designated for sale have chisaron kis status
  • Even if the items are not intrinsically muktzeh
  • Example: Suits/dresses sold from home

Extension: Items Planned for Return

  • Bought a suit, doesn't fit, planning to return it
  • Becomes chisaron kis even though intrinsically not muktzeh
  • Reason: Must keep in pristine condition, can't risk staining
  • Set aside like merchandise

Qualification

  • Still subjective - if you don't care about the merchandise (e.g., 99p tissue boxes sold in bulk), not chisaron kis
  • Default assumption: Usually particular about merchandise

Practical Examples and Applications

1. Stamps

Classification: Chisaron kis

  • Primary use: Sending letters (forbidden - can't instruct non-Jew to post)
  • Particularity: Designated for specific use, not used for other purposes
  • Exception: Stamp collections - not chisaron kis because designated as collectibles, not for mailing

2. Passport/Identity Cards

Classification: Chisaron kis

  • Value: Not monetary but extremely important
  • Particularity: Guarded carefully, especially when traveling
  • Note: If required to carry by law, creates complications

3. Business Contracts

Classification: Chisaron kis (when original/only copy)

  • Forbidden use: Business activities
  • Particularity: Filed away, stored carefully as financial documents
  • Exception: If have email copy, printed copy may not be chisaron kis

4. Receipts

Two types:

  1. Expensive item receipts: Chisaron kis (needed for returns/warranty)
  2. Regular grocery receipts: Muktzeh machmat gufo (no use, just paper)

Important: Depends on intention - if planning to return items, becomes chisaron kis

5. Etrogim

Basic rule: Not muktzeh - can eat and smell

  • Primary use: Eating (like lemon)
  • Permissible use: Smelling
  • Exception: If very expensive and particular about it, becomes chisaron kis during Sukkot
  • Merchandise: If selling etrogim, definitely chisaron kis

6. Matzot (Shmurah)

Basic rule: Food cannot become muktzeh

  • Exception: According to Chacham Ovadia - if designated specifically for Seder night, becomes chisaron kis
  • Depends on: Whether you eat shmurah matzah all Pesach or only Seder night

7. Sefer Torah (Pasul)

Classification: Not muktzeh

  • Reason: Can still be used for study/practice
  • Historical context: People used to study from Sifrei Torah
  • Practical application: Can be returned to ark, moved for study

8. Oven

Classification: Usually kli shemelachto l'issur

  • Primary use: Cooking (forbidden)
  • Secondary use: Warming food
  • Not chisaron kis: People move ovens around, not particular
  • Alternative view (Ben Tzion Abba Shaul): Not muktzeh at all if used for storage

9. Chametz Utensils on Pesach

Analysis: Generally not muktzeh

  • Reason: Can be used for cold, non-fatty foods temporarily
  • Exception: If person is particular and never uses them, becomes chisaron kis or muktzeh machmat gufo

Seif Bet: Size and Weight Don't Matter

Rule: "כל כלי אפילו גדול וכבוד הרבה לא יתבטל שם כלי ממנו"

  • Any utensil, even if big and heavy, doesn't lose its status as a utensil
  • Can still be moved on Shabbat if there's permissible use
  • Even if requires multiple people to carry

Seif Gimmel: Kli Shemelachto L'Issur

Basic Definition

"כלי שמלאכתו לאיסור מותר לטלטלו" - Utensil whose primary use is forbidden, permitted to move it

Two Permitted Scenarios

1. L'Tzorech Gufo (For its secondary use)

Examples given:

  • Kornas (hammer): Primary use - building; Secondary use - cracking nuts
  • Kardom (axe): Primary use - chopping wood; Secondary use - cutting large cakes

2. L'Tzorech Mekomo (For its place)

  • Need to use the space where the utensil is placed
  • Can move it anywhere after picking it up

Prohibition: Protecting the Item

"אבל מחמה לצל אסור" - But from sun to shade is forbidden

  • Cannot move to protect from:
  • Theft
  • Breaking
  • General damage

Advanced Concepts in Kli Shemelachto L'Issur

Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach's Innovation: Enjoyment as L'Tzorech Gufo

Revolutionary concept: Reading for pleasure counts as l'tzorech gufo

Example: Watch/Car catalog

  • Primary use: Information for purchasing (forbidden)
  • Secondary use: Enjoyment of beautiful designs
  • Application: Can read catalog for pleasure, not for purchasing decisions

Key distinction:

  • Still muktzeh (kli shemelachto l'issur status)
  • But permitted l'tzorech gufo for enjoyment
  • Must put down immediately after use

Condition: No Alternative Available

Rule: Can only use kli shemelachto l'issur when no alternative exists

  • Example: Can use hammer for nuts only if no nutcracker available
  • Mahril: "ומהרי"ל שאין לו כלי להיתר מותר להשתמש בזה"

Movement Rules After Use

Principle: Once picked up b'heter, remains permitted

  • Can return item to original place as part of same use
  • If put down and finished using, cannot move again unless:
  • Need to use it again
  • Need its place (l'tzorech mekomo)

Example: Office scissors for grapes

  1. Go get scissors from office
  2. Cut grapes in kitchen
  3. Can return to office as part of same use
  4. If put down in kitchen and finished, cannot move unless need the space

Key Principles and Takeaways

Subjective Nature of Muktzeh

  • Most important concept: Muktzeh classification depends on individual behavior
  • Same item can have different status for different people
  • Must consider: How do I treat this item during the week?

Hierarchy of Muktzeh Levels

  1. Muktzeh machmat chisaron kis: Cannot move even l'tzorech gufo or l'tzorech mekomo
  2. Kli shemelachto l'issur: Can move l'tzorech gufo and l'tzorech mekomo
  3. Kli shemelachto l'heter: Can move freely (not covered in this shiur)

Practical Application Method

For any questionable item, ask:

  1. Is it a utensil (kli) or not?
  2. What is its primary use?
  3. How particular am I about this item?
  4. Do I use it for other purposes?
  5. How do I treat it during the week?

Universal Principle: Tiltul B'Shinui

  • Always available: All muktzeh can be moved unusually
  • Examples: Push with elbow, move with back of hand
  • When needed: When normal movement is forbidden
