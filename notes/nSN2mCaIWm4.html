Topic: Who needs an Eruv Tavshilin, how much food, when to make it and when it may be eaten

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rama

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Monday 30 September 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Eruv Tavshilin also permits lighting Shabbat candles on Friday afternoon, so everyone needs one, even those who have prepared all their meals in advance or a single person eating out at every meal.
  • In the first instance set aside both a cooked dish (an egg, chicken or meat) and a baked one (a hallah or cake). A cooked dish alone suffices. A baked dish alone is disputed; one may rely on it only after the fact or in extenuating circumstances.
  • The Shulhan Arukh requires a kazayit of each, about 27 grams. The Rama has Ashkenazim set aside a kebeisah, double that, in the first instance, though a kazayit is acceptable for them too.
  • One holds the food, says the berakhah, and states that with this eruv one may cook, bake, light candles and so on.
  • It may be made any time in the 24 hours before Yom Tov, even the night before after nightfall. Do it early on Erev Yom Tov, since leaving it to the last moment is how people forget.
  • The food must remain until one no longer needs to prepare anything on Friday for Shabbat, so it is not eaten before Shabbat.
  • The custom is to eat it at a Shabbat meal, Friday night or Seudah Shelishit, doing a further misvah with it.

In one line

Everyone makes an Eruv Tavshilin, ideally a kazayit each of cooked and baked food, early on Erev Yom Tov, and keeps it until Shabbat.
