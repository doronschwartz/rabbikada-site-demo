Topic: Staying in the sukkah in the rain, and whether rain on Sukkot is a bad sign

Sources: The Mishnah; the Rishonim; the Rambam; the Hida

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Tuesday 15 October 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Ashkenazim eat some bread in the sukkah on the first two nights even in rain. During the rest of Sukkot, rain that makes it uncomfortable or would spoil the food exempts one, even a small amount.
  • Staying in the sukkah in the rain to keep the misvah is, the Rishonim write, maaseh hedyotut, an ignorant practice.
  • The Mishnah compares it to a servant whose master throws the cup back at him: rain shows HaShem does not want the service now, and pressing on is wrong.
  • The Hakhamim call rain on Sukkot a siman kelalah, a bad sign. The Rambam says rain at the beginning of the festival shows HaShem does not want our service.
  • Why "the beginning"? One explanation: only when it was dry on Erev Sukkot and rain starts as the misvah begins; if it rained all day before, it is not a sign. Another: only on the first night (and second outside Israel) when eating is obligatory.
  • Many write that this was said in Bavel or Israel, where rain is unusual. Where rain is common it does not apply and would be ridiculous to treat as a bad sign, so one should not be disheartened.
  • Many Hasidic Rebbes stayed in the sukkah in rain, saying it is only hedyotut when done for show. The Hida rejects this; it is not the accepted halacha, and one should go in and enjoy the meal at home.

Sources quoted

  • Mishnah: the servant whose master throws back the cup.
  • Rambam: rain at the beginning of the festival is a sign of rejection.
  • Hida: rejects the Hasidic practice of staying in.

In one line

In rain one should leave the sukkah, and in a rainy country like England it is not a bad omen.
