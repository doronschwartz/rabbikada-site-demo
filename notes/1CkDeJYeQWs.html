Topic: Women's obligation in sedakah and a married woman giving without her husband's consent

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Wednesday 16 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Sedakah is not time-bound, so women are obligated exactly as men: the yearly minimum, not turning anyone away, and the recommended 10%. A single woman gives by the same guidelines.
  • The Gemara, recorded in the Shulhan Arukh, says a married woman may not give sedakah without her husband's express permission, and we may not accept it from her, except a small amount normal for that family (a few pounds, not a large sum).
  • If she says her husband has permitted it, she is believed.
  • The poskim say that even if the husband is failing in sedakah, since 10% is a custom she may not give beyond the small amount without his permission.
  • These halachot belong to a time when women had no income of their own and the husband provided money for the household.
  • Today the poskim write that a woman with her own profession, income or business certainly may and should give from it. Where both spouses earn, all the laws of how much to give apply to each.
  • A woman wholly reliant on her husband's income would still need his consent for more than a small amount.

In one line

Women are equally obligated in sedakah, and a woman with her own income gives freely, while one wholly dependent on her husband needs his consent beyond small sums.
