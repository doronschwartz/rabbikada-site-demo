Topic: A London case of an adopted girl whose mother had remarried without a get

Sources: Rav Ovadiah Yoseph; Rav Yishak Yaakov Weiss (Minhat Yishak)

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 2 July 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Rav Ovadiah Yoseph relied on this in many teshuvot, adding that even those who disagreed would agree today: with assimilation and constant interaction with non-Jews, a non-Jewish father is a real and sometimes strong possibility, so any other doubt, however small, permits the child.
  • In Israel, where the majority is Jewish, this is harder; outside Israel almost every community lives among a non-Jewish majority.
  • The London case: a couple adopted a girl and raised her Jewish. When she came to marry, it emerged her biological mother was Jewish, had been properly married, divorced only civilly without a get, and gave this baby up for adoption. Seemingly the girl was a mamzer.
  • Traced, the mother said the father was an American pilot she met in London, and that he was Jewish. The question went to the London Beit Din and to the Minhat Yishak in Manchester.
  • After investigation it was clear she could not be trusted; she thought saying he was Jewish would help, not realising a non-Jewish father would be better for the child.
  • One rabbi, later Av Beit Din in Johannesburg, permitted her: disregard the claim that he was Jewish, so there is a doubt he was not; and since the mother kept some contact with her husband, perhaps the child was his. Two doubts permit the child.
  • Rav Weiss disagreed on the second point, since after a civil divorce all relationship is assumed severed. But he permitted her because the great majority (rubba derubba) of such pilots are non-Jews.
  • By either rationale the girl was permitted and married a Jewish man.

In one line

An adopted girl in London was permitted to marry, since her unnamed father, a pilot, could be assumed to be a non-Jew.
