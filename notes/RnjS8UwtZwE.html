Part 1: The Text

היסוד האחד עשר: כי הוא יתברך נותן שכר למי שעושה מצות התורה, ויעניש למי שעובר על אזהרותיה. וכי השכר הגדול העולם הבא, והעונש החזק הכרת. וכבר אמרנו בזה הענין מה שיספיק.

Hashem rewards those who keep the Torah's commandments and punishes those who transgress its prohibitions. The greatest reward is Olam HaBa; the severest punishment is karet. (We've already said enough on this.)

והמקרא המורה על היסוד הזה מה שנאמר: ״ועתה אם תשא חטאתם, ואם אין מחני נא מספרך אשר כתבת״. והשיב לו השם יתברך: ״מי אשר חטא לי אמחנו מספרי״. ראיה שיודע העובד והחוטא, לתת שכר לזה ועונש לזה.

After the Golden Calf, Moshe: If You will bear their sin; if not, blot me out of Your book. Hashem: Whoever has sinned against Me, him I will blot out of My book. Proof that Hashem knows the server and the sinner, to reward the one and punish the other.

Why this verse?

"There are so many verses: Adam was told not to eat, ate, and was punished; Noach's generation; Sodom; Avimelech. Far more explicit. Why go to this specific dialogue? It's a great proof, but there are so many before it. I believe it's the root of this principle." (Answer in Part 4.)

Part 3: Everything Is Just: The Moreh and the Me'iri

Moreh III:17: ומכלל יסודי תורת משה רבנו שלא יתכן מלפניו יתעלה עול בשום אופן, ושכל האסון הבא על בני אדם או הטובה המגעת להם, יחיד או ציבור, הכל כפי הדין ומשפט צדק שאין בו עול כלל. אפילו נדקר אדם מקוץ בידו והוציאו מיד, הרי זה עונש עליו… אלא שאיננו יודעים כיצד הוא הדין.

No injustice from Hashem in any way. Every misfortune or good, to an individual or a community, is just: even a thorn in the hand pulled out at once is a consequence. We just don't know how the judgement works.

The Me'iri (Shabbat 55): "Not a direct student of the Rambam, about a hundred years later, but he shared his outlook and often followed him."

מכרע הדת להאמין שכל מה שיקרה לאדם מטוב ועד רע, הכל מושגח מאת השם יתברך בפרטות: everything that happens to a person, good to bad, is under particular providence. Don't be put off by righteous people suffering and the wicked prospering: אין הקב״ה מקפח שכר כל בריה.

Chazal: אין מיתה בלא חטא ואין יסורין בלא עון: no death without sin, no suffering without iniquity. The Rambam emphatically brings this: the correct understanding.

Principles of faith don't hang on the plain sense of individual verses and aggadot. "Beautiful. Don't build philosophy and theology on individual statements of Chazal." The general rule stands: everything is a consequence of one's own deeds.

Part 4: "Reward and Punishment" Is the Wrong Picture

"I've been using the phrase reward and punishment, but the truth is it's really a Christian idea. We don't really have that concept, and I'll prove it."

"Many religious people think Olam HaBa and Gehinnom are places where you get rewarded and punished. Some have a very crude understanding. And truth be told, some Chachamim believed that: on Monday nights we just finished Emunot veDe'ot: Rav Saadia sees Gehinnom as a kind of fire and Olam HaBa as a kind of light. We struggled with it. I reject it, only because I have the Rambam, who explained it far more deeply."

For the Rambam there's no principle called "reward and punishment". Principle 11 is: Hashem responds to our choices. Principle 10: Hashem knows what we do, and we have choice. Principle 11: there are repercussions to what we do with that choice.

Middah keneged middah

Usually translated "measure for measure" or "tit for tat": "you do X, Hashem gives you Y; you do good, He gives you gold coins." The deeper idea:**

"In this world a person creates his or her own identity."

That's the key point to remember from tonight.

This world

The next world

The world of process: choices, doing, forming identity

No longer achieving, but existing on the identity you made

"A kind of simulation of our real identity, which we can change while we can choose"

Our real identity, fully realised and felt

Shabbat: one-sixtieth of Olam HaBa

"So many explanations, but I think it's simpler. ששת ימים תעבד ועשית כל מלאכתך… וביום השביעי תשבת. The six days are human doing; Shabbat is human being. We stop achieving and just exist in the moment."

"That's why the halachah forbids preparing on Shabbat for after Shabbat: start preparing and you're thinking about next week, not living in the moment."

"Exactly what Olam HaBa is: the world of existing, on the essence you made of yourself here."

Olam HaBa is a shared space between Hashem and us: true existence, who we really are.

The Ramchal, opening of Mesillat Yesharim: שהאדם לא נברא אלא להתענג על ה׳ ולהנות מזיו שכינתו, שזהו התענוג האמיתי והעידון הגדול מכל העידונים שיכולים להמצא. ומקום העידון הזה באמת הוא העולם הבא… אך הדרך כדי להגיע אל מחוז חפצנו זה, הוא זה העולם.

Man was created to delight in Hashem and bask in the radiance of His presence, the greatest bliss. Its place is Olam HaBa; the way there is this world. "Simple as that."

"Our reward isn't a bag of gold coins for our trouble, and our punishment isn't a smack for being bad. It's simply what I've chosen for myself." Sechar and onesh = the repercussions of a person's choices.

Back to the verse: nothing is transferable

"That's why the Rambam chose this verse. Moshe offers: take their sin, or blot me out*. Hashem: Whoever has sinned against Me, him I will blot out. It doesn't work like that. Reward and punishment can't be transferred, because it's who you are*."

"With Adam, Noach, Sodom you might picture a system: they sinned, so God punished. The Rambam says: don't think like that."

"In some circles you'll find: do a mitzvah on my behalf, sell me the value of your mitzvah, say Kaddish for my father and I'll pay you. Each has its own context, but generally the Rambam would frown on all of these. That's not how Torah works."

Part 5: Tzaddik, Rasha and the Fair Question

Start point: כל ישראל יש להם חלק לעולם הבא: "that's how we're created to be." How much of that shared existence we have is commensurate with who we become. "The only thing Hashem won't do is make the choices for us."

Why Chazal make Esav and Yaakov the stereotypes, when "in the Torah Yaakov has questionable moments and Esav good ones": the lentil stew. Esav comes home hungry and tired*: feed me. Yaakov: sell me the birthright. Esav: *הנה אנכי הולך למות ולמה זה לי בכרה. "The whim of the moment. Yaakov works hard cooking and plays the long game, always."

Karet

The greatest punishment, karet: you don't share this existence with God. אין לו חלק לעולם הבא.

The Rambam (Hilchot Teshuvah 8:5): הנקמה שאין נקמה גדולה ממנה, שתכרת הנפש ולא תזכה לאותן החיים.

Sechar mitzvah mitzvah (Avot 4:2)

שכר מצוה מצוה, ושכר עבירה עבירה. "Many commentators try to explain it. The Rambam explains it: the reward of a mitzvah is the mitzvah itself: it creates the person I am. The result of a sin is the sin itself: you've tarnished yourself. There's no you went against My word, so I'll hurt you."

Sinners who aren't resha'im

"Many people commit many sins, even terrible things, but deep down they care: they know, believe, want to do good, but they get stuck in muck. That's not a rasha. They have a vision of Olam HaBa and a place in it; they keep getting stuck."

"Is it fair?" The kind atheist

"I've been asked this so many times. He's so kind, so moral, so good, so clever, but an atheist. No portion in the world to come?**"

Through the lens of…

Answer

Reward and punishment

"Doesn't make sense. Why such a big punishment for a great person?"

Identity

"Makes total sense. How can you share a space with something you don't believe in? You lived your life with an identity that denied any existence beyond you, so you don't live with that existence when you die."

("This, by the way, is what Yom Kippur is all about": we've discussed it in the past.)

"Then why does Hashem get angry?"

וחרה אף ה׳ בכם (in the Shema): His nostrils burn. If consequences are natural, why speak like this?

דברה תורה כלשון בני אדם. "We're meat brains."

"A good parent's anger isn't revenge, nor punishing the child for the wrong. Correct anger says: I have a vision for who you are; I'm trying to mould you; you're not living up to it, and I want you to see I'm upset." So too Hashem: I want you to share existence with Me, and you're not.

"People who serve for a prize treat Hashem as a candy machine, a gold-coin machine. That's where all these segulot come from: say this verse twice on that day, then repeat it, and you'll get money."

The Tuesday of Parashat Beshalach

"Last week you were probably bombarded: say Parashat HaMan twice today, there's a segulah for the Tuesday of Beshalach."

"There is a 'segulah', in inverted commas, to say Parashat HaMan every day: it's recorded in the Shulchan Aruch. But the idea isn't mystical magic. It reminds you that parnassah comes from Hashem. Go to work with that in mind, live like that, and of course Hashem gives you hashgachah pratit, because, as we learnt last week, the more you live with Hashem, the more providence you have."

"But only at this hour, on the ninth hour of the ninth day of the eleventh: it doesn't work like that. Certainly not in the Rambam's world."

Why the Rambam includes Olam HaBa and karet in the principle

"It's not enough to believe in reward and punishment if you think of it in an immature, crude way. That's wrong. It means an identity that exists with Hashem, or one that doesn't. That's what sechar va'onesh mean."

Part 7: Is There Reward in This World?

On the Rambam's account, the reward can only be in Olam HaBa. Chazal say clearly: there's no reward for mitzvot in this world (שכר מצוה בהאי עלמא ליכא).

But we say every morning: אלו דברים שאדם אוכל פירותיהם בעולם הזה והקרן קיימת לו לעולם הבא.

The Rambam: those aren't reward; they're perot, fruits. Help from Hashem to live the best life here: encouragement, guidance, support, so that you're not preoccupied with money or health and can focus on building your identity.

"Other Rishonim disagree. But on the Rambam's view of Olam HaBa, reward in this world is philosophically impossible. You can't pay me off with extra money or extra years. You may get them, to support you in creating the best identity."

Not a checklist

"So easy to fall into the trap: I put on tefillin, learn my Torah, go to my shiur, pray, say my Tehillim: tick, and I get my reward. Of course we keep doing all of it, but with the idea that the more I do, the more I develop who I am."

The Rambam's two axes, everywhere: character (middot, moral perfection) and intellect (Torah and all study).

Why doesn't the Torah mention Olam HaBa?

With Rav Saadia we saw several reasons; Ibn Ezra: people can't grasp it; there are many hints, nothing explicit.

For the Rambam, the question doesn't begin. "If Olam HaBa were a prize: why not mention the prize? Everyone asks." But the Torah is the guide to living well, and Olam HaBa is the natural outcome of the identity it builds. "When you do the mitzvot, that is Olam HaBa. You're making yourself the best person: the greatest reward, full stop. It fits so beautifully."

Summary

  1. Principle 11: Hashem rewards and punishes; the greatest reward is Olam HaBa, the severest punishment karet. Ani Ma'amin and Yigdal give only the first part.
  2. It's missing from Hilchot Teshuvah 3's list because it's the consequence of Principle 10.
  3. Everything that happens is just (Moreh III:17; the Me'iri): אין מיתה בלא חטא. Contrary aggadot are exaggeration; principles of faith don't rest on individual statements.
  4. "Reward and punishment" is a crude, Christian-sounding frame (and Rav Saadia's fire and light). The Rambam: middah keneged middah means you create your own identity.
  5. This world is process, the next is existence: Shabbat, one-sixtieth of Olam HaBa; the Ramchal.
  6. The verse of the Golden Calf: you can't transfer merits or sins.
  7. The rasha lives for the moment (Esav); the tzaddik plays the long game (Yaakov).* Karet = *no shared existence. The kind atheist makes sense once you see it as identity.
  8. Antigonos's peras: not a candy machine; segulot such as Parashat HaMan work by reminding us, not by magic.
  9. This world gives only perot, support for the real goal. The Torah needn't mention Olam HaBa: keeping it is Olam HaBa.
