Topic: Not leaving writing for Hol HaMoed, and writing to prevent a loss (davar haaved)

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; the Taz; the Bayit Hadash; Rav Shelomoh Zalman Auerbach

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 30 September 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Recap: Sepharadim may write normally on Hol HaMoed for the moed; the Rema suggests writing sloppily, so some Ashkenazim use the left hand.
  • First point: one may not deliberately leave writing for Hol HaMoed. One who knows of a Shabbat Hol HaMoed invitation and has time to write the thank-you card beforehand should not put it off; if one had no time or did not think of it, it may be written on the moed.
  • Second point: davar haaved. Any melakhah may be done on Hol HaMoed for something that would otherwise be lost.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules that a rav may write a response to a question brought by a messenger who cannot wait, even if the ruling is not needed for the moed.
  • The Shulhan Arukh also rules, though some Rishonim disagreed, that one may write hiddushei Torah or summaries of one's learning, since otherwise they will be forgotten. The Taz writes that his father-in-law, the Bayit Hadash, wrote Torah on Hol HaMoed for this reason.
  • A mistake found in a Sefer Torah should ideally wait until after the moed, but if there is concern it will be forgotten and never fixed, it may be corrected on Hol HaMoed.
  • Rav Shelomoh Zalman Auerbach writes that a notice about a found object may be written on Hol HaMoed, since the owner may be looking for it now.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: writing a ruling for a messenger who cannot wait, and writing hiddushei Torah, are permitted as davar haaved.

In one line

Writing may not be deliberately left for Hol HaMoed, but anything that would otherwise be lost or forgotten may be written then even if not for the moed.
