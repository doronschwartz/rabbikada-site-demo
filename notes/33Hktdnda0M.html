Topic: Typing, texting, emailing, printing and voice messages on Hol HaMoed

Sources: The Bet Yoseph; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Sunday 5 October 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • According to the vast majority of opinions, typing on a computer is melekhet hedyot: the result looks neat, but it is a regular way of writing. So one may type anything needed for the moed.
  • Writing that is not permanent is permitted, as the Bet Yoseph writes: so one may write on sand, or "happy birthday" on a cake, on Hol HaMoed. Likewise drawing letters or playing word games on a computer without saving is completely permitted.
  • Texting and emailing about matters of the moed (how are you, shall we meet later, a request for something on the moed) are permitted as melekhet hedyot.
  • Texts or emails not relevant to the moed, such as scheduling an email for after Sukkot about a non-business matter, are forbidden, whether the prohibition is Torah or rabbinic. Work emails and davar haaved will be discussed separately.
  • Printing: some are stricter, since the result is a professional-looking page. There are views either way; for Torah purposes one may be lenient, but print only what is needed for the moed.
  • Hakham Ovadiah writes that recording on tape, or today a voice message, is not writing at all, even if it can be transcribed. So voice messages are completely permitted, even about matters after the moed.

Sources quoted

  • The Bet Yoseph: non-permanent writing is permitted.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: recording is not writing.

In one line

Typing, texting and emailing for the moed are permitted, printing calls for caution, and voice messages are permitted for anything.
