Topic: Whether medicines containing Hames may be taken on Pesah

Sources: The Rambam; the Rosh; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Wednesday 10 March 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Rambam writes that medicines containing Hames may be taken on Pesah if bitter or tasteless, as there is no enjoyment for the palate, even without danger to life.
  • Food unfit for a dog when Pesah begins is not Hames. But the Rosh says one who eats such food gives it importance, making it forbidden for him. So some forbid medicines containing Hames.
  • Most poskim, Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph, Rav Moshe Feinstein and others, hold that taking medicine does not give the Hames importance, so bitter or tasteless medicine may be taken.
  • Hakham Ovadiah is slightly stricter: only for one who is ill, meaning in bed, with flu for example, not necessarily in danger. For a slight headache he is concerned it would be giving it importance.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein permits it for any condition, since one cares only about the medicine, not the tasteless Hames.
  • Hakham Ovadiah writes that most medicines today contain no Hames, so unless one knows otherwise, one may assume so and take them even for a slight condition.
  • Medicines with taste, such as syrups, must be checked to contain no Hames.

Sources quoted

  • The Rambam: bitter or tasteless medicines with Hames are permitted.
  • The Rosh: eating something gives it importance.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph and Rav Moshe Feinstein: medicine does not give importance; they differ on minor ailments.

In one line

Tasteless or bitter medicines may be taken on Pesah when ill, and most today contain no Hames, but flavoured ones must be checked.
