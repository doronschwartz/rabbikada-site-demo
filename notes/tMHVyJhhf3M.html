Topic: The story of Kamsa and Bar Kamsa, and seeing the whole picture in halacha

Sources: The Gemara (Rabbi Yohanan on Rabbi Zekhariah ben Avkulas)

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Thursday 8 August 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Tisha BeAv, this Tuesday, commemorates five tragedies, including the destruction of both Temples: the first by Nevukhadnessar of Bavel, the second by the Roman Titus. The Gemara says, astonishingly, that Rabbi Zekhariah ben Avkulas was responsible for the second.
  • The story: a wealthy man in Jerusalem invited his friend Kamsa, but the invitation went to Bar Kamsa, whom he disliked. Despite Bar Kamsa offering to pay for half, then all, of the banquet, the host threw him out.
  • Humiliated that the leaders present did not stand up for him, Bar Kamsa told the Caesar the Jews were rebelling: send an offering and they will refuse it. On the way he made a small blemish, on the eyelid, that is halachically a blemish.
  • The Kohanim wanted to offer it to avoid offending the Caesar. Rabbi Zekhariah ben Avkulas objected that it would set a precedent, and rejected each other suggestion too. The offering was refused and the Caesar sent his army.
  • Rabbi Yohanan declared Rabbi Zekhariah ben Avkulas responsible for the destruction of the Temple and the exile of the people. Bar Kamsa's vengeance caused it, yet the blame is placed on the one who was not flexible.
  • The lesson: halachot must be understood within the whole system and its values, not one issue in a vacuum. Hence the Gemara's stress on shimush, apprenticeship, as knowing halacha differs from applying it.
  • The classic example: the Oral Torah was meant to stay oral, but Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi wrote it down to preserve it, as halacha itself demanded.

In one line

Tisha BeAv teaches us to decide with the whole picture and all our values in view, and not lose the forest for the trees.
