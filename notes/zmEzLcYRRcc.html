Topic: Paper poppies on Shabbat without an eruv: adornment, or secondary to the garment

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rama; the Or Zarua of Vienna

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Wednesday 13 November 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A metal pin or poppy is mutar: though it carries a message (solidarity with the captives, remembering the fallen), it is displayed as an ornament that beautifies the garment.
  • A paper poppy may arguably also be an adornment. Even if it is not, there is another reason to permit it.
  • The Shulhan Arukh and the Rama quote the Or Zarua, of 13th-century Vienna, where the authorities required Jews to wear green circles on their clothes. He permits going out with them even if only attached, not woven in.
  • As the poskim explain, something worn on the garment all the time, not just on occasion, becomes batel (secondary) to the garment and part of it, and one will not take it off and carry it since one must wear it.
  • So a poppy is permitted either way: as an adornment, or, since it is the norm to wear it for some weeks in November, as part of the garment.
  • It must not come off easily; a poppy that tends to fall off is a problem, since the Hakhamim feared one would come to carry it.

In one line

A hostage pin or paper poppy, firmly fastened, may be worn on Shabbat as an adornment or as part of the garment.
