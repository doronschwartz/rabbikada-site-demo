Topic: Whether wearing a Remembrance poppy is a forbidden imitation of non-Jewish practice (hukkot hagoy)

Sources: The Torah ("lo telekhu behukkot hagoy"); the Maharik (Rabbi Yoseph Kolon); the Bet Yoseph; the Rama; the Vilna Gaon; Rav Moshe Feinstein; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Monday 11 November 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The poppy, from the battlefields of the First World War, is worn in November to remember those who fell securing our freedoms. Two questions arise: hukkot hagoy, and wearing it on Shabbat (this Shabbat being AJEX Shabbat). Today's topic is the first.
  • Imitating the practices of the nations is a Torah prohibition, one of the 613 misvot. But it must have limits: non-Jews invented cars, yet we drive.
  • The Maharik, about 600 years ago, brought by the Bet Yoseph and the Rama, holds that it covers only practices associated with paganism, Avodah Zarah. A fashion such as a short suit, with no prohibition, immodesty or Avodah Zarah, may be copied, and so may a poppy.
  • The Vilna Gaon famously held that anything initiated by non-Jews is forbidden, unless we would have done it anyway, like driving a car.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein argues that according to the Vilna Gaon wearing suits, a European non-Jewish custom, might be forbidden, which is why Hasidim dress differently. On this view a poppy might be an issue.
  • Most poskim, Hakham Ovadiah included, rule like the Maharik and the Rama, so wearing a poppy is fine, and there are strong grounds that it is the right way to show respect to those who fell for freedom in the UK.

In one line

Since hukkot hagoy covers only practices tied to Avodah Zarah, most poskim permit wearing a poppy, and it is a fitting mark of respect.
