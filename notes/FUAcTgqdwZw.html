Topic: Whether a Kohen who has killed someone may say Birkat Kohanim, including soldiers in the IDF

Sources: Yeshayahu 1 ("Your hands are full of blood"); the Gemara; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Birkat Kohanim & Prayer, Thursday 23 June 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Another of the six disqualifications: a Kohen who has killed a person may not say Birkat Kohanim for the rest of his life, even after teshuvah and even if it was unintentional.
  • The Gemara's source is Yeshayahu: "when you spread out your hands I will hide My eyes from you... your hands are full of blood". Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh rule so.
  • So a Kohen who, Heaven forbid, accidentally caused a death, for example in a car accident even without negligence, may no longer bless. He need not leave the synagogue during Birkat Kohanim, since he has no blemish; he simply no longer has the misvah.
  • Hakham Ovadiah was asked about a Kohen who killed the enemy in combat in the IDF, and permits him to bless.
  • His reasons: the disqualification applies to killing of one's own accord, not under duress or in self-defence, which the halacha requires. He also discusses whether it applies to killing a non-Jew, which is debated.
  • Above all, soldiers stand up for Am Yisrael and the land, defending millions of Jews from enemies seeking to annihilate them. This is a great misvah, so even intentional killing in battle does not disqualify them.

In one line

A Kohen who has killed may not bless again, unless it was in self-defence or in the misvah of defending Israel, so IDF soldiers may return to Birkat Kohanim.
