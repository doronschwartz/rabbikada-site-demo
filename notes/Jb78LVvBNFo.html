Topic: The meaning of Afikoman and the deadline for eating it

Sources: The Ben Ish Hai; the Gemara and the Tannaim; the Rambam; Rabbenu Hananel; the Rosh; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Thursday 18 March 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • At Safun we eat the Afikoman (the Syrian pronunciation is Afikomin), from the Greek for dessert, since it is the last food of the evening.
  • The Ben Ish Hai plays on the word as Aramaic "afiku minei", take away all other foods: after it one eats nothing until the next day, though the third and fourth cups, water, and tea or coffee if necessary, are allowed.
  • The Afikoman recalls the Korban Pesah, so its deadline follows the Tannaim's dispute whether the Korban Pesah could be eaten until midnight or until morning.
  • The Rambam held one may eat it all night; Rabbenu Hananel and the Rosh held only until midnight. The Shulhan Arukh rules one should be careful to eat it before hasot, since according to the other opinions one has not fulfilled the misvah after it.
  • In England this year hasot is 12:07 a.m. on Mosei Shabbat and 1:07 a.m. on Sunday night, after the clocks change. The Rema adds for Ashkenazim to finish Hallel before hasot too; for us the main thing is the Afikoman.
  • Some stretch out Maggid, but the misvah of telling the story lasts all night. People stay up on Shavuot and Hoshana Rabbah nights without a halachic requirement, yet on Pesah night the Shulhan Arukh rules one must discuss the Exodus until sleep overtakes him, with Midrashim, Parashat Vaera and Bo, or the laws of Pesah.
  • So shorten Maggid if needed to reach the Afikoman by hasot on both nights, and continue the divrei Torah afterwards until morning.

In one line

Eat the Afikoman before hasot, even if it means a shorter Maggid, and continue discussing the Exodus afterwards.
