Topic: What defines forbidden melakhah on Hol HaMoed: the principle of tirha

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 16 September 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • According to the Shulhan Arukh and Hakham Ovadiah, the prohibition of melakhah on Hol HaMoed is rabbinic, so in a case of doubt one may be lenient.
  • On Shabbat the 39 melakhot are clearly defined, and Yom Tov is the same with dispensations for okhel nefesh. Hol HaMoed needs its own definition, before its leniencies (such as davar haaved, a considerable loss) can be understood.
  • The main view in the Rishonim: the 39 melakhot are not automatically forbidden on Hol HaMoed. The test is tirha: whether the work is cumbersome, requiring effort, focus and concentration. Striking a match for no reason, for example, raises this question.
  • The Rishonim write that nothing prevents lending money on Hol HaMoed, though it would be forbidden on Shabbat or Yom Tov: lending 50 pounds and writing it down is quick, with no tirha.
  • Hence the distinction between maaseh hedyot (non-professional work) and maaseh uman (professional work). Professional work generally involves great tirha and is forbidden.
  • This explains leniencies such as davar haaved: work that prevents a great loss is not considered tirha. There are other views, but this is the main one.
  • On the view that the prohibition is from the Torah, one must say the Hakhamim were given the power to define its nature.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh and Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: the prohibition is rabbinic.
  • The Rishonim: tirha is the criterion; lending money is permitted.

In one line

Melakhah on Hol HaMoed is forbidden when it involves tirha, cumbersome effort, which is why professional work is generally forbidden.
