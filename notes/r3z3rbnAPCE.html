Topic: Exceptions to the enactment of Rabbenu Gershom found in the Rishonim

Sources: The Rishonim; the Gemara (Avraham and Sarah); the Rema

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 16 February 2026

The halacha

  • Ashkenazim still keep the enactment today, at least as a minhag. The Rishonim list several exceptions, each with much detail.
  • Ten years without children: some opinions hold a man must then marry another wife to fulfil Peru Urevu and Shevet, as the Gemara learns from Avraham, who took Hagar after ten years with Sarah. Rishonim write that Rabbenu Gershom would allow a second wife here. The Rema brings this view and also those who forbid it.
  • Today, if it were certain that the problem lies with the husband, there would be no point in this.
  • A wife who became insane (nishtatit): one cannot divorce a woman who is insane. If she was well at marriage and later lost her mind, or fell into a coma after an accident, many Rishonim allow a second wife even where the enactment applies, since Rabbenu Gershom did not legislate for such a case. Today he would divorce civilly, remaining halachically married to her, with details to be discussed.
  • Yibbum: if a married man's brother dies childless, the Torah misvah of Yibbum falls on him, and the Rishonim call this something that "fell to him from Heaven", so he need not divorce his first wife. Some opinions hold we do only Halisah nowadays, not Yibbum.
  • There are further exceptions, and the most important one, still used today, comes next.

In one line

The Rishonim allow a second wife despite Rabbenu Gershom after ten childless years, when a wife becomes insane, and in a case of Yibbum.
