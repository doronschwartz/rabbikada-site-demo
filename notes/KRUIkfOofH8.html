Topic: Further approaches to Shabbat in the polar regions, and the advice to avoid the doubt

Sources: The Ben Ish Hai; Rav Bleich; Rav Simhah Bamberger, Teshuvot (letter of 1886); Rav Mashash

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Thursday 11 July 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • So far: keep the times of one's place of departure; count six 24-hour periods; treat sunset to sunset as one long day; or no halachic time at all, which Rav Mashash calls unacceptable. Each has problems, and the Hakhamim never conceived of such places.
  • The Ben Ish Hai suggests following the equator, where day and night are 12 hours, 6am to 6pm. The open question is which time zone to set the clock by.
  • Another approach follows Yerushalayim, the centre of the world, as we find time set by Yerushalayim elsewhere.
  • His understanding is that in practice people follow the nearest place with a Jewish community keeping Shabbat; in some places they went by Hamburg.
  • It is very hard to reach a final conclusion.
  • Rav Bleich cites a letter of Rav Simhah Bamberger in 1886 to his son, who was considering a business trip to Norway in summer. After reviewing the views he advised: why put oneself in doubt about Shema and tefillah even on weekdays, and at the very least do not stay there over Shabbat.
  • One who must be in such a place should consult on the best practice.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Simhah Bamberger: if you must go there for business, do not remain in that country on Shabbat while there is doubt what to do.

In one line

No approach to Shabbat at the poles is conclusive, so it is best to avoid being there on Shabbat and to consult if one must.
