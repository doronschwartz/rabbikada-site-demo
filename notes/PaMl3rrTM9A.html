Topic: How long an Israeli can stay abroad and still keep only one day

Sources: Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the Gemara, Bava Batra; Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul; Rav Melamed

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 8 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A holiday, a work trip or a visit of weeks or a month clearly keeps the Israeli status. The hard question is an extended stay with every intention to return.
  • Hakham Ovadiah was asked about teachers from Israel who went to Brazil with their families for a year or two, fully intending to come back.
  • A view from about 500 years ago held that one who left home with his family must keep two days fully, since he does not know when he will return. Many poskim note that travel was difficult then; today one can simply fly back, so the answer is less clear.
  • Hakham Ovadiah: a stay of a year or more, with or without family, loses the Israeli status even with intent to return, and two days are kept; under a year, one day. The Gemara in Bava Batra makes a year the point at which one becomes part of the town and must pay its taxes.
  • Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul: it depends on whether one has uprooted oneself. One who keeps a home or base in Israel and genuinely intends to return keeps one day even for two or three years.
  • Rav Melamed says that no one would allow one day beyond four years.
  • In summary: under a year with intent to return, the status is kept; over four years it is lost; in between there are many opinions, so each case should be discussed in its details.

Sources quoted

  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: a year or more abroad means keeping two days.
  • The Gemara, Bava Batra: a year of residence makes one liable to the town's taxes.
  • Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul: what matters is whether one has uprooted oneself from Israel.
  • Rav Melamed: no one would allow one day beyond four years.

In one line

Less than a year abroad keeps the Israeli status and more than four years loses it, with the years between a matter of dispute to be asked about.
