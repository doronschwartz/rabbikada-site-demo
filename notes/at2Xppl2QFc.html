Topic: Tisha BeAv as a day of Teshuvah rather than only mourning

Sources: The Rambam, Hilchot Taaniyot; Talmud Yerushalmi, Yoma

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The thought is based on the writings of a contemporary rabbi. We think of Tisha BeAv as a day of mourning, but the Rambam implies otherwise.
  • In Hilchot Taaniyot the Rambam writes that we fast on certain days, Tisha BeAv among them, to awaken us to Teshuvah. So the foundation is Teshuvah, not mourning.
  • This is a novelty: we have days commemorating good things (Shabbat, Sukkot, Pesah), and seemingly days commemorating sad ones. Rather, the Hakhamim did not make days to remember bad things but to awaken Teshuvah, and the restrictions of Tisha BeAv and the Three Weeks stem from that.
  • The source may be the Yerushalmi at the start of Yoma: any generation in whose days the Temple was not rebuilt is not, as usually quoted, as if it were destroyed (passive) but "as if we destroyed it" (active). We are in some way culpable for its not being rebuilt.
  • It is also noted that the Three Weeks are 21 days, like the 21 days from Rosh Hashanah to Hoshana Rabbah, a time of Teshuvah.
  • We often go through the motions: can I shower, is this mourning or not. The essence is to think what each of us can do to bring the Beit HaMikdash back into our lives.
  • So the day is called a Moed: its restrictions come from the duty of Teshuvah, and ultimately it will be a day of good.

Sources quoted

In one line

Tisha BeAv is not only mourning a loss but Teshuvah for a Temple we are responsible for not having rebuilt.
