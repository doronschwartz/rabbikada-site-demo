Topic: Shehiyanu only on a recognisably new fruit, and the custom of etrog jam on Tu BiShvat

Sources: The Ben Ish Hai; the Kaf HaHayim (Rabbi Yaakov Hayim Sofer); Hakham Ovadiah, Hazon Ovadiah, Sukkot; the Gemara

Daily Halacha: Tu BiShvat, Wednesday 12 January 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Shehiyanu on fruit is for a Peri Hadash, a fruit of the new season, and it must also be recognisable as new.
  • So, as the Ben Ish Hai and others write, it is not said on nuts (almonds, peanuts), since one season cannot be told from the last.
  • Nor on vegetables that grow in the ground, which can stay in the ground a long time, nor on dried fruit, even from the new season.
  • The Kaf HaHayim, about 100 years ago, records a custom for women to eat the etrog after Sukkot, especially on Tu BiShvat, as a segulah for children or for easier childbirth.
  • Hakham Ovadiah was fond of this custom. In Hazon Ovadiah on Sukkot he writes of eating the etrog of a talmid hakham, and that his own etrog was divided among his family and among women who had not had children for 8 to 12 years, who were blessed with children that same year.
  • Shehiyanu is not said on etrog jam: the etrog stays on the tree all year (the Gemara explains "hadar" this way), so seasons are hard to tell apart; those who took the lulav and etrog already said Shehiyanu on Sukkot; and the pieces in jam are not recognisable.

Sources quoted

  • The Ben Ish Hai: no Shehiyanu on nuts.
  • The Kaf HaHayim: the custom for women to eat the etrog on Tu BiShvat.
  • Hazon Ovadiah, Sukkot: Hakham Ovadiah on eating the etrog of a talmid hakham.
  • The Gemara: "hadar" means it dwells on the tree all year round.

In one line

Shehiyanu needs a fruit recognisably of the new season, so it is not said on nuts, vegetables, dried fruit or etrog jam.
