Topic: Hanukkah as a battle over the meaning of beauty

Sources: Mishlei ("Sheker hahen vehevel hayofi"); the Hida; the Gemara (Shimon HaSaddik and Alexander of Macedon); the Torah on the priestly garments

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Torah admires beauty: Yoseph, Rahel, Sarah and Esther are all described as beautiful. Yet Mishlei says "charm is false and beauty is vain; a woman who fears Hashem is to be praised".
  • The Hida explains: charm and beauty are "sheker" when they lie, when a beautiful exterior does not reflect the interior. The face (panim) should reflect what is inside (penim). When there is fear of Hashem within, the outer beauty too should be praised.
  • Hanukkah was an ideological battle with Greek hedonism and worship of the body. We owe the Greeks gymnastics and physical fitness, and Jews were attracted and became Hellenised (the Oxford Dictionary defines Hellenism as embracing Greek culture, especially a Jew).
  • Our answer: beauty and physical enjoyment are fine when they reflect something deeper.
  • The Gemara tells that when Alexander of Macedon came to Jerusalem, Shimon HaSaddik the Kohen Gadol went out to him in the priestly garments, made "lekhavod uletifaret", to show that our beauty serves the worship of Hashem.
  • So although one candle each night fulfils the misvah, every community adds a candle each night: Hanukkah is about beautifying misvot and giving them an inner dimension.

In one line

Hanukkah teaches that beauty is admirable only when it reflects something within.
