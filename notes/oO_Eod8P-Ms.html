Topic: Which forbidden unions produce a mamzer

Sources: Mishnah Kiddushin (Rabbi Akiva, Shimon HaTimni, Rabbi Yehoshua)

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 7 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Mishnah in Kiddushin gives a principle agreed by all the Tannaim: a mamzer comes from a union in which kiddushin cannot take effect (eino tofes).
  • Some forbidden marriages take effect. A Kohen who marries a divorcee with huppah and kiddushin is married, and she would need a get to remarry, though every relation is a prohibition. Their child is not a mamzer.
  • Others are not recognised at all, the classic case being a Jew and a non-Jew: strictly a Jewish woman who married a non-Jew needs no get.
  • The Torah has three main levels of punishment for forbidden relations: execution by the court (such as adultery), karet (spiritual death), and a negative prohibition punished by lashes.
  • Rabbi Akiva: any negative prohibition prevents kiddushin and produces a mamzer. Shimon HaTimni: only unions punishable by karet. Rabbi Yehoshua: only those punishable by execution.
  • The Mishnah rules like Shimon HaTimni, accepted by all: a union carrying karet or the death penalty (which includes karet) cannot take effect, and its child is a mamzer.
  • This fits the meaning of the word: where the Torah recognises the marriage, even wrongly made, it is not wholly foreign; where it cannot take effect, the union is completely foreign.
  • Examples and exceptions follow.

In one line

A mamzer is born from a union punishable by karet or death, where kiddushin cannot take effect.
