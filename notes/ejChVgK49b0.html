Topic: Where the custom of visiting the cemetery before Rosh Hashanah comes from

Sources: The Gemara on public fasts; Tosafot; the Rema; the Kol Bo; Parashat Shofetim

Daily Halacha: Elul, Tuesday 24 September 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara records going out to the cemetery on a public fast, for two reasons: to declare "we are as the dead before You", a reflection that humbles us; and so that the dead will intercede for us.
  • The difference between the reasons is a non-Jewish cemetery: for reflection it would serve; for intercession it would not.
  • Tosafot explains the custom of going to the cemetery on Tisha BeAv this way. The Rema records it as the Ashkenazi custom on Tisha BeAv after midday, even to a non-Jewish cemetery if there is no Jewish one nearby. The Shulhan Arukh does not record it.
  • The Kol Bo (about 700 years ago) extended it to Erev Rosh Hashanah, when thinking of the new year and the end of life awakens a person. Again only the Rema records this, not the Shulhan Arukh.
  • Some Sefaradi communities (Alexandria and Egypt, for example) went on Erev Yom Kippur. Some poskim were surprised, since Erev Yom Kippur is almost a festive day of eating, and suggested other days of the Ten Days of Teshuvah. The Spanish and Portuguese go on the Sunday before Rosh Hashanah. Among Sefaradim it is not a widely established minhag.
  • One must never pray to the dead, which the Torah forbids in Parashat Shofetim. We pray to HaShem in the merit of those buried there, or ask them to bring our prayers before HaShem.

In one line

Visiting the cemetery before Rosh Hashanah is an Ashkenazi custom of about 700 years, grown from the Gemara's fast-day practice, meant to awaken teshuvah; we never pray to the dead themselves.
