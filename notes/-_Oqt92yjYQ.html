Topic: Further views on a non-Jewish donor, the child's status, and practice today

Sources: Rav Moshe Feinstein; Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Rav Moshe Feinstein permits a non-Jewish donor where there is real need (sorekh), since the concerns of a Jewish donor do not apply.
  • Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach agreed it is halachically permitted, but held it wrong hashkafically to bring non-Jewish lineage into the Jewish people in the first instance.
  • Both agree that if it was done the child is completely kasher. A girl born of a non-Jewish father and a Jewish mother normally may not marry a Kohen, but here, with no prohibited act, she may.
  • Clear to all: if it is to be done, a non-Jewish donor is far better than a Jewish one.
  • In practice, some rabbanim do not allow it, and others allow it discreetly when the husband has no viable sperm and there is no alternative, if it is verified that the donor from the bank is not Jewish. Some who do not allow it will refer the couple to one who does.
  • For a single woman a non-Jewish donor is halachically far better, with very few issues. But bringing a child into the world without a father raises serious ethical concerns; it is generally discouraged and hard to condone, since a child needs a mother and a father.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: permitted in case of need.
  • Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach: permitted halachically, but wrong in the first instance.

In one line

A child from a non-Jewish donor is kasher by all opinions, even to marry a Kohen, though whether to do it is debated.
