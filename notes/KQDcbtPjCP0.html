Topic: May a Yisrael who is a pesua dakka marry a mamzeret?

Sources: The Mishnah; the Raavad; the Rambam; the Baal HaMaor; the Maggid Mishneh; Shulhan Arukh; the Rama

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Mishnah says explicitly that a Yisrael pesua dakka may marry a giyoret. The Rishonim discuss whether he may marry a mamzeret.
  • The question arises only according to the ruling that a Kohen pesua dakka may marry a giyoret; according to the Baal HaMaor, who forbids that, he certainly may not marry a mamzeret.
  • The Raavad: just as a Kohen pesua dakka "goes down a notch" and may marry a giyoret, a Yisrael pesua dakka, no longer in Kahal HaShem, may marry a mamzeret.
  • The Rambam: a Kohen pesua dakka may marry a giyoret, but a Yisrael pesua dakka may marry only a giyoret, not a mamzeret.
  • The Maggid Mishneh explains: the prohibition of a giyoret is specific to Kohanim and is overridden for a pesua dakka, but a mamzeret is forbidden to all (issura shaveh bakol), and that is not overridden. So for the Rambam a pesua dakka, Kohen or Yisrael, may marry a giyoret but not a mamzeret.
  • The Shulhan Arukh follows the Rambam: a Yisrael pesua dakka may not marry a mamzeret. The Rama, for Ashkenazim, quotes the Raavad that some are lenient.

Sources quoted

  • The Raavad: a pesua dakka may marry a mamzeret.
  • The Rambam, explained by the Maggid Mishneh: not a mamzeret, a prohibition common to all.
  • Shulhan Arukh and the Rama: the Shulhan Arukh forbids; the Rama cites those who are lenient.

In one line

The Shulhan Arukh forbids a pesua dakka to marry a mamzeret, while the Rama cites the Raavad's leniency.
