Topic: The origin of children "stealing" the Afikoman, and whether to keep the custom

Sources: Gemara Pesahim; Rashi; the Rambam; Hok Yaakov (Rabbi Yaakov Reischer); Rabbi Yaakov Emden; Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach; Keter Shem Tov

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Tuesday 28 March 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The massah is put away (Safun) simply so that enough remains for the Afikoman at the end of the Seder.
  • Some communities let children hide the Afikoman and return it only for the promise of a present. The earliest source he found is the Hok Yaakov (17th century, Europe), and Rabbi Yaakov Emden also writes it, based on the Gemara in Pesahim, "hotfin massot", we snatch the massot.
  • Rashi explains this as hurrying through the evening so the children stay awake. The Rambam reads it literally, snatching from one another, among the things done to arouse the children's curiosity, like nuts and lifting the Seder plate; this means playful passing, not stealing.
  • Some poskim defended the custom as part of the enjoyment, with everyone forgiving one another. Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach strongly opposed educating children this way.
  • The Keter Shem Tov of the Spanish and Portuguese community calls it a bad custom: children taught to steal when young will steal more expensive things later. It is an Ashkenazi custom, not ours, and one should refrain.
  • His view: no one really steals, it is hiding. A family with the custom has what to rely on; one without it should probably not begin. Hiding and finding is fine, but it must not be framed as stealing, which is bad hinukh.
  • Children must not negotiate past hasot: the Afikoman must be eaten before hasot.

Sources quoted

In one line

Hiding the Afikoman is fine if framed as a game, not stealing; families with the custom may keep it, but never let bargaining delay the Afikoman past hasot.
