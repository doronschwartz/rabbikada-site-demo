Topic: May non-Jewish builders continue building one's house on Hol HaMoed?

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Thursday 13 November 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Whatever is forbidden for a Jew on the moed is forbidden to have a non-Jew do for him. Building a house is melekhet uman, so the starting point is that non-Jewish builders may not build it.
  • But one may give a non-Jew work before the festival on kablanut, not sekhirut. Sekhirut (sekhir yom) is payment by the hour or day; kablanut is payment for the project, however long it takes, even if paid in weekly instalments.
  • With sekhirut the non-Jew works for you, so it is forbidden. With kablanut, adaata denafshei avid: he decides when and how to work, so it is permitted.
  • Classic example: one may take a suit to a non-Jewish dry cleaner an hour before Sukkot or Pesah, paying a fixed sum; when he does the work is up to him.
  • Construction is different. Because it is in public view and was usually paid by the day, the Hakhamim forbade it even on kablanut, lest people think the workers are paid daily (marit ayin). This is codified in the Shulhan Arukh.
  • Some poskim say that today construction is normally paid per project, so the concern no longer applies. Hakham Ovadiah does not allow it, following the Shulhan Arukh. One should ask a hakham.
  • Even the lenient view applies only to genuine payment for the project. Paying a builder per week for as long as it takes is certainly forbidden.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: construction by a non-Jew is forbidden even on kablanut.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: follows the Shulhan Arukh.
  • Some later poskim: lenient today, when construction is paid per project.

In one line

Non-Jewish builders paid by the day may not work on one's house on Hol HaMoed, and even a project contract is forbidden by Hakham Ovadiah, though some permit it today.
