Topic: Whom a pesua dakka may not marry, and whether he may marry a convert, even if he is a Kohen

Sources: The Raavad; the Rambam, Moreh Nevuchim; the Mishnah; the Gemara; the Baal HaMaor (Rabbi Zerahyah HaLevi); Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A further reason: the Raavad writes it is to distance from mamzerut. The Rambam in the Moreh hints at this, writing that the wife will be unsatisfied, and she may then look outside the marriage.
  • The prohibition applies only to men; almost all agree, and this is the halacha. A woman who has lost reproductive organs may marry a regular Jew.
  • The Torah bars him from the congregation of HaShem, meaning a regular Jewish woman. As learned in Siman 4, a giyoret, though fully Jewish, is not part of Kahal HaShem for marriage, so the Mishnah rules, accepted by all, that a pesua dakka may marry a giyoret.
  • A Kohen may not marry a giyoret (to be studied in Simanim 6 and 7). The Gemara asks whether a Kohen who is a pesua dakka loses his special status for marriage, and so may marry a giyoret, or retains it. Its conclusion is not entirely clear.
  • The Rambam and the Raavad: he loses the status for marriage and may marry a giyoret. The Baal HaMaor, Rabbi Zerahyah HaLevi, disagreed.
  • The Shulhan Arukh follows the Rambam and the Raavad: a pesua dakka, Yisrael or Kohen, may marry a giyoret. His other aspects of kedushah are for next time.

Sources quoted

  • The Raavad: the law distances from mamzerut.
  • The Mishnah: a pesua dakka may marry a giyoret.
  • The Rambam and the Raavad: a Kohen pesua dakka may marry a giyoret; the Baal HaMaor disagrees.
  • Shulhan Arukh: follows the Rambam and the Raavad.

In one line

A pesua dakka may marry a convert, and by the Shulhan Arukh's ruling so may a Kohen who is a pesua dakka.
