Topic: What one who forgot Eruv Tavshilin can rely on

Sources: The Gemara; the Mishnah Berurah; Hakham Ovadiah; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Tuesday 1 October 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara says the rav of the community makes an Eruv Tavshilin on behalf of those who forgot, did not know or could not make one. One who forgot may rely on it, but not one who knew and wilfully did not (poshea); he may not cook on Friday for Shabbat.
  • The Mishnah Berurah says forgetting at two consecutive festivals counts as negligence, so one cannot rely on the rav's eruv. Hakham Ovadiah disagrees: even a second time, forgetting is not wilful neglect. One who forgot once should be extra careful next time.
  • The Shulhan Arukh gives another option outside Israel, where the second day is kept out of doubt. On Thursday, the first day, one makes the eruv with a stipulation (tenai): if today is the real Yom Tov, tomorrow is a weekday and I may cook for Shabbat; if not, this is my eruv. No berakhah is said. Some disagreed, but one may rely on it when there is no alternative.
  • Remembering only on the second day is too late. The stipulation does not work on Rosh Hashanah, which has a different status.
  • One who neglected it wilfully, or forgot where no one made an eruv for others, may not cook or prepare for Shabbat.

In one line

One who forgot Eruv Tavshilin may rely on the rav's eruv, or on other two-day festivals but not Rosh Hashanah, on a stipulated eruv made on the first day.
