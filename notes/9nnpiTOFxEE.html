The halacha

  • The Rambam calls the laws of Pesah Hilchot Hames UMassah, so we must first define Hames.
  • The Torah forbids Hames for seven days, and "kol mahmeset lo tokhelu": anything that ferments by itself, is fermented by an agent, or is used to ferment other things is Hames from the Torah.
  • Eating Hames on Pesah carries karet, unlike most food prohibitions, which carry malkot.
  • From hasot (midday) on Erev Pesah there is a separate biblical prohibition, tied to the Korban Pesah, which still applies; it carries no karet. The Hakhamim added two more hours, since midday could be misjudged on a cloudy day, so we stop eating Hames at about 11 a.m., depending on the year.
  • Hames comes only from the five grains: wheat, barley, spelt, rye and oats. Rice, potatoes and the like are not Hames, even for those whose custom is not to eat them.
  • Hames nuksheh, where fermentation began but did not finish and which one would eat only under pressure (such as scribes' glue of flour and water), is forbidden rabbinically, to eat and to own.
  • Unlike other foods, Hames that is spoiled and inedible for people is still Hames from the Torah, since it can still leaven. Only when it is unfit even for a dog does it lose the status of Hames.

Sources quoted

In one line

Hames is fermented produce of the five grains, forbidden from midday on Erev Pesah, and it stays Hames until unfit even for a dog.
