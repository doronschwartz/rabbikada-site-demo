Topic: The law of "yakir": a father's word that his child is a mamzer, and its first limitations

Sources: Parashat Ki Tese ("yakir"); the Gemara (Rabbi Yehudah); the Rambam; the Bahag

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 11 June 2026

The halacha

  • Some fathers in Israel have used this law in recent divorce cases to try to avoid paying child maintenance.
  • In Parashat Ki Tese, a man with two wives must recognise (yakir) the firstborn rights of the son of the less loved wife. From "yakir", Rabbi Yehudah in the Gemara learns that a father is believed to identify his firstborn, and even to say "my child is a mamzer". The halacha follows Rabbi Yehudah, and all the Rishonim rule this way.
  • So if child A was assumed to be older, the father is believed that B is the firstborn. He is equally believed that B is the firstborn because A is not his child, which effectively makes A a mamzer. This is codified in the Rambam.
  • In Israel, where the rabbinical courts govern marriage and divorce, some fathers have claimed that one child is not theirs, to avoid maintenance (mezonot) for that child. Many rulings of the Batei Din address this.
  • In short, there are many reasons it is very rarely accepted. First, the Bahag: he is believed only while conferring firstborn status on another child (hakarat bekhorah); simply saying "this is not my child" is not believed. Not everyone accepts this.
  • Second, from the Rambam, though not explicit: yakir applies only to a fetus, not to a child already born, which covers most divorce cases.
