Topic: The custom of Kapparot, and the Misvah to eat on Erev Yom Kippur

Sources: Rav Hai Gaon; the Rashba; the Ramban; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; the Arizal

Daily Halacha: Yom Kippur, Tuesday 14 September 2021

The halacha

  • Kapparot, waving a chicken over one's head and then slaughtering it as a substitute for one's sins, is recorded already by Rav Hai Gaon in Iraq over 1,000 years ago.
  • The Rashba and the Ramban objected that it imitates the pagan and superstitious ways of the nations, a Torah prohibition. The Shulhan Arukh rules with them that the custom should be stopped.
  • The Rema writes that the custom, based on the Geonim and many later authorities, should be kept; Ashkenazim and Hasidim do it.
  • The Arizal called it a good custom, so it spread among the Oriental Sepharadim (Syrians, Iraqis). The Spanish and Portuguese follow the Shulhan Arukh and do not do it.
  • Each should follow his custom, but never superstitiously: the chicken does not absolve sins. It should arouse Teshuvah, like a Korban in place of oneself, and works only together with Teshuvah.
  • There is a Torah Misvah to eat and drink on Erev Yom Kippur, beginning in the morning, not the night before.
  • Although it is a positive time-bound Misvah, women are equally obligated.
  • It applies even to one who will not fast for medical reasons. Any food or drink counts, though it is customary to wash for bread at least at the Seudah HaMafseket.

In one line

Follow one's custom on Kapparot, never as magic, and eat and drink through the day on Erev Yom Kippur, men and women alike.
