Topic: The berakhah on seeing Jewish graves, visiting cemeteries, and related customs

Sources: The Hakhamim; Rambam; the Talmud; Yirmeyahu; the Arizal

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Wednesday 19 February 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A unique berakhah is said on seeing a Jewish cemetery, or even two Jewish graves together. Death raises questions about divine justice, so the Hakhamim instituted "asher yasar etkhem badin": He created and sustained us with justice, we die with justice, and we will be resurrected.
  • The Rambam adds three words found in no text of the Gemara, that He will resurrect us for the life of the world to come; their deep implications are for another time.
  • It is said once every 30 days for the same cemetery. A different cemetery within 30 days takes the berakhah again, and so does the same one if there have been new burials since.
  • The Talmud records the custom of going to the cemetery on fast days, for two reasons: reflection on death and the futility of life, and asking the dead to intercede for us, not praying to them.
  • The berakhah may and should be said even on Shabbat when passing or visiting a cemetery.
  • Passing graves of the wicked or idol worshippers, the Hakhamim said to recite a verse from Yirmeyahu, "Boshah immekhem", not a berakhah.
  • The customs that women should not go to cemeteries and that one should not visit a grave twice in one day are recorded by the Arizal. They are Kabbalistic, not halachic: follow one's community's custom; otherwise both are halachically permitted.

In one line

On seeing Jewish graves one says "asher yasar etkhem badin" every 30 days, even on Shabbat, and the Arizal's cemetery customs follow one's community.
