Topic: The origin of Tashlikh, who observes it, and how it is done

Sources: The Maharil; the Midrash; the Rema; Mikhah; the Hida; Rabbi Hayyim Vital; the Arizal

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Thursday 26 August 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Tashlikh, "casting away", is the custom of reciting verses and prayers by a lake or river on Rosh Hashanah afternoon. It is first recorded by the Maharil, Rabbi Yaakov Molin of fourteenth century Germany.
  • His reason is a Midrash: on the way to the Akedah the Satan took the form of a river; Avraham went into the water and prayed, and HaKadosh Barukh Hu dried it. So too we merit to have our sins erased when we cast them, metaphorically, into the water.
  • The Rema records it for Ashkenazim; the Shulhan Arukh does not.
  • The essence is only the verses of Mikhah, "He will again have mercy on us, suppress our iniquities, and You will cast into the depths of the sea all their sins". This takes a minute. What makes it long is the beautiful prayer of the Hida said afterwards.
  • Rabbi Hayyim Vital describes it as the Ashkenazi custom and writes that the Arizal called it a Minhag Yafeh. Through this it spread to the Middle Eastern Sepharadim, among whom it is almost universal.
  • It is not universal: the Spanish and Portuguese of London and Amsterdam do not do it, as they generally do not adopt customs based on Kabbalah; nor do the Yemenites. The Gaon of Vilna and the Hazon Ish did not do Tashlikh.
  • Earlier sources make no distinction for women; later it is written that it is not really their custom. Women may do it but are not obliged.
  • Some later added doing it by water with fish, a sign of a fruitful year. One must not throw bread to the fish: on Shabbat and Yom Tov one may not feed creatures one is not responsible for.

Sources quoted

In one line

Tashlikh is a widespread medieval custom, not universal, whose essence is a minute of verses from Mikhah, without feeding the fish.
