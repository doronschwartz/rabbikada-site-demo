Topic: How immersion in a heated mikveh on Shabbat fits with the ban on bathing in hot water

Sources: The Magen Avraham; the Noda BiYhudah; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Monday 16 June 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Mikvaot are open on Shabbat, for men on Shabbat morning and for women on Friday night. Yet bathing in hot water on Shabbat is forbidden even if it was heated beforehand, so how is this possible? There are three approaches.
  • First, men may not go to the mikveh on Shabbat, since there is no intrinsic misvah for them, while a woman's immersion is a misvah and she may go on Friday night. This is the approach of the Magen Avraham and the Noda BiYhudah, among others.
  • Second, the Hakhamim forbade hot water but not lukewarm, roughly under 37 degrees, so men and women may immerse in such water.
  • Third, the Hakhamim forbade rehisah, bathing and showering, but tevilah is only dipping in and out, which was never forbidden. By this view one may dip even in hot water, provided it was heated before Shabbat.
  • Mikvaot are generally hotter than lukewarm, and people rely on the third approach. But one must not linger: going in to relax and enjoy the heat would certainly be forbidden in hot water.
  • Hakham Ovadiah writes that Sepharadi women should where possible immerse on Friday night bein hashmashot, after sunset but before nightfall, though normally immersion is after nightfall. Being twilight and for a misvah, it is permitted even by the stricter approaches.
  • In conclusion, the general approach is lenient, that only bathing was forbidden, not immersion. Sepharadi women who can should immerse before nightfall on Friday night, and men should otherwise try to immerse in lukewarm water on Shabbat.

Sources quoted

  • The Magen Avraham and the Noda BiYhudah: men should not immerse on Shabbat; women may, for the misvah.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: Sepharadi women should immerse on Friday at bein hashmashot.

In one line

Immersion in a heated mikveh on Shabbat is generally allowed as a quick dip rather than bathing, with lukewarm water or twilight immersion as preferable options.
