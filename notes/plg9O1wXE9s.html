Topic: The origins of lighting in the synagogue, and whether to say a berakhah in other public places

Sources: The Gemara; Sefer HaIttur; the Meiri; the Ritva; Rambam; the Rivash; Sefer HaManhig; Shibbolei HaLeket; Bet Yoseph; Shulhan Arukh; Rav Shelomoh Zalman Auerbach; Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara's misvah is "ner ish uveito", in each home; neither the Gemara nor the Geonim mention lighting in public or in the synagogue.
  • The first mention he knows is Sefer HaIttur (Rabbi Yishak ben Abba Mari, Marseille, 12th century): the minhag was to light in the synagogue with a berakhah. The Meiri and Ritva record it; Rambam does not.
  • Five reasons: the Meiri, extending pirsumei nisa; the Rivash (Barcelona, 14th century), for those who could not light at home because of persecution; Sefer HaManhig, the synagogue is a mikdash meat, and the miracle was in the Mikdash; Shibbolei HaLeket (Rome), the hazan lived in the synagogue; the Bet Yoseph, for travellers sleeping there, like the old Friday night kiddush in synagogue.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules that a berakhah is said in the synagogue, notable since elsewhere he holds no berakhah on a minhag. The poskim require ten present, even women and children.
  • Lighting at other public events (a Bar Misvah, a Hanukkah party) is permitted, but a berakhah is controversial. Most poskim, including Rav Shelomoh Zalman Auerbach and Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul, say no berakhah, since the synagogue's reasons do not apply.
  • Hakham Ovadiah, treating pirsumei nisa as the main reason, held there is what to rely on to say the berakhah, so that people who do not light will see and hear it.

In one line

Lighting in the synagogue with a berakhah is a minhag of the Rishonim; at other public events a berakhah is disputed, though one may rely on Hakham Ovadiah.
