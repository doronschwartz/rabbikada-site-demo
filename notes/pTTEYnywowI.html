Topic: Why the Rambam lets a pesua dakka marry a safek mamzeret

Sources: The Rambam; the Raavad; the Hazon Ish and other Aharonim; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Recap: a Yisrael pesua dakka may marry a giyoret, and so may a Kohen. On a mamzeret, the Rambam forbids, the Raavad permits, and the Shulhan Arukh follows the Rambam.
  • Yet the Rambam rules that a pesua dakka may marry a safek mamzeret, and everyone asks: she is either a regular Jewish woman, forbidden to him, or a definite mamzeret, which the Rambam also forbids.
  • For the Raavad this is simple, since he permits even a mamzeret.
  • The Aharonim, the Hazon Ish and others, explain that for the Rambam a safek mamzer is not "either this or that" but a new status of its own.
  • He compares this, though it is debatable, to twilight (bein hashemashot): either an unknown that is really day or night, or a distinct status in itself.
  • So a safek mamzeret is paradoxically neither a definite mamzeret nor part of Kahal HaShem, and may marry a pesua dakka. This ties in with what was learned in Siman 4.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules like the Rambam.

Sources quoted

  • The Rambam: a pesua dakka may marry a safek mamzeret.
  • The Hazon Ish: a safek mamzer is a new status, not merely a doubt.

In one line

Because the Rambam treats a safek mamzeret as a status of her own, a pesua dakka may marry her, as the Shulhan Arukh rules.
