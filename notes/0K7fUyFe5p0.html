Topic: The nature of the Omer restrictions and haircuts on Rosh Hodesh Iyar, as barbers reopen after lockdown

Sources: The Ramban; the Gemara; the Geonim; Shulhan Arukh; the Rambam; the Radbaz

Daily Halacha: Sefirat HaOmer, Monday 12 April 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • On 12 April 2021 English barbers reopened after lockdown; can one have a haircut in the Omer, including on Rosh Hodesh Iyar?
  • The Omer days are essentially happy, unlike the Three Weeks. The Ramban sees them as like Hol HaMoed, linking Pesah to Shavuot, which completes the path to freedom begun at Pesah.
  • The Gemara records that Rabbi Akiva's students died in a plague, about 1,900 years ago, for not treating each other with respect; the period is unclear.
  • From the time of the Geonim it became customary not to hold weddings or have haircuts for 33 or 34 days, as recorded in the Shulhan Arukh, though the Rambam does not mention it.
  • Rosh Hodesh: about 500 years ago the Radbaz (Rabbi David ben Zimra of Egypt) permitted haircuts, it being a happy day. The Shulhan Arukh records this custom but calls it a mistake, so for him it is forbidden.
  • Second point: on Hol HaMoed the Hakhamim forbade haircuts so people would cut their hair before Yom Tov, with exceptions, including one released from prison on Erev Yom Tov.
  • Whether that exception extends to those legally unable to get a haircut is for next time, along with Rosh Hodesh, women and children.

Sources quoted

  • Ramban: the Omer is like Hol HaMoed between Pesah and Shavuot.
  • Shulhan Arukh: no haircuts in the Omer, even on Rosh Hodesh.
  • Radbaz: haircuts permitted on Rosh Hodesh.

In one line

Haircuts are customarily forbidden in the Omer, even on Rosh Hodesh per the Shulhan Arukh, but the lockdown may resemble the released prisoner exception.
