The halacha

  • Shemitah is usually discussed as Shemitat Karkaot, letting the land of Israel rest. Shemitat Kesafim, letting our money rest, is a separate concept.
  • Parashat Reeh: in the seventh year every creditor must release his claim, and afterwards may not demand the loan.
  • The release is at the end of the seventh year of the cycle (around Rosh Hashanah), not seven years from the loan. A loan made at the start of the cycle has seven years; one made now has four or five months.
  • The Gemara and Rishonim discuss whether it applies today, with three opinions.
  • The Meiri and the Raavad: not at all, since the Gemara links it to Yovel (from the juxtaposed words "shemitah" and "shamot"), and Yovel does not apply when most Jews do not live in Israel. The Meiri notes that people still claimed loans after seven years.
  • A second opinion: it remains a binding Torah prohibition today.
  • Rambam and Shulhan Arukh, and the halacha: without Yovel it does not apply from the Torah, but it applies rabbinically, in Israel and outside it.
  • So any loan made in the last six and a half years, or in the coming months, cannot be claimed after Rosh Hashanah 5783, subject to exceptions and remedies to be discussed.

In one line

Loans made during this Shemitah cycle may not be claimed after the coming Rosh Hashanah, a law that applies rabbinically today.
