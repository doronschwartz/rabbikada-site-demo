Overview

  • Kosher (tying) and Matir (untying) go together as paired melachot
  • Whatever is chayav for Kosher is chayav for Matir
  • Whatever is derabbanan for Kosher is derabbanan for Matir
  • Whatever is mutar for Kosher is mutar for Matir

1. Recap of Last Week — The Core Machlokot

Origin of the Melacha

  • Kosher derives from its role in the Mishkan
  • Essential requirement according to all opinions: kesher shel kayama (a permanent knot) is needed for the knot to be de'oraita
  • The nature of Kosher is joining two existing things together

Machlokot: Rif/Rambam vs. Rashi/Rosh

Rif and Rambam — Two Ingredients Required for De'oraita

Level

Requirements

De'oraita

Must be BOTH (1) maaseh kesher uman (professional knot) AND (2) kesher shel kayama (permanent)

Derabbanan

Only ONE ingredient present — either a professional knot for even a short time (even half an hour), OR a regular knot tied for permanence

Mutar

Neither professional NOR permanent

Rashi and Rosh — Only One Ingredient: Time

Level

Requirements

De'oraita

Permanent knot (for perpetuity)

Derabbanan

Knot for an extended period of time

Mutar

Knot for a short period of time

Psak Halacha

  • Maran Shulchan Aruch follows the Rif and Rambam
  • Rama rules: must follow the stringencies of both opinions

2. Practical Rulings — Ashkenazim vs. Sefardim

For Ashkenazim (Following the Rama)

  • Must use only a kesher hedyot (simple knot) — not professional
  • Must be for a short period of time only
  • Both ingredients of leniency must be present to be mutar according to all opinions
  • Everything else is assur:
  • Professional knot for perpetuity — assur
  • Professional knot even for half an hour — assur (following Rif/Rambam)
  • Simple knot for a long period of time — assur

Definition of "short period of time" for Ashkenazim:

  • Two opinions brought by the Rama:
  • Beit Yosef: 7 days
  • Kolbo: 24 hours
  • Mishna Berura leans toward 24 hours
  • Bottom line for Ashkenazim: Simple knot, intended to be untied within 24 hours

For Sefardim (Following Shulchan Aruch — Rif/Rambam)

  • Follow the Shulchan Aruch: Rif and Rambam
  • Chacham Ovadia and Sefardi poskim follow this approach
  • Some poskim limit to 7 days practically
  • Bottom line for Sefardim: All regular (non-professional) knots are permitted if you intend to untie within 7 days
  • Some opinions allow even longer, but practically we say 7 days

3. Key Sub-Topics from Last Week

Does It Depend on Your Intention (Da'at) or the Norm?

  • Turei Zahav (Taz): Depends on YOUR intention
  • Example: If tzitzit unraveled on Shabbat — you could tie with intention to untie after Shabbat
  • Biur Halacha (and this seems to be the halacha): Depends on the norm — what is normally done
  • If the norm for this type of knot is permanence, your personal intention to untie within 24 hours does not help
  • Chacham Ovadia seems to lean lenient, but the Biur Halacha's reasoning and proofs are strong

Status of a Double Knot

  • Rama / Ashkenazim: Since we don't know the exact definition of a "professional knot" (kesher uman), most knots are treated as potentially professional → a double knot is considered kesher uman → cannot be done on Shabbat under most circumstances (unless mitzvah is involved)
  • Sefardim (Chida, Chacham Ovadia): A double knot does NOT have the status of kesher uman → it is a kesher hedyot (regular knot) → permitted to tie a double knot for up to 7 days

Derabbanan for Mitzvah Purposes (Se'if Aleph)

  • The Shulchan Aruch permits tying a derabbanan-level knot for a mitzvah
  • Specifically: a kesher uman (professional knot) that is lo shel kayama (not permanent) — since according to Shulchan Aruch both ingredients are needed for de'oraita, having only one makes it derabbanan, and for mitzvah that is permitted
  • This would allow even Ashkenazim to tie a double knot if a mitzvah requires it and it is not permanent
  • The reverse — a kesher shel kayama that is hedyot (simple) for mitzvah — some allow, some don't; this is debated

4. Overhand Knot (New Point — Se'if Aleph)

Definition

  • Taking a single string/rope and tying a knot within the string itself, pulled tight
  • Example: Taking a single tzitzit string and knotting it on itself

Status

  • The Rama (quoting Sefer Mitzvot Gadol) rules: an overhand knot has the same status as a double knot
  • Ashkenazim: Cannot do an overhand knot on Shabbat (potential kesher uman) unless mitzvah is involved
  • Sefardim: Permitted (same as double knot) — allowed if untying within 7 days

Important Distinction — Do NOT Confuse

Knot Type

Description

Status

Overhand knot

Knot tied within the string itself, pulled tight

Same as double knot

Single knot

Two ends simply tied together loosely (e.g., tying top of rubbish bag)

Very basic — mutar for short period, no status of kesher uman

5. Se'if Bet — Sandal Straps

The Case

  • A sandal/shoe made of multiple parts, with straps tied on
  • One strap comes off or majority become loose

Ruling

  • Permitted to put straps back in place — this is not considered metaken mana (completing a vessel / makeh b'patish) because you're restoring something that was already there
  • Cannot do it for the first time on Shabbat
  • Cannot tie the straps back on — the knot at the end of each strap would be a kesher shel kayama (tied for permanence)
  • According to Rama: de'oraita
  • According to Shulchan Aruch: derabbanan
  • You can slip them back in, but not tie them back
  • If it requires significant effort to return the strap → assur — concern you may come to tie it (shema yikshor)

Modern Application — Shoelaces

  • Some poskim understand that inserting shoelaces into shoes for the first time may be metaken mana (completing the shoe)
  • You should not put new shoelaces into shoes on Shabbat
  • If shoelaces were already inserted once and fell out → you can reinsert them
  • If shoelaces tore and you want to replace with new ones → should not do this on Shabbat
  • Some disagree (if shoe was already complete with laces before, adding new ones isn't "completing"), but there is doubt → better not to

6. Se'if Gimmel — The Laundrette Knot

The Case (Explicit Gemara)

  • The laundryman (כובס) would tie a knot on the collar/neck area of a garment to make it look new and fresh
  • Permitted to untie this knot on Shabbat

Reason — שאינו קשר של קיימא

For Sefardim: Straightforward — the Shulchan Aruch gives no timeframe; the laundryman expects you to untie it → the nature of this knot is temporary, even if weeks pass before you actually untie it

For Ashkenazim: More complex — two opinions in Mishna Berura:

  1. Can only untie within 24 hours (difficult reading of the text)
  2. Permitted to untie even after 24 hours — because the entire nature of this knot is to be untied; unlike a regular knot where you choose a duration, here the inherent purpose is removal → it is not a kesher shel kayama regardless of time (opinion of the Levush)
  • This is a strong sevara (logical reasoning)

Practical ruling: Permitted to untie a laundrette knot on Shabbat — certainly for Sefardim; even for Ashkenazim there is room for leniency since this is a derabbanan and there is a sfek sfeka (double doubt)

The Sewn Collar — Assur

  • Some laundrettes would also sew shut the neck of the garment to make it look fresh
  • Assur to open this on Shabbat
  • Why?
  • Rashi: The garment is unwearable without a neck opening → opening it makes it wearable = metaken mana (makeh b'patish)
  • Rambam: Ripping it open = koreiah (tearing)
  • Even if it was previously open and the laundryman re-sewed it → still assur

New Shoes Sewn Together

  • Shoes that come from the manufacturer sewn together (as craftsmen do)
  • Cannot cut/detach them on Shabbat
  • Machlokot Rishonim on whether tefira she'eina shel kayama (non-permanent sewing) is mutar or assur
  • Sefardim are generally lenient — non-permanent sewing is mutar
  • This will be discussed further in the melachot of Tofer (sewing) and Koreiah (tearing)

7. Se'if Daled — Tying a Bucket to a Well

The Gemara's Rule

  • Cannot tie a bucket to a well with a regular rope (chevel) — this would be permanent (kesher shel kayama)
  • Can tie it with a belt (avnet) or similar improvised item — clearly temporary; no one permanently uses a belt for this purpose

Principle

  • Using an improvised item that is clearly not meant for permanent use → indicates temporary intent → mutar
  • Using the regular/expected item → concern it will be left permanently → assur

The Shulchan Aruch's Update

  • Nowadays, buckets are not permanently fixed to wells → not a kesher shel kayama → permitted

8. Se'if Heh — The Bow (עניבה) — Very Important

Rule 1: A Bow Is Not a Knot

עניבה מותר דלאו קשר הוא — A bow is permissible because it is not a knot

  • Why? A knot must be manipulated to tie/untie. A bow is simply slipped in and out
  • Example: A necktie — called "anivah" in Hebrew precisely because it's a bow
  • A bow can be tied for any duration — even permanently — with no limitations

Rule 2: A Knot and a Bow (Rama)

ואפילו אם עושה קשר אחד למטה נוהגים בו היתר — Even making a knot underneath with a bow on top, we practice leniency

  • This is how most people tie shoelaces: single knot + bow
  • The Rama's position (from Darkhei Moshe): Completely mutar — even for permanence (kayama)
  • Reasoning: Each component alone is nothing problematic:
  • A bow = not a knot at all
  • A single knot = has no real halachic status as a "knot"
  • Together they remain mutar

However — Mishna Berura's Qualification

  • Most Acharonim disagree with the Rama's blanket permission
  • They hold: a knot and a bow has the status of a simple knot (kesher hedyot) → subject to the regular time limits:
  • Ashkenazim: 24 hours
  • Sefardim: 7 days

Practical Ruling

  • A knot and bow = permitted on Shabbat with time limitations
  • The Rama's lenient view is still useful as a snif lehakel (supporting factor for leniency) in combination with other reasons

9. Se'if Vav — Tying a Rope Across a Doorway for an Animal

  • Permitted to tie a rope across a doorway to prevent an animal from leaving
  • Even tying on both sides of the doorway is permitted
  • Reason: We are not concerned that when letting the animal out, you will only untie one side and leave the other permanently → both knots are considered not kesher shel kayama

10. Se'if Zayin — Tying a Rope to a Trough and a Cow

With a Strong Rope (Chevel Gardi)

  • Permitted to tie to both the trough and the cow
  • Not concerned one side will be left permanently (the strong rope wouldn't be left there)

With a Regular Rope (Chevel D'alma)

  • Not permitted to tie to both sides — concern that one side will be left on permanently

Exception

  • If one side is already tied (to trough or to cow) and you only need to tie the other side → permitted with any rope — the nature is that you will untie it

11. Important Observation About the Shulchan Aruch

The Shulchan Aruch never mentions a specific timeframe anywhere in the siman. He repeatedly says things are permitted because they are "לאו קשר של קיימא" (not a permanent knot) — but never says "7 days" or "24 hours."

Implication: According to the Shulchan Aruch himself, there may be no fixed timeframe. As long as:

  • It is a simple knot (kesher hedyot)
  • You intend to untie it in the foreseeable future

→ It is mutar, regardless of exact duration

The Pri Megadim and others understand the Shulchan Aruch this way — even if the knot will only be untied in 6 months, if it's a simple knot and you intend to untie it → mutar.

Practically: We do follow the 7-day guideline (per Chayei Adam and Chacham Ovadia), but this broader understanding is worth bearing in mind.

12. Additional Points from the Rambam

  • Animal food: Permitted to make simple knots with animal food — no one makes a kesher shel kayama with food items
  • Making a rope: Weaving/braiding a rope on Shabbat = chayav mishum Kosher — even though you're not tying two things together, the entire nature of a rope is for tying, so creating one is part of the melacha of Kosher

13. Practical Applications

A. Shoes

  • Knot and bow on shoes: Permitted — subject to time limits
  • Ashkenazim: Plan to untie within 24 hours
  • Sefardim: At least 7 days (perhaps longer)
  • Shabbat shoes that you will untie → no problem
  • Double knot on shoes:
  • Ashkenazim: Not permitted — potential kesher uman
  • Sefardim: Permitted for up to 7 days
  • Key principle: Intention at the time of tying is what matters (מלאכת מחשבת אסרה תורה). If you intended to untie within the time limit but forgot → that's fine

B. Snood (Women's Hair Covering)

  • Same rules as shoes
  • Single knot and bow: Ashkenazim — 24 hours; Sefardim — 7 days
  • Double knot: Sefardim — permitted for up to 7 days

C. Sefer Torah (Spanish & Portuguese Tradition)

Spanish & Portuguese Sifrei Torah use a silk ribbon tied with a knot (not a velvet gartel slipped in and out like Ashkenazi Torah scrolls).

Regular Sefer Torah Used Every Shabbat

  • No issues with either a knot and bow or a double knot
  • Even if the synagogue only has Shabbat services (next use = 7 days), this is within the permitted timeframe for Sefardim

Sefer Torah Used Only on Festivals (Chag to Chag or Year to Year)

This is more complex — the knot may not be untied for months or even a year.

With a Knot and Bow — Ample Grounds to Be Lenient:

Three supporting reasons:

  1. The Rama holds a knot and bow doesn't have the status of a knot at all → can be tied for perpetuity
  2. Some poskim understand the Shulchan Aruch permits a kesher hedyot as long as you intend to untie in the foreseeable future (even longer than 7 days)
  3. Letzorech mitzvah (for a mitzvah need) — some opinions allow even a kesher shel kayama if it is a kesher hedyot; keeping the Sefer Torah tight is clearly a mitzvah

With a Double Knot — Weaker but Still Defensible:

  • Cannot rely on Rama's point (a double knot IS certainly a knot)
  • Must rely on:
  1. It is a kesher hedyot (not professional) — even for Sefardim
  2. Combination of opinions allowing longer than 7 days
  3. Letzorech mitzvah — some allow kesher shel kayama for a simple knot
  • Even at worst, this is only an issur derabbanan (not de'oraita) — because according to the Rambam, you need BOTH kayama and uman for de'oraita; a double knot is not uman
  • This is the established minhag of the Spanish & Portuguese community and is justified based on these combined factors

D. Taking Out Rubbish / Nappies (Diapers)

Preliminary question — Can you take out rubbish on Shabbat?

  • Only if it is bothering you:
  • Bin is full and you need to make space for ongoing rubbish → permitted
  • Bin is in an open area and it's unpleasant → permitted
  • Bin is inside a closed cupboard and not bothersome → no hetter; just close the cupboard and deal with it after Shabbat

The Knot Itself:

  • Simple single knot: Certainly fine — no issues
  • Knot and bow / Double knot: Machlokot

The problem: This knot will never be untied — the rubbish will be discarded with the knot intact, possibly lasting years.

Opinion

Ruling

Reasoning

Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach

Assur

You're making a knot with no plan to untie → kesher shel kayama. For Ashkenazim potentially de'oraita; for Sefardim at least derabbanan

Minchat Yitzchak (R' Weiss of Manchester)

Mutar

The essence of Kosher is tying for a purpose of permanence. Here you have zero interest in the knot's permanence — you just need the bag closed until collection. Since you have no care about it remaining tied → not a kesher shel kayama

Practical ruling for Sefardim: Even following Rav Shlomo Zalman's strict view, the worst case is derabbanan (not kesher uman). Therefore, if there is a need (e.g., worry about leaking/smell), one can be lenient. But if possible, a simple single knot is preferable.

E. Tzitzit Knots

  • Cannot tie tzitzit knots on Shabbat
  • Even if just the top knot unraveled
  • Even if you intend to untie after Shabbat — since the norm is for these knots to be permanent, personal intention doesn't override (per Biur Halacha)

F. Chicken Nets

  • You can tear through a net on chicken (as learned in Siman שי"ד — this is hotamah)
  • You can also untie it — the nature of the net is to be opened when the product is used

G. Lulav — Tying the Minim on Yom Tov

Background

  • Sefardi practice: Tie the hadas and arava to the lulav using a strand of lulav leaf, with a double knot or knot and bow
  • We are always told to do this before Yom Tov
  • Important: לולב אין צריך אגד — the lulav does NOT halachically require binding. You can hold all four minim together in your hands and fulfill the mitzvah. We tie it only for beautification (zeh Eli ve'anvehu / hiddur mitzvah)

The Problem

  • The knot is typically never untied — most people leave the minim tied until they are discarded
  • Even if there's a mitzvah element, it's only a hiddur (beautification), not a strict obligation

Opinions

Authority

Ruling

Reasoning

The Chida

Mutar

It is not a kesher shel kayama and is a kesher hedyot

Shulchan Aruch (Hilchot Sukkah)

Assur

Must tie before Yom Tov

Chacham Ovadia

Should not do it

The "mitzvah" of tying is only a hiddur, not a real mitzvah → cannot rely on the leniency of "letzorech mitzvah" to override the concern of kesher shel kayama

Why the Shulchan Aruch is strict: The Machzor Vitri explains — אינו מתירו — you won't untie it. Something made to stay tied and never untied, even if you don't care about it, is considered a kesher shel kayama.

Practical Halacha

  • Always try to tie the lulav before Yom Tov
  • If you forgot:
  • Do not tie on Yom Tov (following Shulchan Aruch and Chacham Ovadia)
  • Simply hold the four minim together by hand — you fulfill the mitzvah lechatchila
  • Someone who did tie has the Chida to rely on b'dieved

Special Case — Person Who Regularly Changes Aravot Mid-Chag

  • If you have an established practice (chazaka) of cutting and replacing aravot during Chol HaMoed every year → you will untie the knot within a few days
  • In this case, there are grounds to be lenient to tie on Yom Tov
  • It's a double knot at worst / kesher hedyot for Sefardim
  • Both the norm (for this person) and the intention are to untie within days
  • Still — better to tie before Yom Tov if possible

Note on Aravot Kashrut

  • Even wilted/dry aravot remain kosher in most cases
  • To be pasul, ALL the green must be completely gone from the majority of leaves (kulo yerakrokit) — this is quite rare
  • Changing aravot mid-chag is a matter of hiddur (beautification), not strict requirement
