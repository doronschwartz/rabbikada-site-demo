Topic: The status of the children of the conversos (Bnei Anusim) in the 1500s

Sources: Rav Eliyahu Mizrahi, Teshuvah; the Radbaz; Rabbi Shimon Duran, grandson of the Tashbes; the Rishonim on a returning penitent

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 19 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • After the expulsions from Spain (1492) and Portugal (1497), many outwardly took on Christianity while living secretly as Jews, sometimes for generations. Do those claiming descent from them need conversion? We trace the Teshuvot from then until today.
  • Perhaps the first to discuss it was Rav Eliyahu Mizrahi of Constantinople (died 1525), author of the foremost commentary on Rashi. He wrote that the children of the Anusim are Jewish and need no mikveh, only a brit milah, which they could not do while in hiding.
  • The Radbaz, Rabbi David ben Zimra of Egypt (died 1573), also ruled them Jewish, from the presumption (hazakah) that they married only among themselves, so they may join the community and marry.
  • He recommends immersion in the mikveh, based on the Rishonim's idea that one who left Judaism and returns immerses as a fresh start. It is a recommendation for one publicly giving up Christian status, not a conversion.
  • Rabbi Shimon Duran, grandson of the Tashbes, wrote at the end of the 1500s that marrying non-Jews was repulsive to the conversos and all married Jews, so their children are fully Jewish and may marry in.
  • So 50 to 70 years after the Inquisition, all the poskim accepted them as Jewish, with immersion recommended. From the 1600s this began to change.
