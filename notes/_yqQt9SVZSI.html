Topic: May a kosher restaurant open on a fast day?

Sources: Vayikra, "Lifnei Iver"; the Gemara's "two sides of the river"; Rav Ovadia Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Asarah BeTevet

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • "Do not place a stumbling block before the blind" forbids, from the Torah, causing another person to sin.
  • Shops may clearly open: food bought may be eaten after the fast. The question is restaurants serving food to be eaten on the fast.
  • The Torah prohibition applies only when the sinner could not sin without you (the Gemara's case of handing forbidden food across a river). If he could do it himself or elsewhere, it is not a Torah prohibition.
  • Even then there is a rabbinic prohibition on helping someone sin. A restaurant would seemingly fall under this, since a diner could eat elsewhere but you are still helping.
  • Rav Ovadia Yoseph and other poskim permit restaurants to open: the rabbinic prohibition on helping applies to helping with a Torah sin. Here there are two rabbinic layers (rabbinic "help" with eating on a rabbinic fast), so it is permitted.
  • Besides, we do not know why a person eats: he may be ill or exempt. A sign reminding people that it is a fast day is the right thing. Kashrut organisations worldwide certify restaurants that open on fast days.

In one line

A kosher restaurant may open on the minor fasts (ideally with a reminder sign), since helping with a rabbinic sin that the diner could commit anyway is permitted.
