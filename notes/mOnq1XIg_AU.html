Topic: The source of the prohibition of melakhah on Hol HaMoed: Torah or rabbinic?

Sources: The Torah; the Gemara, Hagigah; Rashi; the Sheiltot; the Geonim; Tosafot (Rabbenu Tam); Rambam; the Rosh; the Ramban; the Rif; the Bet Yoseph; Shulhan Arukh; the Mishnah Berurah

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Monday 15 September 2025

The halacha

  • The Torah explicitly forbids melakhah only on the first and last days of the festivals, not on Hol HaMoed.
  • The Gemara in Hagigah seeks sources that it is forbidden, for example "et hag hamassot tishmor, shivat yamim": keep the festival seven days, that is, no work for seven days. It brings verses and logical arguments.
  • The question matters: if the prohibition is from the Torah, doubts are treated strictly; if rabbinic, there is more room for leniency.
  • Rashi, the Sheiltot, some Geonim and other Rishonim held it is from the Torah, since the Gemara searches for verses.
  • Tosafot (Rabbenu Tam) asked how the Hakhamim could then make distinctions and exceptions, and concluded it is rabbinic, the verses being only an asmakhta (a hint). Rambam and the Rosh held likewise.
  • The Ramban and others take a middle view: certain activities involving great toil are forbidden from the Torah, while melakhah generally is rabbinic.
  • The Bet Yoseph seems to indicate a Torah prohibition, while rulings in the Shulhan Arukh imply a rabbinic one. The conclusion reached is that the Shulhan Arukh follows Rambam, the Rosh and likely the Rif: rabbinic only. The Mishnah Berurah seems to take a stricter approach. The ramifications follow.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara, Hagigah: verses suggesting work is forbidden on Hol HaMoed.
  • Rashi and others: a Torah prohibition.
  • Tosafot, Rambam and the Rosh: rabbinic, the verses an asmakhta.
  • The Ramban: a compromise.
  • Shulhan Arukh: understood as holding it rabbinic.

In one line

The Shulhan Arukh is understood to hold that melakhah on Hol HaMoed is forbidden only rabbinically, though Rashi and others held it Torah law.
