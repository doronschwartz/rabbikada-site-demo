Topic: The source and laws of annulling vows on Erev Rosh Hashanah and Erev Yom Kippur

Sources: The Gemara; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara says one who wants not to stumble over vows during the year should declare on Rosh Hashanah that any vows he makes are void. This is the basis of Kol Nidrei; the Rishonim write it moved to Yom Kippur because more people attend.
  • Hatarat Nedarim on the day before is a much later custom. It is accepted almost everywhere, though not recorded for the Spanish and Portuguese. Ashkenazim do it on Erev Rosh Hashanah only, Sepharadim on both eves.
  • As the Gemara makes clear, this general annulment works only for forgotten vows or misvot taken on. A vow one remembers is not annulled by it, nor even by Kol Nidrei; it needs a proper sitting before three, explaining one's regret, and one should consult.
  • Strictly, three people suffice. By custom the one asking stands and the three sit.
  • Both he and the three must understand at least the basics of what is said; just reading a text nobody follows does not help.
  • Some Sepharadim do it before ten, since some say a vow or excommunication in a dream needs ten. Strictly three suffices unless one knows of such a vow.
  • A wife may rely on her husband to annul her forgotten vows, which is why women need not attend; vows she remembers she must deal with herself.

In one line

The eve-of-festival annulment, before three who understand it, covers only forgotten vows, and a remembered vow needs a proper annulment.
