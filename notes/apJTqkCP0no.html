Topic: The darkness of Greek wisdom and the hidden light of the Oral Torah, illustrated by the olive

Sources: Bereshit 1:2-3; the Midrash on the darkness of Greece and the hidden light; Midrash Tanhuma, Parashat Noah; Rabbi Jonathan Sacks

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • "Darkness was on the face of the deep... and God said let there be light". The Midrash identifies this darkness with Greece. The light of day one is not sunlight (created on day four) but the primordial light of insight and understanding, which Hashem set aside for the righteous.
  • Midrash Tanhuma (Noah) compares the Oral Torah to darkness: those who study Talmud toil in darkness while learning, but at its end they see a great light.
  • Hanukkah is the victory of the Oral Torah. Greek art, culture and beauty are light only for the eye; real light penetrates beyond what meets the eye.
  • The olive: it looks like mere fruit, yet becomes the oil of light. There is light hidden in everything, waiting to be expressed through our use and understanding of it.
  • The heretic (min) sees things fragmented, through personal place, time and convenience. Natural science gives what, never why; however unified the theory, it leaves nature without soul. So Greek wisdom looks like enlightenment but the Torah calls it darkness.
  • The Oral Torah seems like dark detail, but toiling in it reveals its depth and a world of direction that others cannot see.
  • As Rabbi Sacks used to say, science tells you what, religion tells you why. Hanukkah asks us to seek the why: our purpose, and the Creator behind existence.

In one line

Take the light of Hanukkah into the year by looking beyond the surface, as the olive hides its oil, to the why behind everything.
