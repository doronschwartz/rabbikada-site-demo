Topic: Does "the majority of relations go after the husband" apply to civil marriages?

Sources: Rav Moshe Feinstein; Rav Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 19 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The key question: is rov beilot ahar habaal a halachic concept, or a purely statistical reality?
  • Case one: a Jewish woman married only civilly to a Jewish man, or simply living with him, has a child from a one-off adultery with another Jewish man.
  • If the principle is statistical, what matters is the man she lives with, so the child is kasher. If it is halachic, it applies only to a marriage with huppah and kiddushin, and the child would be a safek mamzer.
  • Many hold, and this was Rav Moshe Feinstein's view, that it is a statistical truth: one goes after the man she is with most of the time, so here the child is kasher.
  • That leniency can create a big stringency. Case two: a woman married halachically to man A is divorced only civilly, with no get, and then marries man B civilly and has children with him.
  • If the principle is statistical, the children are attributed to man B, with whom she lives; since in the eyes of the Torah she is still married to man A, they are mamzerim.
  • If it is halachic, her husband is still man A, so the children are presumed his and are kasher, even though by all logic they came from man B.
  • This is a contemporary dispute with poskim on both sides. Rav Ovadiah Yoseph famously held that even with a civil marriage one need not necessarily attribute the child to the civil partner, and wanted to be lenient in some cases. It is a huge topic.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: the majority principle is a statistical truth.
  • Rav Ovadiah Yoseph: one need not necessarily attribute the child to the civil partner.

In one line

Whether the majority principle is statistical or halachic decides cases of civil marriage, for leniency in one case and stringency in another.
