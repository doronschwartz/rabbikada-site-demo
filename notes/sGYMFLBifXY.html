Topic: Doubts about the conversos' Jewish status in the 1600s

Sources: The Maharitas (Rabbi Yom Tov Sahalon), Teshuvah; the Maharival (Rabbi Yoseph ibn Lev)

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 19 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • In the 1500s the conversos were assumed Jewish; in the 1600s disagreements began.
  • The Maharitas (died 1618) was asked, 120 years after the Inquisition, about two brothers who came from Portugal to live as Jews. One married a Jewish woman and died childless. Must the other perform Yibbum?
  • Yibbum applies only if the deceased was Jewish. If a non-Jewish woman entered the line in those 120 years, there would be no obligation.
  • The Maharitas ruled that within 120 years one would know if a mother, grandmother or great-grandmother had been Christian, so the brothers are Jewish, the marriage was valid and the surviving brother must do Yibbum.
  • Others disagreed: so much time had passed that the lineage could not be assumed intact, so perhaps there is no Yibbum.
  • Already in the late 1500s the Maharival wrote that though the conversos took Jewish wives, over time they had no qualms about taking Christian women as a pilegesh, a concubine, perhaps to keep up their Christian appearance. Whether a pilegesh is permitted at all will be discussed much later.
  • So some poskim in the 1600s held one can no longer assume they are Jewish, as the lineage had become mixed.

In one line

In the early 1600s some, like the Maharitas, still presumed the conversos Jewish, while others doubted it because Christian women had entered their lines.
