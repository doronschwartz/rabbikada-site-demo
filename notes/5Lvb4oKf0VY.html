Topic: Meat and wine in the Nine Days: the custom, Havdalah, children, Shabbat leftovers and health needs

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Thursday 8 July 2021

The halacha

  • By the strict law of the Gemara, meat and wine are forbidden only at the final meal on Erev Tisha BeAv, and the Gemara speaks sharply against eating them then.
  • The Shulhan Arukh records three older customs: from the 17th of Tammuz, the Nine Days, or the week of Tisha BeAv. The widespread Sepharadi custom is from the 2nd of Av until after Tisha BeAv; some keep only the week. Follow your custom: the Shulhan Arukh writes sharply against one who breaches it.
  • The reason is not mourning, since a mourner may eat meat during shivah, but to remember the exile and the Temple, with its animal offerings and wine libations.
  • On Mosei Shabbat the Havdalah wine may be drunk as a continuation of Shabbat and a misvah; Sepharadim do not give it to a child as Ashkenazim do.
  • Children may have meat and wine in the Nine Days, though not at the final meal. Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul allows until Bar or Bat Misvah; Hakham Ovadiah until a year before (12 for a boy, 11 for a girl), with the final year educated to refrain if possible.
  • Shabbat leftovers: give them to children, or freeze them. If neither is possible and they will go to waste, they may be eaten, provided one did not cook extra deliberately. One who normally eats meat at Melaveh Malkah may do so.
  • For medical needs, such as a pregnant woman or someone weak, one may be lenient, as it is only a custom.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: meat and wine are forbidden at the final meal before Tisha BeAv.
  • Shulhan Arukh: the three customs, and that breaching the custom is penalised.
  • Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul and Hakham Ovadiah: the age until which children may eat meat.

In one line

Sepharadim avoid meat and wine from the 2nd of Av, except Havdalah wine, children, unavoidable leftovers and medical need.
