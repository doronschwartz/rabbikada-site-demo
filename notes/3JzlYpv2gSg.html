Topic: The Taz on females, and a third level: removing the desire to reproduce

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Taz (Rabbi David Segal); the Gemara (the hen's crest); the Rama

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Shulhan Arukh forbids directly removing a female's ability to reproduce. But the Taz, Rabbi David Segal, held that following Rabbi Yehudah it is entirely permitted for a female, human or animal, since her reproductive organs are internal.
  • For animals the only concern he saw is causing them pain (saar baalei hayim), which is overridden whenever there is a need. The halacha does not follow the Taz, but his view can be added where there are other grounds for leniency.
  • Beyond direct destruction and indirect destruction (such as a potion) there is a third, lower level: acting so that the creature no longer wants to reproduce, leaving the organs intact.
  • The Gemara's example: removing a hen's crest takes away its desire to reproduce, yet this is permitted. The Rama rules from this, and it is accepted, that putting something on the animal externally to remove its desire is permitted.
  • The poskim seemingly deduce that hormones which do not damage the organs, even indirectly, but remove the desire to reproduce are permitted, certainly for females and even for males, even if permanent.
  • So: direct, Torah prohibition for males; indirect, rabbinic; removing desire, permitted. This has major applications for vets.

Sources quoted

  • The Taz: neutering a female is permitted, as her organs are internal.
  • The Gemara: removing a hen's crest is permitted.
  • The Rama: removing an animal's desire to reproduce is permitted.

In one line

Hormones that only remove the desire to reproduce, leaving the organs intact, are permitted even for males.
