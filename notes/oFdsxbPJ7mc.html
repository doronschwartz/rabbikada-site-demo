Recap: Where We Are

Chapter 21 explains the word עבר, in order to explain ויעבר ה' על פניו ויקרא (Shemot 34:6) — and with it the whole dialogue between Moshe and HaKadosh Baruch Hu in Parashat Ki Tisa.

So far we have used עבר 5, עבר 1 and עבר 3. Now comes the fourth and final interpretation — which uses עבר 2.

The key to it: the Targum (and Rav Saadia Gaon, as we saw) is content to add words into a verse, especially in the context of HaKadosh Baruch Hu. The Rambam says: my preferred explanation is the first one — עבר as diversion, which needs no added word. But if you want to use the Targum's principle, I'll use it too — and add a different word.

Part One — Interpretation 4: "The Voice of Hashem Passed"

The Rambam's text

ומוכרח לפרש באופן של השמטת הנסמך כדרכו של אונקלוס תמיד, שלעתים מפרש שהמלה המושמטת היא כבוד — יקרא, ולעתים שכינה, ולעתים מימרא, כל מקום כפי ענינו —

If we are going to follow Onkelos's interpretive method — supplying a missing word, which he does constantly, sometimes יקרא (glory), sometimes שכינה, sometimes מימרא (word), each according to its context —

הרי שאנחנו גם כן נפרש, שהנסמך המושמט כאן הוא קול.

— then we too will supply a missing word here: not שכינה or כבוד, but קול, voice.

ויעבר קול ה' על פניו ויקרא — וכך יש לקרוא.

This is how you read the verse: the voice of Hashem passed in the presence of Moshe, and it called — the voice called.

וכבר ביארנו את ההשאלה בלשון של העברה ביחס לקול: ויעבירו קול במחנה. לפי זה, הקול הוא אשר קרא.

This is meaning 2 of עבר: a proclamation, a reverberation — just as Moshe "passed a voice" through the camp, stop bringing donations for the Mishkan. On this reading, the voice of Hashem reverberated over the presence of Moshe — על פניו — and the voice is what called.

The reading of the verse — and the double ה'

(The Rabbi skipped paragraph 12 to read paragraph 13 first, then came back.)

הפירוש לפי הקריאה הזו יהיה אם כן כך: ועבר קול מלפני ה' בנוכחותו של משה, וקרא: ה' ה'.

A voice from before Hashem passed — reverberated — in Moshe's presence, and called out: "ה', ה', אל רחום וחנון..."

וכפילות ה' תהא עבור הקריאה, משום שהוטלה בו קריאה — כמו משה משה, אברהם אברהם. וגם זה פירוש נאה מאוד.

This changes how the verse divides. Until now — in interpretations 1, 2 and 3 — ויקרא always went with the next word: ויקרא ה', and Hashem called. That is why we said last week that ה' ה' does not go together: the first ה' is the one calling, and what He called begins with the second.

But now the verse reads ויעבר קול ה' על פניו, ויקרא — and the voice called — so the subject of ויקרא is the voice, and ה' ה' is what it called: a doubled expression, the way one calls out a name twice — משה משה (Shemot 3:4), אברהם אברהם (Bereishit 22:11). "And this, too, is a very fine interpretation."

What this shows us: the Rambam understands all the other interpretations without a double Hashem. Only on reading 4 is there a double ה' ה'. And this is the reading reflected in how we say it in shul — on Yom Kippur, and whenever we say the Thirteen Attributes: ויעבר ה' על פניו ויקרא — ה' ה'..., as if the doubled Name is part of the attributes. (More on this below.)

Paragraph 12: a voice that speaks, a voice that calls

אל ירחק בעיניך ייחוס הקריאה לקול.

Don't think it strange that I attribute calling to a voice —

כי במילים האלה עצמן סופר על דברי ה' עם משה: וישמע את הקול מדבר אליו. (Bamidbar 7:89 — the end of Parashat Naso)

— the Torah itself describes Hashem's speech with Moshe this way: Moshe heard the voice speaking to him. Just as speech is attributed to the voice, so calling can be attributed to the voice. In Naso the voice speaks; here in Ki Tisa the voice calls.

And another example: קול אמר קרא, ואמר מה אקרא (Yeshayahu 40:6) — a voice says, "Call!" and he said, "What shall I call?" Again, a voice in the context of calling.

A side point for those who love dikduk: מִדַּבֵּר

וישמע את הקול מדבר אליו — a very interesting, very peculiar, very rare word.

What it looks like

An ordinary מְדַבֵּר, a פיעל — speaking

What it really is

מִדַּבֵּר = מִתְדַּבֵּר, a התפעל — speaking to itself

The grammar

The ת of the hitpael falls away, and the דגש in the second letter, the ד, replaces the missing ת

The meaning (Rashi)

God speaks to Himself. He doesn't speak to humans — you listen in

This is what the Rambam talks about at length in many places: prophecy is tuning in. There is a beam — a prophecy, a calling, a speaking, a light — coming down constantly, and it is, as it were, talking to itself. It is up to you to come into that beam, to come into that prophecy, to tune in. That is the meaning of מִדַּבֵּר.

Part Two — The Rambam Concludes: "Choose Whichever You Want"

Don't be disturbed

אל יהא מגונה בעיניך קיומם של פירושים רבים לעניין הקשה הזה.

Calm down. Don't be disturbed that I have now given you four ways to read a single verse in the Torah, each using a different meaning of עבר. Perhaps you'll think, what does this verse mean? Stop confusing me! — No, it shouldn't disturb you. There is nothing wrong with that.

ואתה בחר אחת מהסברות.

The Rambam gives us permission: whenever you read these verses — every morning in Shacharit, at Mincha, when we read the Torah, on Yom Kippur — you choose one of the four.

The four options, in the Rambam's words

1. The Rambam's own reading (עבר 5)

אם שיהיה מעמד הגדול זה כולו במראה נבואה בלא ספק, וכל השאיפה של משה — השגה שכלית, שביקש ושנמנעה ממנו, ומה שהשיג — הכל שכלי, בלי שום חוש, כמשפט שבתחילה.

The entire great event is a prophetic vision; Moshe's whole desire was a deep intellectual understanding of God — to see His face; that was withheld, but he did attain God's back. Completely intellectual — no physical sense at all. A complete intellectual journey.

2. Onkelos, simple reading (עבר 1)

או שיחד עם זאת הייתה השגת חוש הראייה על דבר נברא, שבראייתו מושגת שלמות השגה שכלית — כמשפט אונקלוס.

Or, together with that, there was a perception by sight of a created thing: Hashem created a physical manifestation, it passed Moshe, and that is what Moshe saw — a corporeal, physical, created manifestation of HaKadosh Baruch Hu.

3. Onkelos, deeper reading (עבר 3)

אלא אם השגת הראייה הזאת הייתה גם היא במראה נבואה, כפי שנאמר אצל אברהם: והנה תנור עשן ולפיד אש אשר עבר בין הגזרים האלה.

Or this "seeing" was itself in a prophetic vision — like the smoke and fire passing between the pieces at the Brit Bein HaBetarim, which (as we saw) happened in Avraham's mind. The Shechinah passed in Moshe's imagination.

4. The Rambam using Onkelos's principle (עבר 2)

או שיחד עם זאת הייתה השגת חוש שמיעה גם כן, וזה הקול אשר עבר על פניו.

Or there was a perception by hearing: a created voice reverberated in Moshe's presence, and that voice called ה' ה'.

The one thing that matters

בחר איזה דעה שתחפוץ — כי כל המטרה היא שלא תחשוב שעבר כפשוטו... כי ה' יתעלה ויתרומם אינו גוף, ולא שייך בו תנועה.

Choose whichever you want — "I don't care." The whole purpose is that you should not read ויעבר as meaning 1 applied to God Himself — God actually passing — because Hashem has no body, and movement cannot apply to Him. That is impossible.

The Rabbi: if there is one thing we take from this chapter, it is the Rambam's conclusion — there is no physical passing and no physical face, because God has no corporeality.

Question 1 — What does עבר mean?

It depends on the reading: Rambam (1) — meaning 5, diversion; Targum simple (2) — meaning 1, physical passing; Targum deeper (3) — meaning 3, prophetic imagination; Rambam with Targum's principle (4) — meaning 2, reverberation and proclamation.

Question 2 — Whose face?

On reading 1, the Rambam's preferred, פניו is God's face. On readings 2, 3 and 4 — both readings of the Targum and the Rambam's fourth — פניו is Moshe's face, i.e. his presence.

A question from the class: why can't Moshe see God's face?

Because God has no face. "God's face" means the very deep and intimate understanding of God, which is impossible for a human to comprehend. I can look at another human being, study their face, look into their eyes, and come to know them — that is not possible with HaKadosh Baruch Hu.

"What about looking at Him from far?" — There is nothing to look at, because there is nothing physical to look at. That's the whole point.

"Even like a storm, or like lightning?" — No. God is far more encompassing than that. Whatever you try to limit God into is limited — even if it is the biggest thing in the world, it still has a framework; it is still physical.

Question 3 — How do we read ויקרא ה' ה'?

Readings

How the verse divides

1, 2 and 3

ויעבר ה' על פניו — ויקרא ה': "ה' אל רחום וחנון..." — and Hashem called: "Hashem, merciful God..."

4

The טעמים

As Doron pointed out last week, the cantillation fits reading 4 much better: ויקרא carries a זרקא, and ה' ה' follows with a סגולתא — there is a pause after ויקרא. On readings 1, 2 and 3 you would expect the verse to run ויקרא ה' together.

And Doron sent the Rabbi a source during the week: Ibn Ezra already asks this — because Rav Saadia Gaon also read it ויקרא ה', and Hashem called, and Ibn Ezra asks: if so, the טעמים should reflect it.

The Rabbi's assessment: this is a registered, very early question on readings 1, 2 and 3. It's not a huge question, because the טעמים are very, very late, so he isn't too worried. But it is important to recognize that the טעמים are not so "glatt" — not so clean — according to readings 1, 2 and 3.

The Rabbi used to count them on his fingers — and stopped, when he came across this. Chacham Ovadia's position is totally logical: the Arizal held that way, fine — but look at the Rishonim, and all the different ways of reading the verse we've now seen.

If you're convinced the Arizal and the Ben Ish Chai are right, fine, do that — "then you probably wouldn't be coming to these shiurim!" Otherwise: just have in mind that whatever the 13 attributes are, they are the 13 attributes.

Part Five — Loose Ends: עבירה, המעביר על מדותיו, and Doron's Question

עבירה — overreach

Doron raised this last time: is עבירה meaning 4 (overreaching) or 5 (diverting)?

Rabbi Sacks — beautifully, in his usual beautiful way — says עבירה is meaning 4: an overreach. A sin is, essentially, going beyond what we are meant to be doing — beyond the boundaries, beyond where we are meant to be in life.

So meaning 4 finally finds its use. We used meanings 5, 1, 3 and 2 in the four interpretations — never 4. Now we see that 4 is the meaning behind the concept of sin.

כל המעביר על מדותיו מעבירין לו על כל פשעיו

The famous statement of the Chachamim (Rosh Hashanah 17a): whoever "passes over" his middot, all his sins are "passed over." When people insult you and you are not strict in judgment — when you overlook what others do to you — God overlooks what you do wrong to Him.

Which meaning of עבר is מעביר? Certainly not physical passing (1), not proclamation (2), not a prophetic vision (3). It's 4 or 5.

Meaning 4 — overreach

Meaning 5 — diversion

המעביר על מדותיו

Your middot are telling you to take retribution, to stand up, to be angry, to take revenge — and you pass that over; you overreach it. I'm going to ignore those middot

You divert from your middot — from being strict in judgment — like the Rambam interpreted ויעבר

מעבירין לו על כל פשעיו

Your sins, which are meant to be connected to you — we overreach them from you; we say they're not part of you

We divert from you all your sins

The Rabbi

"Probably better is number five" — and that is certainly the best reading of the second half

The lesson: what we're learning gives us utensils to understand many statements. All the verbs we have learned over these 21 chapters can be applied to other statements of the Chachamim. Try to remember and apply what we've learned.

"Very nice, beautiful… probably yours is actually better."

The danger is in the diversion itself: taking someone away from trying to penetrate God's face, towards His back. Once that is done, there is no more danger in contemplating the back.

Summary of Chapter 21

Over three shiurim (the end of the one two weeks ago, last week and this week), we went through the five meanings of עבר and applied them to four interpretations of ויעבר ה' על פניו ויקרא and the whole passage — with ramifications for how to read the verses, how to divide them, the טעמים, bowing, and counting the attributes.

The main thing: do not think God passed Moshe. That is impossible, because God has no body and God has no face.

Part Six — Chapter 22: בא, "To Come"

A much easier, more straightforward, very short chapter. The Rambam continues his theme throughout these chapters: multi-meaning verbs that the Torah uses of Hashem.

Why בא, and why now?

Explanation

The connection to Chapter 21

Rav Makbili

In the same chapter of the Torah as ויעבר ה' על פניו (Shemot 34), about 20–30 verses later, it says ובבא משה לפני ה' לדבר אתו (34:34) — when Moshe came before Hashem to speak with Him. So having discussed one multi-meaning verb from that chapter, the Rambam discusses another: בא.

The Rabbi's own suggestion

See the end of the chapter: the Rambam himself adds a word here. In Chapter 21 he accepted Onkelos's method of adding words; now he shows that he does it too.

The four meanings of בא

הביאה בלשון עברית means four things — "really three," since the fourth is an extension of the third.

Meaning 1 — coming to

נקבע לביאת בעל חיים, כלומר לגישתו למקום מסוים או לפרט אחר: בא אחיך במרמה.

Any creature coming to somewhere. (We'll read it in Parashat Toldot in a couple of weeks.) Your brother came — he literally came.

Meaning 2 — coming into

עוד נקבע לכניסת בעל חיים למקום מסוים. Don't mix 1 and 2: 1 is just coming to a place; 2 is coming into somewhere — Yosef came into the house; Bnei Yisrael will come into the land.

Rav Kapach's observation: whenever the Torah means meaning 1, Targum uses אתא (alef-tav-alef); whenever it means coming into an enclosed place, meaning 2, Targum uses על (ayin-lamed). "All these years I never realized this when I read the Targum — but now, beautiful. Now I'm looking out for this all the time."

Meaning 3 — something non-corporeal "comes": it happens

הושאל לחלותו של דבר שאינו גשמי כלל.

Manoach and his wife ask the מלאך ה': tell us your name, so that when your words come — when we have the baby boy, Shimshon — we may honour you. Words don't come. They don't come to a place or into a place. It means: when something non-corporeal is manifested in a happening.

Yeshayahu mocks those who go to the astrologers, the stars, the avodah zarah: יעמדו נא ויושיעך הברי שמים, החזים בכוכבים, מודיעם לחדשים מאשר יבאו עליך — let them tell you what will come upon you. Nothing is coming onto you geographically: it's a happening.

Meaning 4 — even a privation

עד כדי כך שהושאל להעדר מסוים: ויבא רע, ויבא אפל.

The Rambam holds that there is no such thing as bad, and no such thing as darkness — we'll discuss this much later in the Moreh. Bad, darkness and evil are all privations (העדר), all lackings — not creations. Yet the verse says bad came, darkness came — it happened — even though these things never really "come," since they are only a privation. בא can be extended even that far.

There are even halachic ramifications. A famous piece of Rav Elchanan Wasserman: is darkness a creation, or a privation? When it says ויהי ערב ויהי בקר, does ערב mean a creation of evening and darkness — or simply a privation, a lack of light, a lack of day? The Rambam holds it's the latter: not a creation, but a privation.

When בא is used of Hashem: always meaning 3

בהתאם להשאלה זו — שהוראתה שהתרחש דבר מה בכלל — הושאל גם לגבי תוארו יתרומם: אם לחלות הגעת דברו, או לחלות השראת שכינתו.

Whenever the Torah says Hashem "comes," it can't mean 1 or 2 — He has no physicality — and it can't mean 4. It must be meaning 3, and it means one of two things:

Hashem "coming" means…

Explanation

The resting of His Shechinah

הנה אנכי בא אליך בעב הענן (Shemot 19:9) — at Har Sinai, Parashat Yitro

I am resting My Shechinah on you in the thickness of a cloud. Rav Makbili: בעב הענן = in the thickness and cloudiness of human nature — even though a human is made of חומר, of thickness and corporeality, I will still rest My presence amongst you and upon you

The resting of His Shechinah

השער הזה סגור יהיה, לא יפתח, ואיש לא יבא בו, כי ה' אלהי ישראל בא בו (Yechezkel 44:2)

The gate of the Third Temple will be closed until Hashem "comes in it" — until He decides to rest His Shechinah there

The fulfillment of His word

ובא ה' אלהי כל קדשים עמך (Zechariah 14:5)

חלות דברו — כלומר, קיום הבטחותיו שהבטיח ביד נביאיו. The promises He made through His prophets will come — will happen

The Rambam adds a word

On Zechariah, the Rambam writes: כאלו אמר: ובא דבר ה' אלהי על ידי כל קדשים עמך — as if the verse said, the word of Hashem my God will come through all the holy ones with you — i.e., through the prophets amongst Yisrael. His words will be fulfilled; His prophecies will be accomplished.

Notice: the Rambam has to add words here. It's not so clean; he struggles a bit. And that, perhaps, is the connection between this chapter and Chapter 21. In Chapter 21 the Rambam had his preferred reading of ויעבר, which needed no added word — but he said of Onkelos's method, "I'll accept that." Here he shows that he isn't, in principle, against the method of Rav Saadia and Targum Onkelos of adding words: he does it himself. "Even according to my own interpretation, I agree that sometimes I do need to add in words."

And ביאה?

What about ביאה in the sense of intercourse? The Torah itself uses בא this way — ובא אליה. The Rabbi: that must be meaning 2 — a physical coming into: geographical, physical, but into.

Summary

Chapter 21 complete:

  • Interpretation 4: ויעבר קול ה' על פניו, ויקרא — the Rambam uses Onkelos's principle of adding a word, but adds קול: a created voice reverberated in Moshe's presence (עבר 2), and the voice called "ה' ה'" — the only reading with a doubled ה', like משה משה. A voice can call, just as וישמע את הקול מדבר אליו.
  • The Rambam's conclusion: בחר איזה דעה שתחפוץ — choose any of the four — but never think God Himself physically passed, because He has no body and no movement.
  • Rav Makbili's ladder: reading 1 is the highest (intellect alone); 2 the lowest (sight of a created glory); 3 (prophecy) and 4 (hearing — subtle) in between.
  • The four questions: עבר = 5 / 1 / 3 / 2; פניו = God's face only on reading 1, Moshe's on 2–4; ויקרא ה' on 1–3, ויקרא: ה' ה' on 4 (which the טעמים favour — Ibn Ezra's question on Rav Saadia); and no reading counts the first ה' as an attribute — so, with Chacham Ovadia, don't count them on your fingers.
  • עבירה = overreach (meaning 4, Rabbi Sacks); המעביר על מדותיו = 4 or, probably better, 5; and Doron's reading of והסרתי את כפי: the danger is in the diversion — afterwards you see the back freely, all your life.

Chapter 22: בא = (1) coming to; (2) coming into (Onkelos: אתא vs על); (3) a non-corporeal happening; (4) even a privation. Of Hashem it always means 3 — His Shechinah resting, or His word fulfilled — and the Rambam himself adds a word to explain ובא ה' אלהי כל קדשים עמך.
