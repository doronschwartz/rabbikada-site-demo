Class Notes — Rabbi Daniel Kada

Sefer: HaMaspik LeOvdei HaShem (Rabbenu Avraham ben HaRambam), Chapter 10

Chapter: Perek Yud (10) — Perishut, abstinence

Topic: The proof from the prophets that a person must be willing to step back from his relationships: Eliyahu and Elisha. Then the sign that tells you whether you have reached inner perishut (how much gaining or losing something physical moves your heart), and the fruits of perishut, spiritual and material.

When: Monday 23 November 2026, week of Parashat Vayishlah 5787, on Zoom.

Schedule note: The first part of the shiur was on the parashah: six ideas on Vayishlah from Rabbenu Avraham's commentary on the Torah. They have their own episode and notes in the series Rabbenu Avraham ben HaRambam on the Parashah, and are only listed briefly below. The remaining 20 minutes were HaMaspik, "two very small parts", so the shiur finished a little early.

Part One — On the Parashah (in brief)

  • ויותר יעקב לבדו: Yaakov was alone because he was practising hitbodedut, solitude, the subject of the 13th and final chapter of HaMaspik. He reached prophecy, and the wrestling was a prophetic vision, as the Rambam says in the Moreh. Why then the limp (the Ramban's famous question)? As after a bad dream, שהאיברים מתפעלים על ידי כח הדמיון: the limbs are affected by the imagination, all the more so by prophecy.
  • The order of the camp: the maidservants first, then Leah, then Rahel and Yoseph last. Rashi, Radak, Ibn Ezra and Bekhor Shor explain that he was protecting those he loved most. Rabbenu Avraham: a procession in Esav's honour, the most honoured last, as the kallah walks in last.
  • הילדים אשר חנן אלהים את עבדך: Yaakov answered only about the children, out of modesty, and because Esav could work out the rest. And, quoting his father in the Moreh, חנן means an undeserved gift.
  • Sukkot: the Sukkot Yaakov reached is not the Sukkot of the Exodus, near Egypt; "unlike my grandfather," says Rabbenu Avraham. "He's not afraid to argue with his grandfather."
  • לא יעקב יאמר עוד שמך כי אם ישראל: Avram is never again called Avram, but Yaakov is still called Yaakov, more than Yisrael: so it means no longer Yaakov alone. The Rabbi's addition (from Rabbi Sacks): Yaakov (עקב, the heel) works around people, Yisrael stands up to them; a wise person knows when to be which.
  • Binyamin: "son of days." His grandfather, Rabbi Maimon: I am old and have few days left to enjoy you. Rabbenu Avraham: your mother is dying, so the days, life's experience, will bring you up. (A participant asked about "son of my right hand"; the Rabbi noted there are other explanations in the Rishonim and Midrashim, and that mem and nun often interchange.)

Part Two — Where We Are in Chapter 10

A quick recap before continuing:

  • We have defined perishut, and learned how to reach it intellectually and practically: you need to train for it.
  • The most important part of perishut is in the heart.
  • Last week, fasting as the example: don't go too fast, and beware of weakening yourself physically, harming yourself emotionally, and spiritually "thinking you're very holy, which defeats the whole purpose". All these are external things, worth nothing without the inner work.
  • And one of the major obstacles to perishut is having many people under your responsibility and dependent on you. So, within reason, one should make do with the basic relationships one needs.

Now Rabbenu Avraham shows this from the prophets. "I wanted to do this properly, rather than rush it last week."

Part Three — Eliyahu and Elisha

The cloak

וכאן אעיר לבך על העובדה שנביאים ניתקו את קשרי משפחתם. The prophets were willing to put their family relationships second to prophecy and their relationship with HaKadosh Barukh Hu, "again, within balance and reason."

When Eliyahu first met Elisha, before Elisha was his full student, והוא חרש: Elisha was ploughing. Eliyahu threw his cloak over him, לרמז ובשורה, as a hint and as good news. "I've got news for you. Why am I putting my coat on you? You're going to be like me, you're going to walk in my path, you're going to continue my path of prophecy."

A digression: who copies whom

Here Rabbenu Avraham digresses. Throwing the master's cloak onto the disciple is a very old concept, from the Torah and the Prophets. But in his day, the 1200s, the Jews no longer did it. Only the Sufis (פרושי אסלאם), in the Islamic schools, did: the teacher passes his cloak to the student to show he is ready to lead in his own right.

As the pasuk says in Vezot HaBerakhah, ישא מדברותיך: the other nations take from our laws and practices, and this is one example.

Then, in a side sentence, Rabbenu Avraham adds something unfortunate: not only did they copy us, we have ended up copying them, in a modest form of dress that they had taken from us. The tables have turned. "It's a good thing, but why do we have to learn it from them? Why don't we have this intrinsically?"

Leaving home

Back to the point. Elisha recognised what the cloak meant and ran after Eliyahu: אשקה נא לאבי ולאמי ואלכה אחריך, let me first kiss my father and mother, and then I'll come after you. Eliyahu said: go and do that.

But in the end Elisha understood: Eliyahu is waiting for me, he is giving me the cloak, it is time to go now. He did not delay, because he already understood (שזיכה לו רוח הקודש) that it was not right to put off following Eliyahu. Even though he had said he would go home first, he backed out of it. שחט תיכף ומיד את צמד הבקר: he slaughtered his oxen, gave the people their food, cut his connections, וילך אחרי אליהו, and followed Eliyahu.

What it teaches

This finishes last week's point: too many relationships are an obstacle to perishut. Cut them down as much as possible, though the balance is not always easy.

Would we do what Elisha did nowadays? "I don't know if that's the correct thing to do nowadays. But that's not the point." The point is that you need to be willing. "You can't be a person who's around people all the time and be in perishut, because an aspect of perishut means to be able to have time to be alone, to have a relationship with HaKadosh Barukh Hu directly."

Part Four — The Sign: How Do I Know I Have Perishut?

Two short parts remain today: the evidence for perishut, and its benefits. והשלישי, סימניה של השגת הפרישות: the signs that you have reached perishut.

Not time, not effort

Remember: real perishut is internal. Not "I wear simple clothes, I have a simple house, I eat simple food", but in the heart. How do I know if I have it?

Don't measure by how long you have spent or how hard you have worked. "Sometimes you can spend ages and loads of effort and you don't really achieve much, and sometimes with very small efforts you're able to achieve perishut. It's not about time or effort."

The test

Rabbenu Avraham says he said this at the very beginning of the chapter. The measure is simple: when you gain or lose something physical, how much happiness, or how much unhappiness, does it cause you?

The Rabbi's example: for your birthday someone gives you the new iPhone, the 18 Pro Max. Very special, very generous. What happens in your heart?

  • "Wow, I've got a new phone, a new camera, everything's amazing." Then דע כי אין פרישותך אמיתית: your perishut is not yet real.
  • Or: "Barukh Hashem, I can do things quicker now." It doesn't really change you. Then דע כי פרישותך אמיתית: know that your perishut is real.

So the rule:

The thing you gain or lose

How much it moves you

Your perishut

Valuable, precious, expensive

Hardly at all

Great

Small, unimportant

Great excitement

Weak: "then you've got terrible perishut"

"Of course there are levels in between." As Rabbenu Avraham says about many character traits in this book, it is a scale. "Of course it's nice, you can have a feeling. But not that everything revolves around it." Just know your level.

Yaakov leaves everything

Rabbenu Avraham brings Yaakov, from last week's parashah. Avraham and Yishak were both very wealthy. פרישות הפנימית של יעקב אבינו... הגיעה עד כדי כך שעזב בבת אחת את עושרם של יצחק ואברהם: Yaakov's inner perishut reached the point that he left all their wealth at once, escaped, and travelled with nothing on his back, and it did not bother him.

How could it not? "How can I care when I'm on the way to prophecy, when I've got such an ideal, such a great aspiration in life?" As the saying goes (כמו שאומר המשל): היודע משאת נפשו, יקל עליו לעזוב כל הון שבעולם. Whoever knows what his soul truly longs for finds it easy to leave all the wealth in the world.

A purpose carries you

The Rabbi compared this to a famous line in modern philosophy: "He who has a why can bear almost any how." "Why is there so much depression and so much anxiety nowadays? Because people don't have a why for life." With a goal, a vision, a purpose, you can bear all the pain that life throws at you.

Rabbenu Avraham was essentially saying the same thing centuries earlier, quoting it as an accepted proverb of his time. When you have a purpose, you can leave everything, and so Yaakov was not bothered about leaving the wealth.

And when Yaakov did suffer, over the loss of Yoseph, לא היה צער גשמי בלבד אלא ברובו צער רוחני: it was not only physical pain but mostly spiritual. It was the relationship, and the loss of his prophecy: "How could this happen to me?"

Elisha again

Elisha was offered great wealth, ככרות כסף וזהב, talents of silver and gold, לא קיבלם: he did not take them. Kings came to see him, and he did not want to look at them. As he told one of them: לולי פני יהושפט מלך יהודה אני נשא, אם אביט אליך ואם אראך. "Only because of him am I going to see you. Really, I don't want to see you."

The prophets didn't care, because they had a clear aspiration in life. When we have a clear aspiration, we can work on perishut, and gaining or losing things will not move us.

"That's an interesting test we can do in our own lives": how much does your heart flutter, for the good or for the bad, when you gain or lose physical possessions? That tells you your level of perishut.

Part Five — The Fruits of Perishut

"What are the practical benefits of living a life of perishut, as we've described over the past few weeks?" Rabbenu Avraham lists two kinds: spiritual fruits and material fruits.

Spiritual fruits

1. It saves you from many prohibitions in the Torah. הרי הם הצלה מפני הרבה איסורי תורה. Rabbenu Avraham lists them: גזל, גנבה, אונאה, שקר, deceit in business, false weights, gluttony, חמדנות, desire, sadness and disappointment.

"If I live a life of perishut, I don't need to steal. I don't need to be dishonest in business. I don't need to overcharge people or lie. I don't need to be greedy. I don't need to be jealous of people." Nor do you end up in forbidden foods or forbidden relationships. "How can I do these things? I'm living a life of perishut."

2. Fewer unnecessary relationships. ועוד הפסקת שיחה עם הבריות. "Like I said last week, people measure how successful they are by how many connections they have on LinkedIn or Facebook. The opposite." You will have less of this, and be able to focus more on life.

3. It helps you keep misvot. וכן מסייעת הפרישות לקיום כמה מצוות עשה: perishut helps you fulfil positive misvot that people hold back from, הן משום טרדת ממונו, והן משום טרדת ענייני העולם הזה, because of worry over money or the distractions of this world. Spending money on misvot won't bother you, "because this is not the important thing in life."

4. Love, fear and purity. וכשתעשה הפרישות קבע, תיכון בזכותה גם יראת שמים, וגם אהבה, וטהרה, ועבודת השם. Live like this and you will have love of Hashem, fear of Hashem and purity, and a really special connection with Hashem, "as we will talk about in the following chapter, Chapter 11."

Material fruits

כל אלה פירותיה הרוחניים של הפרישות: all of these are the spiritual fruits. אבל יש לה גם פירות גשמיים: perishut has tangible benefits too.

1. Tranquility. שלוות הנפש, העדר הדאגות. "I don't care if I lost something or if I made something." You are not living your whole life in worry: I'm going to make this, I mustn't lose that, running after this, running after that. You live calmly, because you recognise what these physical, mundane things really are.

2. No fights. כי אז ירווח לרוחך... מקנאת הזולת, מאיבת האויבים, מקטטות. You will not have fights with people, because "lots of fights come from money," and much of the enmity between people comes down to jealousy, which comes from a lack of perishut.

והוא שאמר שלמה (Mishlei 17:1): טוב פת חרבה ושלוה בה מבית מלא זבחי ריב. "I prefer a dry piece of bread and tranquility to the biggest palace full of arguments."

And in Pirkei Avot: מרבה נכסים מרבה דאגה. The more possessions you have, the more worry you have.

"Full stop. That's the end of this section."

Summary

  1. Eliyahu and Elisha: the cloak thrown over Elisha as he ploughed. Elisha meant to go home first, but cut his ties and followed Eliyahu. The prophets were willing to put family second, within balance and reason.
  2. The lesson: we may not do what Elisha did nowadays, but you must be willing. Perishut needs time alone with HaKadosh Barukh Hu, and you can't have it if you are around people all the time.
  3. The sign of perishut is not time or effort. It is how much your heart moves when you gain or lose something physical: the more valuable the thing and the less it moves you, the greater your perishut.
  4. Yaakov left his father's wealth without a second thought, because he had a purpose: whoever knows what his soul longs for finds it easy to leave all the wealth in the world.
  5. The spiritual fruits: freedom from theft, deceit, greed and jealousy; fewer unnecessary connections; ease in spending on misvot; love and fear of Hashem, purity and closeness to Him.
  6. The material fruits: tranquility and peace with people. A dry piece of bread in peace is better than a palace full of strife, and the more possessions, the more worry.
