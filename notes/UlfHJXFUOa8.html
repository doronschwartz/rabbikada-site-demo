Topic: The first reason for the fast: the translation of the Torah into Greek

Sources: Megillat Taanit (quoted in the Gemara); the Gemara on the 72 sages

Daily Halacha: Asarah BeTevet

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • In 2020 Asarah BeTevet fell twice in the secular year (7 January and 25 December). It is also the only fast that can fall on a Friday, so we go into Shabbat fasting.
  • Megillat Taanit, an ancient work already quoted by the Gemara, lists the days of tragedy in our history and gives three reasons for this time of year.
  • The first: on 8 Tevet King Talmai (Ptolemy), successor of Alexander of Macedon, had the Torah translated into Greek for the first time. Wanting a faithful translation for his library, he shut 72 sages in separate rooms. Miraculously all of them made the same changes in 13 places, to keep the Torah from being misunderstood.
  • Hazal say darkness came on the world for three days. Why, when the Torah is now translated into almost every language? Because translation loses the beauty and context of the original, as Shakespeare loses his in any other language. More than that, in Lashon HaKodesh a word (davar) gives the essence of a thing (davar).
  • Today the Torah must be made accessible in translation, but we must never forget that the original way of learning is from the text itself.

In one line

The first of three reasons for Asarah BeTevet is the darkness of the Torah's first translation, a reminder that the Torah is truly learnt in its own language.
