Seif 54: the five women who cannot bring a get

The Rabbi began with Seif 54, which is fairly straightforward. The Gemara and Yerushalmi list five women who may not bring a get in places where the agent must say "in my presence it was written and in my presence it was signed": a mother-in-law, a mother-in-law's daughter (a sister-in-law), a co-wife (even a former one who has since married someone else), a yevamah, and a husband's daughter (a stepdaughter).

The concern is that these women may act out of hostility and try to spoil the get, so where the agent need not say the formula they are not accepted. Where it is required, as in Eretz Yisrael, they are believed if they say it.

If she said the formula even where it is not required, the Rema (following the Ramban) believes her, since a get delivered with the formula is valid in any case. The Rashba disagrees: she may not know that saying it prevents the husband from calling the get forged, and may still think she can spoil it. The Shulhan Arukh brings both views.

Seif 55: the husband says "I only gave it for safekeeping"

This is the main subject of the day, and it affects practice whenever husband and wife are in the same town.

The Gemara

The get is in the hand of an agent. The husband says, "I gave it to him only for safekeeping," and the agent says, "He gave it to me to divorce with." Who is believed?

  • Rav Huna says the husband is believed. If he had really meant to divorce her, he would have handed it to her himself. That he gave it to an agent shows it was for safekeeping.
  • Rav Hisda says the agent is believed. The husband trusted him with the get, so he must be believed about what he was told to do with it.

Which agent?

Rashi and Tosafot explain that the Gemara is talking about a shaliah kabbalah and not a shaliah holakhah: with a shaliah holakhah the husband could simply cancel the appointment. Rav Huna's reasoning ("he could have given it to her himself") only works if they are living in the same town. Otherwise Rav Huna agrees that the agent is believed.

Rashi and Tosafot on Rav Hisda

  • Rashi: the agent is believed because of a migu. He could have passed the get on to the woman, and then it would be in her hand, which is the best proof. Since he could have done that, he is believed to say he was given it for divorce.
  • Tosafot disagree. Perhaps the woman would not have accepted it, so there is no migu. Instead it is a plain reasoning: by trusting him as an agent, the husband gave him full trust in all circumstances, even where it will lead him to lie. The Beit Yosef and other Rishonim support this.

One student observed that Rashi's approach also requires them to be in the same place. The Rabbi called this a good point, and said there is a disagreement about it at the end of the Seif.

Who does the halachah follow?

Normally one follows Rav Huna, who was the teacher of Rav Hisda, and the Rosh does. But the Rif and the Rambam rule like Rav Hisda, since the flow of the Gemara suggests it. The Shulhan Arukh brings the Rif and the Rambam first as the plain ruling, and "some say" like the Rosh. The Ran, following Rashi, says the Rif and Rambam believe the agent only where there has been contact between him and the woman.

Five situations where even Rav Huna agrees the agent is believed

  1. They are not in the same town. This is agreed by everyone.
  2. A shaliah holakhah. According to the Ran, Rav Huna's reasoning fails because the husband may have wanted to delay the proceedings, perhaps to arrange his finances, whereas a shaliah kabbalah acts immediately. Tosafot and the Rashba disagree and apply the dispute to both kinds of agent. The Rema follows Tosafot and the Rashba.
  3. The husband says he appointed him as a shaliah holakhah and the agent says shaliah kabbalah. Everyone agrees the agent is believed, because the husband has admitted that he gave it to him for divorce.
  4. The get is already in her hand. Then she is divorced.
  5. The agent says the get was written and signed in his presence (the formula of Siman 142). The Rosh holds that the agent is then believed. Tosafot disagree, since the husband can still claim that it was for safekeeping. The Rema seems to side with Tosafot, though the Beit Shemuel reads him as speaking only where they are in different towns.

In the text of the Seif, the first lines cover a husband who says the get is forged: witnesses to the delivery or confirmation of the signatures settle that. The rest of the Seif is about a husband who accepts the get as genuine but says he gave it for safekeeping.

"It is good to be careful": the Rema

The Shulhan Arukh adds: "It is good to be careful not to divorce through an agent when they are in the same town." This is a stringency following the Rosh, and it applies even to a shaliah holakhah.

The practical weight is large. A woman will often say that she will accept the get but does not want to see her husband. According to this Seif, if they live in the same town, one would insist that he give it to her directly. The Rema, though, says that when a harshaah is written, as it is today, there is no difference, because the harshaah says clearly that the agent was appointed to divorce. How can the husband then claim it was for safekeeping?

The Rabbi said this reasoning is very strong, since the husband cannot claim safekeeping when there is a harshaah. But the Pithei Teshuvah brings many later authorities who say that one should still be careful even with a harshaah. The Beit Shemuel says we do use an agent in the same town, because the harshaah is confirmed.

The Rabbi said that in practice, where the woman requests it, she is allowed to receive the get through a separate agent, based on what we learn here. The Rabbi explained the reasoning of those who allow it, based on the Rema and the Maharam Padua: the concern is only a stringency, which has to be balanced against other sensitivities.

The end of the Seif: when she says she saw it

If the woman says, "I saw the agent receive the get for my divorce," the Rambam and the Rosh disagree. The Rambam believes her only if the get is in her hand, because she then has a strong migu. The Rosh believes her even without the get, since "a woman does not lie at her own cost" (ein isha mizapenet). The Shulhan Arukh brings both views.

The Rema adds that the agent is only trusted when he is holding the get. If witnesses say they saw it in his hand, the Rema says that is not enough. The Beit Yosef and the Beit Shemuel disagree: if he has it in front of us, or two witnesses saw it, it is the same.

Seif 56: the get is lost

Seif 56 is clearer. If the woman holds the get, she is divorced. If it was lost, and there are no witnesses who saw it in her hand, then even if the husband agrees he gave it and the agent agrees he received it, she is only a safek megureshet, doubtful. This seems to contradict the previous Seif, where she was believed. The difference is that here the husband supports her, and the principle "a woman does not lie at her own cost" no longer applies: she may be prepared to lie when her husband backs her. The Rabbi also noted that the Rema says that nowadays, as boldness has increased, a woman who says to her husband's face "you divorced me" is no longer believed, though the Gemara believes her.

Seif 57: she received it but does not know who sent it

An agent received the get and sent it to her in front of witnesses. She received it, but does not know who sent it. The husband says, "I did not make him my agent," or says the get is invalid. If the witnesses say it came from the agent's hand to hers, she is divorced, as long as the get has been confirmed (kiyum). If there was no confirmation, she is not divorced, because the husband claims it was forged. The Rema repeats his distinction that the agent is trusted only when he holds the get, and the Beit Shemuel again disagrees.

As long as she has not received the get, even if he has appointed an agent, the husband must still pay her maintenance. The Rabbi then asked whether the husband may remarry while it is not yet confirmed that the get arrived. The Nodah BiYehudah takes the stricter line and requires a letter from the Beit Din confirming delivery. The Hida and the Ginat Varadim allow it when delivery is very likely. The last section of the Seif, on the husband's death or the end of the agency, was left for the next shiur.

Practical summary

  • If the husband says he gave the get to the agent only for safekeeping, the Shulhan Arukh follows the Rif and the Rambam that the agent is believed, and also brings the Rosh, who believes the husband.
  • Even according to the Rosh, the agent is believed where they live in different towns, where the husband admits he made him an agent for divorce, or where the get has reached her hand.
  • The Rema says it is good to avoid using an agent when husband and wife live in the same town, but also that a harshaah removes the concern.
  • In practice, where the woman asks to receive the get through an agent even in the same town, this is allowed.
  • A woman who says she saw her husband give the get to the agent is believed according to the Rosh, but only if the get is in her hand according to the Rambam.
  • If the get is lost and only the husband and agent say it was given, she is only doubtfully divorced.
