Topic: Character traits the Hakhamim see as signs of doubtful lineage

Sources: Gemara, the last chapter of Kiddushin; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 23 February 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • From the last chapter of Kiddushin, codified by the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh: certain traits suggest a person or family may not be Jewish, and one should avoid marrying into them.
  • A family or person who regularly and continually fights with others: constant mahloket is a siman pesul.
  • One who regularly, without foundation, alleges that others are mamzerim, of tainted lineage or not really Jewish is likely projecting a problem of his own, consciously or not, as psychology also describes. This means real allegations about yihus, not just using such words as insults.
  • The Gemara gives three signs of the Jewish people: baishanim (humble), rahmanim (merciful) and gomlei hasadim (doing kindness).
  • Their opposites, arrogance, cruelty, and living for oneself without kindness or social ties, are signs to keep away; such people are probably from the Givonim, who were cruel in Tanakh and executed seven descendants of Shaul.
  • Whether one trait suffices or all three are needed is debated; most understand it to be the combination of all three.

In one line

Constant quarrelling, baseless slurs on others' lineage, and the combination of arrogance, cruelty and unkindness are warning signs in choosing a family to marry into.
