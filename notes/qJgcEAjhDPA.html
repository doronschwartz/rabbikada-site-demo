Topic: May a woman read the Megillah for a man, for another woman, or for a group of women?

Sources: The Rishonim; the poskim; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Purim, Thursday 25 February 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Most Rishonim hold a woman may read the Megillah for a man, since both are obligated, and whenever two people are equally obligated one can fulfil the misvah for the other, just as a woman may say Kiddush for her husband if necessary.
  • Many poskim explain that the prohibition of hearing a woman's voice concerns singing, and reading the Megillah is at most a chant, so it does not apply.
  • Others forbid it, for two reasons: a man's obligation is to read and a woman's only to hear, so the obligations differ; and it was considered not respectful for a community, since in times gone by women were generally less educated in Torah, and it would reflect poorly on the community.
  • The final halacha, the opinion of the Shulhan Arukh, is that in a case of need a woman may read for a man. Because of the minority view we do not do so in the first instance. A man at home who cannot go out, choosing between hearing it on Zoom and from his wife who can read it, should certainly hear it from his wife.
  • A woman may certainly read for another woman, since their obligations are identical.
  • For a group of women it should seemingly be fine, just as a man reads for many men. One opinion calls it inappropriate, but it is questionable whether that applies in today's society, and many communities have women's readings of the Megillah.

Sources quoted

  • The majority of the Rishonim: equal obligation allows a woman to read for a man.
  • Shulhan Arukh: in a case of need a woman may read for a man.

In one line

In the first instance a man should hear the Megillah from a man, but in need he may hear it from a woman, and a woman may read for other women.
