Topic: How contemporary poskim apply the doubt about Kohanim today

Sources: Rav Ovadiah Yoseph, Teshuvot; Rav Shemuel HaLevi Wosner of Bnei Brak (the Shevet HaLevi); the Rambam; the Maharashdam

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 27 April 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The debate continued through the centuries. Two major contemporary poskim show how it is applied: Rav Ovadiah Yoseph and Rav Wosner of Bnei Brak.
  • Rav Ovadiah Yoseph: we must assume Kohanim today are full Kohanim, forbidden in everything, from the Torah or rabbinic, including marrying a divorcee. The view that they are doubtful is not enough to be lenient alone.
  • But where there is another doubt it can join to form a double doubt (sefek sefeika). For example, if there is a real doubt whether the Kohen's mother had relations with a non-Jew before marrying his father, which would make the children halalim, not Kohanim, one may perhaps be lenient about his marrying a divorcee.
  • Rav Wosner: the Rambam calls today's Kohanim Kohanei hazakah, and a hazakah requires generation after generation keeping Torah and misvot and acting as Kohanim.
  • One raised non-religious, told on becoming religious that a great-grandfather in Russia or Poland was a Kohen, lacks that hazakah; doing Birkat Kohanim once at Neilah is not acting as a Kohen. Together with the general doubt one may be lenient and allow him to marry a woman a Kohen may not.
  • A break in the chain raises the real possibility that one ancestor married a woman forbidden to him, which ends the line. A surname means nothing.
  • This question arises often, every case is different, and it must be investigated. To say simply that any Kohen may marry someone forbidden because Kohanim are doubtful is completely wrong.

In one line

Kohanim today are treated as full Kohanim, but an added doubt, or a break in observance across generations, can allow leniency case by case.
