Topic: Why Rosh Hashanah precedes Yom Kippur, and the two ways Hashem conducts the world

Sources: The Zohar; the Ramhal, Derech Hashem, end of chapter 2, section 2; the Gemara

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Friday 23 September 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Zohar writes that those who pray on Rosh Hashanah for children, life and sustenance are like dogs barking at their owners to be fed. And why does the Day of Judgment come before the Day of Atonement?
  • The Ramhal explains that Hashem usually runs the world by Hanhagat HaMishpat, strict justice: He has, as it were, bound Himself to a legal system in which actions have consequences, good and bad. Hence the Gemara says that one who claims Hashem will simply overlook his sins is punished.
  • Occasionally Hashem acts by Hanhagat HaYihud: He is in complete control and suspends the system, as in "I will be gracious to whom I will be gracious".
  • An analogy: a parent must discipline a child who runs into the street, but sometimes the child's innocent smile makes the parent let it go.
  • On Rosh Hashanah we crown Hashem as King, moving from the throne of judgment to the throne of mercy. This can be through Mituk HaDin, a softening within the system, or far more powerfully, through suspending judgment altogether, leaving only Hanhagat HaYihud.
  • This is the purpose of the shofar. One who spends Rosh Hashanah on personal details loses the bigger picture, which is why Rosh Hashanah precedes Yom Kippur, the day of details. If Hashem deals with us through Hanhagat HaYihud, everything else falls into place.

Sources quoted

In one line

Rosh Hashanah and the shofar seek to bring Hashem beyond strict justice to deal with us in pure mercy, so we focus on our relationship with Him, leaving the details for Yom Kippur.
