The halacha

  • Women are obligated in the Haggadah (Sippur Yesiat Misrayim) like men. Though time-bound, it is compared to massah and the Korban Pesah, in which women are obligated. Some held it is only rabbinic, but the consensus is a biblical obligation equal to men.
  • The main misvah is telling one's children, "levinkha" meaning son and daughter, though one may tell anyone, even oneself.
  • As the Gemara shows, one person may read on behalf of everyone. The Haggadah is not a prayer each must recite but a springboard for discussion; listeners, women and teenagers included, fulfil the misvah by listening and understanding. Reading the Hebrew without understanding is not enough.
  • The core parts, from the Mishnah and Gemara, are: the question and answer (Mah Nishtanah and Avadim Hayinu), recounting the past, and explaining Pesah, Massah and Maror. The rest of Maggid was compiled in the time of the Geonim, roughly 1,000 to 1,500 years ago.
  • The Hallel at the end of Maggid is like a berakhah or tefillah: everyone should listen attentively, or better, say it together.
  • The leader should explain the core parts clearly, and if Hebrew is not spoken at home, translating them is essential to the misvah.
  • Any further discussion is welcome: "kol hamarbeh lesapper harei zeh meshubah".

In one line

Women are fully obligated in the Haggadah and, like everyone at the table, fulfil it by listening to and understanding the leader's explanation of its core parts.
