Topic: Where the custom of hiding ten pieces of Hames before the search comes from

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; the Raavad; the Kabbalah

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Tuesday 2 April 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • From the Torah, according to most Rishonim, renouncing ownership suffices. The Hakhamim required actual removal (Biur Hames), lest one eat it on Pesah or not renounce it wholeheartedly.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules that it is enough to put Hames in a public place: the Gemara and Shulhan Arukh give the sea; today a communal bin, or one's dustbin collected before Pesah. The Rema writes that Ashkenazim, and some Sepharadim, burn it, an extra level.
  • The Hakhamim also instituted a search the night before, when people are free and a candle reaches crevices; if missed, it is done in the morning.
  • If one searches and finds nothing, the vast majority hold the blessing is not in vain, since the Misvah is to search, not to find.
  • One opinion held it might be in vain, so pieces should be hidden in advance to be found. The main halacha does not require this, but the custom developed from it.
  • The Rema, who brings the custom, gives no number. Ten pieces, among Sepharadim and apparently Ashkenazim too, comes from the Kabbalah, corresponding to ten kinds of impurity; the Raavad, earlier, called putting pieces away a ridiculous custom with no need for it.
  • The Misvah is to search for real Hames in the house. Too often the search becomes a hunt for ten pieces, which misses the point.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: disposal in a public place suffices.
  • The Rema: burning, and the custom of putting out pieces.
  • The Raavad: the custom is ridiculous and unnecessary.
  • The Kabbalah: ten pieces for ten kinds of impurity.

In one line

The ten pieces are an extra custom; the real Misvah is searching the house for Hames.
