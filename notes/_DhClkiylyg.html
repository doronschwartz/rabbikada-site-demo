Topic: Closing points on castration and veterinary issues

Sources: The Hida; the Gemara; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A non-Jewish lecturer in a Jewish vet school may of his own accord perform a castration forbidden to Jews, when the school does not demand it, certainly if most students are non-Jews.
  • If the degree requires students to take part in a full castration of a male's organs, that is a problem; seemingly the only way round it is to see it at another, non-Jewish university.
  • The Hida, surprisingly, held the prohibition applies to fish as well. One could argue, since "in your land" seems not to include fish and fish are never mentioned.
  • Sirus ahar sirus: castrating a further part of an already castrated animal is also forbidden, potentially from the Torah (the Gemara, Rambam and Shulhan Arukh).
  • An animal castrated in a forbidden way: the Hakhamim fined (knas) that one may not benefit from the castration, such as ploughing with it, but its meat may be eaten if kosher.
  • A man who became infertile indirectly, by radiotherapy or chemotherapy, did wrong, but is not a pesua dakka and may still marry into Am Yisrael.
  • Finally, to think about: changing gender involves major Torah and rabbinic prohibitions from this perspective alone, besides other issues; not discussed further.

Sources quoted

  • The Hida: the prohibition of castration applies to fish too.
  • Gemara, Rambam and Shulhan Arukh: sirus ahar sirus is forbidden.

In one line

Further castration of a castrated animal is forbidden, its meat may be eaten, and indirect infertility does not make a man a pesua dakka.
