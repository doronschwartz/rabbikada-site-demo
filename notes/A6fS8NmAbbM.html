Topic: The two meanings of guarding the massot, and whether regular Pesah massah can be used at the Seder

Sources: Torah, Parashat Bo; Shulhan Arukh; the Rif

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Wednesday 17 March 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Torah says "you shall guard the massot". The massot for the first night (and the second night outside Israel) need special guarding.
  • One meaning: extra vigilance that no hames enters them, beyond what we do for other foods, where we do not presume things go wrong.
  • There are three opinions on when guarding begins: from harvesting (attached grain cannot become hames), from grinding, or from kneading.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules like the most stringent view, that of the Rif, from harvesting, and branded Massah Shemurah is guarded from harvesting. He also says one fulfils the misvah with guarding from grinding.
  • All kosher-for-Pesah massot are guarded at least from grinding, because the Shulhan Arukh rules we no longer use grain that was wetted before grinding (Letitah). So if one cannot get Shemurah massah, regular box massah fulfils this aspect, though not in the first instance.
  • The second meaning: the massot must be baked Leshem Misvat Massah, with intent for the misvah. Massot baked by a child or a non-Jew, who cannot have that intent, are invalid for the Seder even with no hames.
  • For machine-made massot not marked Shemurah, he assumes a Jew operates the machine, which would suffice, but is still verifying this.
  • Conclusion: one should certainly use Massah Shemurah on the Seder nights, but if it is unavailable one may fulfil the misvah, with a berakhah, on regular kosher-for-Pesah massah.

Sources quoted

In one line

Massah Shemurah is guarded from harvesting and baked for the misvah; ideally use it at the Seder, but regular Pesah massah still fulfils the misvah with a berakhah.
