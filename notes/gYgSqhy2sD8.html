Topic: Which foods must be eaten in the sukkah, and what to do without one

Sources: The Mishnah; the Gemara

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Monday 20 September 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Strictly, only bread over a beisah (about 56 grams) must be eaten in the sukkah, with Leshev BaSukkah. Rice, meat, chicken and fish need not be, though eating them there is praiseworthy; the Mishnah says even drinking there is praiseworthy.
  • Cake and other foods of the five grains over a beisah should also be eaten in the sukkah, without a berakhah.
  • Without a sukkah, the Shabbat and Yom Tov meal can be fulfilled with a kezayit of bread (about 27 grams), keeping it under a beisah, and eaten outside.
  • Once a proper meal with bread has begun in the sukkah, everything in that meal must be eaten and drunk there. Taking a drink in the kitchen mid-meal, or walking out chewing, is not correct.
  • The first night of Sukkot is different: a Torah obligation to eat at least a kezayit of bread in the sukkah, equal to eating Massah on Pesah night, since the Gemara learns one from the other. Outside Israel one should try for the second night too.

Sources quoted

  • Mishnah: even drinking in the sukkah is praiseworthy.
  • Gemara: learns the first night of Sukkot from the first night of Pesah.

Only bread over 56 grams must be eaten in the sukkah, except everything once a meal has begun there, and a kezayit of bread on the first night.
