Topic: What reducing simhah means in the Nine Days

Sources: The Mishnah; Tosafot; the Mishnah Berurah; the Magen Avraham; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Gemara; Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • From Rosh Hodesh Av until Tisha BeAv, the Mishnah teaches, "When Av begins we reduce simhah".
  • Tosafot say that since no measure is given, one should not be happy at all; the Mishnah Berurah, the Magen Avraham and others codify this. The Rambam quotes the Mishnah as it is, and he reads him literally: one is in a less happy spirit than usual.
  • So any entertainment that is not a necessity is avoided, and each person must judge what is necessity and what is luxury.
  • Building for simhah is forbidden (Shulhan Arukh, from the Gemara). An extension needed because the house is too small for the family may continue; a garden swimming pool, unless for medical purposes, may not.
  • Business purely for simhah is not allowed, such as buying for a mundane (not misvah) party to be held after Tisha BeAv.
  • Weddings are not held from Rosh Hodesh, but engagements are permitted even on Tisha BeAv, as the Gemara says such an opportunity should be grasped.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein on a new car: an upgrade is not allowed; if one has no car or it broke down it is permitted; if there is no pressing need, wait until after Tisha BeAv unless the price will rise or it will not be available.
  • Haircuts remain permitted until the week of Tisha BeAv. The days are for introspection about the exile.

Sources quoted

  • The Mishnah: "When Av begins we reduce simhah".
  • Tosafot, the Mishnah Berurah and the Magen Avraham: no simhah at all.
  • The Rambam: a literal reduction of simhah.
  • The Gemara: engagements permitted even on Tisha BeAv.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: a new car in the Nine Days.

In one line

The Nine Days call for less simhah: no weddings, building or purchases for pleasure, while needs and engagements remain permitted.
