Topic: How far the permission to cook on Yom Tov extends, and food that could have been made beforehand

Sources: The Hakhamim; the Rama; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 27 May 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Okhel nefesh is not limited to what one needs. One who has enough food but is in the mood for something else may cook it on Yom Tov, as long as it is for that day and one intends to eat some of it. One may not cook on the first day for the second.
  • The Hakhamim understood that food is fresher cooked the same day, so there is no stringency to cook before Yom Tov. Even with fridges today, a hallah just out of the oven cannot be compared with yesterday's, and fresh food is generally tastier. So most foods may be cooked on Yom Tov even if they could have been made before, as part of the simhah of the hag.
  • The caveat: the permission applies only where there is some advantage, even a small one, to cooking on Yom Tov. A food that is essentially the same whether made before or on Yom Tov must be made beforehand. So rules the Rama, and for Sepharadim Hakham Ovadiah rules the same.
  • If one could not make it before Yom Tov, one may make it on Yom Tov with a shinui, doing something differently to show it should have been done before.
  • The permission covers preparing the food itself. Melakhot to make utensils for preparing food are generally not allowed on Yom Tov.

Sources quoted

  • The Rama and Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: food that gains nothing from being made on Yom Tov must be made beforehand.

In one line

On Yom Tov you may cook whatever you would like for that day, unless the food gains nothing from being made then, and not melakhah on the utensils.
