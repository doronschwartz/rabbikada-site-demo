Topic: Why the breaking of the Luhot belongs with the other tragedies of 17 Tammuz

Sources: Mishnah (Taanit); Parashat Ki Tissa; Ramban

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Friday 11 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Mishnah lists five tragedies on 17 Tammuz. Four concern the Bet HaMikdash or Yerushalayim: the walls were breached, the Tamid ceased because of the siege, Apostomos burnt the Torah, and an idol was placed in the Bet HaMikdash. The fifth, Moshe breaking the Luhot, seems the odd one out.
  • At the Egel HaZahav the people said Moshe was gone, so "make us a god who will walk before us". As Ramban says, they wanted an intermediary, finding a direct relationship with HaKadosh Barukh Hu too intense or too abstract.
  • The Torah calls it an "Egel Massekhah", which can also mean a mask: something standing between us and God.
  • Moshe saw that the Luhot, another wonderful symbol, would also become an intermediary, a charm in itself, so he broke them.
  • All the losses of the day (the Luhot, the Tamid, the walls, the Torah, the Heikhal) were our holiest things, yet they are means to a direct relationship with God, not ends. When we neglect that relationship, Hashem removes the crutches.
  • The same applies to red strings, amulets and symbols: easier than a real relationship, they are never a substitute for it.

In one line

As the three weeks begin, seek the direct relationship with Hashem, because symbols that become distractions are broken.
