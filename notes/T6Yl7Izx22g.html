Topic: The seudah of Purim Katan and the Rama's closing words, "tov lev mishteh tamid"

Sources: Mishnah Megillah; Tosafot; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Rama (end of Orah Hayim); Mishlei

Daily Halacha: Purim Katan & Adar Rishon, Friday 23 February 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Mishnah in Megillah says the only difference between Adar Rishon and Adar Sheni is the Megillah reading and gifts to the poor. Most Rishonim, the Rambam included, understand that none of the misvot of Purim apply in Adar Rishon.
  • Tosafot, 800 to 900 years ago, quotes Rishonim, and a practice in parts of France, that the Purim seudah is obligatory on Purim Katan, since the Mishnah excludes only the Megillah and gifts to the poor. Tosafot rejects this.
  • The Rambam holds gifts to the poor the greatest misvah of Purim, far above the seudah, so without them there is no obligation of a seudah. The Shulhan Arukh writes that the practice is not to hold one, and that is most people's custom.
  • The Rama quotes the opinions that there is a misvah of a seudah, and ends all 697 chapters of Orah Hayim with "tov lev mishteh tamid": one with a good, generous heart lives a life of constant feasting and positivity.
  • The Rama reads it as: even without an obligation, when there is an opportunity to feast, why not enjoy it.
  • Especially with difficult news from Israel, we need reasons to celebrate. Though there is no obligation, it is good to be merry on Purim Katan, find the good in life and thank HaKadosh Barukh Hu.

Sources quoted

  • Mishlei: "tov lev mishteh tamid", a cheerful heart is always festive, with which the Rama closes his work.

In one line

There is no obligation of a seudah on Purim Katan, but we should seize every excuse to be cheerful and thank Hashem.
