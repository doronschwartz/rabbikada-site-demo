Topic: Whether a berakhah is said on the half Hallel of Rosh Hodesh

Sources: The Gemara; Rambam; Rabbenu Tam of Tosafot; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; the Hida

Daily Halacha: Birkat Kohanim & Prayer, Thursday 20 October 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • On Rosh Hodesh (Heshvan falls on Tuesday and Wednesday) we read Hallel, but not the complete Hallel as on Sukkot. The Gemara calls it a minhag, and since it is not a Hag we skip parts. The custom is to skip "Lo Lanu" and "Ahavti", though any skipping would do.
  • Because it is a custom and not an obligation, the Rishonim disagree about the berakhah.
  • Rambam and Shulhan Arukh: no berakhah, following the Rambam's principle that one does not bless on a minhag, as with beating the aravot on Hoshana Rabbah.
  • Rabbenu Tam of Tosafot: one blesses even on a minhag, even when reading alone at home.
  • A middle opinion: bless when reading with the community, not when alone.
  • Practice: oriental Sepharadim follow the Shulhan Arukh and never bless, even with a sibbur; Ashkenazim follow the Rema and always bless, even alone; Spanish and Portuguese and some Moroccan communities bless only with the sibbur.
  • The Hida rules that one whose own custom is not to bless, who leads the service in a community that does (Ashkenazi or Spanish and Portuguese), may say the berakhah for them.

In one line

On Rosh Hodesh Hallel, oriental Sepharadim never bless, Ashkenazim always bless, and the Spanish and Portuguese bless only in a community, and a hazan follows the community he leads.
