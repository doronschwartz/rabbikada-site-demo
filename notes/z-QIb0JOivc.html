The halacha

  • The Torah mentions counting twice, days in Emor and weeks in Re'eh, so the Gemara (Abaye) learns that one must count both: "23 days, which are 3 weeks and 2 days".
  • The Rambam holds the counting is a Torah misvah independent of the offering. Others, based on the Gemara calling it zekher lamikdash, hold it is rabbinic today.
  • A third view: days are biblical, weeks rabbinic, since the Torah ties the weeks more closely to the Omer.
  • The final halacha, apparently the Shulhan Arukh's view, is that it is rabbinic today.
  • This matters for timing: a Torah misvah, like eating Massah, must wait for nightfall, while a rabbinic one may be done from sunset, during bein hashemashot.
  • The Shulhan Arukh says one may count from sunset, but the medakdekim wait for nightfall. In England this week sunset is about 8:05 and nightfall about 9:00, so preferably wait; if one fears forgetting, one may count with the berakhah from just after sunset. Before sunset, from plag haminhah, does not work, unlike Arvit.
  • Shulhan Arukh also offers a solution: after sunset, count without a berakhah, stipulating it counts only if one forgets later. If one remembers after nightfall, one counts again with the berakhah.
