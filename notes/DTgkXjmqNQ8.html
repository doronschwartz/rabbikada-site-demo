Topic: The prohibitions on owning Hames, the duty to dispose of it, and Hames belonging to a non-Jew

Sources: The Torah (Lo yeraeh lekha; Lo yimase; Akh bayom harishon tashbitu, Parashat Bo)

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Wednesday 27 March 2024

The halacha

  • Unique to Pesah, the Torah also forbids owning Hames, twice: it may not be seen in your possession or found in your house. These carry malkut, not karet.
  • There is also a positive commandment from Parashat Bo to dispose of Hames from midday on Erev Pesah, when the Korban Pesah was brought.
  • The Hakhamim added one hour before midday for disposal, not two as for eating, since owning is less severe. This is why the time to dispose of Hames is an hour after the time to stop eating it.
  • The Hakhamim read "lo yeraeh lekha" as Hames that is yours. Hames of a non-Jew in your home does not violate the Torah prohibition, but the Hakhamim required a barrier (a mehisah, or a locked, sealed cupboard) so one does not come to eat it. Sold Hames must likewise be sealed off.
  • Strictly speaking a non-Jew may even eat Hames in your house, though it may not be advisable. One may not eat Kasher LePesah food at the same table, even with a reminder (unlike meat and milk), in case crumbs get into each other's food, and one cleans up afterwards.

Sources quoted

  • The Torah: lo yeraeh lekha and lo yimase, two prohibitions on owning Hames.
  • The Torah, Parashat Bo: "akh bayom harishon tashbitu seor mibatekhem", the positive duty to dispose of it.

In one line

Only Hames one owns is forbidden to keep; a non-Jew's Hames in the house must be sealed off and not eaten at one's table.
