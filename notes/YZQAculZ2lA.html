Topic: Must an employee take Hol HaMoed off work?

Sources: The poskim

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Monday 10 November 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • In the past employees had fewer rights. If taking time off would cost a person his job, or harm his ability to hold it down, he may certainly work on Hol HaMoed.
  • Even though one may not deliberately arrange a davar haaved (shelo yekhaven melakhto bamoed), the poskim permit taking a job knowing from the start that it requires working on Hol HaMoed, since he has no choice.
  • Today, in most Western countries, employees have holiday rights and can take leave with proper notice. So where possible one should try to take Hol HaMoed off, though it is hard enough taking off the festival days themselves.
  • Some poskim hold it is required when one is able. If taking eight consecutive days in the middle of an intense project would harm one's standing at work, even if legally allowed, one may work.
  • Where it would not affect one at all, and it only means less leave for the rest of the year, strictly it seems one should take it off.
  • Whether losing holidays for the rest of the year counts as davar haaved is a very difficult question, to be discussed individually with someone who understands it.

In one line

An employee should take Hol HaMoed off where possible, but may work if leave would endanger or harm his job.
