Topic: The Heter Meah Rabbanim, permission of 100 rabbis for a second marriage

Sources: The Rishonim; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 17 February 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The best known way around the Herem of Rabbenu Gershom is the Heter Meah Rabbanim. It is not written by Rabbenu Gershom, but the Rishonim already say he would agree to it.
  • If 100 rabbis from three communities and three different lands or cities all agree that the circumstances make it right for a man to marry a second wife, he may do so.
  • The poskim write that they need not be fully ordained rabbis; even a reverend or minister who officiates at weddings and funerals would count, as long as they are people who understand halacha.
  • Three lands means three states in America, and presumably three cities elsewhere. Even if they gather in one place, they must come from three different communities.
  • Example: a man married a woman who was fully sane, so the kiddushin is valid, and she later became insane and cannot understand a get. He cannot divorce her and would otherwise be barred for life from remarrying.
  • Some poskim hold the enactment does not apply at all in such a case, but most hold, and this is the halacha today, that a Heter Meah Rabbanim is still required.
  • He must divorce civilly so as not to break secular law, though halachically he remains married to her. This still happens nowadays, though not often.

In one line

With the agreement of 100 learned men from three cities and communities, an Ashkenazi may take a second wife in extenuating cases such as a wife who has become insane.
