PART 1 — Chapter 10: עלה and ירד (Ascending and Descending)

Rambam's Opening Note — Why These Terms Are Here

כבר הקדמנו שאנחנו מזכירים בפרקי הספר הזה כל מונח שיש בו שיתוף — we treat only multi-meaning (homonymous) terms that risk being misunderstood about God.

(He is not writing a dictionary — only defusing the dangerous words.)

The Two Literal Meanings

Term

Literal Meaning

ירד (yarad)

Moving from a higher place to a lower place — physical descent

עלה (alah)

Moving from a lower place to a higher place — physical ascent

The Borrowed Meaning — Status and Rank

Just as with makom (Ch. 8), the terms were borrowed for greatness and status:

הושאלו שני מונחים אלו לגדולה ולכבוד When a person's standing declines — we say he "descended" (ירידה); when his standing rises — he "ascended" (עלייה).

The Torah's Examples

Verse

Usage

הגר בקרבך יעלה עליך מעלה מעלה, ואתה תרד מטה מטה (the Tochacha)

The stranger will rise higher and higher over you, and you will descend lower and lower — status, not stairs

ירוד ירדנו (Yosef's brothers)

"We have surely declined"

Modern Hebrew Preserves This

  • עולה לארץ / עלייה — one who moves to Eretz Yisrael "ascends" (a rise in spiritual status)
  • יורד — one who leaves Israel "descends"
  • עלייה לתורה — being called up to the Torah (an honor — status) (Even in English: "moving up in the world," "a step down.")

Applied to Hashem — Three Distinct Usages

Usage 1 — Prophecy: The Prophet "Ascends," the Shefa "Descends"

When the term relates to prophecy and apprehension:

ירידה — the descent of the divine overflow (shefa) onto the prophet עלייה — the prophet's ascent of the mind toward apprehension

Examples:

  • וירד ה' על הר סיני — "Hashem descended on Har Sinai" = the prophetic revelation descended; not God moving down
  • עלה אלי ההרה — "ascend to Me on the mountain" = rise in apprehension (though Moshe also physically climbed — remember the rule: the literal stays unless there's reason to remove it; the physical climb is real, and the deeper meaning rides on it)
  • וירד ה' לראות את העיר ואת המגדל (Bavel) — Hashem "descended to see" the city and tower: His knowledge engaged the lowly deeds of men, not spatial motion

Usage 2 — Punishment: "Descent" Before Striking the Low

When Hashem is described as "descending" toward punishing a people:

ירידה here = the turning of the divine attention downward toward those who have made themselves low through their deeds (Bavel; Sedom — ארדה נא ואראה "I will descend and see").

(And the deeper lesson of ארדה נא ואראה — see below: judges must not rule on hearsay.)

Usage 3 — The Shechinah's "Descent" to a Place

ירידת השכינה to a mountain or the Mishkan — the settling of the divine presence/glory upon a location: a manifestation, not movement of God (who has no body and no place — Ch. 8).

The Beautiful Derash — "I Will Descend and See" (Sedom)

ארדה נא ואראה: הכצעקתה הבאה אלי עשו — כלה; ואם לא — אדעה

Does Hashem need to "go down and check"?! Obviously not — He knows all. The Midrash (quoted by Rashi):

לימד לדיינים שלא יפסקו דיני נפשות אלא בראייה He taught judges never to rule in capital matters except by direct examination — not by report, not by rumor.

If the Omniscient One "descends to see" before judging Sedom — a human judge certainly may not condemn on hearsay. (A model of Hashem performing pedagogy through the Torah's very phrasing.)

PART 2 — Chapter 11: ישיבה (Sitting)

The Literal Meaning

תחילת קביעתו של המונח הזה בלשוננו היא ישיבת האדם על המקום Sitting — a person settling on a place.

The Borrowed Meaning — Stability, Permanence, Unchangingness

הושאל מונח זה לכל מצב יציב קבוע שאינו משתנה Borrowed for any stable, fixed, unchanging state.

Why? Think about what sitting is: standing is effortful and temporary; sitting is settled. A person who sits has come to rest — stability.

The Example — Yerushalayim

ורבתי עם... ישבה בדד (Eicha — appropriate as we approach Tisha B'Av) And the promise: וירושלים עוד תשב על מקומה — "Yerushalayim will yet sit in her place" = will endure, established and unchanging.

Applied to Hashem — The Unchanging One

אתה ה' לעולם תשב (Eicha 5) — "You, Hashem, sit forever" ישב בשמים (Tehillim) — "He who sits in the heavens"

"Sitting" applied to Hashem = His absolute permanence and unchangeability — לא מצד עצמו ולא מצד יחסו לזולתו:

  1. He does not change in Himself — no aging, no development, no mood-shifts
  2. He does not change in relation to anything else — His relationship to the cosmos is constant

Combined with kisei (Ch. 9): "Hashem sits on the throne" = the Unchanging One in His majesty — not a seated figure on furniture.

The Rambam's Closing Cross-Reference

The full meaning of "sitting" as applied to God will deepen later (when treating God's knowledge and unity). For now: whenever Tanakh says Hashem "sits" — read permanence, stability, immutability.

The Verse That Ties It All Together

ישב בשמים ישחק (Tehillim 2) — "He who sits in the heavens laughs"

The nations rage and conspire (למה רגשו גוים) — and He who sits — the Stable, Unchanging One, unthreatened and unmoved — "laughs." Their plots change nothing in Him. The metaphor of sitting carries the entire theology: God's unshakeable constancy over against human turbulence.

Tonight's Summary

Chapter 10 — Yarad & Alah

  • Literal: physical descent/ascent → Borrowed: decline/rise in status (the Tochacha; aliyah to Israel; aliyah laTorah)
  • Applied to God: (1) prophecy — the shefa "descends," the prophet's mind "ascends" (Sinai); (2) judgment — attention turned to those made low by their deeds (Bavel, Sedom); (3) the Shechinah's settling on a place
  • ארדה נא ואראה → judges must see, not rely on hearsay
  • The literal meaning remains where nothing forces its removal (Moshe really climbed)

Chapter 11 — Yeshiva

  • Literal: sitting → Borrowed: any stable, fixed, unchanging state (ישבה בדד; עוד תשב על מקומה)
  • Applied to God: אתה ה' לעולם תשב — He is unchanging in Himself and in His relations
  • ישב בשמים ישחק — the Unmoved One over the raging nations
