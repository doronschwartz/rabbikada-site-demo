Topic: The berakhah of She'asah Nissim on seeing the Hanukkah lights

Sources: Rambam; the Gemara; the Rashba; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Rambam calls the Hanukkah lights "a most beloved misvah", shown in a halacha unique to Hanukkah: one who sees the lights burning, though not lighting, says a berakhah.
  • The Gemara: one who sees the lights says "She'asah nissim laavotenu bayamim hahem bazeman hazeh"; not "lehadlik", since he is not lighting.
  • Rambam (and some Rishonim): this applies to anyone not lighting himself, even if his family lights for him at home, for example when travelling.
  • The Rashba: only when he is not lighting and no one lights for him at home, such as in a hotel where he cannot light.
  • The Shulhan Arukh notes that the Gemara fits the Rambam better, yet because of "safek berakhot lehakel" rules like the Rashba.
  • In practice: a traveller who is not lighting and for whom no one lights, walking in a Jewish area and seeing Menorahs in windows, says She'asah Nissim.
  • Nothing similar exists elsewhere: seeing others in a Sukkah brings no berakhah. This shows the beauty of the Menorah and its purpose of pirsumei nisa.

In one line

One who neither lights nor has anyone lighting for him says She'asah Nissim on seeing a Menorah.
