Topic: Almanat Isah: a widow from a family suspected of containing a halal

Sources: Shulhan Arukh, Even HaEzer, Siman 2; the Gemara; the Beit Shemuel

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 26 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Shulhan Arukh discusses almanat isah: a woman who married into a family where there is a concern that someone, unknown, is a halal.
  • A halal is the child of a Kohen's forbidden marriage. A halal is fully Jewish and may marry anyone except a Kohen; more on this in later chapters.
  • If she is widowed, she may not marry a Kohen, despite a double doubt: perhaps the family has no halal, and even if it does, perhaps her husband was not the one.
  • A double doubt (sefek sefeika) normally permits, but the Gemara forbids here because the Hakhamim raised the standard for lineage (maalah asu beyuhasin), to keep it perfectly pure.
  • The Gemara speaks only of a Kohen. The Shulhan Arukh makes a sweeping extension to a family with a doubtful mamzer, forbidding marriage into it.
  • The Beit Shemuel and many others disagreed, and the halacha does not follow the Shulhan Arukh here: the law is specific to Kohanim.

In one line

A widow who married into a family suspected of having a halal may not marry a Kohen, but the rule is not extended to a doubtful mamzer.
