Topic: Timing the Purim seudah on a Friday, and continuing it into Shabbat

Sources: Gemara Gittin; the Rishonim; the Rama; the later poskim; the Maharil; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Meiri

Daily Halacha: Purim, Wednesday 12 March 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara in Gittin forbids, and speaks strongly against, holding on Erev Shabbat a large meal one is not used to on weekdays. The Rishonim give reasons: it detracts from the kavod of Shabbat, and it stops people preparing for Shabbat. This is codified.
  • It does not apply to a misvah that falls that day, such as a brit milah on Friday morning, and the same applies to the Purim seudah. Strictly (meikar hadin) one may hold a large seudah with meat and wine any time on Friday and another meal for Shabbat.
  • Yet even the Rama, whose custom was the afternoon, writes that on Friday it should be in the morning, which later poskim explain as preferably before midday; some allow until a few hours before sunset, and the Maharil wrote up to three hours before. Otherwise one enters Shabbat full and cannot enjoy the Friday night meal properly.
  • So arguably the best practice this Friday is to sit down after the Megillah, sometime before midday.
  • Another option, from the Talmud and recorded in the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh: a meal on Friday afternoon that runs into Shabbat need not be stopped. One covers the bread and makes Kiddush on wine, omitting Borei Peri HaGefen since one already drank, then eats more bread (at least a kezayit, preferably a kebeisah) and says Birkat HaMazon with Resseh.
  • Whether to add Al HaNissim is disputed and there are practices either way, so whatever one does is fine, though generally Sepharadim would say it is better to leave it out.
  • The Meiri, Rabbi Menahem HaMeiri of 1300s southern France, records that this was his custom when Purim fell on Friday. Some places are doing it this year and it is acceptable, though others argue it is not the first choice; one could have a quick seudah with bread, meat and wine in the morning and the big one in the afternoon into Shabbat.
  • There is no set practice: best to hold the main seudah in the morning, but early afternoon is acceptable, and even a late start continuing into Shabbat works.

Sources quoted

  • Gemara Gittin: against a large meal on Erev Shabbat.
  • The Rama: on Friday, hold the seudah in the morning.
  • The Meiri: his custom was to begin late and continue into Shabbat with Kiddush mid-meal.

In one line

When Purim falls on Friday, hold the main seudah in the morning if you can, though beginning late and making Kiddush in the middle is an accepted option.
