Topic: The prohibitions on owning Hames and the Misvah to dispose of it

Sources: The Torah, Parashat Bo

Daily Halacha: Pesah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Uniquely, Pesah forbids owning Hames, not just eating it: twice the Torah says it shall not be seen in your possession or found in your houses for seven days.
  • There is also a positive Misvah, "on the first day you shall remove leaven" (Parashat Bo), which applies from midday on Erev Pesah, when the Korban Pesah was offered.
  • From nightfall the two negative prohibitions apply as well. They carry lashes, not Karet.
  • The Hakhamim moved disposal one hour before midday, not two as for eating, since owning is less severe than eating. So the time for disposal is an hour after the time to stop eating.
  • The Hakhamim read "it shall not be seen to you" as Hames that is yours. A non-Jew's Hames in one's house does not violate the Torah prohibition, but the Hakhamim require a barrier (Mehisah), such as a locked or sealed cupboard, so one does not eat it by mistake. Sold Hames must likewise be sealed off.
  • Strictly, a non-Jew may even eat Hames in one's house, though it may not be advisable, provided one cleans up afterwards.
  • One may not eat Pesah food at the same table as someone eating Hames, even with a reminder (unlike meat and milk), lest crumbs fall into one's food.

In one line

The Torah forbids owning Hames, not having someone else's Hames in the house, which must be sealed off, and one may not share a table with Hames.
