Topic: Women and the misvah of shofar: blowing for them, the berakhah, and missing a year

Sources: The Gemara ("greater is one who is commanded")

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Wednesday 25 August 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Women are exempt from shofar, biblically and rabbinically, since it is a time-bound positive misvah. Yet there is an old custom for women to come and hear it, because the shofar is so central to awakening us.
  • The Gemara says one commanded who does a misvah is greater than one not commanded, implying there is still reward for one who is not. So there is a misvah for women to hear, and a man who has already heard may blow for them.
  • Sepharadi custom is not to make a berakhah for women. If the berakhah is made in synagogue for the men, women may be included, but not at a later blowing for women or in a woman's home, since the berakhah says "who commanded us".
  • One who regularly does a voluntary misvah has in effect accepted it like a neder, and to stop entirely would require hatarat nedarim.
  • That applies only to stopping completely. A woman who cannot come one year, or for a few years while her children are young, but intends to continue, needs no hatarat nedarim.

In one line

Women are not obliged but have a misvah to hear shofar, which may be blown for them without a berakhah, and missing some years needs no annulment.
