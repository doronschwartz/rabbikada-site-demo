Topic: Why Kohanim cover their faces and hands with the tallit, and why the congregation covers its children

Sources: The Torah on blemishes of Kohanim; Bet Yoseph; Rav Hayyim David HaLevi, Aseh Lekha Rav

Daily Halacha: Birkat Kohanim & Prayer, Monday 27 June 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Kohanim cover their faces and hands during Birkat Kohanim, and the congregation covers itself and holds a tallit over its children.
  • Most blemishes that disqualify a Kohen from Temple service do not disqualify him from blessing. Those that do are the ones that draw attention away from the blessing, such as bent or crooked fingers, fingers with white spots, or blindness.
  • This matches the reason we do not look at the Kohanim: to focus on the berakhah.
  • The Bet Yoseph writes that in Egypt the Kohanim wrapped the tallit over their hands and faces. The minhag probably arose because when face and hands are covered, a Kohen with such a blemish may bless.
  • So today, when Kohanim everywhere cover themselves, such Kohanim may bless. It is not strictly required, but it includes many who could not otherwise bless.
  • Rav Hayyim David HaLevi, rabbi of Tel Aviv, author of Aseh Lekha Rav, found no source for covering the children, but calls it a good custom: not so they will not look and go blind, but to instil the importance of focusing on the Kohanim's blessing.

In one line

Covering by the Kohanim lets those with visible blemishes bless, and covering our children teaches them to focus on the blessing.
