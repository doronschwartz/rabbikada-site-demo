Topic: Which shops may stay open on Hol HaMoed: food shops and others

Sources: The Gemara; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Thursday 6 November 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • All melakhah, even melekhet uman, is permitted for food on Hol HaMoed, so supermarkets, groceries, butchers and fishmongers may stay open and sell as usual, and should. The Gemara and later sources speak of selling less openly, but today, when people buy food daily, all poskim agree they may open normally.
  • Everything associated, such as writing or printing receipts, is permitted even if it were melekhet uman.
  • Strictly the heter is to sell to Jews buying for the moed, not for after, and one may not do business even with a non-Jew on Hol HaMoed. But one need not question customers, and selling only to Jews would be wrong and unworkable today.
  • A food shop whose customers are almost all non-Jews, in an area with no Jews, may not open on Hol HaMoed, unless the circumstances discussed next apply.
  • Non-food shops may not open by default. In a small village or moshav, someone needing a suit or dress for the moed can ask the owner to open for him.
  • In big cities that is not practical, so such shops may open when their business is for people's needs for the moed, and should make this clear, perhaps with a sign.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: selling food less openly on the moed, no longer required today.
  • The poskim: food shops may open as usual.

In one line

Food shops serving a Jewish public may open freely on Hol HaMoed, while other shops may open only for needs of the moed, and should say so.
