Topic: A parent who asks the child not to mourn, and a father who asks a child not to say Kaddish for the mother

Sources: Hilchot Avelut; the halacha of honouring parents after death

Daily Halacha: Honouring Parents, Monday 25 January 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Mourning for a full year applies only to parents; for other relatives it is thirty days. The year is because of honouring parents; the first thirty days are the general laws of mourning (Hilchot Avelut).
  • So if a parent asks the child not to keep the first thirty days (shivah, haircuts and the like), the child does not listen, as with any request to break the Torah.
  • If the parent asks the child not to keep the rest of the year (music, functions), the child should listen and end mourning after thirty days.
  • If a mother has died and the father asks the child not to say Kaddish for her: if it is out of ignorance (believing it a bad omen for himself, which is utterly wrong), the child listens, since honour of a living parent outweighs honour after death. He should try to explain, and if that fails, learn Torah and do other misvot in her memory, which matter as much or more.
  • If the father asks out of spite, to deny the mother's soul its elevation, he is asking the child to act against halacha, and the child should say Kaddish.

In one line

A child keeps the first thirty days of mourning whatever a parent says but may follow a parent's wish about the rest of the year; a father's request not to say Kaddish for the mother is followed only if it comes from ignorance, not spite.
