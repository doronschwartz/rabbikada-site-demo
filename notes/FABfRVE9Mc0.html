Class Notes — Rabbi Daniel Kada

Sefer: HaMaspik L'Ovdei Hashem (Kifayat al-Abidin) — Rabbi Avraham ben HaRambam Chapter: Perek 7 (ענוה — Humility) — Part 1 Edition: Feldheim

Recap: Chapters 1–6

  • Chapter 1: Two paths — the Tzaddik (basics) and the Chasid (beyond)
  • Chapter 2: All middot are interrelated
  • Chapter 3: Purity of motive — four levels
  • Chapter 4: Compassion — first of the 13 Attributes. When in doubt → mercy
  • Chapter 5: Generosity — four kinds: money, influence, intellect, faith
  • Chapter 6: Calmness — intellect over emotion. Anger + arrogance linked; patience + humility linked. Sometimes anger is appropriate for spiritual matters.

Key connection: Rabbi Avraham taught in Chapter 6 that calmness and humility are interrelated, anger and arrogance are interrelated. This is why Chapter 7 (humility) follows Chapter 6 (calmness) — the less prone to anger, the more humble you are.

Note on structure: The chapters get longer as the book progresses — Rabbi Avraham seems to move from easier middot to harder ones. Chapter 7 is the longest chapter so far (expected 3–4 classes).

Part 1: How Bad Arrogance Is / How Beautiful Humility Is

Arrogance — Despised by God

הגאוה מאוסה לפניו יתעלה

  • Arrogance is despicable before Hashem
  • Shlomo HaMelech: תועבת ה' כל גבה לב — it's an abomination to God
  • Mishlei lists seven kinds of people Hashem hates — the very FIRST is arrogant people (רום עינים)
  • Then: murderers, liars, plotters, those who rush to evil, false witnesses, those who cause fights
  • Arrogance leads to denying God:
  • Parashat Ekev: ורם לבבך ושכחת את ה' אלהיך — when everything goes well (nice houses, good food), you become haughty → you forget Hashem

Humility — One of the Most Beautiful Traits

ענוה היא מן המעולות שבמדות הטרומיות

  • Humility = the prerequisite for completeness as a human being
  • Shlomo: ולפני כבוד ענוה — before honour comes humility
  • "Kavod" here doesn't mean honour/aggrandizement — that would be the opposite of humility
  • Kavod = completion, wholeness of a human being (as in: וכבוד והדר תעטרהו — Tehillim about God crowning humanity with grandeur)
  • The Zohar also calls the neshamah "kavod"
  • Meaning: before you can be completed as a human → you need humility
  • Humility brings the fear of God: עקב ענוה יראת ה' — on the heel of humility comes yir'at Hashem

Part 2: The Definition of Humility

Four Kinds of Humility

מתחלקת לכמה וכמה סוגים

Dimension

Two Sides

Inner vs. Outer

Inner humility (in your heart) vs. outer humility (behaviour, dress, conduct)

Vis-à-vis God vs. Humans

Humble before Hashem vs. humble before other people

Inner and Outer Must Be Symmetrical

  • Some people act humble externally but aren't humble internally
  • It's not enough to feel humble inside — your external actions must reflect it too
  • David HaMelech's testimony (Tehillim):
  • לא גבה לבי — "My heart wasn't arrogant" = inner humility
  • ולא רמו עיני — "My eyes didn't act haughty" = outer humility
  • אם לא שויתי ודוממתי נפשי — "I kept silent" = outer humility
  • ולבי חלל בקרבי — "My heart is hollow within me" = inner humility
  • The Rambam in Hilchot Melachim: Just as the Torah tells us to honour the king, so too the king is commanded to be humble: ולבי חלל בקרבי — same verse Rabbi Avraham quotes (he gets this from his father)

The Rabbi Yisrael Salanter Story

  • Saw someone praying the Amidah with great kavvanah
  • In Elokai Netzor: ונפשי כעפר לכל תהיה — "let my soul be like dust to everyone"
  • Thought: this must be a very humble person
  • A few moments later → saw the same person arguing arrogantly with someone
  • Asked him about it → the man said: "Vis-à-vis Hashem I'm humble, but vis-à-vis other people I'm greater"
  • The lesson: It's not enough to be humble before Hashem if you're arrogant before people

Vis-à-vis God AND Humans

Before God:

  • Avraham: ואנכי עפר ואפר — "I am but dust and ash"
  • David: ונקלותי עוד מזאת — when dancing with the Torah, Michal criticized him → he said "I'll be even more degraded than this"

Before other humans:

  • David after killing Goliath → Shaul wants to marry him to Michal → David says: הנקלה בעיניכם התחתן במלך? ואנכי איש רש ונקלה — "Who am I? I'm a simple person"
  • David to Shaul (who's chasing him): אחרי מי אתה רודף? אחרי כלב מת, אחרי פרעוש אחד — "Why chase me? I'm just a flea"

Part 3: What Humility Actually Means — The Surprising Definition

Arrogance Defined

שהכח השכלי באדם רואה עצמו נעלה מכפי שהוא

  • Arrogance = a person thinking about themselves more than they really are
  • You think you deserve more than you deserve
  • You think you're cleverer, more beautiful, calmer than you really are
  • Rabbi Avraham equates arrogance with self-love (loving yourself excessively)

Humility Defined — The Corollary

ומכאן מובנת הגדרת הענוה... היא הפוכה של גאוה

  • First statement: Humility = seeing yourself as less than you are (the opposite extreme of arrogance)
  • But then he clarifies:

אם יראה אדם עצמו פחות ממצבו בעולם או כפי שהוא באמת בלא הוסף ובלי טעות

  • If a person sees himself as less than he really is, OR as he really is without exaggeration

The True Definition

  • Seeing yourself less than you are = a temporary measure to counterbalance arrogance (like the Rambam's method: bend to the opposite extreme for a period)
  • The real, stable definition of humility: See yourself exactly as you are — without adding, without exaggerating
  • Recognize your talents — don't downplay them
  • Recognize your deficiencies — don't hide them
  • Don't think more of yourself than you are
  • Don't think less of yourself than you are
  • Later in the chapter, Rabbi Avraham writes explicitly: a person shouldn't think badly of himself

What Humility Is NOT

אך נאירה צימת לבך על ההבדל בין מי שמזגו מחייב הענוה הגבולה או ההתנברות והשפלות

  • Humility ≠ cowardice (מורך)
  • Humility ≠ feeling low about yourself (נברות ושפלות)
  • Humility ≠ debasing/embarrassing yourself (ביזיון)

אדרבה ענוה פירושה שלא תינתן לגאוה ועם זאת לא תהיה שפל

  • Humility means: don't become arrogant, but also don't become lowly

The Gemara in Sotah — Can a Humble Person Say "I'm Humble"?

  • End of Masechet Sotah: when someone died, the Mishnah says "humility ceased"
  • One of the rabbis said: "That's not true — I'm still around"
  • This shows: asserting that you're humble doesn't mean you're not humble
  • If you've worked on yourself and genuinely recognize it → that's fine
  • But: Going around telling everyone "I'm a tzaddik" = obviously not humility (what's the purpose?)
  • The key: it's about internal honesty, not external proclamation

Part 4: Nature vs. Training

Born With a Tendency

  • Like all middot: some people are born with a propensity toward humility, others toward arrogance
  • The Rambam (Eight Chapters + Hilchot De'ot): every person has free will, but humans are born with different tendencies
  • This doesn't excuse anyone — if you're born with more arrogance, you have a harder job, but you still must work on it

The Two Extremes

Reaching the highest humility:

  • Born with propensity toward humility
  • PLUS chinuch musari (ethical training)
  • PLUS hitkashrut datit (spiritual/Torah training)
  • PLUS histaklut mada'it (intellectual meditation)
  • → Can reach the highest level of humility

Reaching the worst arrogance:

  • Born with propensity toward arrogance
  • PLUS habits of this world (competition, bossing people around)
  • PLUS lack of spiritual training
  • PLUS ignorance (never studying, never reflecting)
  • → Can reach the worst level of arrogance

Most people: Somewhere in between — contradictions, sometimes working on it, sometimes not

The Foolish Person vs. David HaMelech

  • Am ha'aretz shoteh: buys fancy clothes, walks with shtolz (arrogant bearing) → Shlomo says: גם בדרך כשסכל הולך לבו חסר — "When a fool walks, you can tell he's a fool"
  • Yeshayahu: בנות ציון הלכו נטויות גרון — daughters of Zion walked with necks stretched high, eyes raised
  • Contrast with David: מי אנכי ומי חיי משפחת אבי — "Who am I?"

Part 5: Training for Humility — Method 1: Working on Oneself

External Actions That Build Inner Humility

Stay away from:

  • שררה והתנשאות — domineering, asserting authority over others
  • קנאה ומדון — jealousy and competition
  • התהדרות במלבושים ובמרכבה — showing off with clothes and vehicles (horses then, cars now)
  • ישיבה בארמונות — living in the most extravagant accommodation
  • חברים בעלי גאוה — associating with arrogant people (company is crucial)

Actively pursue:

  • הכנעה לחכמים ולשמשם — act with humility before those greater than you, serve the wise
  • Example: Elisha poured water on Eliyahu's hands — willing to serve his master
  • The Mishnah: הוי מתאבק בעפר רגליהם — "Bask in the dust of the Chachamim"
  • סלוח למי שהרע לו — try to forgive those who wrong you
  • סבול שמיעת החרפה — learn to bear hearing insults without reacting
  • הצטמצם במלבושיו — dress simply, as needed — no more
  • Example: Eliyahu wore only a hairy cloak and a belt
  • דירה פשוטה — live simply
  • Example: Elisha was given a small attic room
  • The Nevi'im were content with simplicity: "Let's take a beam and make a little place to sit"
  • התחבר עם ענוים — associate with humble people
  • Example: When Shaul was with Doeg (bad company) → killed the Kohanim of Nov in anger
  • When Shaul was with Shmuel (good company) → he was able to humble himself, threw off his royal garments, and prophesied

The Principle

ואלו דברים משותפים לפרישות ולענווה ולשאר סגולות המידות הטובות

  • Working on humility also brings you to other good traits — especially abstinence
  • Ultimately brings you toward encountering Hashem

Negative Example — Nadav and Avihu

The problem with the Midrash:

  • Chazal say one reason Nadav and Avihu died was because of their arrogance
  • Also: הורו הלכה בפני רבם — "they gave a halachic ruling in front of their teacher"
  • Rabbi Avraham says: DON'T take this at face value
  • Nadav and Avihu were prophets and anointed with the shemen hamishchah
  • David said: אל תגעו במשיחי ובנביאי אל תרעו — don't criticize prophets
  • It would be a great sin to call them genuinely arrogant people

Rabbi Avraham's interpretation:

  • They weren't arrogant in any normal sense
  • Their sin: they acted with courage and initiative WITHOUT consulting Moshe first
  • They offered an unauthorized fire → without asking Moshe
  • אומץ לב ללא נטילת רשות — courage without taking counsel
  • For people on THEIR level → that tiny lack of humility = a serious deficiency
  • "They ruled halacha before their teacher" doesn't mean an actual question was asked
  • It means: they decided the answer themselves without bothering to consult
  • They were so confident → they didn't think to ask
  • Even the shocking Midrash — "when will Moshe and Aharon die so we can lead?" — same idea
  • Not literally wishing them dead
  • Acting as if they could make decisions independently → behaving like leaders before their time
  • Not humble enough to recognize: there is a Moshe here — go ask him

Key Takeaways

  1. Arrogance = thinking more of yourself than you are. Humility = seeing yourself as you truly are.
  2. Humility ≠ self-debasement. Don't confuse humility with cowardice, depression, or thinking badly of yourself.
  3. If you're good at something → acknowledge it. Downplaying genuine strengths is NOT humility.
  4. Humility must be both inner AND outer, vis-à-vis God AND humans.
  5. Company matters enormously. Associate with humble people; distance from arrogant ones.
  6. Work on the external (dress, behaviour, speech) → it will eventually affect the internal.
  7. Everyone is born with tendencies — some toward humility, some toward arrogance. Either way, you must work on it.
  8. Nadav and Avihu — their "arrogance" was not consulting Moshe. On their level, that was a fatal deficiency. Lesson: always take counsel, even when you're confident.
