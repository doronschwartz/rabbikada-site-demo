Topic: A child born more than 12 months after the husband left: the Rambam, the Meiri and the Bahag

Sources: The Rambam; the Meiri; the Bahag (Baal Halachot Gedolot); the Gemara (the father of Shemuel)

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Wednesday 13 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Up to 12 months the child is kasher, and this is the halacha today. What of 13 months?
  • The Rambam: the Gemara's 12 months is precise. Beyond it a delayed pregnancy is impossible, so the child is presumed a mamzer. Why he speaks of a presumption is for later.
  • The Meiri, who greatly esteems the Rambam, disagrees. He was involved in a case of a husband away 15 months; the baby had long nails and hair, and there was no suspicion about the woman. For him 12 months is an example, and beyond it there is a very remote possibility the child is kasher.
  • The Bahag (7th or 8th century) learns from the Gemara's story of the father of Shemuel, who came back from abroad for a night and conceived Shemuel, that the husband may have returned secretly. So even after 18 months away the child is not a mamzer.
  • A modern parallel: in America families summer in the mountains while husbands work in the week and come for Shabbat; a husband may come quietly on a weekday night when his wife needs the mikveh, unknown to others.
