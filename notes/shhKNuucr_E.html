Topic: Standing for the Ten Commandments, and why there are no shortcuts in Torah

Sources: The Talmud; Rambam; Saadiah Gaon; the Talmud (Hillel and the convert)

The points

  • On the first day of Shavuot we read the Aseret HaDibberot in Parashat Yitro. Middle Eastern Sepharadim do not stand; Ashkenazim and the Spanish and Portuguese do.
  • Not standing follows the Rambam, based on the Talmud: the Hakhamim abolished the daily recital of the Ten Commandments lest people think they replace the Torah. Everyone agrees with this principle.
  • Those who stand do so because the Ten Commandments contain the whole Torah in summary, as Saadiah Gaon shows: "do not steal" includes all deception and honesty between people. They must not imply that this part is more important; nothing in the Torah is more or less important.
  • The principle is that there are no shortcuts to heaven. The Ten Commandments contain the 613 Misvot but do not replace them. As George Bernard Shaw said, the only golden rule is that there are no golden rules.
  • People seek religion with minimum inconvenience, but a shortcut often becomes a short circuit. The life of Torah is too complex and delicate for an unthinking summary.
  • Hillel told the convert who asked for the whole Torah on one foot: what you would not want done to you, do not do to others. But he added: the rest is commentary, go and learn it.
