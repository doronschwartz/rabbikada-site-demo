SIMAN 308, SEIF 46: SILKWORMS UNDER THE ARMPIT

Source: Responsa of the Rashba

Hebrew: אסור לשאת תחת אצילו זרע התולעים שעושים המשי מפני שאסור בטלטול ועוד שהוא מוליד בחמו

Translation: It is forbidden to carry silkworm eggs under one's armpit because they are forbidden to move [due to muktzeh], and furthermore, because one causes them to hatch through body heat.

Two Distinct Reasons for Prohibition:

Reason 1: Muktzeh Status of Animals

  • Fundamental Principle: All animals and moving creatures are muktzeh on Shabbat
  • Application: Silkworms fall under general category of living creatures
  • Exception: Pets have different halachic status (discussed in previous shiurim)
  • Practical Result: Cannot carry, move, or handle such creatures on Shabbat

Reason 2: Creating New Material (Molid)

  • Hebrew Concept: שהוא מוליד בחמו - causing production through body heat
  • Physical Process: Body heat and perspiration cause silkworm eggs to hatch
  • Halachic Classification: Constitutes molid (creating something new) on Shabbat
  • Status: Molid is classified as issur d'rabbanan (rabbinically forbidden)
  • Connection: Falls under category of makeh b'patish (finishing touch)

Mishnah Berurah's Critical Analysis: Pesik Reisha Principle

Hebrew Source: רוצה לומר שעל ידי החום יוציא התולעים מן הזרע, ואף על גב דאין מכוון לזה מכל מקום פסיק רישא הוא ומלאכת מחשבת הוא

Key Points:

  • Definition: Pesik reisha = inevitable consequence that will certainly occur
  • Application: Body heat will inevitably cause hatching
  • Irrelevance of Intent: Even if person has no intention for hatching to occur
  • Principle: Cannot avoid liability by claiming unintended consequence when result is inevitable

Contemporary Application: Smartwatches on Shabbat

Drawing the Parallel: Both silkworms and smartwatches involve wearing something on the body from before Shabbat with inevitable consequences occurring through normal body movement, raising identical questions of intent and pesik reisha.

Detailed Scenario Analysis

Typical Contemporary Use Case:

  • Person puts on smartwatch before Shabbat begins (Friday afternoon)
  • Primary purposes: step counting, heart rate monitoring, sleep tracking
  • Deliberately avoids looking at screen or actively using device during Shabbat
  • Plans to review collected data only after Shabbat concludes
  • Connects to phone app after Shabbat to analyze complete 24-hour period data
  • Wants comprehensive health data including Shabbat period activities

Multiple Halachic Issues Identified

Issue 1: Continuous Measurement (Medidah)

  • Halachic Concern: Measuring is rabbinically forbidden on Shabbat
  • Specific Application: Watch continuously measures steps, heart rate, movement patterns throughout Shabbat
  • Central Question: Does passive measurement while wearing constitute active measuring?
  • Counter-argument: Person performs no measuring action during Shabbat itself - device operates automatically
  • Response: The measuring process happens continuously throughout the entire Shabbat period
  • Parallel: Similar to using ruler or scale - the act of measurement itself may be problematic

Issue 2: Pesik Reisha of Screen Activation

  • Inevitable Consequence Issue: Normal arm movement during walking naturally triggers screen activation
  • Specific Examples: Walking to shul, gesturing while talking, moving arms during davening, reaching for seforim
  • Knowledge Factor: Person knows this activation will certainly occur when wearing watch during normal Shabbat activities
  • Parallel to Silkworms: Both involve inevitable consequences of body-worn items that person cannot avoid
  • Electricity Concern: Screen activation involves electrical issues (separate from muktzeh considerations)
  • Frequency: Unlike occasional motion sensor, this happens repeatedly throughout Shabbat

Issue 3: Intent Analysis (Kavanah)

  • Fundamental Question: Does wearing the watch demonstrate intent to benefit from Shabbat measurements?
  • Evidence for Problematic Intent:
  • Primary purpose of wearing on Shabbat is obtaining activity data from Shabbat period
  • Without screen functionality during Shabbat, only remaining purpose is data collection
  • No aesthetic value when screen is deliberately not being viewed
  • Person specifically wants measurements from Shabbat activities (walking to shul, etc.)
  • Quote from shiur: "The whole benefit of the watch on Shabbat is to provide results of what you're doing on Shabbat"
  • Evidence for Permitted Intent:
  • No active measuring performed by person during Shabbat itself
  • Similar to wearing regular watch that happens to have additional measurement capabilities
  • Data collection is background function, not active engagement with device

Comprehensive Framework: Distinguishing Types of Pesik Reisha

Category 1: Performing a Melacha Action with Inevitable Result

Example 1 - Slaughtering Scenario:

  • Action: Cutting off chicken's head to make ball for children
  • Intent: Want chicken to live, desperately need ball urgently for children
  • Halachic Analysis: Person is performing the actual act of shechitah (slaughtering)
  • Result: Forbidden even without intent to kill chicken
  • Reason: The action itself is fundamentally a melacha action

Example 2 - Watering Scenario:

  • Action: Washing hands (netilat yadayim) over garden area
  • Intent: Hand washing for ritual purposes, complete indifference about grass
  • Consequence: Water inevitably waters grass beneath
  • Halachic Analysis: Person is performing actual act of watering/pouring water
  • Result: Forbidden because the action itself constitutes the melacha of watering

Category 2: Performing Permitted Action with Inevitable Melacha Consequence

Primary Example: Walking Past Motion Sensor Light

Detailed Analysis:

  • Setting: Walking home from shul on Friday night in complete darkness
  • Knowledge: Person knows specific house has motion sensor that activates when passing
  • Action Performed: Walking down street (completely permitted activity)
  • Inevitable Result: Light activation (electrical melacha occurs)
  • Key Distinction: Person is not performing any action of "switching on lights"
  • Permitted Action: Regular ma'aseh (action) of walking - normal Shabbat activity

Halachic Ruling: PERMITTED when no intent for light activation

Critical Conditions for Permission:

  • Must have no desire whatsoever for light to come on
  • Cannot be relying on light for illumination needs
  • If person wants light because afraid of dark, becomes forbidden due to intent
  • Must make no difference to person whether light activates or not
  • Cannot slow down or speed up to trigger/avoid activation

Fundamental Principle: When performing completely permitted action (walking), inevitable forbidden consequence doesn't create prohibition if there is genuinely no intent for that consequence.

Application to Smartwatch: The Proposed Reasoning

Argument for Permission:

  • Primary Shabbat Actions: Walking to shul, dancing on Simchat Torah, lifting hands during davening, moving around house - all completely permitted activities
  • Background Consequence: Watch automatically measures these permitted activities
  • Key Distinction: Person is not performing "act of measurement" but rather normal physical activities that happen to be measured
  • Parallel to Motion Sensor: Similar to walking past sensor - permitted action with automatic technological consequence

Significant Complications and Counter-Arguments

Problem 1: Personal Setup and Intent

  • Challenge Raised: Installing/wearing device for personal benefit differs fundamentally from external sensors
  • Key Distinction:
  • External Sensors: (streetlights, store sensors) - no personal involvement in setup or maintenance
  • Personal Devices: Deliberately worn with full knowledge of Shabbat operation
  • Implication: Personal setup may demonstrate intent to benefit from Shabbat operation
  • Analogy Proposed: Could person install motion sensor at own house entrance?
  • Scenario: Install sensor during week knowing will walk home Friday night
  • Question: Does this show intent to benefit from Shabbat activation?
  • Connection: Similar to wearing watch - deliberate setup for inevitable Shabbat benefit

Problem 2: Primary Purpose Analysis

  • Core Challenge: If main purpose of wearing on Shabbat is obtaining activity data, does this invalidate permission?
  • Evidence for Problematic Intent:
  • Without screen functionality during Shabbat, only remaining purpose is comprehensive data collection
  • No aesthetic value when deliberately not looking at screen
  • Person specifically wants to measure Shabbat activities (walking to shul, dancing, davening movements)
  • Clear benefit desired from Shabbat period measurements
  • Response to Counter-arguments:
  • Cannot claim "just wearing it" when primary function during Shabbat is data collection
  • Clear "nicha lei" (person wants this result) - fundamental requirement for pesik reisha prohibition
  • No other meaningful purpose serves when screen not being actively used

Problem 3: Weekly Usage Pattern

  • Habit Analysis: Most smartwatch users wear device continuously throughout entire week
  • Implication: Person wants device to function during all seven days including Shabbat
  • Argument: Cannot claim no intent for Shabbat operation when deliberately maintained throughout week
  • Counter-response: Device serves legitimate weekday purposes; Shabbat operation is incidental
  • Question: Does continuous wearing pattern demonstrate weekly intent that includes Shabbat?

Practical Guidelines Firmly Established

Definitely Forbidden Actions:

  1. Putting on smartwatch on Shabbat itself
  • Reason: Actively initiating the measuring process on Shabbat
  • Creates direct causation between Shabbat action and forbidden result
  • Clear violation regardless of other considerations
  1. Actively turning wrist to view screen during Shabbat
  • Reason: Constitutes deliberate activation of electrical device
  • Involves both muktzeh handling and electricity prohibitions
  • Clear intentional action to cause forbidden result
  • No pesik reisha analysis needed - direct violation
  1. Looking at display during bein hashmashot (twilight)
  • Reason: May create muktzeh status for entire Shabbat through migo d'itkatzai principle
  • Involves actively using device during transition period
  • Could affect status for entire Shabbat period

Potentially Permitted Scenarios:

  1. Wearing from before Shabbat for passive monitoring
  • Conditions: Absolutely no active use during Shabbat, data review only after Shabbat
  • Based on: Principle of permitted action with automatic consequence
  • Requirements: Must genuinely have no intent to benefit from screen activation
  1. Always-on screen models
  • Significant Advantage: Eliminates screen activation issue since display remains constant
  • Better Halachic Case: Maintains legitimate watch functionality throughout Shabbat
  • Reasoning: Person wearing for timekeeping purpose with additional background data collection
  • Assessment: Much stronger case for permission
  1. Hybrid analog watches with digital data collection
  • Description: Traditional watch hands for time display plus background sensors for health data
  • Major Advantage: Clear primary purpose (timekeeping) with secondary data function
  • Halachic Assessment: Shiur noted being "far more comfortable" with this type for Shabbat use
  • Reason: Demonstrates legitimate watch usage rather than pure data collection device

Remaining Complex Questions for Further Analysis

Advanced Considerations:

  • Whether continuous wearing pattern throughout week demonstrates intent including Shabbat
  • How smart home integration affects analysis (watch controlling heating, lights, security systems)
  • Future developments in watch intelligence and automatic purchasing capabilities
  • Comparison to other continuous monitoring devices (home security systems, medical monitors)
  • Integration with other smart devices and automatic responses

Future Technology Implications:

  • Watches that automatically adjust home temperature based on body heat
  • Devices that place orders when detecting low supplies
  • Integration with car systems for automatic climate control
  • Medical alert systems with automatic emergency responses

Conclusion on Smartwatches: Requires further detailed analysis and may depend on specific device configuration, but active use is clearly forbidden while passive wearing has reasonable arguments for permission in certain configurations, especially always-on displays and hybrid analog models.

SIMAN 308, SEIF 47: SHA'ATNEZ GARMENTS

Source: Rabbenu Asher citing machlokes Rishonim

Hebrew: יש אוסרים לטלטל בגד שעטנז ויש מתירים

Translation: There are those who forbid moving a sha'atnez garment and there are those who permit it.

The Two Opinions:

First Opinion: Forbidden Movement (Oser)

  • Classification: Muktzeh machmat gufo
  • Reasoning: Owner cannot wear garment due to sha'atnez prohibition
  • Key Point: Item designated for person who cannot currently use it
  • Distinction from Kli Shemelachto L'Isur: Sha'atnez has NO permissible use for owner
  • Result: Complete prohibition - cannot move for any purpose

Second Opinion: Permitted Movement (Matir)

  • Reasoning: Garment retains practical utility despite owner's limitations
  • Permitted Uses: Non-Jewish guests could wear it, animal covering, other household uses
  • Philosophy: Focus on objective utility rather than owner's subjective limitations

Halachic Resolution

  • General Rule: In "yesh v'yesh" cases, halacha typically follows second opinion
  • Exception: Mishnah Berurah recommends first opinion (strict position)
  • Practical Result:
  • Ashkenazim: Treat sha'atnez garments as muktzeh
  • Sephardim: May rely on lenient ruling

Contemporary Extension: Unchecked Garments

Modern Application: Garments not yet tested for sha'atnez

  • Status: Also muktzeh according to strict opinion
  • Reasoning: Cannot be worn until verification completed
  • Important Distinction: Not "safek muktzeh" - definitely muktzeh due to inability to use

Modern Sha'atnez Context

Mi'ut Hamatzui Principle: Three categories of likelihood:

  1. Rov (majority) - Must check every item
  2. Mi'ut Hamatzui (significant minority) - Must check every item
  3. Mi'ut She'eino Matzui (rare) - No checking required

Contemporary Assessment:

  • Modern manufacturing has high precision
  • "100% wool" labels generally accurate
  • Question: Has sha'atnez become mi'ut she'eino matzui in modern times?
  • Clear case: Non-wool garments need no checking

SIMAN 308, SEIF 48: FANS FOR CONTROLLING FLIES

Hebrew: מותר לטלטל מניפה בשבת להבריח הזבובים

Translation: It is permitted to move a fan on Shabbat to chase away flies.

Basic Framework

  • Classification: Normal household utensil
  • Default Status: Permitted to move for any purpose

Advanced Application: Kli Shemelachto L'Isur

  • Scenario: Fan specifically designed for killing flies during weekdays
  • Weekday Function: Killing insects (forbidden on Shabbat)
  • Shabbat Usage: Chasing away flies (permitted purpose)
  • Classification: Kli shemelachto l'isur l'tzorech gufo - permitted

Permitted vs. Forbidden Actions

Clearly Permitted:

  • Chasing flies away from area
  • Creating air movement to disperse flies
  • Indirect methods: treating pathways where insects travel (ant example)

Strictly Forbidden:

  • Direct killing of flies or insects
  • Intentional targeting for elimination
  • Any direct action targeting specific creatures for death

Emergency Situations: Even during ant invasion, cannot resort to direct killing methods

SIMAN 308, SEIF 49: BROOMS AND SWEEPING EQUIPMENT

Hebrew: מכבדות שמכבדים בהן את הקרקע מותר לטלטלם

Translation: Brooms that are used to sweep the ground are permitted to be moved.

Historical Context

  • Original Concern: Prohibition of chorash (plowing) on earth floors
  • Historical Reality: Many houses had dirt floors
  • Worry: Sweeping might level earth surface

Shulchan Aruch vs. Rama Positions

  • Shulchan Aruch (Sephardic): Permits sweeping and moving brooms on all floors
  • Rama (Historical Ashkenazi): Forbade even on non-earth floors as gezeirah (protective decree)
  • Mishnah Berurah: Notes Rama's historical position classifying brooms as muktzeh

Contemporary Consensus

Modern Ashkenazi Position: Universal permission for broom use

Reasoning for Change:

  • All contemporary homes have non-earth floors
  • No realistic possibility of earth floor encounter
  • Rama's gezeirah not universally accepted
  • No plowing concern with modern materials

Current Status:

  • NOT muktzeh in any contemporary context
  • May be moved for any purpose
  • Sweeping permitted on all modern floors
  • Regular household implement without restrictions

SIMAN 308, SEIF 50: SECULAR BOOKS AND SCIENTIFIC INSTRUMENTS

Hebrew Opening: הרשב"א מתיר לטלטל האיצטרולב וכן ספרי חכמה ולדעת הרמב"ם יש להסתפק בדבר

The Astrolabe Case

  • Description: Sophisticated scientific instrument for astronomical observations
  • Function: Calculating celestial body positions
  • Rashba's Position: Explicitly permits moving such instruments

The Fundamental Machlokes: Secular Learning on Shabbat

Rambam's Position (Restrictive)

  • Source: Peirush HaMishnayos (not found in Mishneh Torah)
  • Hebrew: אסור ללמוד בשבת ויום טוב זולת בדברי תורה, אפילו בספרי חכמות אסור
  • Translation: Forbidden to study anything other than Torah on Shabbat; even scientific books are forbidden
  • Scope: All secular subjects including beneficial knowledge

Rashba's Position (Permissive)

  • Approach: Permits reading and studying scientific materials
  • Supporting Text: ויש מי שמתיר ולפי סברתו מותר להביט באיצטרולב בשבת
  • Implications: Scientific study actively permitted, broader secular knowledge not problematic

Muktzeh Extension

Logical Connection: If secular books cannot be read (per Rambam), they should be muktzeh machmat gufo

Reasoning Chain:

  1. Cannot read secular books on Shabbat → 2. No permissible use → 3. Should be muktzeh → 4. Cannot move even for other purposes

Rashba's Alternative: Reading permitted → Clear utility → No muktzeh issues

Profound Theological Question

Core Issue: Does studying science/philosophy fulfill the mitzvah of Torah study?

Rambam's Paradox: Elsewhere strongly advocates scientific study as path to knowing God

Practical Test: Birkat HaTorah - if person studies astronomy instead of traditional texts, has obligation been fulfilled?

Contemporary Practice

  • Mishnah Berurah: Recommends "yirei shamayim" be strict, focus on Torah
  • Practical Reality: Long Shabbatot, need for varied intellectual stimulation
  • Common Practice: Many observant Jews read secular materials
  • Balance: Primary Torah focus with recognition of lenient positions

SIMAN 308, SEIF 51: TIME-MEASURING INSTRUMENTS (HOURGLASSES)

Hebrew: מה שמראה על השעות שקורים אורזיל"ו, בין שהוא של חול בין של שבת יש להסתפק אם מותר לטלטלו, וכבר פשט המנהג לאסור

Description and Function

  • Item: Hourglass/sand timer
  • Hebrew Term: "אורזיל"ו" (borrowed term for timing device)
  • Mechanism: Sand trickles through opening, turn over to measure time
  • Variations: Different sizes for different durations

The Halachic Concern: Medidah (Measuring)

  • Primary Issue: Potential violation of measuring prohibition on Shabbat
  • Question: Does time measurement = spatial measurement (length, volume, weight)?
  • Distinction: Time is abstract/temporal rather than physical dimension

Resolution Process

  • Status: "יש להסתפק" - legitimate doubt about permissibility
  • Meaning: Reasonable arguments on both sides, requires analysis
  • Practical Resolution: "וכבר פשט המנהג לאסור" - custom spread to forbid
  • Significance: Custom resolves theoretical doubt through practical application

Contemporary Applications

  • Modern Devices: Digital timers, stopwatches, smart device timing functions
  • Parallel Analysis: Similar reasoning applies to contemporary timing devices
  • Exceptions: Medical needs, religious obligations, emergency situations

SIMAN 308, SEIF 52: OWNER'S DESIGNATION CONTROLS MUKTZEH STATUS

Hebrew: דבר שהוא מוקצה לחשוב ומוקצה, אפילו אנינין מטלטלין

Translation: Something that is muktzeh for a particular person, even others may not move it.

The Fundamental Principle

Core Rule: Ba'al habayit's (property owner's) personal muktzeh designation determines status for everyone

Key Elements:

  • Owner considers item muktzeh for himself
  • Owner's personal status extends to all others
  • Applies regardless of others' potential utility

Application Scenarios

Scenario 1: Religious Stringency

  • Owner follows strict opinion prohibiting item
  • Others might follow lenient opinions
  • Result: Muktzeh for everyone based on owner's position

Scenario 2: Physical Limitations

  • Owner cannot use due to medical/personal restriction
  • Others could use normally
  • Result: Muktzeh for all based on owner's limitation

Scenario 3: Temporary Issues

  • Owner temporarily cannot use (broken, needs repair)
  • Others could potentially use
  • Result: Status follows owner's current inability

Underlying Logic

  • Property Rights: Ba'al habayit has primary relationship with possessions
  • Designation Authority: Owner's intent and ability control classification
  • Respect for Ownership: Halachic system honors owner's status

Limitations

  • Must be genuine limitation or religious position
  • Cannot arbitrarily declare items muktzeh to restrict others
  • Personal designation must reflect real circumstances

CONTEMPORARY ELECTRONIC DEVICE APPLICATIONS

Security Cameras During Shabbat

Basic Principle: Generally permitted when running continuously from before Shabbat

  • No active human intervention during Shabbat
  • System operates automatically
  • Common in synagogues, including "very frum ones"

Bar Mitzvah Recording Considerations:

  • Problematic: Special setup specifically for recording celebration
  • Issues: Participants' behavior changes when aware of recording
  • Permissible: Existing security camera that incidentally records
  • Key Factor: Knowledge and intent of participants affects permissibility

Methodological Framework for Technology

Established Principles:

  1. Action Analysis: Distinguish performing melacha vs. permitted action with consequence
  2. Intent Evaluation: "Nicha lei" test - genuine desire for result?
  3. Setup vs. Operation: When/why system established affects permissibility
  4. Pesik Reisha Sophistication: Analyze nature of primary action being performed

Revolutionary Examples:

  • Rav Moshe on Revolving Doors: Permitted because person performs walking, not door operation
  • Motion Sensors: Walking past sensors generally permitted (permitted action with automatic response)

Future Considerations: Smart home integration will require sophisticated analysis rather than blanket prohibitions
