Review of Major Muktzeh Categories Previously Covered

Categories of Muktzeh:

  1. Chissaron Kis - Items that may cause financial loss
  2. Kli Shemelachto L'Issur - Tools whose primary use is forbidden on Shabbat
  3. Kli Shemelachto L'Heter - Tools whose primary use is permitted on Shabbat
  4. Muktzeh Machmat Gufo - Items with no use at all
  5. Muktzeh Machmat Issur - Items forbidden due to prohibition

Major Principles:

  • Migo D'Itktzai Bein Hashmashot Itktzai L'Chulei Yoma - If something is muktzeh at the beginning of Shabbat, it remains muktzeh for the entire Shabbat
  • Kavod HaBriyot - Human dignity overrides muktzeh restrictions
  • Public endangerment allows doing a d'rabbanan even if it stems from a d'oraita
  • Graf Shel Re'i - Concept of something unpleasant that may be moved

Se'if 38: Earth and Sand

The Halacha:

Shulchan Aruch: Before Shabbat, a person may bring in a boxful of earth (מכניס אדם מבעוד יום מלוא קופתו עפר), designate a corner for it (ומייחד לו קרן זוית), and use it freely on Shabbat for his needs (ועושה בו כל צרכיך בשבת) - such as covering something unpleasant or saliva.

However: If one did not designate a specific corner for it (אבל לא ייחד לו קרן זוית), it becomes secondary to the rest of the earth in the house and is forbidden to move (בטל אגב עפר הבית ואסור לטלטלו).

Rama: Therefore, it's permitted to take fruits buried in sand because that sand is not muktzeh (ולכן מותר ליקח פירות טמונים בחול כי אין אותו עפר מוקצה) - since the sand was clearly designated to protect the fruit.

Understanding the Historical Context:

  • In ancient times, houses had earth floors, not modern flooring
  • If earth was brought in but left on the floor without designation, it mixed with the existing earth floor and became indistinguishable
  • If placed in a designated corner, it remained distinct and usable

Modern Applications:

Sand Usage:

  1. Beach sand: Cannot be moved on Shabbat - it's like pieces of wood or stone (עצים ואבנים)
  2. Designated sand: If brought home before Shabbat for children to play with, it's permitted
  3. Walking on beach: Permitted through shinui (indirect movement)
  4. Sand stuck to shoes/legs:
  • Cannot brush off directly with hands
  • Can wash off with water
  • Cannot directly remove with hands as it would be moving muktzeh

Key Differences Today:

  • Since modern homes don't have earth floors, any earth brought inside remains distinct
  • No need to designate a specific corner as in ancient times
  • Main application is understanding sand restrictions

Se'if 39: Animals - Basic Prohibitions

The Halacha:

Shulchan Aruch: It is forbidden to move any kind of creature - domesticated animals, wild animals, or birds (אסור לטלטל בהמה חיה ועוף).

Source: This comes directly from Gemara Shabbat 138a - animals are explicitly muktzeh.

Exception for chicks: Despite this prohibition, one may overturn a basket in front of chicks (מותר לכפות הסל לפני האפרוחים) so they can climb up and down (כדי שיעלו וירדו בו). This doesn't constitute ביטול כלי מהיכנו (nullifying a utensil from use) because the chicks can be shooed away at any moment.

Important caveat: If the chicks are on the basket during bein hashmashot (twilight), the basket remains muktzeh for the entire Shabbat due to the principle of מיגו דאיתקצאי.

Mishna Berura's Additions:

  • Even if there will be financial loss (the bird will escape), there's no heter to move it
  • Even domesticated animals remain muktzeh (avoiding trapping issues doesn't make them non-muktzeh)

Se'if 40: Moving Animals - Permitted Methods

The Halacha:

Shulchan Aruch: All animals may be prodded in your courtyard (כל בהמה חיה ועוף מדדין אותם בחצר).

What constitutes prodding: Holding by the neck or sides and leading them if necessary (דהיינו שאוחז בצווארו ובצדדין אוליכן אם צריכים).

Critical limitation: One may not lift them in a manner that their feet leave the ground (ובלבד שלא יגביהם בענין שיעקור רגליו מן הארץ).

Special rule for chickens: Cannot prod chickens because they jump and fly when touched (חוץ מתרנגול שאין מדדין אותה מפני שמקפדת עצמה מן הארץ), which would constitute lifting them. However, one may push them from behind into their coop (אבל דוחפים אותם מאחוריהם ביד).

Domain restrictions: This is only permitted in a private domain (chatzer). In a public domain, there's concern that while prodding, one might lift the animal and violate carrying in reshut harabim.

Modern application: In areas considered karmelit (most modern streets), many rule that prodding animals is permitted.

Se'if 41: Walking with Children

The Halacha:

Shulchan Aruch: A woman may prod her child, even in a public domain (האישה מדדה את בנה, אפילו ברשות הרבים).

Critical conditions:

  • She may not drag the child (ובלבד שלא תגררהו)
  • The child must always have one foot on the ground (אלא היא מגביה רגל אחת ומניח שניה על הארץ)
  • She can support the child while maintaining this requirement

Understanding the Principles:

Why is prodding a child permitted in public domain?

Mishna Berura explains: Even if one completely carried a child, it would only be an issur d'rabbanan, not d'oraita, because of the principle חי נושא את עצמו (a living being carries itself). This applies to any human who can move of their own accord (excluding very young babies under 4-5 months).

Dragging restrictions:

Two opinions in Rishonim:

  1. Rashi: Dragging is like carrying - forbidden even in karmelit
  2. Ran: Dragging is forbidden because one might come to lift the child - only applies to children who cannot walk at all

Practical ruling: In a karmelit, one may be lenient to drag if necessary.

Modern Applications:

Scenario: Child stops walking without eruv

Options in order of preference:

  1. Non-Jew available: Ask them to carry the child (שבות דשבות במקום צורך גדול)
  2. Child crying/frantic: Parent may carry the child themselves based on:
  • חי נושא את עצמו (issur d'rabbanan)
  • Modern streets are karmelit (second d'rabbanan)
  • Tzorech gadol/mitzvah allows שבות דשבות
  1. Before reaching dramatic stage: Prod the child along with one foot always on ground
  2. In karmelit: May drag child if necessary

Important limitations:

  • Ill child: If child is truly ill and cannot walk, חי נושא את עצמו doesn't apply - carrying would be d'oraita
  • Cannot plan this: This heter is for emergencies, not a weekly procedure
  • Age considerations: Child must be capable of movement (not bound, not severely ill)

Rav Moshe Feinstein's ruling: If child is crying and unlikely to change mind in near future, may carry child home, provided child is not ill, due to צורך מצוה in karmelit context.

Se'if 42: Touching Muktzeh

The Halacha:

Shulchan Aruch: Something that is muktzeh is permitted to touch (דבר שהוא מוקצה מותר לגוע בו), provided one doesn't move it even slightly (ובלבד שלא יניענו אפילו מקצתו).

Practical Applications:

  • May lean against a car on Shabbat (unless very heavy person/light car combination that would move it)
  • Can touch any muktzeh item as long as no movement occurs
  • Even minimal movement of muktzeh is forbidden

Se'if 43: Moving Muktzeh Through Blowing

The Halacha:

Shulchan Aruch: One is permitted to move muktzeh by blowing it (מותר לטלטל מוקצה על ידי נפיחה).

Reasoning: This is a very unusual way of moving objects, so it's not considered normal טלטול.

Se'if 44: Broken Utensils

The Halacha:

Shulchan Aruch: A utensil that broke - one may not fix it to cut food with it (כלי שנתרעע לא יתקננו לחתוך בו את האוכל).

Understanding the principle:

  • Earlier we learned that broken pieces may be used in their natural broken state
  • However, one cannot modify, reshape, or carve the broken pieces
  • This would constitute תיקון כלי (fixing/creating a utensil), at least on a rabbinical level
  • Must use broken fragments exactly as they are without any alterations

Se'if 45: Playing with Balls

The Machloket:

Shulchan Aruch: Forbidden to play with balls on Shabbat and Yom Tov (אסור לשחק בשבת וביום טוב בכדור).

Rama: Some permit it, and the Ashkenazi custom is to be lenient (ויש מתירים ונהגו להקל).

Understanding the Positions:

Shulchan Aruch's reasoning (Sephardic position):

  • Balls are מוקצה מחמת גופו - they have no utility
  • Entertainment/play is insufficient utility to remove muktzeh status
  • Contemporary Sephardic poskim (Rav Ovadia Yosef, Rav Ben Zion Abba Shaul): Maintain this prohibition
  • Children may be permitted to play with balls

Rama's reasoning (Ashkenazi position):

  • Balls are specifically designated for entertainment
  • This constitutes sufficient utility (based on Tosafot)
  • Entertainment is a legitimate use that removes muktzeh status
  • Contemporary Ashkenazi poskim: Generally maintain this permission

Modern considerations favoring leniency:

  1. Manufacturing purpose: Modern balls are specifically manufactured for play
  2. Cleanliness: Unlike historical balls which were dirty, modern balls are clean and proper
  3. Clear designation: Today's sports equipment has obvious intended use

Practical Guidelines:

For Ashkenazim:

  • Generally permitted to play with balls
  • However, may not be appropriate for Shabbat spirit
  • Consider alternatives and context

For Sephardim:

  • Officially forbidden according to major poskim
  • Children may be permitted
  • Some contemporary authorities suggest modern balls might be different

Contextual considerations:

  • Age and religiosity level: Younger, less religious individuals might be given more lenient guidance
  • Alternatives available: If person would otherwise engage in inappropriate activities, ball playing might be preferable
  • Time and place: Adult organized sports vs. casual backyard play
  • Shabbat atmosphere: Whether activity enhances or detracts from Shabbat spirit

Games Requiring Level Ground:

Additional restrictions:

Completely forbidden: Games requiring flat surfaces on earth due to concern of אשוויי גומות (leveling holes) - a potential Torah violation related to plowing:

  • Marbles
  • Golf
  • Bowling
  • Any game where uneven ground would lead to leveling

Extended prohibition: Cannot play even on concrete surfaces - גזירה lest one come to play on earth.

Questionable cases:

  • Tennis: Debatable whether it requires completely level surface
  • Football: Generally permitted as it doesn't require perfectly level ground

Playing near vegetation:

If ball goes into bushes/trees, cannot retrieve it due to משתמש בעילן (using a tree) - would need to touch branches to retrieve ball.

Contemporary Pet Animals - Extended Discussion

Historical Position:

  • Gemara and Rishonim: All animals are muktzeh
  • Ra'ash (750 years ago): Even animals kept for pleasure remain muktzeh due to לא פלוג (we don't differentiate)
  • In his time, only wealthy people had pet animals, so לא פלוג made sense

Modern Reality:

  • Most urban animals are kept for companionship, entertainment, security
  • Clear utility exists: companionship, emotional support, entertainment
  • Question: Does modern reality change the halachic status?

Contemporary Positions:

Strict Position (Majority of Poskim):

Rav Ovadia Yosef, Rav Ben Zion Abba Shaul, most Ashkenazi poskim:

  • Animals remain muktzeh despite modern utility
  • Difficult to overturn comprehensive Talmudic and Shulchan Aruch ruling
  • Rav Ovadia adds: Even animal cages/enclosures are muktzeh when designated for animals
  • Exception: צער בעלי חיים (animal suffering) - may move cage if animal is in pain from sun, etc.

Moderate Position:

Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach:

  • Main text: Animals are muktzeh (following standard ruling)
  • Footnote: Raises significant doubt (צריך עיון) - suggests modern animals with clear utility might not be muktzeh
  • Guide dogs: Definitely not muktzeh - anyone may move and play with them

Lenient Application:

Guide dogs specifically:

  • Rav Shlomo Zalman: Not muktzeh at all
  • Rav Ovadia: Still muktzeh, but blind person may lead with leash (like prodding)
  • Practical: Guide dogs permitted in synagogue with blind congregants

Practical Guidelines:

What to tell questioners:

  1. Children under Bar/Bat Mitzvah: May be lenient - don't need to be strict on disputed matters
  2. Adults:
  • Default position: Follow majority who say muktzeh
  • If there's genuine need (emotional support, etc.): May rely on lenient opinions
  • For regular entertainment: Better to avoid

What not to do:

  • Don't admonish: Those who move pet animals shouldn't be criticized - there's legitimate basis for leniency
  • Don't assume: Stray animals clearly remain muktzeh according to all opinions
  • Context matters: Pet animals in home vs. random animals outside are different categories

Practical scenarios:

  • Stroking/petting: Permitted according to all - touching without moving is allowed
  • Children playing: Generally acceptable, especially for young children
  • Adults with emotional needs: Case-by-case basis with rabbinic guidance
  • Regular weekly reliance: Should not become standard practice without clear need

Summary of Key Practical Applications:

Beach/Sand Activities:

  • Cannot play with sand on beach
  • Can walk/sit on sand (through shinui)
  • Can use pre-designated sand at home
  • Must wash off sand, cannot brush off with hands

Pet Animals:

  • Majority opinion: remain muktzeh
  • Guide dogs: dispute between authorities
  • Children: generally may be lenient
  • Don't criticize those who are lenient

Children Walking:

  • May prod with one foot on ground
  • Emergency carrying permitted in karmelit when child crying
  • Cannot plan weekly reliance on this
  • Ill children present additional complications

Ball Games:

  • Ashkenazi custom: generally permitted
  • Sephardic ruling: forbidden for adults, children may be lenient
  • Games requiring level ground: completely forbidden
  • Consider Shabbat appropriateness and alternatives

General Muktzeh Principles:

  • Touching permitted, moving forbidden
  • Blowing to move is permitted
  • Cannot modify broken utensils
  • Context and intent matter in practical application
