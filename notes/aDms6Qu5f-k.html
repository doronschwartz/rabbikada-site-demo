Topic: Opening the series: the two misvot of honour (kavod) and fear (yirah) of parents

Sources: Shemot (Parashat Yitro); Vayikra (Parashat Kedoshim); the Gemara; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Honouring Parents, Monday 4 January 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • With the Ten Commandments approaching in the weekly parashah, the series turns to the fifth: honouring father and mother. It looks simple but has many details: parents-in-law, step-parents, older siblings, honour after death, and whether the misvah applies to a parent who abused a child.
  • Yitro says "Honour your father and your mother"; Kedoshim says "A person must fear his mother and his father". Hazal see two distinct misvot.
  • Kavod (honour) is positive action: shopping for them, getting their medicines, helping them.
  • Yirah (fear) is mostly about what not to do: not contradicting them to their face, not sitting in their place without permission, not calling them by their first name.
  • The Gemara asks why honour puts the father first and fear puts the mother first. A child naturally fears a father more and feels closer to a mother, so the Torah stresses fearing the mother too and honouring the father too. A generalisation, but a nice insight.
  • The Shulhan Arukh opens these laws with "one must be very careful" with the honour and fear of parents, rare wording in a code of law, urging care with a basic misvah of great reward.

In one line

Honouring parents is two misvot, active honour and reverent fear, and the Shulhan Arukh urges special care with both.
