Topic: Why Sukkot falls in autumn: the Tur and the Rambam, and reading a Rishon in his context

Sources: Vayikra ("Ki vasukkot hoshavti et Benei Yisrael"); the Gemara; the Tur; the Rambam, Moreh Nevuchim

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Thursday 28 September 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • "For in sukkot I made Benei Yisrael dwell when I brought them out of Egypt": the Gemara records a dispute whether these were actual booths in the desert or the Ananei HaKavod, the clouds of glory.
  • Unusually, this misvah requires intent for its reason, since the Torah says "so that your generations may know". On first sitting in the sukkah one should have in mind the booths and the clouds of glory.
  • Why now? Other festivals fall when their event happened, but the desert booths could be remembered any time.
  • The Tur: in summer people sit outside and go camping, so it would not be clear we do it for the misvah. When the weather turns and everyone brings their huts in, HaShem says go out, and it is clear we do it to remember His miracles.
  • The Rambam, in Moreh Nevuchim, says the opposite: in the heat of Tammuz and Av it would be too hot to enjoy the sukkah, so HaShem gave it when the weather cools.
  • The answers are diametrically opposite, but the Rambam lived in the Middle East, where summer heat is unbearable, and the Tur in Europe, where July and August are for camping and October is cold.
  • When a Rishon gives an answer, ask where he lived and what his historical and social context was; it sheds light on what he means.

Sources quoted

  • The Tur: Sukkot is in autumn so that it is clearly for the misvah.
  • The Rambam, Moreh Nevuchim: Sukkot is in autumn because summer is too hot to enjoy the sukkah.

In one line

The Tur and the Rambam give opposite reasons for the timing of Sukkot, each making sense in his own climate.
