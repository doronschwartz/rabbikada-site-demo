Topic: Nahshon ben Amminadav and the courage to take the plunge

Sources: The Gemara; G. K. Chesterton

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Wednesday 20 April 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The seventh and eighth days commemorate Keriat Yam Suf. Of the twelve Nesiim, the best known is Nahshon ben Amminadav of Yehudah.
  • The Gemara relates that with the Egyptians behind, the sea ahead and the desert alongside, Moshe told the people to travel, but no one wanted to be first. Nahshon jumped in, the water rose to his neck, the sea split, and the people followed him and were saved.
  • The seventh day commemorates his courage to take the plunge, literally and metaphorically. As G. K. Chesterton said, the paradox of courage is that a man must be a little careless of his life in order to keep it.
  • A life without risk can become dull. Risks must be calculated and planned, but nothing is accomplished without some uncertainty, and the greatest human achievements came from the Nahshons of each generation.
  • A risk that succeeds is called wise, one that backfires reckless. A risk is worth taking when there is a reasonable chance of success.
  • Nahshon surely had doubts, but given the miracles they had seen and HaKadosh Barukh Hu's promises to bring them to Sinai and the Land, he judged his jump had a good chance, and he was right. Hence "hazak ve-ematz", be strong and courageous.
