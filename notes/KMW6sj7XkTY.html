Topic: Saying the night Shema when nightfall is very late in the summer

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; Rabbenu Tam; the Shevet HaLevi; Rav Elyashiv

Daily Halacha: Birkat Kohanim & Prayer, Tuesday 28 June 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Around the summer solstice nightfall in London is about a quarter past ten, and further north it can be after eleven. Those who must sleep early (young people, the elderly, those who rise early for work) face a question about the night Shema.
  • The Torah requires Shema morning and evening. The evening time, "uvshokhbekha", is nightfall according to many Rishonim and the Shulhan Arukh, so even one who prays Arvit early after Plag HaMinhah must repeat the Shema after nightfall.
  • Rabbenu Tam held that from Plag HaMinhah (now just after half past seven in London) is already the time people wind down, so one may fulfil the night Shema then. This is not the halacha of the Shulhan Arukh, but many later poskim accept it in necessary circumstances.
  • On this basis the Shevet HaLevi, Rav Elyashiv and other sages of our generation ruled that in extenuating circumstances one may rely on Rabbenu Tam: pray Arvit, and say the Shema just before going to sleep.
  • One who can wait until nightfall without ruining the next day should do so.
  • Another option is to wait until half an hour after sunset, which many opinions already consider nightfall for Shema, though not for Shabbat ending.

In one line

Ideally the night Shema is said after nightfall, but in extenuating circumstances one may say it from Plag HaMinhah and go straight to sleep.
