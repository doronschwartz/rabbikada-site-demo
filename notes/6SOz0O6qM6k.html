Topic: At what age children fast on Yom Kippur, and how

Sources: Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Yom Kippur, Friday 30 September 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • For Sepharadim and Ashkenazim alike, from age 11 (two years before Bar Misvah for a boy, one year before Bat Misvah for a girl) a child should try to fast the whole day, without endangering himself. A weak child who finds it hard need not, and does a fast of hours instead.
  • At ages nine and ten, the child does a Taanit Shaot, a fast of hours: breakfast and lunch are delayed by an hour or two so the child feels a little hunger and gets used to fasting. A small night snack may be skipped, but they must eat in the morning.
  • The Shulhan Arukh writes clearly that a child under nine is not made to fast at all, not even by delaying meals. If the child wants to be strict, we tell him not to; he eats normally.
  • Children from nine who eat should have proper food with protein and energy, not sweets, which are inappropriate on Yom Kippur.
  • The other restrictions, such as washing and anointing, follow eating: practice begins only from nine.
  • Not wearing leather shoes is easy, so even young children of four, five or six can practise it.

In one line

Under nine, no fasting; at nine and ten, delayed meals; from eleven, try to fast the full day.
