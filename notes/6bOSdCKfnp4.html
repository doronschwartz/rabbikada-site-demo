The halacha

  • Outside Israel there are three customs: every day (the Oriental Sepharadim), only on Yom Tov (generally the Ashkenazim), and on Shabbat and Yom Tov (the Spanish and Portuguese, some Moroccan, Tunisian and North African communities). In Israel almost everyone does it daily.
  • The Gemara assumes a daily misvah: it explains that Birkat Kohanim was dropped from Minhah because a Kohen must not be intoxicated and people drink at lunch. Rambam, Sefer HaHinukh and other Rishonim codify it as daily at Shaharit.
  • Oriental Sepharadim also say it at Minhah on a fast day, when that concern does not apply.
  • Medieval Ashkenazi Rishonim record the Yom Tov-only custom, at least 700 to 800 years old. The first to explain it, to his knowledge, is the Maharil (Germany, 14th century).
  • His first reason: Kohanim took on blessing only after immersing in a mikveh, which was too hard daily in the cold, but was done anyway before Yom Tov.
  • The Bet Yoseph rejects this: a stringency may not lead to a leniency, here the loss of a daily Torah misvah.
  • His second reason: daily Birkat Kohanim would burden and delay the congregation (tirha detsibbura), as some poskim limit Shabbat aliyot to seven. This too is very difficult, for a Torah misvah.

In one line

The Gemara and Rishonim treat Birkat Kohanim as daily; the Ashkenazi Yom Tov-only custom rests on reasons the Bet Yoseph and others find hard.
