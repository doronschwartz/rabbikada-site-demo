Now

Perek Helek is the final or penultimate chapter of Sanhedrin (the penultimate in the Rambam's version), named for its opening: who has a חלק, a portion, in the World to Come. Since the Mishnah raises who has and hasn't a portion, the Rambam takes the opening to discuss what Olam HaBa is, and the actions and beliefs that include or exclude a person, along with Mashiach, techiyat hametim, Gan Eden and Gehinom.

The first Jewish creed

Before the Rambam, Sages told us what Jews believe (Rav Saadia's Emunot veDeot, studied on Monday nights). But no one formulated a creed: these you must believe to be part of the Jewish people. The Rambam was the very first, and "much has been written, Torah and academic, on whether it responded to the cultures around him, to Christianity." It "stuck":

- יגדל, sung on Friday night in many communities, condenses the 13 Principles.

- אני מאמין באמונה שלמה, said after Shacharit (Ashkenazi siddurim and some Sephardi).

- Today, denying one makes you "a kofer."

The halachic stakes

The Rambam writes (we'll see it inside): one who rejects any of the 13 is not part of Israel.

- Shechitah: kosher shechitah must be done by a Yisrael. One who rejects the 13 "isn't a Yisrael", so his meat isn't kosher. "Unbelievable."

- Minyan: seemingly he can't count either. "We don't interrogate people. But if someone publicly renounced a principle, it's a real halachic issue."

The Mishnah

כל ישראל יש להם חלק לעולם הבא, שנאמר: ועמך כלם צדיקים, לעולם יירשו ארץ, נצר מטעי, מעשה ידי להתפאר.

  • ירשו ארץ = ארץ החיים, the metaphorical land above (לולא האמנתי לראות בטוב ה׳ בארץ חיים).
  • נצר מטעי: the planting of Gan Eden: ויטע ה׳ אלהים גן בעדן.
  • The default: every Jew has a portion, commensurate with his deeds, even one executed for Shabbat-breaking, adultery or murder. (Today, with no executions, an unrepented capital sin is "a different question.")

The exceptions

האומר אין תחיית המתים

Some texts add מן התורה (denying it's hinted in the Torah). The Rambam's text lacks it: you must believe in resurrection, not necessarily that it's in the Torah

אין תורה מן השמים

What that means is itself huge. "Those who know British Jewish history will know the greatest rabbinic scandal of the century: the Louis Jacobs affair in the '50s and '60s, over exactly this"

אפיקורוס

Two explanations: a follower of Epicurus's heretical philosophy; or from הפקר, treating the Torah and its Sages as ownerless, free to denigrate

Rabbi Akiva: הקורא בספרים החיצונים

Reading forbidden books

הלוחש על המכה: כל המחלה אשר שמתי במצרים

Using verses as medicine: "the Rambam was very sharp on this"

Abba Shaul: ההוגה את השם באותיותיו

Pronouncing the Divine Name as written, the four letters, instead of Ad-nai

ראיתי לדבר כאן עיקרים רבים באמונה, גדולים ונכבדים עד מאד. "Here is the right place to discuss many great principles of faith."

Five Mistaken Views of Reward

He begins with the wrong answers. Even people of Torah disagree widely on the good that comes from keeping the mitzvot and the harm from transgressing:

…ונשתבשו בה הסברות שבוש גדול, עד שכמעט לא תמצא בשום פנים אדם שנתבאר לו הדבר הזה.

There's such confusion that you'll hardly find anyone clear on it.

"I think not much has changed. Ask the average Jew on the street: you'll get all kinds of answers, but rarely a clear one. There are so many statements (in the Torah, the Sages, different shiurim) that seem to contradict, and no one puts it all together."

"Gan" and "Eden" are two places

The Rambam (in Rav Shilat's and Rav Kafih's text) writes גן ועדן, which the printed abbreviation hides. Many Gemaras treat them as two:

- Berachot: עין לא ראתה אלהים זולתך: Rabbi Levi: wine preserved in its grapes since the six days of creation; Rabbi Shmuel bar Nachmani: that's Eden, which no eye has seen. But Adam was there? He was in the Gan. ונהר יצא מעדן להשקות את הגן: Gan is one thing, Eden another. Eden is reserved for the righteous.

- Another Gemara: Egypt is 1/60 of Kush, the world 1/60 of the Garden, the Garden 1/60 of Eden, Eden 1/60 of Gehinom. "What that means, I can't tell you. But they're two places."

The common denominator

Class answer: they're all physical. "Excellent." Wine, a long-lived king, family, bread on trees: all physical.

What People Should Ask, and What They Do Ask

אבל זה הענין הנפלא ורעיון העולם הבא, מעט תמצא בשום פנים שיעלה על לבו.

Hardly anyone even asks where Olam HaBa fits: is it the goal, or one of the five? Which is the goal, and which the means to it?

What everyone asks instead:

Will the dead rise naked or clothed? In the shrouds they were buried in, or in their fine clothes ("so I can still show off my Prada shoes")? When Mashiach comes, will there be rich and poor, strong and weak?

A dig at Rav Saadia (unnamed): his essay on resurrection asks exactly these, ten questions (what clothes? will I recognise my loved ones?). The Rambam: you can discuss them, but that's not the essence.

In fairness, the Gemara (Sanhedrin 90b) records Queen Cleopatra asking Rabbi Meir whether the dead rise clothed. He said clothed. The Rambam would say he answered because she asked.

"And what age will we be resurrected at? If it's one, the clothes won't fit!" "I'll worry about it when it happens. The Rambam writes elsewhere that no one knows these answers, about Mashiach or resurrection. When it comes, you'll see."

The Parable of the Child

He knows he's going against the masses, so first, an analogy.

A young child is brought to a teacher to learn Torah. It's the greatest good for him, but being young and immature, he doesn't see its value. So the teacher, more mature, must motivate him with things he loves.

"Look at that gadol, how people honour him; learn properly and you'll be like him. We hear this all the time. The Rambam says 'unfortunately'; I'm not sure it's unfortunate. At that age, it's probably what you need to tell yeshivah students."

וכל זה מגונה. All of this is unworthy, but necessary, because people take time to mature. The Sages call it שלא לשמה, not for its own sake, and warned: אל תעשם עטרה להתגדל בהם ולא קרדום לחפור בהם.

The true goal:

אין תכלית לימוד החכמה אלא לדעת אותה, ואין תכלית האמת אלא שתדע שהוא אמת. והתורה אמת, ותכלית ידיעתה לעשותה.

The purpose of wisdom is to know it; the purpose of truth is to know that it's true. The Torah is truth, and the purpose of knowing it is to do it.

"What do I get?"

A mature person mustn't ask what reward will I get for these mitzvot? That's the child asking what will you give me if I read? The question itself shows immaturity, so we answer him according to his level: ענה כסיל כאולתו.

The implication, though he doesn't say it outright: all those Rabbinic statements about wine, bread on trees, family reunions and fire are the sweets, clothes, money and honour. Tell people there's no reward, just learn, and they'll reject the Torah.

Antigonos

אל תהיו כעבדים המשמשין את הרב על מנת לקבל פרס, אלא… שלא על מנת לקבל פרס.

He meant: believe the truth because it's the truth. That's serving from love.

  • במצותיו חפץ מאד: Rabbi Elazar: in His commandments, not in their reward.
  • The Sifrei: I'll learn to be rich, to be called Rabbi, to earn Olam HaBa. לאהבה את ה׳: do everything only from love, not even for Olam HaBa.

The four species. (Told of the Ba'al Shem Tov, or the Vilna Gaon.) Before Sukkot no arba minim could be found; one man in a nearby town had a set. "In Poland you import them; Sephardim, among the date palms, didn't have this problem." The rabbi sent a messenger to pay any sum. The man replied: not for money, only for the reward of the mitzvah. The messenger returned crestfallen. The rabbi was thrilled: Run back! Finally I can test myself, doing a mitzvah for nothing at all. "You can always psychoanalyse the subconscious. But that's the ideal.**"

Why the Sages allowed the incentives

That's the level of Avraham Avinu, who served from love. But the Sages knew it's very hard; few reach it, and slowly. A person does nothing except for a benefit, or to avoid a loss. "We're not naturally altruistic. We can be, with training." Imagine telling your children, your congregation: do mitzvot for nothing.**

So the masses were permitted to keep mitzvot hoping for reward and avoid sin fearing punishment, to strengthen them until they reach completeness, as with the child.

Antigonos was criticised: not for the idea, but for preaching it constantly. His students Tzadok and Baitos concluded there's no reward at all, and broke away.

And the masses lose nothing by serving from fear and hope: it builds strength and habit, from which knowledge of the truth grows, and they come to serve from love. לעולם יעסוק אדם בתורה ובמצות אף שלא לשמן, שמתוך שלא לשמן בא לשמן.

Summary

  1. Perek Helek is the Rambam's philosophical introduction, and the birth of the first Jewish creed, with halachic teeth (shechitah, minyan).
  2. The Mishnah: every Jew has a portion, even the executed, except those who deny resurrection or Torah from heaven, the apikoros, readers of forbidden books, verse-healers, and pronouncers of the Name.
  3. Five mistaken views of the ultimate reward: Gan Eden (wine; Gehinom's fire), Mashiach's days (bread on trees), resurrection (family), this world (Ki Tavo), all combined. Their common thread: they're all physical. People ask about shrouds vs Prada shoes, not where Olam HaBa fits.
  4. Gan and Eden are two places.
  5. The parable of the child: sweets → clothes → money → honour, all she-lo lishmah, necessary but not the goal. Truth is learned because it's true. Those physical descriptions are answers to the immature, at their level.
  6. Serve from love (Antigonos, Avraham), but don't preach it to all; mitoch she-lo lishmah ba lishmah.
