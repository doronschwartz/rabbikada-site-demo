Topic: The rulings of the Shulhan Arukh, the Rema and the Hazon Ish on a self-declared Kohen

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; the Rambam; the Ramakh; the Hazon Ish

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Wednesday 15 April 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Shulhan Arukh rules like the Rambam: one who says he is a Kohen is not believed for the first aliyah, Birkat Kohanim, rabbinic Terumah, Pidyon HaBen and so on.
  • The Rema, for Ashkenazim, follows the Ramakh with a caveat: outside Israel there is no Terumah at all, so there is no concern, and he may be called up first.
  • The Rema adds that outside Israel he may also do Birkat Kohanim. Many disagree, since a non-Kohen doing Birkat Kohanim is a strong prohibition.
  • This leaves difficulties: for Sepharadim a self-declared Kohen seemingly should not be believed, and even by the Rema, in Israel, where rabbinic Terumah exists, he should not be.
  • The Hazon Ish, followed by most contemporary poskim, explains the practice of Sepharadim and Ashkenazim alike: one who says he is a Kohen is believed, is called up first and does Birkat Kohanim.
  • His reason: the Gemara's concern was gain from Terumah, but today, even in Israel, since there is doubt whether anyone is a certain Kohen, Kohanim do not eat Terumah and it is burnt. Lying for the tiny benefit of using it as fuel or feeding it to animals is remote.
  • He adds that we no longer have the records of lineage to monitor who is a Kohen anyway.

In one line

Though the Shulhan Arukh follows the Rambam, today's practice, following the Hazon Ish, is to believe one who says he is a Kohen for the aliyah and Birkat Kohanim.
