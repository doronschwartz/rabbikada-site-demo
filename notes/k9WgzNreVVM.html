Topic: Why the request for rain outside Israel begins on a solar date

Sources: The Gemara (Shemuel's calculation of the solar year); Bet Yoseph, Siman 117, quoting Rabbi David Abudarham

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Outside Israel, in the northern hemisphere, we began asking explicitly for rain ("ten tal umatar livrakhah") from the night of 4 December, the 5th by day. In Sepharadi practice the summer text asks for blessing and the winter text for rain.
  • This is the only date in our halacha set by the civil calendar.
  • From Shemini Aseret we only mention rain (a statement, not a request). In Israel, which needs rain more, the request would have begun right after Sukkot, but the Hakhamim delayed it to 7 Heshvan so that pilgrims could get home dry; that is the practice in Israel today.
  • In Bavel, lower lying and needing rain less, the Hakhamim set the request 60 days after Tekufat Tishrei, the halachic autumn, which depends on the solar year.
  • The Gemara cites Shemuel's solar year of 365 days and six hours, giving seasons of about 91 days and a third. This puts Tekufat Tishrei on 24 September and the request 60 days later on 22 November.
  • The Bet Yoseph, quoting Rabbi David Abudarham (14th century), writes 22 November explicitly, or 23 November when the following February has 29 days, naming the English months.
  • So it stood until the 16th century, when Pope Gregory changed the calendar, to be continued next time.

In one line

We ask for rain on a secular date because the Hakhamim tied it to the solar seasons: 60 days after the halachic autumn.
