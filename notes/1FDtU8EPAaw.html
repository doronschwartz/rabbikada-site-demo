Topic: How the misvah of remembering Amalek is fulfilled today: Sefer Torah or Humash, minyan, pronunciation, and women

Sources: The Gemara; Tosafot; the Rambam; Rav Moshe Feinstein (a teshuvah on Halisah)

Daily Halacha: Purim, Thursday 14 March 2024

The halacha

  • Waging war on Amalek no longer applies in practice, but the misvot not to forget Amalek and to remember him positively still do. The Gemara explains that remembering means verbally.
  • Since a person's memory lasts twelve months, we do this once a year, on the Shabbat before Purim, Shabbat Zakhor.
  • Some Rishonim, such as Tosafot, held it must be read from a Sefer Torah, and some understood with a minyan: waging war on Amalek is a national misvah, so we remember it as a kahal.
  • The Rambam and many others hold the misvah is to remember verbally, so one who reads the pesukim from a Humash, even alone, fulfils it. In the first instance we read from the Sefer Torah with a minyan; if that is not possible, one reads it alone from a Humash.
  • One should hear Zakhor in one's own traditional pronunciation (Sepharadim from Sepharadim, Ashkenazim from Ashkenazim), following the masorah of one's parents and grandparents, and preferably not from others. Still, any accepted pronunciation fulfils the misvah, and there is no need to hear it in all the different havarot.
  • Women are not really obligated, since the root of the misvah is waging war, in which women were not obligated. A woman who wants to fulfil it may read Parashat Zakhor from a Humash on the Shabbat morning before Purim.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: remembering is verbal, and memory lasts twelve months.
  • Tosafot: the reading must be from a Sefer Torah.
  • The Rambam: remembering verbally suffices, even from a Humash and alone.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein on Halisah: the declaration the Torah requires need not be said in every pronunciation, only the accepted way; the same applies to Zakhor.

In one line

Hear Zakhor from a Sefer Torah with a minyan in your family's pronunciation if you can, but reading it from a Humash, in any accepted pronunciation, fulfils the misvah, and women are strictly not obligated.
