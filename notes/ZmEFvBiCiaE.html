Topic: Meat and wine customs in the Nine Days, and why haircuts, laundry and showers are permitted this year

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • By the law of the Gemara, meat and wine are forbidden only at the final meal after midday before Tisha BeAv, which this year falls on Shabbat and so does not apply.
  • The Shulhan Arukh brings three customs from the Rishonim: refraining all three weeks, all nine days, or the week of Tisha BeAv. Ashkenazim generally keep all nine days.
  • Sepharadi communities differ: nine days, the week of Tisha BeAv, only Erev Tisha BeAv, or nine days excluding Rosh Hodesh. Keep your custom; one without a custom should follow the common Sepharadi practice of all nine days. The Shulhan Arukh writes sharply against breaching a communal custom.
  • Haircuts, shaving, laundry and showers are forbidden by the Gemara only in the week of Tisha BeAv.
  • When Tisha BeAv falls on Shabbat and is postponed to Sunday, the Shulhan Arukh brings two opinions: either there is no week of Tisha BeAv, or it is the whole preceding week.
  • This year (2025) Tisha BeAv falls on Sunday itself, not postponed, so according to all opinions there is no week of Tisha BeAv.
  • So for Sepharadim haircuts, shaving and laundry are permitted right up to Tisha BeAv, and showers until the Friday before. Ashkenazim, who began some of these from the Three Weeks or the Nine Days, should keep their customs.
