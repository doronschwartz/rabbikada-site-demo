Topic: Sifting flour, kneading and separating hallah on Yom Tov

Sources: The Hakhamim; the Mishkan

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 24 June 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Meraked, sifting, was done in the Mishkan to remove impurities from ground flour and is forbidden on Shabbat. On Yom Tov the Hakhamim also forbade sifting flour for the first time, normally done on an industrial scale.
  • But flour already sifted once may be sifted again on Yom Tov, a household-scale act. So flour bought before Yom Tov in which one notices impurities, or slight infestation, may be sifted on Yom Tov, with a small shinui (for example not sifting in the usual way into a bowl) to recall that sifting unsifted flour is forbidden.
  • Lash, kneading, is fully permitted on Yom Tov, since it is normally done at home on a small scale. One may knead dough from the bought flour and bake it.
  • The complex Shabbat laws of kneading do not apply on Yom Tov. One may mix aubergine, tahini or hummus with oil, combine avocado with lemon and onion, or mash liver and egg with oil.
  • If one made bread before Shabbat without separating hallah, one cannot simply do so on Shabbat. On Yom Tov, when baking hallot, the Hakhamim allowed separating hallah, even with the berakhah.
  • One may not burn the separated piece on Yom Tov, even if one usually does during the week, since burning it is not for food. Set it aside and dispose of it respectfully.

In one line

On Yom Tov already-sifted flour may be re-sifted with a shinui, all kneading is permitted, and hallah may be separated with a berakhah but not burnt.
