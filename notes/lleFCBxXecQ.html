Sefer: Derech Hashem by Rabbi Moshe Hayim Luzzatto (the Ramhal) Section: Part 1, Chapter 3, paragraphs 12-13 and Chapter 4, paragraphs 1-2 Shiur: From Dayan Daniel Kada's Derech Hashem class for the Hazak girls' group (Wednesday evenings), Wednesday 3 February 2021. It fills a gap in his Derech Hashem series at Tiferet Eyal, where these paragraphs were taught but not recorded.

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Questions on the world of souls

The class opened with questions on the previous paragraphs. A participant asked what happens to someone who is truly bad. The Rabbi answered that if a person is so rotten that nothing can be repaired, like the apple bruised all over, nothing happens to him: he has no future identity, so there is no point in cleansing. Gehinnom and the twelve months of Kaddish are for those who will reach Olam HaBa but still have some bruises. Kaddish is "not magic": a parent who left a child ready to sanctify Hashem's name is shown by that to have been a worthy person.

Another asked what comes after the twelve months. Olam HaNeshamot, the world of souls, includes both Gan Eden and Gehinnom. Gehinnom lasts at most twelve months; the soul then waits in Gan Eden until the resurrection, however long that is.

Paragraph 12: the soul set free

The Rabbi summarised the last two paragraphs of Chapter 3 before reading them quickly. Besides being a waiting room, the world of souls helps the soul prepare for the resurrection.

While the soul is in the body, it is "darkened and dim." Even the good deeds we do give it perfection that cannot yet shine, and this is not the soul's fault but the body's. Both lose: the body is not refined, and the soul cannot do the very thing it was made for, which is to elevate the body.

The Rabbi drew out a line he called beautiful: every creature feels complete when it can do what it was designed to do. When we find our calling in life and are able to do it, that brings great happiness; when we cannot, we are frustrated. So too the soul. In the world of souls, free of the body, it begins to strengthen itself and to shine according to its deeds, so that it is ready for its task at the resurrection.

Paragraph 13: an immediate elevation

In this life, every good deed should elevate the body, but because of the decree after Adam's sin it happens only in potential. When the soul returns to the body at the resurrection, it will come with all its light and elevate the body at once: "a whole different being." That does not mean you stay at that level; you continue to rise, "elevation after elevation", each person according to his deeds.

The picture so far

The Rabbi then summarised Part 1 up to here: Chapter 1, the principles of belief in Hashem; Chapter 2, the purpose of creation, that Hashem gives us the chance to choose the good and so enjoy His goodness; Chapter 3, man made of body and soul, this world as the time of work and the world to come as the time of reward. Had Adam chosen the good, the body would have been elevated at once; because of the sin, man and the world go through death and renewal, and meanwhile the soul waits in the world of souls.

Questions

  • How can we keep growing in Olam HaBa without a body pulling us down? The Rabbi said he could not explain how, but there will be a constant feeling of growth. The Gemara in Berakhot says the righteous have no rest in this world or the next, which the commentators explain as continual growth.
  • His own open question. If the world of souls lets the soul recover, why does one soul get two thousand years there and another a hundred? Participants suggested that time there may not be like our time, or that souls today may need less repair. The Rabbi found these plausible, though the second has to be squared with the Gemara's idea that generations decline, and said he would keep thinking about it.

Chapter 4: man's situation in this world

Chapter 4 is called "Man's situation in this world and the paths before him, in detail." Until now everything was abstract; now the Ramhal turns to practice and to why the misvot matter.

Paragraph 1: there are two things to examine, man himself and the world around him. Paragraph 2 begins with man. He is one being made of two opposites, but the material side clearly dominates. The Hovot HaLevavot already noted that no other creature takes so long to become independent: for years a child is entirely physical and self-centred, and only around bar or bat misvah can it begin to grasp ideas and be altruistic. Even so, a person who trains his intellect can control his urges and choose the path of the intellect.

This explains, the Rabbi said, the Gemara's teaching that the heavenly court does not punish before the age of twenty, since until then a person is still developing. At twelve or thirteen we become obligated, because society needs people to take responsibility, but full responsibility comes at twenty.

A participant asked whether misvot done before then count. The Rabbi answered that reward is not "you do X, you get Y": if you have refined yourself, "I don't think that can be taken away from you." A child who controls himself and fasts on Yom Kippur has done something special. We are rewarded for things we are not obligated in, as women are for hearing shofar or taking the lulav.

Since body and soul will never finally separate, the soul must strengthen itself against the darkness of the body, and against a world that is also material. If we make no effort, the default is to fall. From paragraph 4, the Ramhal will show how the misvot help.

Is the material world simply negative? A participant raised the Nazir. The Rabbi agreed that the Nazir is criticised: the Torah set the boundaries, and outside them you are free, for example to enjoy kosher wine. The problem of the Nazir, and of extremism in general, is failing to tell apart what is forbidden from what can be used well. "I can have a good time and not fall into sin."

Summary

  • The world of souls is a waiting room where the soul, free of the body, begins to shine.
  • At the resurrection the soul will elevate the body at once, and growth continues for ever.
  • Chapter 4 turns to practice: man and his world are material, so the soul must make an effort.
  • There is no heavenly punishment before twenty, but good done earlier is never lost.
  • The misvot are boundaries within which we may enjoy the world.
