Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 271, seifim 10-15 Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 2 November 2023

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

Seif 10: a full cup, not pagum

Kiddush is made over a cup that is full (malei) and not pagum.

Full does not mean overflowing. People think the cup must spill over. Nowhere does it say so. Full means it looks full, close to the top. The only time we deliberately spill wine is at Havdalah on Mosei Shabbat, based on the Gemara that a house where wine is not poured like water does not see blessing. Making the Kiddush cup overflow every Friday night and throwing the wine away is, in the Rabbi's view, a waste (bal tashhit). If it overflows a little and you drink it later, that is fine.

Pagum means wine that has already been drunk from, for example the leftover from Friday night's Kiddush. It is not respectful to do a misvah with leftovers. How do you fix pagum wine? Neither the Rambam nor the Shulhan Arukh mentions a fix. Later sources say you add more wine, and the halacha today is to be lenient and do that. Pouring the leftover back into the bottle only helps when the bottle is mostly full; if little is left in the bottle and you pour back the same amount, it may make the whole bottle pagum. The cleanest solution is to pour off what you need before anyone drinks, so it never becomes pagum.

The same rules as the cup for Birkat HaMazon: - Rinse the cup inside and out (hadahah and shetifah). Some say a cup clean from the dishwasher does not need it, but the custom is to rinse it anyway. At the Seder, rinsing before the first cup covers all four. - Look at the wine (noten einav bo) while saying the berakhah, to give the cup your full focus. Today we are less particular because most people read from a book.

Standing or sitting. The Shulhan Arukh says Vayekhulu is said standing, because it is testimony to creation. The Rema says it is better to sit for Kiddush. The principle: whenever one person fulfils the obligation for others, everyone should ideally be seated together, which makes them one fixed group. That is why we sit for the berakhot on the Megillah. Shofar and Sefirat HaOmer are different because standing is part of those misvot. Ashkenazim stand for Havdalah to escort Shabbat on its way out.

The Mishnah Berurah adds that even those who stand must stay in one place and pay attention. What is wrong is people walking back and forth from the kitchen during Kiddush. The Rema also says to glance at the Shabbat candles when beginning Kiddush, which is why many Ashkenazim look at them.

Seif 11: only one cup of wine

If you only have one cup of wine for both Friday night and Shabbat morning, make Kiddush at night, but before drinking pour some off into a second cup and drink from that one. The original cup stays untouched, not pagum, for the morning. The Rabbi pointed out that the Shulhan Arukh does not suggest topping up pagum wine here; he has you pour off before drinking.

A related point came up in the discussion. On Friday night, bread is no worse than wine for Kiddush; we simply prefer wine. On Shabbat morning, though, the Kiddush is so short that over bread alone it would look like an ordinary HaMosi, and there would be no real Kiddush at all. That is why wine matters more for the morning Kiddush (see Siman 272:9 next week).

Someone asked whether everyone at the table must drink. There is no such halacha. It is a nice extra, as we will see next week; the misvah is fulfilled when the required amount is drunk.

Seif 12: if you washed before Kiddush

The Shulhan Arukh (following the Rambam): if someone washed his hands for bread before Kiddush, that shows he values the bread more than the wine. Kiddush has to be made over something important to you, so he makes Kiddush over the bread instead.

The Rema (following the Rosh): washing first does not show a preference. He may have washed first to keep the gap between Kiddush and the meal as short as possible. So Kiddush is still over the wine. Some Ashkenazim, notably German Jews, still do this: wash, make Kiddush, drink, then HaMosi.

The Rabbi took the chance to correct a common idea. There is no halacha forbidding speech between washing and HaMosi; the rule is not to take your mind off the meal (hesah hadaat). Answering an email on your phone is worse than a word in passing on the way back to the table.

Seifim 13-14: how much to drink, and who drinks it

How much. Someone must drink a cheekful (melo lugmav), which is the majority of a reviit. A reviit is 86 ml, so a cheekful is about 44 ml: "very little." This amount begins to settle a person, which is why it is the measure. He said there is no requirement to drink half the cup; that idea comes from the four cups on Pesah.

Who. Everyone agrees one cheekful must be drunk. The question is by whom:

  1. The Geonim (strictest): one person may drink the cheekful, but the one making Kiddush must taste something himself.
  2. The first opinion in the Shulhan Arukh: one person at the table drinks the whole cheekful. Ten people each taking a sip does not add up.
  3. The lenient opinion: the sips of everyone at the table combine.

The basis is shomea keoneh, hearing is like saying. Everyone around the table counts as part of the Kiddush, so one of them can drink. The Rabbi noted that nobody would accept a stranger walking in from outside to drink on your behalf.

The common case. You are a guest and the host only sips. The Rabbi's advice: drink a cheekful yourself. Since the host tasted, you have fulfilled even the view of the Geonim. Relying on everyone's sips combined is only after the fact, and has a second problem: drinking must be done within a short time, and by the time a cup goes round ten people too long has passed. If small cups are poured out and everyone drinks at once, that problem falls away.

Cup size. The Rabbi said that to his memory the cup must hold a reviit, though he wanted to check the source. What is certain is that one may not make Kiddush on a tiny lehaim cup and then drink three of them to make up a cheekful.

Seif 15: talking before drinking

If someone made Kiddush and then, before drinking, spoke about something unrelated ("Is everything all right in Israel?"), the Kiddush itself is done. He only repeats Borei Peri HaGefen. Talking about the drinking itself, such as asking someone to be careful not to spill, is not an interruption.

Practical summary

  • Fill the cup so it looks full; do not make it overflow except at Havdalah.
  • Use wine nobody has drunk from; if you must reuse it, pour off before drinking or top it up with fresh wine.
  • Rinse the Kiddush cup inside and out, and look at it while saying the berakhah.
  • Whether standing or sitting, stay in one place and listen together.
  • One person, ideally the one making Kiddush, should drink about 44 ml. If the host only sips, drink a cheekful yourself.
  • If you talk between the berakhah and drinking, repeat only Borei Peri HaGefen.
