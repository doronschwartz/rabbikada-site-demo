Topic: Why haircuts and shaving are forbidden on Hol HaMoed, and the exceptions

Sources: The Mishnah

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 21 October 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • By the principles so far, a haircut or shave should be permitted on Hol HaMoed as a bodily need, even if it counts as melekhet uman (which is not clear).
  • Yet the Mishnah says the Hakhamim forbade haircuts on Hol HaMoed, concerned that people busy before the festival would postpone their haircut to Hol HaMoed, when they have more time, and so come into Yom Tov unkempt, detracting from the simhah of the festival.
  • The Mishnah permits one who arrives from a journey on Hol HaMoed, having had no chance beforehand. Journeys today are not so long, but this year he applied it to the hostages released shortly after Sukkot: they would certainly have been allowed, never having had the opportunity.
  • The prohibition applies to the hair of the head and shaving the face, not to cutting hair elsewhere on the body.
  • Still to discuss: whether one who shaves daily and shaved before the festival may shave again, and a halakah (upsherin) on Hol HaMoed.

Sources quoted

  • The Mishnah: haircuts are forbidden on Hol HaMoed, except for one returning from a journey.

In one line

Haircuts and shaves are forbidden on Hol HaMoed so that people do them before the festival, except for one who had no opportunity.
