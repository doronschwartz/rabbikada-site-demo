Topic: The custom of eating dairy on Shavuot, its reason and its limits

Sources: Sefer HaMinhagim; the Kol Bo; the Rema; the Noam Elimelekh; Hakham Ovadiah; Keter Shem Tov

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Thursday 26 May 2022

The halacha

  • Eating dairy on Shavuot is not in the Torah or Talmud. It first appears around the 14th century, in Sefer HaMinhagim or the Kol Bo.
  • The Rema's reason: to recall the two loaves offered on Shavuot. A dairy course with bread was followed by a meat course, which required a fresh loaf, so two loaves were on the table.
  • So the custom never replaced the Misvah of eating meat (or chicken) and wine on Yom Tov. The Rishonim speak of dairy as a late afternoon snack, not a main meal. Making all of Shavuot dairy is incorrect.
  • Dairy may be a first course before the meat meal, or later once one's usual waiting time has passed.
  • The Noam Elimelekh (Rabbi Elimelekh of Lizhensk) held one need not wait the usual time between meat and milk on Shavuot. Hakham Ovadiah rightly rejects this completely: a custom of 600 or 700 years cannot override the halacha, so one keeps one's usual wait.
  • The Keter Shem Tov records that the Spanish and Portuguese communities of London and Amsterdam never adopted this custom.

Sources quoted

  • Sefer HaMinhagim or the Kol Bo: the first mention of the custom.
  • The Rema: dairy recalls the two loaves.
  • Hakham Ovadiah: rejects shortening the wait between meat and milk.
  • Keter Shem Tov: not the Spanish and Portuguese custom.

In one line

Dairy on Shavuot is a custom alongside, not instead of, a meat meal, and it never shortens the wait between meat and milk.
