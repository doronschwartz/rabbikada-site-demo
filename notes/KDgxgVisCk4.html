Topic: The sukkah as exile outside and home inside

Sources: The Midrash

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Monday 20 September 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Midrash links the sukkah to galut: after Yom Kippur we leave our homes for voluntary exile in frail huts.
  • The halacha reflects this: the sukkah must be a temporary dwelling, its roof not so thick as to keep out rain, with more shade than sun. It represents human vulnerability.
  • We would expect the inside to be bare, with broken chairs and an old table. Instead the halacha wants it decorated, with fruits hung from the roof, the best furniture, proper plates and cutlery, and more elaborate meals.
  • The message: the walls and leaking roof show our uncertainty in exile, but within our lives we must make things warm, bright and joyful.
  • Jews have survived every exile by carrying the spirit of home wherever they are, keeping optimism and happiness in the Jewish home whatever is happening outside.

In one line

The sukkah's frail walls stand for exile, and its beautiful interior for the warm Jewish home that has carried us through it.
