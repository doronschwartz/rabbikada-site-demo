Topic: The three separate obligations within sedakah, and the yearly minimum

Sources: The Torah, Devarim and Bereshit; the Gemara; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Monday 31 January 2022

The halacha

  • Sedakah needs clear, orderly treatment: to whom to give, how much, what kind of obligation, and how to ensure it reaches the right people.
  • There are three separate concepts, not to be mixed up: proactively giving a certain amount each year; not refusing one who asks; and maaser kesafim, a tenth of one's income.
  • The Torah in Devarim gives both a positive command, "open your hand to your brother", and a negative one, "do not harden your heart or close your hand". The Gemara says sedakah weighs as much as all the other misvot.
  • The Rambam writes that one who gives sedakah properly shows he is of the seed of Avraham, whom Hashem chose so that he would command his descendants to keep His way, doing sedakah and justice.
  • The first two are certainly Torah obligations. The halachic status and origin of maaser is unclear and will be discussed.
  • The proactive obligation, from the Gemara and codified in the Shulhan Arukh, is a third of a shekel a year: about 6 grams of silver, roughly 3 to 4 pounds or about 15 shekels, depending on the price of silver. Every person must give this even unasked.
  • The maximum is a fifth of one's income, with details to follow.

In one line

Sedakah comprises a small yearly Torah obligation to give unasked, a duty not to refuse one who asks, and maaser, each a separate concept.
