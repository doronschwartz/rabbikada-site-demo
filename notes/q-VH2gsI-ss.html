Topic: Electric light for Havdalah, Shabbat candles and the Menorah

Sources: Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Havdalah requires a naked flame, so electricity is certainly not acceptable for it.
  • Shabbat candles are for shalom bayit and enjoying the meal. Many poskim, Hakham Ovadiah among them, regard electric light as a ner (the wires as wicks, the current as fuel), so if there is no other choice one may light for Shabbat with electricity and say the berakhah.
  • This seems to apply to a filament that becomes red hot, not to LEDs.
  • So some poskim advise switching off the electric light, lighting the Shabbat candles, and then switching it back on, so that the candles clearly give light in their own right.
  • For Hanukkah some poskim, though not the prevalent view, allow electricity with a berakhah when there is no choice.
  • Hakham Ovadiah and others say Hanukkah is different: we commemorate the miracle of the oil, so physical fuel is needed. Wax is acceptable, electricity is not.
  • One with no choice, for example on a plane, should light something electric, such as a torch, as a reminder of the miracle but without a berakhah.

In one line

Electricity is never valid for Havdalah, may serve for Shabbat candles in need, and for the Menorah only as a reminder without a berakhah.
