Topic: Why Hallah may not be separated on Shabbat, and what to do if it was forgotten

Sources: The Mishnah (Bameh Madlikin); Hazon Ovadiah; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Hallah, Wednesday 13 July 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Mishnah said on Friday night lists three things to ask at home before Shabbat: have you lit the candles, made an eruv, and tithed (isartem)?
  • Tithing on Shabbat is rabbinically forbidden, derived from makkeh bepatish, the final hammer blow: tithing completes the food and makes it fit to eat. This matters especially in Israel.
  • Ashkenazim apply terumot and maasrot outside Israel too; according to Hazon Ovadiah, produce that has left Israel is no longer obligated.
  • But Hallah applies everywhere, and the poskim say it is like tithing, completing the food, so it may not be taken on Shabbat. Though Hallah can be taken after baking, one cannot quickly take it at the Friday night table.
  • If one forgot, outside Israel, where Hallah is purely rabbinic, one may eat as much as one wishes, leave a small piece, and after Shabbat separate it with the berakhah and "harei zo Hallah", which works retroactively.
  • In Israel this is not allowed, and one would not be able to eat the hallot.

Sources quoted

  • Mishnah: "isartem", tithing must be done before Shabbat.
  • Hazon Ovadiah: Israeli produce abroad is exempt from terumot and maasrot.
