Topic: Selling a Sefer Torah to make a marriage possible

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 3 February 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Three components so far: the misvah of marriage itself (even without children), "Peru urevu" (a son and a daughter), and Shevet (continuing beyond that).
  • The Gemara forbids selling a Sefer Torah, and says no blessing comes from the money. The Shulhan Arukh forbids selling one even to buy tefillin or mezuzot.
  • There are only two exceptions: to fund Torah study (one's own or others'), and to make a marriage possible.
  • The Rishonim dispute the second exception three ways: only for a man who has not yet fulfilled "Peru urevu"; also for one who has, to have more children; or even for a marriage where children are impossible, because of the independent misvah of marriage. The Shulhan Arukh brings two of these and the final ruling is not entirely clear, but many authorities allow it for any marriage.
  • Many Rishonim say it applies to marrying off a woman too, which supports the idea that there is some misvah for a woman to marry.
  • The purpose of a Sefer Torah is study and bringing people into the world to keep it, which is why these two exceptions exist.

In one line

A Sefer Torah may be sold only for Torah study or to make a marriage possible, a measure of how important marriage and children are.
