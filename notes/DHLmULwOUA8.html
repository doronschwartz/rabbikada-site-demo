Topic: Changes to the Kedushah and to Sim Shalom on Tisha BeAv

Sources: The Abudarham; Hakham Ovadiah; the Gemara; the Kaf HaHayim; the Arizal; the Beit Yoseph

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Thursday 31 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • All year Sepharadim begin the Kedushah with "Nakdishakh VeNaarisakh", likening our praise to the angels' pleasant speech; Ashkenazim say "Nekadesh et Shimkha", without a specific reference to the angels.
  • The Abudarham, about 600 years ago, writes that the Sepharadi custom on Tisha BeAv at Shaharit is to say "Nekadesh" like the Ashkenazim.
  • Hakham Ovadiah brings a reason: the Gemara says that after the Temple was destroyed the angels, metaphorically, have four wings instead of six, so we refer to them less directly. At Minhah the usual text returns.
  • Most Sepharadim today say the regular text. The Kaf HaHayim says to keep it, as the Arizal taught. But the Spanish and Portuguese siddurim have had "Nekadesh" for hundreds of years, and it was the custom in Baghdad; to his knowledge the Ben Ish Hai does not discuss it.
  • Second, also from the Abudarham: Sim Shalom is replaced by a short blessing, "bless Your people Israel with peace... Blessed are You, Hashem, who makes peace".
  • The Beit Yoseph rejects this as changing the fixed text of a blessing, and many Sepharadim kept Sim Shalom.
  • The Spanish and Portuguese say the short blessing at both Arvit and Shaharit, though the Abudarham mentions only Shaharit; at Minhah Sim Shalom returns.

Sources quoted

  • The Abudarham: "Nekadesh" and the shortened final blessing on Tisha BeAv.
  • Hakham Ovadiah, from the Gemara: the angels' wings reduced from six to four.
  • The Kaf HaHayim: keep the regular text, following the Arizal.
  • The Beit Yoseph: one should not change the text of a blessing.

In one line

On Tisha BeAv the Spanish and Portuguese say the Ashkenazi-style Kedushah at Shaharit and a shortened final blessing, customs going back to the Abudarham.
