Topic: The basis and definition of forbidden work on Hol HaMoed

Sources: The Torah; the Gemara; Rashi; the Rambam; the Ramban; the Mishnah

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Tuesday 3 October 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Torah forbids "melekhet avodah" on the first and last days of the festival but does not say so of Hol HaMoed, implying work is allowed. Yet Hol HaMoed is called mikra kodesh, with its own festival offerings, so it has kedushah and some work is forbidden.
  • In brief: on Shabbat and Yom Kippur all 39 melakhot are forbidden; on Yom Tov melekhet avodah is forbidden but melakhah for food is permitted; on Hol HaMoed, work involving tirhah (bother) is forbidden unless it is for the festival or prevents a loss.
  • The Gemara cites verses: Rashi and some Rishonim hold the prohibition is from the Torah; the Rambam holds the verses are hints and it is rabbinic; the Ramban takes a middle view. Either way it is definitely forbidden.
  • The Mishnah says one who disrespects the moadim, meaning Hol HaMoed, loses his standing, since Hol HaMoed is part of keeping the festivals.
  • Tirhah has two meanings. First, work needing concentration and skill, maaseh uman, such as repairing a car.
  • Second, work that takes a long time.
  • Work that is neither is permitted even if not for the festival: lighting a match, or cleaning stains on the carpet yourself (not a professional company), even in a room not used on Yom Tov.

In one line

On Hol HaMoed, work that needs professional skill or takes a long time is forbidden; simple quick tasks are allowed.
