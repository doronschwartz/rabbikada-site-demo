Topic: Seven differences between Sephardim and Ashkenazim in sitting in the sukkah, and the one principle behind them Source: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Sukkah, with the Rema; the Rambam, Hilchot Sukkah, Chapter 6 Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada at the Shasha minyan, Sunday 19 September 2021, the day before Sukkot

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Seven questions, one answer

"In yeshivah we're always told: if you have seven questions and seven answers, it's okay. If you have seven questions and one answer, it's much better." The Rabbi laid out seven differences between Ashkenazim and Sephardim in the misvah of sitting in the sukkah, then offered one principle that explains them all. He asked the group to note the names of the authorities on each side, because "they'll all add up".

The seven differences

1. When is the berakhah made? The Gemara (Sukkah 45b-46a) says that whenever one goes into the sukkah one makes a berakhah, and the Rambam rules so (Hilchot Sukkah 6:12). It says nothing about eating. The Yemenites still make the berakhah on entering the sukkah. Rabbenu Tam, the grandson of Rashi in twelfth-century France, held that the essence of the misvah is eating, so one blesses only when eating. The Shulhan Arukh records that the custom has become to bless only on eating. In practice Sephardim bless only when eating a meal of bread, more than a kabeisah (about 56 grams).

2. Rain on the first night. The Shulhan Arukh rules that one who is uncomfortable is exempt all seven days and nights, so in rain one need not eat in the sukkah even on the first night. If it stops, go out and eat. The Rosh, followed by the Rema, holds that on the first night one must eat a kazayit in the sukkah even in the rain, which is why Ashkenazim wait (the Mishnah Berurah says until midnight). The Ashkenazi practice is to eat a kazayit there, then go back indoors to finish the meal, relax and sleep. For them eating can stand apart from dwelling.

A note on sleep: if rain drives you indoors in the night and then stops, once you are in bed and undressed you need not go back out. "In a place like England, you wouldn't get much sleep."

3. Which berakhah first on Hol HaMoed? At a meal without Kiddush, the Shulhan Arukh says Leshev BaSukkah and then Hamosi. The Rema says Hamosi and then Leshev BaSukkah.

4. Standing or sitting for Kiddush. All year Sephardim sit for Kiddush, so that those listening are joined together as a group (and likewise for Havdalah). On Sukkot, however, the Shulhan Arukh says to stand for Kiddush, say Leshev BaSukkah, and only then sit. The Rema says to make Kiddush sitting. If Kiddush is followed only by cake and not a bread meal, there is no Leshev BaSukkah, so one sits as usual.

5. A Shabbat day meal with no sukkah. At night you must find a way to eat in a sukkah. But someone without a sukkah on Shabbat or Yom Tov day seems stuck between seudat Shabbat and the sukkah. This happens, the Rabbi noted, to people in flats who go to a communal sukkah for the first nights. The solution: the obligation of a Shabbat meal is fulfilled with a kazayit of bread (Mishnah Berurah, Siman 291), and the sukkah is required only for more than a kabeisah. So eat a kazayit but less than a kabeisah of bread, outside the sukkah. The Hida and the Ginat Veradim rule this way. Rabbenu Avigdor, an Ashkenazi Rishon, forbids it: just as Shabbat makes any eating "fixed" for the laws of maaser, so it makes even a small meal fixed for the sukkah. The Eliyah Rabbah follows him.

6. Why a kabeisah? Everywhere else in the Torah, "eating" means a kazayit. Why does the sukkah require a kabeisah?

7. Why leshev? Some berakhot use al ("al netilat yadayim") and others the infinitive ("lehaniah tefillin", "leshev basukkah"). Is there a reason?

The principle: dwelling or eating

For the Rambam, the Rif and most Rishonim, teshevu, "you shall dwell", means exactly that: living in the sukkah. Eat, drink, sleep, relax, make your phone calls, do your emails. "It's wrong to think, the sukkah is a holy place, I can't do my work." Even permitted work on Hol HaMoed should be done there. For Rabbenu Tam and the Rosh, the essence of the misvah is eating. Now the seven points fall into place:

  • Berakhah. If dwelling is the misvah, you bless on entering, as the Gemara and the Rambam say. If eating is the misvah, you bless on eating. Our custom follows Rabbenu Tam out of doubt over berakhot, but "really, for us, teshevu means teshevu." The Rambam means a real stay, at least something like half an hour, not stepping out to the bathroom and back.
  • Rain. If eating is the essence, the first-night obligation to eat can survive even when dwelling is excused.
  • Order. If the berakhah on the sukkah really belongs to the meal, Hamosi comes first and Leshev follows without being an interruption. If it belongs to the dwelling, Leshev comes first.
  • Kiddush. We want to bless before doing the misvah. If the misvah is sitting, we must bless before we sit, so we stand. If the misvah is eating, sitting down is fine as long as we have not yet eaten.
  • Shabbat. If eating and dwelling are one, a meal that Shabbat makes fixed is a fixed sukkah meal. If eating is just one part of dwelling, a fixed meal does not yet make a fixed dwelling.
  • Kabeisah. A kazayit is a measure of eating. A kabeisah is what turns eating into dwelling, a meal where you settle and linger. The Torah's word ezrah, citizen, also suggests something settled.
  • Leshev. One view among the Rishonim is that al marks a one-off act and the infinitive a continuing misvah, though the Rabbi noted every explanation has exceptions. A side question about tallit katan led him to note that the Shulhan Arukh's berakhah on a new tallit katan is lehitatef besisit, and that after two hours without it, as after swimming, a new berakhah is needed. He noted that the Ben Ish Hai rules al misvat sisit.

A thought on geography

"I've got no proof for this," he said, but consider where these Rishonim lived. The Rambam and the Rif were in southern Spain and North Africa; Rabbenu Tam in France. "Every year I get so upset, we can't do the misvah of sukkah properly" in England, where even on a dry day everything is damp. In Spain, Bavel and the Land of Israel you can truly live in a sukkah.

He added that the Rambam in the Moreh Nevuchim explains why Sukkot is in Tishrei: it is the perfect season, neither too hot nor too cold. That is a different answer from the well-known one, that we go out in the autumn to show we do it for the misvah and not for pleasure.

Practical summary

  • Today we bless Leshev BaSukkah only on a bread meal of more than a kabeisah.
  • If it rains, even on the first night, Sephardim are exempt; eat in the sukkah when it stops.
  • Stand for Kiddush, say Leshev BaSukkah, then sit; at a meal without Kiddush, say Leshev before Hamosi.
  • Without a sukkah on Shabbat day, eat a kazayit of bread but less than a kabeisah, outside.
  • Live in the sukkah as much as you can: relaxing, working and talking there are part of the misvah.
