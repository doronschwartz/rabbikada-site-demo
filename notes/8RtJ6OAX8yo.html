The halacha

  • This year Shavuot falls on Mosei Shabbat (sunset in London 9.15, Shabbat out 10.15). Three topics: a regular year, Mosei Shabbat, and preparing from Shabbat. Today is the regular year.
  • There is a Misvah to add from the weekday to Shabbat and Hag, which is why they are brought in early, and this would seem to apply to Shavuot too.
  • In the 16th century Rabbi Avraham Horowitz argued that the Omer requires seven complete weeks, so bringing Shavuot in early cuts the 49th day short and Kiddush must wait for nightfall. This is not in the Shulhan Arukh.
  • A century later the Taz extended it to Arvit, which in London means about 10.15, and later further north.
  • Rabbi Yoseph Yuspa of Frankfurt rejected it at the time: people prayed and made Kiddush early, then learned all night. The Rishonim require nightfall only for Massah on Pesah and the Sukkah, never for Shavuot, so Kiddush may be made from sunset.
  • Rabbi Ephraim Laniado (18th-century Syria) and Rabbi Hayim Palagi (19th-century Turkey) agreed. Some Sepharadim wait, but most communities were not particular.
  • Hakham Ovadiah is lenient, especially in long northern summer days: pray Arvit before sunset (say 8.30) and make Kiddush and eat after sunset (9.15). This is the custom of many Sepharadim.
  • Shavuot on Mosei Shabbat adds complications, discussed tomorrow.

In one line

In a regular year Sepharadim may pray Arvit before sunset and make Kiddush right after it; Mosei Shabbat comes next.
