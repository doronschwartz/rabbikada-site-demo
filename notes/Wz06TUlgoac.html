Topic: Counting the Omer when unsure of the night, and why the Diaspora counts only once

Sources: The Gemara

Daily Halacha: Sefirat HaOmer, Thursday 22 April 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The case: someone alone (say on Friday night, unable to ask) who does not know whether tonight is the 25th or the 26th. Why not say the Berakhah and count both, since one is right?
  • The answer lies in a wider question. Outside Israel we keep two days of Pesah and Shavuot; the Gemara says the custom continues even though today we know the date. Consistency would require two counts each night, ending with 49 or 48.
  • A fundamental principle of the Omer: a count where you do not know the number is not a count, just as counting money is pointless without a conclusive total. So outside Israel we count only once.
  • Likewise the person unsure of the night should not say the Berakhah on both counts. He should count both possibilities without a Berakhah, since arguably one cannot fulfil the misvah without knowing the exact night.
  • When saying the Berakhah one must already know which night it is, not say it and rely on others to tell him.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: the custom of keeping two days of the festivals outside Israel continues even today.

In one line

A count without a known number is no count, so the Diaspora counts once and one who is unsure counts both nights without a Berakhah.
