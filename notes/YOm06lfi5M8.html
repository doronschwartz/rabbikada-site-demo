Topic: Further limits on treating silence as an admission of tainted lineage, and practical conclusions

Sources: The Rambam; the Raavad; the Ramban; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Wednesday 25 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • So far: for the Rambam an investigation neutralises the allegation; for the Raavad silence during an argument is today a good sign, though outside an argument one must object.
  • Third, the Ramban: silence counts only where there is already some rumour about the family (not two witnesses, which require an investigation anyway). Rumour plus silence requires a full investigation.
  • Fourth, also the Ramban, reconciling the Gemara that praises silence: it counts only if the person objected on other occasions. One who always stays silent is following the Gemara; one who objected last month and is silent now has shown a contrast. These opinions are all brought in the halacha.
  • Fifth, the Rishonim: only an allegation against oneself, not against one's children, which one is not expected to answer.
  • In practice it is very rare: it applies only when there is already a rumour, he objected before but not this time, the allegation is against him, and it is not during an argument. Then he would need to be investigated before marriage.
  • Practical takeaways: do not tolerate anyone tarnishing your lineage; stand up and object. Otherwise, when it is not about lineage, it is greatly praiseworthy, though very hard, to stay quiet and not fight back.

In one line

Silence counts against one's lineage only in a rare combination of conditions, but one should always object to an attack on one's lineage.
