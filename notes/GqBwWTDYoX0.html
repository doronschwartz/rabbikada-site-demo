Topic: Calling a Kohen or Levi again after the first three, the maftir and the last aliyah, a Kohen who is not there, and moving a Sefer Torah Source: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Siman 135:10-14, with the Rema and the Mishnah Berurah Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada at the Thursday Shulhan Arukh class, Tiferet Eyal, Thursday 21 July 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

A quick quiz

After a week without a class, the Rabbi opened with a quiz on what had been learnt two weeks before.

  • An Israel was called because there was no Kohen. He said Barekhu and then the Kohen walked in. The Israel stands aside and the Kohen is called. If the Israel had already said "Barukh atah Hashem", the Kohen has lost his right. The same applies between a Kohen and a Levi.
  • Why can you not call two Levi'im one after the other? Because people would say the first was not a real Levi.
  • Then why may two Israelim follow each other? Because there is no such thing as a flawed Israel as far as the Torah reading goes. Even a mamzer may be called up, so no one can suspect the first Israel of anything.

A Kohen after the first three

Siman 135:10. Once Kohen, Levi and Israel have been called, may another Kohen be called fourth? Would people not say: why another Kohen? The first one must not have been a proper Kohen?

The Shulhan Arukh says the custom is to call a Kohen after the Israel, and the hazan announces "even though he is a Kohen" (af al pi shehu Kohen). The same applies to a Levi.

The Mishnah Berurah explains. There is no worry about the first Kohen: if there had been a problem with him, he would have been replaced at once, not after three aliyot. The worry is about the second Kohen. Why is a Kohen being called fourth? Perhaps he is not a real Kohen at all? That is why the announcement is made: everyone hears that he is a Kohen.

The Rema adds that when the maftir is called without a name, the concern does not arise. The Rabbi linked this to the Spanish and Portuguese custom of calling simply "Maftir", without the person's name. Someone asked why, by that logic, the concern would not fall away for every aliyah called without a name. The Rabbi said it was a good question and that he would think about it.

Not within the seven: the maftir and the last aliyah

The Rema writes that a Kohen or Levi should not be called within the seven aliyot of Shabbat, only after the seven are complete. So if a community calls eight or nine, the Rema would allow a Kohen for the eighth or ninth.

The Mishnah Berurah records that the custom became narrower still: a Kohen or Levi is called only for the maftir or for the very last aliyah. People like the last aliyah because it completes the reading, so it is often given to an important person. The Rabbi added that in Kabbalah the order is quite different: the seventh is considered the weakest, and the sixth the highest, followed by the third.

Two follow-up points:

  • Last aliyah, then maftir. If a Kohen had the last aliyah, can another Kohen be the maftir? Only if a Kaddish separates them. Kaddish is an interruption, so the two are no longer one after the other.
  • A riddle. How can three Kohanim be called one after the other? On a Shabbat with three Sifrei Torah, such as Shabbat Rosh Hodesh Hanukkah, Kaddish is said between the readings, so a Kohen can have the last aliyah, the next reading, and the maftir. Where two Sifrei Torah are read without a Kaddish in between, it does not work.

The Rabbi also spoke about people who are called up and say they cannot say the Kaddish. "That's so wrong, superstitious, it's wrong." Kaddish is a beautiful prayer, and saying it is a merit.

For Ashkenazim on Simhat Torah, the Mishnah Berurah allows Kohanim within the aliyot, because everyone must be called and there is no other way to do it. Someone asked about a bar misvah where several relatives are Kohanim. The Rabbi said that for Ashkenazim he would consider that a case of need, though other authorities might not, and that it is still better to call them after the seven if possible.

The Kohen who is not there

The hazan calls the Kohen or Levi by name, and he is not there: perhaps he stepped out. The Shulhan Arukh says not to call the next person by name. Let someone simply go up. Announcing a new name makes much more of a scene; if someone just goes up, people will not even notice what happened.

Someone asked about the opposite worry: will people now think the man who went up is the Kohen? The Rabbi called it an excellent point. It is exactly like the case where there is no Kohen at all: you call an Israel, not a Levi, and you cannot follow with a Levi in second place.

When the Kohen comes back, may he be called for the third aliyah? He has no right to it, but there is no reason he cannot be given it if the gabbai wishes.

A hazan who is himself a Kohen can call up other people. No one will wonder why he did not call himself. That is normal.

A synagogue of Kohanim, and a child among the seven

Where a synagogue has only Kohanim and no Israelim, Kohanim are called one after another. There is no slur here, since everyone knows there is no one else. That is the second answer to the riddle of three Kohanim in a row.

The Shulhan Arukh rules later that a child may be called as one of the seven, and that is the Sefaradi custom. The Spanish and Portuguese community calls a child only for the maftir.

Bringing the Torah to people

The class began Siman 135:14. People who are in prison cannot come to the synagogue, but the Sefer Torah is not taken to them to read, because it is disrespectful to move the Torah for people. You cannot just take it somewhere to read and take it back.

If a proper place is prepared for it in advance, so that it rests there for some days, that is permitted, because the new place is its home for now. An important person, such as a great talmid hakham, is also a reason to bring a Torah. This raises practical questions, for example a family taking a Sefer Torah somewhere for a bar misvah, and the Rabbi said the conditions would be discussed at the next class.

Practical summary

  • After Kohen, Levi and Israel, a Kohen or Levi may be called again, and the hazan announces "even though he is a Kohen" (or Levi).
  • Ashkenazim call a Kohen or Levi only for the maftir or the last aliyah. A Kohen cannot follow a Kohen unless a Kaddish comes between them.
  • Do not refuse to say the Kaddish when called up: it is a merit.
  • If the person called is not there, let the next person go up without being named.
  • A Sefer Torah is not carried to people for a reading, unless a place has been prepared for it in advance or for an important person.
