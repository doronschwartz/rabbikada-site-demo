Topic: Gratitude, even to inanimate things, as the root of the berakhot

Sources: The Torah (the first three plagues); the Hakhamim; the Gemara

Daily Halacha: Berachot, Tuesday 9 January 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The series on berakhot prepares for Tu BiShvat and links to the parashah of the plagues. Moshe is charged with the plagues, yet Aharon brings the first three: blood and frogs by striking the Nile, lice by striking the earth.
  • The Hakhamim explain that Moshe owed gratitude to the water, which hid him as a baby among the reeds, and to the earth, in which he hid the Egyptian he killed, so it was not fitting for him to strike them.
  • The same idea appears every Friday night: bread normally takes precedence over wine, but kiddush must come first, so we cover the hallot so that the bread is not "embarrassed".
  • These are inanimate objects. The lesson is that true gratitude shows in how a person treats even what cannot respond or reciprocate; thanks given to people may be insincere, followed by the next request.
  • From this the Hakhamim instituted the berakhot. All are rabbinic except Birkat HaMazon, which is from the Torah; blessings on food and those of tefillah were instituted by the Hakhamim.
  • The Gemara says one who enjoys this world without a berakhah is as if stealing from HaKadosh Barukh Hu.

In one line

Berakhot rest on hakarat hatov, a gratitude that extends even to things that can never know we are grateful.
