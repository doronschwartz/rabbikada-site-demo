Topic: Oseh Maaseh Bereshit or Oseh HaYam HaGadol on seas and oceans

Sources: Mishnah Berakhot (Rabbi Yehudah); the Torah, Parashat Masei; the Rosh; the Vilna Gaon; the Mishnah Berurah; Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Monday 3 February 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Mishnah says that on mountains, rivers, seas and deserts one blesses "Oseh Maaseh Bereshit", since they date from creation. On the sea it is said if one has not seen it for 30 days, as when a city dweller goes on holiday.
  • Rabbi Yehudah adds that on the Yam HaGadol one says "Oseh HaYam HaGadol". In Parashat Masei the Yam HaGadol, a border of Israel, is clearly the Mediterranean, so many Rishonim understood the Mishnah so: far smaller than the oceans, but "great" through its link to Eres Yisrael.
  • The Rosh concedes the Torah means the Mediterranean, but holds the Hakhamim meant the biggest sea, the oceans, which are really one body. So the Mediterranean is an ordinary sea.
  • Ashkenazim follow this, with the Vilna Gaon and Mishnah Berurah: Oseh Maaseh Bereshit on the Mediterranean and other seas, Oseh HaYam HaGadol on an ocean.
  • For Sepharadim it is more complex: the Shulhan Arukh rules the Yam HaGadol is the Mediterranean, and many say the oceans all the more so; but some Sepharadi poskim worried about the other Rishonim because of safek berakhot lehakel.
  • Where one sees the Mediterranean and the Atlantic together, as at Gibraltar or Tarifa in Spain, where he has seen the two waters in different colours, everyone would say Oseh HaYam HaGadol.
  • On the Mediterranean alone, Hakham Ovadiah holds the main halacha is Oseh HaYam HaGadol. The later poskim add that one may say "Oseh Maaseh Bereshit" straight after, covering both views.

In one line

Ashkenazim say Oseh HaYam HaGadol only on oceans, while Sepharadim say it on the Mediterranean too and may add Oseh Maaseh Bereshit straight after.
