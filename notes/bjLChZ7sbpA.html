Topic: A child born more than nine months after the husband travelled abroad

Sources: The Gemara (Rava Tosfaah); Tosafot; the Rambam; the Rosh; Shulhan Arukh, Even HaEzer, Siman 4

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 12 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Having defined the mamzer and the exception of the child of a niddah, the series now turns to practical cases of suspected mamzerut.
  • The Gemara's case: a husband goes overseas for a long time, and 10 to 11 months later his wife, still married to him, gives birth. Is the child from another man, and so a mamzer?
  • Rava Tosfaah, one of the Amoraim, holds the child is kasher even 12 months later: the child may have been conceived by the husband before he left and stayed in the womb longer than usual.
  • The Rishonim dispute whether this is accepted. Tosafot hold the halacha does not follow it; the majority, including the Rambam and the Rosh, accept it.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules like the Rambam: if the husband has been away up to 12 months, the child is kasher.
  • Scientifically this is hard to say today, though there is little evidence either way, since a woman is induced once she enters the tenth month.
  • Technically this applies even where the husband could not have been with his wife: imprisoned on 1 January, his wife gives birth on 1 December, and the child is kasher.
  • Next time: births after 12 months, and the possibility that the husband came back in between.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: Rava Tosfaah's view that a pregnancy can be delayed up to 12 months.
  • Tosafot: the halacha does not follow Rava Tosfaah.
  • The Rambam and the Rosh: the view is accepted.
  • Shulhan Arukh: rules like the Rambam, up to 12 months the child is kasher.

In one line

A child born up to 12 months after the husband left is kasher, since the pregnancy may have been delayed.
