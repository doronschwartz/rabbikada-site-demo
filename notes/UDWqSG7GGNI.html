Sefer: Maamar HaIkarim by Rabbi Moshe Hayim Luzzatto (the Ramhal), printed at the end of Derech Hashem Section: Maamar HaIkarim, essay 7 (last paragraph) and essay 8: redemption (beginning) Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Derech Hashem series at Tiferet Eyal, Thursday 30 June 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Recap: levels of prophecy

The class opened with Hodesh Tov and the last paragraph of the seventh essay. Prophecy, the Rabbi repeated, is a connection with God, through which a person may come to understand more about God or about the future, and the prophet always knows he is in a prophetic state. His body gives way, and his prophecy never reaches the level of Moshe. Below prophecy is Ruah HaKodesh, when a person without that intense connection is given understanding.

The fourth level: the idea that falls into your mind

The Ramhal adds a lower level still. With Ruah HaKodesh a person knows he has been given something. Sometimes there is a smaller version: a kind of energy through which a thought falls into his mind, without any feeling of trance. This too is broadly called Ruah HaKodesh. The Rabbi gave a Yiddish word for it, an einfal, something that "fell in". A participant called it a light-bulb moment.

So there are four levels of transcendent knowledge:

  1. The prophecy of Moshe.
  2. Prophecy: an intense relationship with God, in which the body stops functioning normally and secrets are revealed.
  3. Ruah HaKodesh: a transmission of understanding that a person knows he is receiving.
  4. The light-bulb moment: an idea you did not work for falls into your mind, and you realise, "this is the right decision to make".

Asked whether the difference between the third and fourth levels is length, the Rabbi said it is depth: Ruah HaKodesh is more of an experience. He thought the fourth level is the one most of us know.

Dreams

A participant noted that great Sages sometimes received information in dreams. The Rabbi called that a lower level again, a fifth. As learned in Part 3, the soul rises during sleep and receives information, sometimes from the wrong sources, which is why most dreams are nonsense.

He told how the halacha says one should not sleep in the day more than "the sleep of a horse", sixty breaths, which opinions measure from two or three minutes to half an hour.

Essay 8: redemption

The eighth essay is on geulah, redemption, and the Rabbi said it is very important. Most of the earlier essays summarised the book, but nowhere in Derech Hashem did the Ramhal discuss Mashiah or the resurrection. The book speaks only of Olam HaBa, the ultimate goal after 7,000 years. Gan Eden and Gehinnom are the waiting room. So where do Mashiah and the resurrection fit? The group discussed the timing: Mashiah must come within the 6,000 years, and if he comes now there would be about two hundred years before the year 6000.

Israel as the chosen people

The Ramhal opens: the choicest part of humanity is Israel, the nation destined to cleave to God, adorned with holiness and greatness, with the Divine spirit resting on it. That is the utopian vision.

The Rabbi was candid. "I have to be honest, I'm a bit uncomfortable with this idea of Israel being the prime species and being greater than everyone else." He recalled that in Part 2 the Ramhal described the nations as attached to the Jewish people, and he finds that difficult too. The Torah certainly says in several places that we are the chosen nation, a segulah. But he understands chosenness like choosing a head boy or head girl. They are the most prominent and have an extra role, but they are not necessarily more important than the rest of the class. Similarly, a Kohen serves in the Temple and we honour him first, but that does not make him greater than an ordinary Israelite. Israel is the example and the light, serving in God's Temple in the world, so more is expected of us.

A participant asked about a non-Jew who keeps the seven Noahide laws, lives morally and even understands God as a philosopher, yet ends up behind a Jew who did less. The Rabbi said that, according to the Ramhal, two people doing exactly the same deeds would not be equal: the Jew would be higher. The Rambam disagrees. For him a Jew and a non-Jew can reach exactly the same levels. Being Jewish gives more guidance, many directives rather than seven, which makes it easier to refine oneself, but it does not add a bonus of status.

A collective task

Although each person acquires his own goodness, the Ramhal says there is also a collective responsibility. Humanity as a whole, and the Jewish people as a whole, must reach a certain level for the world to be rectified and the Shekhinah to rest on it. Only then does each person receive his individual reward.

This has not happened yet. As soon as Adam was created he sinned. Later we came close, at Matan Torah, but the golden calf and other sins pushed us back from completion. "We've blown some opportunities, and so we have to wait until we're ready."

A participant traced a pattern through the parashiyot: the Flood, the Tower of Bavel, Sodom, the plagues in the wilderness, and God's offer after the spies to destroy the people and start again from Moshe. He argued that Moshe's plea meant entering the land with an imperfect generation. The Rabbi said no one can know whether God tried other worlds before ours, and the discussion was left for Monday's class on what will happen in the days of Mashiah.

Summary

  • Below prophecy and Ruah HaKodesh is the "einfal", an idea that falls into the mind unearned; dreams are lower still.
  • Derech Hashem never discussed Mashiah or the resurrection; essay 8 places them in the overall picture.
  • The Rabbi understands chosenness as a role, like a head boy or a Kohen, not as superiority.
  • The Ramhal gives a Jew a higher place for the same deeds; the Rambam says a non-Jew can reach the same level.
  • Beyond individual growth there is a collective task to rectify the world, which we came close to but have not yet completed.
