The halacha

  • Recap: the Maharil explained the Ashkenazi Yom Tov-only custom by the mikveh and by burden on the congregation.
  • The Rema (Rabbi Moshe Isserles, 16th century) gives another reason: Birkat Kohanim must be said with love and simhah. Some even held a bachelor cannot bless, since the Gemara says one without a wife is without joy, though in practice we allow it.
  • He argues that on weekdays, and even on Shabbat, minds are taken up with earning a living, and only on Yom Tov are the Kohanim in a fitting state of simhah. Today, when Yom Tov means taking time off work, one might argue the opposite, and the argument is hard to understand.
  • Oriental Sepharadim bless daily, the reading of the Talmud, Rambam and most Rishonim.
  • The Spanish and Portuguese: in London it was Yom Tov only, as with Ashkenazim; in Amsterdam it was also done on Shabbat. That Shabbat practice was instituted under the influence of Shabbetai Sevi; after he was exposed many wanted to drop it, but Hakham Yaakov Sasportas, who fought him tooth and nail, upheld it in a teshuvah, as one does not abolish a Torah misvah.
  • In London the S&P community added Shabbat about 20 years ago, after a vote of the kahal.
  • Hakham Ovadiah writes that the best minhag is daily, unless a community has an established custom. The Vilna Gaon's students who came to Israel adopted it daily, so Ashkenazim in Israel bless every day.

In one line

Daily Birkat Kohanim is the simple meaning of the misvah; the Ashkenazi and Spanish and Portuguese limits are later customs.
