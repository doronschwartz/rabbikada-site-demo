Topic: Whether one may pray Arvit and make Kiddush before nightfall on the first night of Shavuot

Sources: Rabbi Avraham Horowitz; the Taz; Rabbi Yoseph Yuspa of Frankfurt; Rabbi Ephraim Laniado, Degel Mahaneh Efrayim; Rabbi Hayim Palagi; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Wednesday 12 May 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • On summer Friday nights it is a Misvah to bring Shabbat in early, adding from the weekday to the holy. One would expect the same on Shavuot, when in London sunset is about 8.45 and nightfall about 9.45.
  • In the 16th century Rabbi Avraham Horowitz of Prague, father of the Shelah, argued that bringing Shavuot in early cuts short the 49th day of the Omer and spoils the seven complete weeks, so one must wait until nightfall for Kiddush. It is not in the Shulhan Arukh, but Ashkenazi poskim adopted it.
  • A century later the Taz (Rabbi David Segal, Poland) extended this to Arvit. So Ashkenazim pray after nightfall and eat very late, after 11 in places like Gateshead.
  • Rabbi Yoseph Yuspa of Frankfurt rejected it at the time: the custom was to pray early, eat, and return to study. The Rishonim insist on nightfall only for Massah on Pesah and the Sukkah on Sukkot, never mentioning Shavuot, so one may make Kiddush from sunset.
  • Sepharadi poskim agreed: Rabbi Ephraim Laniado of Aleppo wrote it was not the custom in Syria, and Rabbi Hayim Palagi, chief rabbi in Turkey, was also lenient.
  • Hakham Ovadiah concludes one may certainly make Kiddush after sunset, especially in England. One may pray Minhah and Arvit from Pelag HaMinhah (from about 7.45 or 8 this year), go home and make Kiddush after shekiah.
  • Sepharadim were never particular to wait for nightfall; Ashkenazim are.

In one line

Sepharadim may pray Arvit early on Shavuot night and make Kiddush straight after sunset; Ashkenazim wait for nightfall.
