Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 291, seifim 2-6 Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 29 February 2024

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

Review: the time of the third meal

Last week covered the hundred blessings, sleeping and learning on Shabbat (290), and the start of Siman 291 on the three meals: one should eat Seudah Shelishit even when full, ideally a keveisah of bread, at least a kezayit, and leave room for it at lunch.

Seif 2 again: the time is from Minhah time, six and a half hours after daybreak. Before that, one has not fulfilled it. But the Rambam holds any two meals during the day are enough, and we rely on this on Erev Pesah that falls on Shabbat. After the fact, the Rabbi said, if you will be out all afternoon on a long walk or important task and cannot eat, have Seudah Shelishit in the morning, since that fulfils it according to some Rishonim.

He returned to the midrash that one should not drink between Minhah and Arvit on Shabbat because the souls are returning to Gehinnom, and the water is "taken" from them. On that basis Tosafot and the Rosh say to eat before Minhah, calling Seudah Shelishit a small meal. The Rabbi stressed that this builds a halacha on a midrash. The Rambam simply says to pray Minhah first, as one does not begin a meal once Minhah time has come.

The midrash was limited in many ways:

  • The Rema: it applies only to water drawn directly from a river, not water already drawn. Tap water was discussed in class without a firm conclusion.
  • The Aguddah and the Mordekhai, Ashkenazi Rishonim, say it applies only to someone in the year of mourning for a parent.
  • Others say it referred to a different time altogether.

"No one seems to know what this midrash is talking about," so we are not particular about it. The Rabbi mentioned a Syrian custom of pouring away water left from Shabbat, including the urn, on Saturday night, and suggested it may come from this idea. He added that all this is metaphorical: "there's no fire in Gehinnom."

For Sefaradim: eat Seudah Shelishit after Minhah time, before or after praying Minhah as you wish, with a fixed Minhah time or someone to remind you. When Yom Tov begins on Saturday night, have Seudah Shelishit in the afternoon and pray Minhah later at the shul's fixed time.

Splitting the lunch into two meals

Seif 3: if the morning meal ran on into Minhah time, stop, say Birkat HaMazon, wash again and eat the third meal. The Rabbi pointed out that this was unusual in the Shulhan Arukh's day, since the morning meal began around nine. Today, with late Shaharit and lunch at two, even in summer it is hard to eat again.

The problem: this seems to create a berakhah sheeinah serikhah, an unnecessary blessing. Normally one may not say an after-blessing in order to say a new blessing. The answer: here you are not doing it for the blessings but for the misvah of Seudah Shelishit. The extra blessing is a result, not the aim. "That shows us how important Seudah Shelishit is."

  • The Rema prefers to finish lunch, pray Minhah and eat Seudah Shelishit afterwards.
  • Some Aharonim were uneasy, and the Mishnah Berurah says one should get up and take a break in between. Some say that if one stays in place, it counts as one meal.

What to do in winter:

  • If you go back to shul for Minhah and Seudah Shelishit, that is perfect.
  • Otherwise, split the lunch: say Birkat HaMazon after the first part, take a break, learn something, then wash and eat the main course as Seudah Shelishit.
  • At a large Kiddush in shul he washes and says Birkat HaMazon, so that it counts as a meal. He said it is wrong for a big Kiddush not to provide hallah, since people fill up and have no room for a proper lunch. Better three meals than two.

No Kiddush, but two loaves

Seif 4: no Kiddush is made at Seudah Shelishit, but two whole loaves are needed. The Mishnah Berurah brings the Rosh's comparison: one Kiddush at night, one by day. The Rabbi explained this is a comparison in the loose sense, not the Talmudic hekesh.

The Rambam writes that all three meals should be with wine. The main understanding is not Kiddush, but that drinking wine is part of a proper meal. The Zohar says the same, "one of the only places where the Rambam and the Zohar come together." So it is good to drink some wine or grape juice at Seudah Shelishit, but there is no obligation.

The Rema notes a custom of using one loaf at extra meals, and explains its basis in the double portion of the man. One should still try to have two. A frozen hallah may be used as the second loaf. Asked about Melaveh Malkah, he said it is purely rabbinic, "out of respect for the Queen," with no connection to the man and no Lehem Mishneh.

Bread, cake, fish or fruit?

Seif 5: the default is bread. Some say any of the five grains, such as cake; some say foods eaten with bread, like meat and fish; some say even fruit. The first view is the main one: the three meals come from the three "hayom", so why should the third differ? The others hold it is an extra meal, since in Talmudic times people ate only twice a day, so whatever one eats is enough.

He gave a side point: the six hours between meat and milk reflect the gap between two daily meals. He quoted Rav Eliezer Melamed's suggestion that three hours reflects three meals a day.

Everyone agrees one should ideally have bread. If one cannot, the order is cake, then meat or fish, then fruit, sitting down with the intention that it is a meal.

Erev Pesah on Shabbat: we try to satisfy both views. Eat Seudah Shelishit with bread in the morning before the time Hames is forbidden, and again in the afternoon with something else, such as cake made from massah meal, fish or fruit, without eating too much before the Seder.

A question was raised: the lenient views assume a meal after Shabbat, which many people no longer eat. He agreed eating habits have changed.

Forgetting Reseh, and women's obligation

If one forgets Reseh in Birkat HaMazon at Seudah Shelishit, one does not repeat, since some say bread is not required, as on Rosh Hodesh or most of Yom Tov. Among Sefaradi poskim there is a three-way debate:

  • Rav Ovadia Yoseph, following halacha: do not go back.
  • Rav Ben Sion Abba Shaul: Kabbalah overrides halacha only where it is explicit; here it is not, so do not go back.
  • Rav Yaakov Hillel: even an implied Kabbalistic source is enough, so go back.

Rav Ben Sion adds that the second meal is by definition a morning meal. If one began lunch after midday and forgot Reseh, he does not go back. A question about Pesah nights led to a discussion: one goes back for Yaaleh VeYavo on both Seder nights and both nights of Sukkot.

Seif 6: women are obligated in the three meals. Women are included in all the positive misvot of Shabbat, since they are included in its prohibitions. The Rabbi suggested the Shulhan Arukh spells it out because women tended to be lax about Seudah Shelishit.

Practical summary

  • Eat Seudah Shelishit after midday, ideally with bread, and leave room for it at lunch.
  • In winter, if you do not eat again later, split your lunch with Birkat HaMazon and a break in between.
  • Use two whole loaves; a frozen hallah may be the second. No Kiddush is needed.
  • If bread is impossible, eat cake, then meat or fish, then fruit, as a meal.
  • If you forget Reseh in Birkat HaMazon at Seudah Shelishit, do not repeat.
