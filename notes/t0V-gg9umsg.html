Topic: Parents who cannot be served, and whether a child abused by a parent must still honour them

Sources: The Gemara (the story of Rav Assi); Shulhan Arukh; a modern teshuvah on mourning an abusive parent

Daily Halacha: Honouring Parents, Thursday 14 January 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Talmud's stories of bearing humiliation are one-off incidents. If such things happen regularly, one should do all one can to live elsewhere and limit contact.
  • Rav Assi's elderly mother asked for jewellery, then for a husband, then for one as handsome as him. He left and moved to the Land of Israel.
  • From this the Shulhan Arukh rules that if a parent is not of sound mind (for example through illness) and it is too hard to serve them, one may leave them and live elsewhere. This should be used sparingly; try first.
  • A teshuvah of recent years dealt with a woman abused by her father who did not want to sit shivah. It ruled she need not: shivah expresses grief, and she could not grieve.
  • The Rabbi raises, without a final answer, whether this could extend to honouring parents. If abuse was serious and repeated, it is questionable whether the obligation remains. Best is to keep away, as with a parent not of sound mind; if that is not possible, one could argue there is no obligation. It needs further analysis.

In one line

One may leave a parent who is not of sound mind if serving them is too hard, and a seriously abused child need not sit shivah and arguably need not honour the abuser, a question the Rabbi leaves open.
