Topic: Adjusting manual timers on Shabbat compared with Yom Tov

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Friday 30 May 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Timers can really help on Yom Tov, for the hot plate, oven, heating thermostat or air conditioning. This concerns manual timers; electricity itself comes next time.
  • On Shabbat one may adjust a manual timer only to maintain the status quo. At 1 o'clock, a hot plate due to go off at 2 may be set to go off at 3 because the guests are late. Likewise, a hot plate that is off and due on at 12 may be pushed back to 1.
  • But on Shabbat, in the first instance, one may not bring the time closer: at 10 o'clock, setting a hot plate due on at 12 to come on at 11 because guests are coming early indirectly causes it to come on. Nor may one make something due off at 4 go off at 2, indirectly "extinguishing".
  • On Yom Tov all of this is permitted, because it is grama, indirect, and indirect melakhah is permitted on Yom Tov.
  • The condition: moving the dials must not cause anything to switch on or off immediately, meaning within a few seconds. If it happens five or ten minutes later, adjusting the timer earlier or later is permitted on Yom Tov, whether hot plate, air conditioning or heating.

In one line

On Shabbat a manual timer may only extend the present state, but on Yom Tov it may be moved earlier or later as long as nothing switches immediately.
