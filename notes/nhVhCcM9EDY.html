Where we are

Siman 13 has two parts. Seifim 1-10 are about havhanah, the waiting period between marriages. Seifim 11 to the end are about a pregnant or nursing woman (meuberet umeneket haverah). Last month we saw the strong law, a comprehensive decree of Hazal (lo plug), that a woman who was divorced or widowed may not marry, or even be engaged, until three months (shelosha hodashim, in practice 90 days) have passed. Today's focus was Seifim 5-8, mainly one big sugya in Seifim 6-8, with Seifim 9-10 left for next time.

Two practical questions drive the class: must a couple who convert together wait, and must a Jewish man and his non-Jewish girlfriend, who is converting, wait three months before marrying?

The Mishnah and the Gemara

The Mishnah describes two weddings held at the same time where the brides were mixed up, and each couple spent the night with the wrong partner. They were all mistaken, so each woman is a eshet ish who was with another man. They return to their own husbands, but have to separate for three months, like the rule of havhanah we learned in Seifim 1-4.

The Mishnah then says that a ketanah (a girl too young to conceive) does not need to wait, and can be returned to her husband immediately. The Gemara then brings the statement of Shmuel about zenut (relations outside marriage): a ketanah with zenut does not need to wait, since Hazal did not decree where it is not shahiah (not a normal case). The clear implication is that a gedolah with zenut does need to wait.

Three opinions in the Gemara.

Rabbi

Does she need to wait three months?

Rabbi Yehudah

Always, in all circumstances, even after relations outside marriage (zenut)

Rabbi Yose

Never, because a woman makes sure not to become pregnant (mitapehet, she turns herself over)

Shmuel

In between: a ketanah with zenut does not need to wait, but a gedolah with zenut does

The reason in Rabbi Yose's opinion is that a woman who is careful to avoid pregnancy will take steps afterwards, even if she was surprised. So Hazal did not apply the decree to cases like a convert (giyoret), a freed maidservant (meshuhreret), a raped woman or a seduced woman (anusah, mefutah).

Shmuel is a middle position: he agrees with Rabbi Yose that these cases are mitapehet, but says that a gedolah with zenut is still included, so a woman who had an extramarital relationship and wants to marry a month later must wait three months. Rashi explains why Shmuel is still strict: Hazal decreed against zenut on account of marriage, where havhanah is a real concern. But for a convert, even in marriage there would be no problem, because she is careful to avoid pregnancy, so they did not decree for her.

The question on the Gemara: the Ger and Giyoret who marry

The Gemara also says that a convert couple, a ger and a giyoret, must wait three months. This does not fit Rabbi Yose, so Tosafot gives three answers:

  1. We do not follow that Gemara.
  2. It is speaking where we know for certain that she did not take steps, so she may be pregnant.
  3. It is speaking of a couple who are married to each other, and who convert together. Here even Rabbi Yose would agree, because she is going to marry the same man and has the same seed, so she has no reason to be careful (mitapehet).

This third answer is important for what follows.

The Rishonim

The Rabbi summarised five approaches:

  1. Rabbenu Hananel: the halakhah is like Rabbi Yehudah. In all cases she waits.
  2. The Rif: the halakhah is like Rabbi Yose. Even a gedolah with zenut does not wait, and neither does a convert. The Rif is the most lenient. The Rabbi noted that the Mishnah of the two couples is not a proof against him, since that was a mistake, where the woman wanted to become pregnant by her own husband.
  3. The Baal HaMaor and the Rosh follow Shmuel: a giyoret or a freed maidservant does not need to wait, but zenut of a gedolah does. They add that a convert couple who are married to each other do need to wait, following the third answer of Tosafot. It is not clear whether the Baal HaMaor means a couple married to each other, or also a giyoret who was married before. The Baal HaMaor argues that although the halakhah usually follows Rabbi Yose, here we are strict about the lo plug, so we follow Shmuel.
  4. The Rambam, according to the Magid Mishneh: the Rambam wrote that a shifhah who was freed and a giyoret must wait 90 days. This looks like Rabbi Yehudah, but he also says an anusah and mefutah need not wait, which is Rabbi Yose. The Magid Mishneh makes the Rambam speak of a giyoret who was married, either to her husband or to another man. A single giyoret can marry at once. The Rabbi noted that this is the exact opposite of the Baal HaMaor: for the Rambam zenut does not need to wait, and a married giyoret does.
  5. The Rambam, according to the Beit Yoseph: the Beit Yoseph is not satisfied with the Magid Mishneh. He says the Rambam rules like Rabbi Yose, but for a giyoret he follows Rabbi Yehudah because of the Gemara, so even a single giyoret has to wait three months. The Rabbi said this is a major difference between the Magid Mishneh and the Beit Yoseph.

The Shulhan Arukh and the Rema

Seifim 5-6. The Shulhan Arukh follows the Magid Mishneh's reading of the Rambam. In all cases where there was no marriage, only zenut (an anusah, mefutah, any woman who had relations outside marriage, not only a girl), she does not need to wait. But a convert who was married, or who converts together with a man they are married to, must wait three months. This means a giyoret who was not married can marry at once. The Beit Shemuel notes that the Beit Yoseph, who argued with the Magid Mishneh in the Rambam, did not insist on his own reading when he came to rule. The Rabbi suggested that perhaps it was difficult to rely on one passage of the Rambam, since the Gemara can be explained in other ways, as Tosafot did.

The Rema says that if they are gedolot, they do need to wait. This is the Rosh and the Baal HaMaor. So the Shulhan Arukh follows the Magid Mishneh and the Rambam, and the Rema follows the Baal HaMaor.

In practice. According to the Shulhan Arukh, a single Jewish woman who had relations and wants to marry a few months later can do so. According to the Rema, she must wait. A single non-Jewish woman who converts and then falls in love with a new man may marry him straight away.

The couple who convert together, and the pregnancy test

The Helkat Mehokek asks: the Shulhan Arukh says a married convert must wait three months. But for Jewish women it is clear that even a ketanah who was married has to wait, because of lo plug. So how far does this go? Take an elderly non-Jewish woman, say 80 years old. Does she have to wait?

The Pithei Teshuvah brings the Noda BiYehudah. He says there are two cases:

  • A woman converts, leaving her non-Jewish husband, and wants to marry a Jewish man. This is like a Jewish woman who gets divorced and marries someone else, so she must wait, even if she is old.
  • A couple convert together and marry each other. There is no parallel in a Jewish case. The nearest is a man taking back his divorced wife (mahzir grushato), which does not need a wait. So the lo plug does not apply, and if the woman is past the age of fertility, they can go to the mikveh and marry immediately.

From this comes the practice of relying on a pregnancy test. If a woman who cannot conceive is exempt, then a test, which is more reliable than being elderly (some older women do become pregnant), can serve the same purpose. This is the source for the pregnancy test used in conversions today, where a couple marry each other, or a Jewish man and a non-Jewish woman stay together. The Rabbi said it is the source of the whole heter of relying on a pregnancy test, and the alternative would be to tell a couple to separate for three months. The Rabbi added a thought of his own: once they are Jewish, relations between them are more serious (including niddah), and three months of separation is a long time. He said he did not know how widespread the practice of relying on a pregnancy test is.

Seif 6 (the Rema), and Seifim 7-8

A woman who was raped while married and has not had relations with her husband since must wait three months, according to the Rema. This cannot be according to the Shulhan Arukh, where an anusah does not need to wait. It follows the view that there is a concern for a gedolah. The Helkat Mehokek adds that if she has not had relations with her husband for the last three months, she must also wait before remarrying.

Seif 7: a concubine (pilegesh). She must wait, even according to the Rambam (Rabbi Yose), because it is not zenut, she was in a relationship with one man, and so has no reason to be careful.

Seif 8: a woman who had relations with a man who she thought was her husband. If she is a gedolah she must wait even according to the Rambam, as the Rif also says, because she did not turn herself over. A ketanah does not need to wait, since the decree does not apply to a girl who cannot conceive.

Practical summary

  • A woman who had relations outside marriage (zenut) does not need to wait three months according to the Shulhan Arukh, but she does according to the Rema (a gedolah).
  • A single non-Jewish woman who converts can marry at once according to the Shulhan Arukh, following the Magid Mishneh. The Beit Yoseph understands the Rambam more strictly.
  • A woman who was married before converting, or a couple who converted while married to each other, must wait three months, unless the woman cannot conceive.
  • The Noda BiYehudah's reasoning is the basis for relying on a pregnancy test when a couple convert together.
  • A woman who was raped while married and has had no relations with her husband must wait (the Rema), and a concubine must wait.
  • A woman who had relations with a man she thought was her husband must wait if she is a gedolah.
