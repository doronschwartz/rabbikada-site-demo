Recap

The Thirteen Principles fall into three categories. 1–5: Hashem Himself. 6–9: revelation, the relationship between Hashem and humans. Last week, Principle 6: there is such a thing as prophecy: Hashem communicates with intellectually and morally developed human beings.

"The principle isn't that the words of the prophets are true. It's that Hashem relates to humans."

Tonight: Principle 7 quickly, then Principle 8, Torah min HaShamayim, "a very important principle, with lots to discuss." Seven is the longest principle; eight the second longest.

Part 1: Principle 7: The Text

היסוד השביעי: נבואת משה רבנו עליו השלום. והוא שנאמין כי הוא אביהם של כל הנביאים אשר היו לפניו ואשר קמו אחריו, כולם הם תחתיו במעלה.

Moshe surpasses the prophets before him (Avraham, Yitzchak…) and all who came after.

"This is interesting. As a principle of faith, the Rambam asserts Moshe is greater than all who will come after. So Mashiach, whoever and whenever he will be, won't be on Moshe's level. Not everyone agreed, as we'll see."

Question: Is that prophecy, or just closeness?

"Other prophets foretold the future; Moshe didn't really. He was close to Hashem. Is that nevuah?"

"Remember the key thing from last week. The essence of prophecy is not (A) telling other people information, nor (B) knowing the future. Often it includes those. The essence is a connection with Hashem through which you understand things about godliness, the philosophy and theology of Hashem, more than you ever did before."

Part 2: The Four Differences

ואחזור לכונת זה היסוד השביעי, ואומר שנבואת משה רבנו עליו השלום נבדלת מנבואת כל הנביאים בארבעה דברים.

"Spoke": a borrowed term

"When we say Hashem spoke: remember Principle 3. Hashem has no mouth; there are no sound waves, since nothing physical about Him emits them. 'God spoke to Moshe' means a vision, a recognition, an understanding of God."

The source: Bamidbar 12

ויאמר שמעו נא דברי: אם יהיה נביאכם ה׳ במראה אליו אתודע, בחלום אדבר בו. לא כן עבדי משה, בכל ביתי נאמן הוא. פה אל פה אדבר בו, ומראה ולא בחידת, ותמנת ה׳ יביט. ומדוע לא יראתם לדבר בעבדי במשה?

Hashem to Aharon and Miriam: other prophets, in a vision or a dream. Not so My servant Moshe: mouth to mouth, openly, not in riddles; he beholds the image of Hashem. Why weren't you afraid to speak against him?

On the intermediary:

"The more intermediaries between your vision and God, the greater the effort to understand the message. The Kabbalah and the Ramchal speak of the aspaklaria, a lens: the prophets see through dull, unpolished lenses and must decipher. Moshe: nothing in between."

("This is why Miriam and Aharon spoke against Moshe… let's not go into that now.")

Questions

Tehillim 99:6: משה ואהרן בכהניו ושמואל בקראי שמו, קראים אל ה׳ והוא יענם. Some commentators say Shmuel is being compared to Moshe and Aharon.

"The Gemara learns שמואל בדורו כמשה בדורו. It's making a point: don't think you only listen to Moshe and Aharon and Shmuel, a later and lower generation, less. Give him the same kavod, listen to him equally. It doesn't mean Shmuel is greater than Moshe. Of course not."

Bilaam: In Balak, did Bilaam lose his strength and fall?

Part 3: Why So Much Emphasis?

The Rambam is elaborate on both 7 and 8: Moshe's prophecy is unique, and the Torah today is exactly as in Moshe's day.

"I imagine there's an element of rebutting the Muslims and the Christians, who claimed the Torah changed. No prophet can arise like Moshe, so it's not logical to substitute the Torah of Moshe with the New Testament or the Quran. So-called prophets who called themselves prophets can't replace it."

Part 5: Principle 8: Torah min HaShamayim

"I wouldn't say controversial, but there's a lot to dissect. As we read, count how many points he makes."

היסוד השמיני: היות התורה מן השמים. והוא שנאמין כי כל התורה הזאת הנתונה על ידי משה רבנו עליו השלום, היא כולה מפי הגבורה. כלומר שהגיעה אליו כולה מאת ה׳, בענין שנקרא על דרך השאלה ״דבור״, ואין ידוע איך הגיעה אליו אלא הוא, משה עליו השלום, שהגיעה לו.

  • The whole Torah given through Moshe is entirely from Hashem.
  • "Speech" is a borrowed term. No one knows how it reached him except Moshe himself.

וכי הוא היה כסופר שקורין לפניו והוא כותב כולה, מאורעותיה וסיפוריה ומצוותיה. ולפיכך נקרא מחוקק.

Moshe was like a scribe being dictated to: events, stories, commandments. Hence he's called מחוקק, "like a copyist."

No verse is "less" Torah

ואין הפרש בין ״ובני חם כוש ומצרים ופוט וכנען״, ״ושם אשתו מהיטבאל בת מטרד בת מי זהב״, ״ותמנע היתה פילגש״, ובין ״אנכי ה׳ אלהיך״ ו״שמע ישראל״. כי הכל מפי הגבורה, והכל תורת ה׳ תמימה טהורה קדושה אמת.

Standing for the Ten Commandments

The Rambam's famous teshuvah: he opposed standing for the Aseret HaDibrot: it implies one part of the Torah is more important, "and that's not true."

"Ashkenazim do, and the Spanish and Portuguese communities do. When we stand in our community, I say every time: we're not standing because it's more important, but because within it are principles from which we learn the rest of the Torah: a way to remember the whole Torah. Anyone who says it's more important is going against this principle."

The worst heretic

וזה שאומר שכמו אלה הפסוקים והסיפורים משה ספרם מדעתו, הנה הוא אצל חכמינו ונביאינו כופר ומגלה פנים יותר מכל הכופרים, לפי שחשב שיש בתורה לב וקליפה, ושאלה דברי הימים והסיפורים אין תועלת בהם ושהם מאת משה רבנו עליו השלום.

Whoever says Moshe wrote such verses on his own is, for our Sages and prophets, a heretic, more brazen than all heretics: he treats the Torah as having a core and a shell, with useless stories Moshe added.

וזה ענין ״אין תורה מן השמים״, אמרו: הוא האומר שכל התורה מפי הגבורה חוץ מפסוק זה, שלא אמרו הקב״ה אלא משה מפי עצמו אמרו. וזהו ״כי דבר ה׳ בזה״.

Denying Torah min HaShamayim isn't only saying the Torah wasn't given; it's saying "all of it, except this one verse Moshe added." That is "he has disgraced the word of Hashem."

(On the text: "ה׳ יתברך וינזר ממאמר הכופרים — this is a different text; it should read something else. He means: may Hashem be exalted above the words of the heretics.")

אלא כל דבור ודבור מן התורה יש בהם חכמות ופלאים למי שהבינם, ולא הושג תכלית חכמתה, ״ארוכה מארץ מדה ורחבה מני ים״. ואין לאיש אלא להלוך בעקבות דוד משיח אלהי יעקב…

Every word holds wisdom and wonders for one who understands; its depth is "longer than the earth, wider than the sea." Follow King David (in Tehillim) in valuing every verse.

The Oral Torah

וכן פירושה המקובל גם כן מפי הגבורה. וזה שאנו עושים היום תבנית הסוכה והלולב והשופר והציצית והתפילין וזולתם, הוא בעצמו התבנית שאמר השם למשה ואמר לנו, והוא מוסר השליחות נאמן בשליחותו.

The received explanation is also from Hashem. The form of sukkah, lulav, shofar, tzitzit, tefillin we make today is the very form Hashem told Moshe at Sinai 3,330+ years ago.

והמאמר המורה על היסוד הזה הוא מה שנאמר: ויאמר משה בזאת תדעון כי ה׳ שלחני לעשות את כל המעשים האלה כי לא מלבי.

Moshe to Korach's followers: "not from my own heart." "I'm just a copyist, passing the message on."

"I used to have a congregant, I won't say which shul, who would follow my Torah reading from his Samaritan copy, and after Shabbat point out the differences."

Rashi asks: surely He finished on the sixth day? The Midrash: on the seventh Hashem created rest: "a deep idea." The Samaritan text just says the sixth. "Important differences."

Only the words

Not what this principle requires:

- Te'amim, the cantillation: "a machloket among our Sages when they came about; some say as late as the 8th or 9th century";

- Parashah petuchah / *setumah, the open and closed sections;

- Tagin, the crowns on the letters;

- The script (ketav): "they may have had a different script.*"

"We're concerned with the exact words."

3. Keri and ketiv

Written one way, read another. Usually minor, but some major: in Ki Tavo's curses, ישגלנה written, ישכבנה read.

The Radak: variant texts arose during the first exile; when the Anshei Knesset HaGedolah restored the Torah they found small differences and kept both, one written, one read.

The Rabbi's conclusion: "To say every single letter today is entirely the same as Moshe received: I think that's difficult, and I don't know that the Rambam meant it. We see chaser and maleh, keri and ketiv, Tannaim with different texts. Of course we believe in Torah min HaShamayim, the whole idea. But the vav in Yaakov? I can't imagine that's part of this principle."

Summary

  1. Principle 7: Moshe is father of all prophets before and after: angelic, no barrier, pure intellect, no intermediary. For the Rambam, not even Mashiach will equal him (Albo disagrees).
  2. Prophecy's essence is connection to Hashem, not foretelling the future.
  3. Four differences (Bamidbar 12): no intermediary (no riddles, no aspaklaria); awake, by day; no fear, as friend to friend; at will.
  4. Why the emphasis: a rebuttal of Christian and Muslim claims that the Torah was superseded or changed. קם = past, עוד = future is the Rambam's reading.
  5. The Arizal/Tanya claim about Rashbi: "the Rambam would call it heresy."
  6. Principle 8, four points: our Torah is Moshe's; wholly divine; all equally holy (hence the Rambam against standing for the Ten Commandments); the Oral Torah from Sinai.
  7. "The same Torah" means the words, not te'amim, tagin, sections or script. Even so, chaser/maleh, Rabbi Meir's Torah and keri/ketiv make letter-perfect identity "difficult," and probably not the Rambam's intent.
  8. The last eight verses: Yehoshua, or Moshe in tears.
