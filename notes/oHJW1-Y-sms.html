Topic: Why the child of a niddah is not a mamzer, and the Rambam's word pagum

Sources: The Gemara and the verse on niddah; the Rambam

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 8 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Classic cases producing a mamzer are adultery by a married woman with another Jew, and incestuous unions such as brother and sister. Before more examples, there are exceptions.
  • A niddah, who has had her period and not yet immersed in the mikveh, is forbidden by the Torah on pain of karet. Yet a child conceived while she was a niddah is not a mamzer.
  • The reason: kiddushin takes effect with a niddah. If a man betroths her with a ring before two witnesses, all agree the kiddushin works. The Gemara learns this from the verse about niddah.
  • Whether the second stage, nissuin, can take place with a niddah is debated; the Rambam held not, since its purpose is to make her permitted, which is impossible then. But all agree kiddushin works.
  • The logic: a mamzer comes from a foreign union. A niddah is not intrinsically forbidden to him, only for a limited time.
  • The Rambam adds, not found in the Gemara, that such a child, though not a mamzer even rabbinically, is pagum, blemished.
  • One opinion took this to mean a daughter may not marry a Kohen, but this is rejected, mainly because the Rambam does not list her among those forbidden to a Kohen.
  • The accepted view: the child may marry any Jew, but is not the first choice, lacking the finest lineage.

In one line

The child of a niddah is not a mamzer, since kiddushin takes effect with her, though the Rambam calls the child pagum, not the ideal match.
