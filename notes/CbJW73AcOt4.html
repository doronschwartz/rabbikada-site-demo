Topic: Why Sukkot falls in autumn: the Tur and the Rambam, and reading a Rishon in his context

Sources: Vayikra ("Ki vasukkot hoshavti et Benei Yisrael"); the Gemara; the Tur; the Rambam, Moreh Nevuchim

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Thursday 28 September 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • "For in sukkot I made Benei Yisrael dwell": the Gemara disputes whether these were real booths in the desert or the Ananei HaKavod, the clouds of glory.
  • Unusually, one must have the reason in mind when doing this misvah, since the Torah says "so that your generations may know". On sitting down in the sukkah, think of the booths and the clouds of glory.
  • Why is it in Tishri? Other festivals fall on the date of their event, but this could be at any time.
  • The Tur: in summer everyone sits outside and camps, so a sukkah would not stand out. In autumn, as people pack their huts away, HaShem says go out, so it is clear we do it to remember His miracles.
  • The Rambam, in Moreh Nevuchim, says the reverse: summer in Tammuz and Av is too hot to enjoy a sukkah, so it was given when the weather cools.
  • The opposite answers reflect where they lived: the Rambam in the Middle East with its unbearable summers, the Tur in Europe, where summer is for camping and October turns cold.
  • Whenever a Rishon gives an answer, ask where he lived and what his historical and social context was.

Sources quoted

  • The Tur: autumn makes it clear the sukkah is for the misvah.
  • The Rambam, Moreh Nevuchim: autumn because summer is too hot.

In one line

The Tur and the Rambam explain the timing of Sukkot in opposite ways, each true to his own climate.
