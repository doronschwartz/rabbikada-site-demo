Topic: Castration of females, and causing sterility indirectly

Sources: The Hakhamim (Tanna Kamma and Rabbi Yehudah); the Rambam, Mishneh Torah; Shulhan Arukh; the Gemara (kos shel ikkarin); Rashi on the Torah

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Tanna Kamma holds the Torah prohibition applies equally to females, human or animal. Rabbi Yehudah holds that for females it is only rabbinic, since it is not the same as the male reproductive organs, or perhaps because women do not have the misvah.
  • The halacha follows Rabbi Yehudah: the Rambam writes clearly, in his count of the misvot and in the Mishneh Torah, that the Torah prohibition concerns the male organs, and for a female it is rabbinic. The Shulhan Arukh rules likewise.
  • The difference matters greatly. The Gemara discusses a kos shel ikkarin, a potion that causes sterility; Rashi on the Torah says people did this at the beginning of Bereshit.
  • Drinking or giving such a potion causes sterility only indirectly. The Gemara concludes it is forbidden for a male and permitted for a female, human or animal, and so rule the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh.
  • The logic: for a male, direct castration is a Torah prohibition, so the indirect is rabbinic. For a female, direct is already only rabbinic, so the indirect, one level down, is permitted.
  • Summary: a male directly, Torah prohibition; a male indirectly, rabbinic; a female directly, rabbinic; a female indirectly, permitted.
  • How to define "indirect" follows next time.

In one line

Castrating a female is rabbinically forbidden, and sterilising her indirectly, as with a potion, is permitted, while for a male both are forbidden.
