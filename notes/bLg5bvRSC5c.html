Chapter 3 has two sections: Halachot 1–5, the "weighing," and Halachot 6–14, those with no share in the World to Come.

Part 1: The First and Second Sins Aren't Counted (Halacha 5)

בשעה ששוקלין עוונות אדם עם זכויותיו, אין מחשבין עליו עוון שחטא בו תחילה ולא שני, אלא משלישי ואילך. אם נמצאו עוונותיו משלישי ואילך מרובין על זכויותיו, הרי אותן שתי עוונות מצטרפין ודנין אותו על הכל. ואם נמצאו זכויותיו כנגד עוונותיו אשר מעוון שלישי ואילך, מעבירין כל עוונותיו ראשון ראשון, שהשלישי נחשב ראשון, שכבר נמחלו השניים, וכן הרביעי הרי הוא ראשון, שכבר נמחל השלישי, וכן עד סופן

When a person's sins are weighed against his merits, the first and second time he committed a sin are not counted, only from the third onward. If, from the third onward, his sins outweigh his merits, the first two join in and he is judged on everything. But if his merits balance his sins from the third onward, all his sins are passed over one by one: the third counts as the first, since the first two were already forgiven; the fourth is the first, since the third was forgiven; and so on to the end.

"Ma'avir rishon rishon"

We say it again and again in Selichot: אל מלך יושב על כסא רחמים… מוחל עוונות עמו, מעביר ראשון ראשון. "I wonder how many people actually know what it means." He passes them over, first, first. Now you do.

The maths

Your overall standing

1st and 2nd time

3rd time onward

Merits ≥ sins (tzaddik or beinoni)

Not counted

Each repeat "becomes the first" and is wiped: מעביר ראשון ראשון

Sins > merits (rasha)

Not counted, until…

…from the 3rd, all of them, including the first two, are weighed

What's going on?

"This is from the Gemara." And it is good news for perfectionists who give up after one slip: humans are fallible.

Sins come from fragmentation. If you live an integrated life, aligned with your core, a sin is a break in that: "I was angry, I was tempted, I was weak." If you're good on the whole, each sin, even repeated, is a fragment, not who you are. But if your sins outweigh your merits, this is your identity, so from the third time it counts.

Not a free pass

Every individual sin still has consequences. "Are we going to say to someone who broke Shabbat once, with witnesses and warning, 'it's the first time, you're exempt'? No, of course not." Yom Kippur deals with individual sins. The weighing in this chapter is about identity, and there, if your values are aligned with Hashem's will, repeated lapses don't define you.

Question: isn't "generally a good person" subjective? Hashem decides. And "good" doesn't mean having a nice heart; it means your value system is aligned with the will of Hashem: tzaddik or beinoni, as opposed to rasha.

Community: three, not two

במה דברים אמורים? ביחיד, שנאמר: הן כל אלה יפעל אל פעמים שלוש עם גבר. אבל הציבור תולין להם עוון ראשון שני ושלישי, שנאמר: על שלושה פשעי ישראל ועל ארבעה לא אשיבנו

This is for an individual ("twice, three times with a man," Iyov). For a community, the first, second and third are held over ("for three transgressions of Israel, and for four I will not turn it back," Amos, the haftarah of Vayeshev), and counting begins from the fourth.

The beinoni and tefillin

בינונים, אם היה בכלל מחצה עוונות שלהן שלא הניח תפילין מעולם, דנין אותו כפי חטאו

Normally Hashem tips the beinoni toward kindness (mateh kelapei chesed) and the sins are passed over. Exception: if among his sins is that he never once put on tefillin, he is judged for everything. The Gemara: קרקפתא דלא מנח תפילין, "a head that never wore tefillin."

"To Chabad's credit, putting tefillin on people randomly is very valuable. Even if a person doesn't wear tefillin his entire life, if he's worn it once, he leaves this category."

Even the wicked have a share

וכן כל הרשעים שעוונותיהן מרובין, דנין אותן כפי חטאיהם, ויש להם חלק לעולם הבא, שכל ישראל יש להם חלק לעולם הבא… שנאמר: ועמך כולם צדיקים, לעולם יירשו ארץ, ארץ זו משל, כלומר ארץ החיים, והוא העולם הבא. וכן חסידי אומות העולם יש להם חלק לעולם הבא

Every Jew has a share, smaller and different for the wicked, but a share. "The land" is a metaphor for the land of the living, Olam HaBa. And the righteous of the nations have a share too.

Part 2: Why the Chapter Turns to "No Share in Olam HaBa" (Halacha 6)

Many commentators are troubled: Halachot 1–5 are all about the weighing; Halacha 6 suddenly lists those with no share in Olam HaBa. The simple link: having said even the wicked have a share, he lists the exceptions. The deeper link: the whole chapter is about identity, and identity is fully realised in Olam HaBa, where everything you built becomes your relationship with Hashem. Some identities are "so rotten to the core" that there is nothing left.

ואלו הן שאין להן חלק לעולם הבא, אלא נכרתים ואובדין ונדונין על גודל רשען וחטאתם לעולם ולעולמי עולמים

These have no share in the World to Come; they are cut off, they perish, and they are judged for the magnitude of their wickedness forever and ever.

The rotten apple

Purification comes through suffering in this world, or Gehinnom, "12 months maximum." Think of an apple with bruises: cut out each bad spot and there's still a core. The more bruises, the more cutting. But an apple rotten to the core? Cutting would remove everything, so you throw the whole apple away. That is karet: your identity ceases to exist.

Then why "judged forever"?

Someone asked, back when the class learned Derech Hashem: "I live a hundred years of debauchery, die, and cease to exist. How would I know I'm missing anything? I got a hundred years of enjoyment!"

The Rambam, the Ramchal and Rav Kafich all say: though the identity ceases, an element remains that perceives what is missing, eternally. Otherwise you'd have a system where you could do as you please with no worry. "That's scary."

Part 3: The 24 with No Share (Halachot 6–13)

המינים, והאפיקורוסין, והכופרים בתורה, והכופרים בתחיית המתים ובביאת הגואל, המורדים, ומחטיאי הרבים, והפורשים מדרכי ציבור, והעושה עבירות ביד רמה בפרהסיא כיהויקים, והמוסרים, ומטילי אימה על הציבור שלא לשם שמים, ושופכי דמים, ובעלי לשון הרע, והמושך ערלתו

These are the first five of the 13 Principles.

  • האומר: "one who says." Rav Kafich: says means declares, a motto, like הוא היה אומר in Pirkei Avot, "he used to say." Someone who struggles with doubts isn't included; "that happens, and that person needs to work on it."
  • Sources. Each has a Gemara source: אמר נבל בליבו אין אלהים ("the fool said in his heart, there is no God"); Adam was created alone שלא יאמרו שתי רשויות, "so the minim wouldn't say there are two powers."
  • Except corporeality, for which there is no Gemara source. The Monday class spent chapters of Emunot VeDeot Essay 2 proving that anthropomorphic verses are metaphors. But the Ra'avad objects: why call this person a min? Many greater and better than him read the verses literally. This is the Rambam's own innovation. His logic: a God with a body isn't God. "A God who sits on a throne in heaven with a long white beard… you may say you believe in God, but you don't." You can't have a relationship with such a thing.
  • "It's not your fault?" Rav Chaim of Brisk: נעבעך אן אפיקורס איז אויך אן אפיקורס, "a poor unfortunate heretic is still a heretic." Through no fault of his own, his understanding is too warped for a relationship with Hashem.
  • #4 "first and Former of all": by adding וצור לכל, the Rambam rejects not only Aristotle (an eternal world) but Plato: a pre-existing hylē, unformed matter, from which God made the world. Some Rishonim considered that acceptable; the Rambam says Hashem created everything, matter included.
  • Why min? Rav Moshe Shapira: min means species, category. With such beliefs you are "a different category of human"; you have lost your identity as Israel.

"When we come to Yom Kippur, it's not just about our actions. It's also about our outlook. In fact it's probably more about our outlook than our actions."

Three apikorsim

#

Belief

6

There is no prophecy at all: no knowledge reaches people from God

7

Denying the unique prophecy of Moshe

8

God doesn't know what people do

  • Moshe's uniqueness was a point the Rambam "really, really scored home," more than Rav Saadiah did, because in his time Christianity and Islam each claimed a newer revelation.
  • אפיקורוס has two meanings:
  • Here: Epicurus, the Greek philosopher who denied any connection between God and humans. (The Rambam argues with him in the Moreh.)
  • Elsewhere, from הפקר: someone who has thrown off every rein. That's how someone who denigrates the Sages is called an apikores.

Three koferim baTorah

#

Denial

9

That the Torah is from God, even one verse, even one word: "Moshe made it up himself"

10

Its interpretation, the Oral Torah, and its transmitters (like Tzadok and Baitos)

11

Saying God replaced the Torah with another, even if He gave it (as the Christians and Muslims claim)

  • Standing for the Ten Commandments: the Rambam ruled against it in a responsum. It implies those words matter more than the rest, as if only they were from God. Every word is from God.
  • The two parts of the Oral Torah: (1) what was passed down from Moshe; (2) what was left to the Sages to interpret. Once the Sanhedrin decided by majority, it binds. Hence the Mishnah's story of a sage who rejected the majority and whose coffin was stoned (the Rabbi recalled it as R. Eliezer ben Hyrcanus; in the Mishnah, Eduyot, it is Elazar ben Chanoch).

  • Mark: what about conversos forced under the Inquisition? "You won't be considered a meshumad." Converting to Islam under duress? "Of course it's the wrong thing to do," but many say you needn't give your life for it, since it isn't actual idolatry.
  • Robert: someone who believes in no God at all, "we live and then we're eaten by worms"? Not a meshumad (who changes faith), but a min.

Robert: "cultural Jews"

Today many people don't keep Shabbat but are very involved in the community. "Of course you won't fall under this category." If you share in the community's troubles and joys, you are not poresh. A person who cares for his fellow Jews but believes "it's all Darwin" isn't poresh or an apostate either, "just a regular min."

Mesirah today

"This is why people are very reluctant to ever tell the police about another Jew." Many responsa explain that the classic mesirah doesn't apply in a democratic society under the rule of law, where Jews and non-Jews are, in theory, judged equally. The Sages' severity was about handing a Jew to a system where he'd be treated differently, to be killed, beaten or robbed.

Part 4: Lesser Sins Done Habitually (Halacha 14)

ויש עבירות קלות מאלו, ואף על פי כן אמרו חכמים שהרגיל בהן אין לו חלק לעולם הבא… המכנה שם לחבירו, והקורא לחבירו בכינויו, והמלבין פני חבירו ברבים, והמתכבד בקלון חבירו, והמבזה תלמידי חכמים, והמבזה רבותיו, והמבזה את המועדות, והמחלל את הקדשים

The Gemara says, e.g., המלבין פני חבירו ברבים אין לו חלק לעולם הבא: one who shames another publicly has no share. Why isn't it among the 24? The Rambam's own reading: the 24 are identities, philosophies, or blatant and constant public conduct. These are lesser sins; once is a mistake ("you do teshuvah, fine"). It's the habitual offender who loses his share.

Part 5: Nothing Stands Before Teshuvah

במה דברים אמורים שכל אחד מאלו אין לו חלק לעולם הבא? כשמת בלא תשובה. אבל אם שב מרשעו ומת והוא בעל תשובה, הרי זה מבני העולם הבא, שאין לך דבר שעומד בפני התשובה. אפילו כפר בעיקר כל ימיו ובאחרונה שב, יש לו חלק לעולם הבא, שנאמר: שלום שלום לרחוק ולקרוב… כל הרשעים והמומרים וכיוצא בהן שחזרו בתשובה, בין בגלוי בין במטמוניות, מקבלין אותן

All this applies only if he died without teshuvah. If he repented, he has a share, for nothing stands before teshuvah. Even if he denied the fundamentals all his life and repented at the end: "Peace, peace to the far and to the near." All who return, openly or in secret, are accepted.

Even someone who has come back privately but not yet publicly: "we take you on and we encourage you."

Aldous Huxley, an atheist writer of the 20th century, wrote late in life that he and others had wanted there to be no Creator, because a Creator would bring moral consequences, and he later changed his mind. "If at the end of his life he recognised Hashem, it's a different story. A person can always do teshuvah."

Summary

  1. The first and second occurrences of a sin are never weighed. If your merits hold, every repeat "becomes the first" and is passed over: מעביר ראשון ראשון. Sins are fragmentation, not identity, unless they are your identity.
  2. Individual sins still carry consequences; this weighing is about who you are.
  3. A community gets three, not two. Tefillin, even once, matters enormously.
  4. Every Jew has a share in Olam HaBa, and so do the righteous of the nations.
  5. The 24 without a share are identities rotten to the core: 5 minim, 3 apikorsim, 3 koferim baTorah, 2 meshumadim, and the rest. Karet is the apple thrown away, though something remains that knows its loss.
  6. Corporeality as heresy is the Rambam's own innovation: a God with a body isn't God.
  7. Lesser sins done habitually (nicknames, public shaming, disgracing the festivals…) also forfeit Olam HaBa.
  8. Nothing stands before teshuvah.

"These are the big things we need to be thinking about before we do teshuvah, not just 'I did this, I did that.'"
