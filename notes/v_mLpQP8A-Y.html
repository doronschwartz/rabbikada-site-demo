Topic: The origins of lighting in the synagogue, and whether to say a berakhah in other public places

Sources: The Gemara; Sefer HaIttur; the Meiri; the Ritva; Rambam; the Rivash; Sefer HaManhig; Shibbolei HaLeket; Bet Yoseph; Shulhan Arukh; Rav Shelomoh Zalman Auerbach; Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah, Wednesday 13 December 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The misvah is "ner ish uveito", lighting in each home. Neither the Gemara nor the Geonim mention lighting in public places or in the synagogue.
  • The earliest mention he knows is Sefer HaIttur (Rabbi Yishak ben Abba Mari, Marseille, 12th century): the minhag was to light in the synagogue with a berakhah, though people lit at home. The Meiri and Ritva record it; Rambam does not.
  • Five reasons are given: the Meiri, an extension of pirsumei nisa; the Rivash (Barcelona, 14th century), for those who could not light at home because of persecution; Sefer HaManhig, the synagogue is a mikdash meat, and the miracle happened in the Mikdash; Shibbolei HaLeket (Rome), the hazan lived in the synagogue, so it was his home; the Bet Yoseph, for travellers who slept there, like the old minhag of kiddush in synagogue on Friday night.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules that a berakhah is said, notable since elsewhere he holds there is no berakhah on a minhag. The poskim require ten people present, even women and children.
  • Lighting at other public settings (a Bar Misvah, the Kotel, a Hanukkah party) is of course allowed, but a berakhah is controversial. Most poskim, including Rav Shelomoh Zalman Auerbach and Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul, say no berakhah, since the synagogue's reasons do not apply.
  • Hakham Ovadiah, seeing pirsumei nisa as the main reason, held there is what to rely on to say a berakhah, so that those who do not light will see and hear it.

In one line

The synagogue lighting with a berakhah goes back to the Rishonim; at other public events a berakhah is disputed, though one may rely on Hakham Ovadiah.
