Topic: The blessing and blowing of the shofar, Part 2: changing blowers, the hazan who blows, calling out the notes, and payment Source: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Siman 585, paragraphs 3-5 Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada at the Thursday Shulhan Arukh class, Tiferet Eyal, Thursday 15 September 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

When the blower cannot continue

The class was dedicated to the recovery of an unwell woman. The Rabbi reviewed the previous week: the shofar is heard standing, the misvah is to hear and not to blow, and the hundred sounds are built up in stages.

Paragraph 3 deals with a practical problem. The baal tokeah has said the berakhah, blown one or two sounds, and then cannot blow any more. Someone else may continue, and a third or fourth person after him if needed, and none of them says a new berakhah. The berakhah said at the start covers everyone who heard it.

Even if the first blower said the berakhah and nothing came out of the shofar at all, it is not a berakhah levatalah. The berakhah was said to fulfil the misvah for the whole community, and they do fulfil it when someone else blows.

Someone asked whether a long gap or a different shofar requires a new berakhah, as when you take a biscuit you had not intended to eat. The Rabbi explained the difference: a berakhah on food is on that particular food, but the berakhah here is "to hear the sound of the shofar", not on this shofar. "You don't care about the shofar. You care about hearing."

If no one present can blow. Someone must be brought from elsewhere. Everyone stays where they are without talking. The person sent to find him may talk, since that is part of the misvah. If the new blower has already fulfilled the misvah, he simply continues on the original berakhah. If he has not, and was not there to hear it, he says the berakhah himself.

If the shofar fails during the blowing of Musaf, the missing sounds are made up later.

Shehiyanu on the second day, and answering Amen

Someone described a Sefaradi shul in which the blower on the second day had in mind members who had not heard the shofar on the first day, so that his Shehiyanu would cover them. The Rabbi said this was "100% correct", but it must be announced clearly, otherwise people will object that Shehiyanu is not said. He compared it to Purim: Sefaradim say three berakhot at night and two by day, but someone in hospital who missed the night reading says Shehiyanu in the day, because for him it is the first time.

This led to a question about answering Amen. If a woman said Shehiyanu when lighting candles for Yom Tov, should she answer Amen to her husband's Shehiyanu in Kiddush? Rav Ovadia Yosef says she should not, since the Amen comes between borei peri hagefen and drinking, and she does not need that blessing. Other poskim allow it. In the same way, according to Rav Ovadia, those who already heard the shofar on the first day should not answer Amen to a Shehiyanu said for others on the second day.

Rav Ovadia goes further: someone who hears Ashkenazim, the Spanish and Portuguese or Moroccans say a berakhah on half Hallel should not answer Amen. The Rabbi found this very difficult. For them it is a correct berakhah following their custom, and "if they were to ask me, I'd say of course you say the berakhah." At the other extreme, the Hida writes that a Sefaradi praying in a minyan that says the berakhah may say it too. "I rely on this every month for Rosh Hodesh," the Rabbi said, though he wanted to check the Hida's exact reasoning.

Should the hazan blow during Musaf?

Paragraph 4: for the blowing during the Amidah, whether in the silent Amidah or only in the repetition, the hazan should not blow himself, in case he becomes confused and loses his place. If he is confident that he can blow and return to his prayer smoothly, he may. The Rabbi compared this to Birkat Kohanim, where a hazan who is a Kohen goes up only if he is confident he can return to his place.

Fluency in prayer matters. The Rabbi recalled Rabbi Hanina ben Dosa in Berakhot: "If my prayer is fluent in my mouth, I know it is accepted." A hazan must be "in control" of every word. If he is nervous about the prayer or the blowing, he should do one or the other.

The same blower for all the sounds. If the hazan of Musaf will not blow during Musaf, he should not blow the first thirty either. Whoever blows in Musaf should also blow the first thirty, just as opening and closing the ark are not split between two people: "You begin the misvah, you finish it." The Rabbi mentioned a frightening midrash about someone who began a misvah and did not complete it. It is not forbidden to split it, but it is better not to.

When no one else can blow. The Mishnah Berurah brings two views for a hazan who is not confident and has no one else: leave the blasts until after Musaf, or trust that Hashem will help him. The Rabbi drew the principle: bitahon is for when there is no alternative. "Don't rely on bitahon when you've got other alternatives."

Calling out the notes

The Rema records the custom that someone calls out each sound before it is blown, so the blower makes no mistake. The Rabbi explained that the Rema is the first to record it.

Community

Calling out the notes

Ashkenazim

Yes, and the Rabbi should be the one to call them if he is not blowing

Spanish and Portuguese

Yes

Oriental Sefaradim

No; the Arizal was very much against it

Calling the first note. Should the caller say "tekiah" for the very first sound, between the berakhah and the blowing? Asking for salt between the berakhah and eating is permitted because it serves the meal, but it is more problematic before the berakhah has "landed" on anything. The Magen Avraham says even the first note is called out; the Mishnah Berurah is not clear about it.

With Birkat Kohanim the hazan reads out each word because the Torah says "say to them". Even there, the old Iraqi custom, the Spanish and Portuguese custom and the Syrian custom was not to read out the first word. The Ben Ish Hai writes that in Baghdad they did not, until he changed it because of the Arizal. For the shofar there is no source at all for calling out the notes, so the Rabbi's view was that where there is no clear custom, it is better not to call out the first one.

A segulah when the blower is stuck

The Mishnah Berurah brings a story of a blower who could not produce a sound, turned the shofar around and said verses into it, and was then able to blow. May one interrupt with verses like this? If we knew for certain it works, it would be part of the misvah; if we knew it did not, it would be forbidden. The Shaarei Teshuvah limits it to the later blasts and forbids it during the first thirty, since we do not know it works. But he adds that sending for someone from outside would be a greater interruption.

The Rabbi's conclusion: if there is someone else to blow, use him. Saying verses just because you are embarrassed is forbidden. But if there is no one else at all, "what's there to lose?" He noted that the Rambam permits certain amulets on Shabbat even though he writes that amulets have no power, because psychologically they can calm a person.

Being paid to blow

Paragraph 5 says that one who takes payment for blowing the shofar, leading the prayers or preaching on Shabbat or Yom Tov will never see blessing from that money. The Rabbi said this is easily misunderstood. Being paid for a religious service is not the problem; the problem is earning money on Shabbat. Some permit payment for a misvah on Shabbat, and the Shulhan Arukh says that even according to them, one does not see blessing from it.

The accepted solution is that rabbis, readers, hazanim and blowers are paid for their preparation during the week. So they must really prepare, "however experienced you are." He had seen hazanim who looked as if they had not opened the mahzor since last year, which is "a real problem". Preparation on Shabbat does not count: when he was young and prepared the parashah on Shabbat afternoon, he made sure to spend ten minutes on Friday as well.

Practical summary

  • If the blower cannot continue, another person present continues without a new berakhah. Someone brought from outside says a berakhah only if he has not yet fulfilled the misvah himself.
  • Following Rav Ovadia Yosef, one who already fulfilled a misvah does not answer Amen to a Shehiyanu said for others when it would interrupt. The Rabbi himself relies on the Hida to say the berakhah on half Hallel in a minyan that says it.
  • The hazan of Musaf should blow during Musaf only if he is confident he will not lose his place; ideally the same person blows the first thirty and the Musaf blasts.
  • Ashkenazim and the Spanish and Portuguese call out the notes; Oriental Sefaradim do not. Where there is no established custom, better not to call out the first note.
  • Verses or segulot to get the shofar working are only for when there is no one else to blow.
  • Those paid to blow, read or lead prayers should prepare before Shabbat and Yom Tov so their payment is for weekday work.
