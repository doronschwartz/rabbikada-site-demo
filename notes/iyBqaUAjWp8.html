Topic: The halacha for a child born more than 12 months after the husband left, and two caveats

Sources: The Rambam; the Meiri; the Bahag; the Rosh; Shulhan Arukh; the Ran

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 14 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Beyond 12 months the Rambam holds the child is a mamzer, while the Meiri (a longer delay is possible) and the Bahag (the husband may have come back unseen) disagree. The Rosh also rejects the Bahag: otherwise the Gemara would not need the delayed pregnancy.
  • As it is a dispute among the Rishonim, the Shulhan Arukh rules that beyond 12 months the child is a safek mamzer.
  • A safek mamzer is in some ways worse off: a mamzer may marry a mamzeret or a convert, but a safek mamzer can marry neither a regular Jew nor a mamzer. Yet a further doubt can make things easier.
  • First caveat (the Ran, accepted): this applies only when there is no evidence, or the couple are silent or contradict each other. If both say he came back secretly and they were together, they are believed and the child is kasher by all opinions, even after 18 months away.
  • Second caveat: both arguments, the delayed pregnancy and the husband's return, apply only if the woman has not acted suspiciously. If there are doubts about her conduct during his absence, the child is presumed a mamzer, even at 11 and a half months.
  • So for a husband in prison: up to 12 months the child is kasher if she has not acted suspiciously; beyond that a safek mamzer, unless both say they were somehow together.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: beyond 12 months, a safek mamzer.
  • The Ran: not when both spouses affirm that he came back.

In one line

Beyond 12 months the child is a safek mamzer unless both spouses say he returned, and no leniency applies if the woman acted suspiciously.
