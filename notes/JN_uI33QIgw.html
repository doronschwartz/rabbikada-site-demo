Coming and waiting

Ketz yamin, with a final nun! "I've always thought it was yamim with a mem. Looking now, it's yamin, a play on yamim: days. I wonder if anyone notices when they sing it."

Both stress not only the coming but the waiting.

"In distinction to other religions in which the Mashiach has already come": "Rabbi Sacks used to talk about this a lot. It's a source of hope: we haven't yet experienced the Golden Age; we're awaiting the redemption of mankind. There's comfort in that."

Part 4: What "Mashiach" Means

Root מ־ש־ח: to anoint, to smear. Both the Kohen and the king were anointed with oil: the Kohen Gadol is HaKohen HaMashiach.

"At the coronation after Queen Elizabeth passed away, an essential part of the ceremony was the oil from Jerusalem."

Mashiach is primarily a king, so he's "the anointed"; but HaMashiach, the anointed one: the finale, the highest of all the kings. "That's where the Rambam gets his greatness above all kings."

Restoring the judges, the Sanhedrin, Yerushalayim: "not specifically Mashiach"

"We pray for wisdom, parnassah, health: people focus there, and towards the later berachot we lose focus. But two (or three) are about Mashiach. Praying them three times a day, or as women do, that's how we fulfil awaiting Mashiach."

The source

Shabbat 31a: among the questions asked of a person after 120: צפית לישועה? Did you await the salvation?

Is waiting really part of the principle?

The Rambam says so here and in Mishneh Torah (או מי שאינו מחכה לביאתו). The Brisker Rav wrote a lot on this: the principle is to believe and await.

"The difficulty: to call someone who believes but doesn't actively await a denier of a principle: quite severe, quite harsh. So others reinterpret it. It doesn't seem like that to me. Perhaps because it's so distant, believing intellectually isn't enough; you have to live with it, and that's waiting."

"Every day"?

The Ani Ma'amin's bechol yom isn't in the Rambam. "The Gemara says there are times Mashiach can't come: Shabbat and Yom Tov, and so on." "I don't know if the Rambam meant every single day. The point is it's in your mind, regularly."

The Chafetz Chaim (R. Yisrael Meir HaKohen): "it's told he had a suit and a suitcase packed for Mashiach. Legend or not, it's only 100 years ago, so it's very likely true."

Part 6: Don't Calculate

Why does the Rambam put this right after waiting? "Calculate a date and you've said he's coming then, in however many years. That pushes it off and stops you thinking he can come at any moment."

The Rambam never calculated: forbidden, and part of this principle. Ibn Ezra also forbids it. Yet:

Calculated a date

Roughly

Rav Saadia Gaon

20–30 years after his own death

The Ramban

The 1200s ("I think")

Abarbanel

The 1500s

The Malbim

The 1900s

"Many great Chachamim calculated dates. And the reality is they were all wrong."

Defending them: "The most basic answer: these weren't definitive dates but yearnings and hopes: a ripe time for Mashiach."

The Rambam's reply: "Even predicting a ripe time 10 or 20 years off takes away from the yearning. Don't do this. It's forbidden. Stay away."

Will: The Talmud often mentions 6,000 years. Would the Rambam say that's the wrong approach?

"Excellent question. Two points:

1. No one in the Talmud makes a calculation. The calculations are by later authorities. The 6,000 years says the world will exist 6,000 years, not that Mashiach comes by then (though that would follow).

2. The Rambam says explicitly it's not to be taken literally: one opinion we don't follow, or read differently. It could be longer, even eternal. No 6,000-year limit."

Part 7: A Process, Not a Moment

"Not going into it tonight, but the Rambam 100% believes it's a gradual process, and so did Rav Saadia: not from one moment to the next."

Where are we going? "Towards Eretz Yisrael: full sovereignty, the Beit HaMikdash rebuilt, the Sanhedrin sitting again, as a sovereign nation lived in Temple times, like the empires of David and Shlomo, when all the nations around came."

Rav Kook and many others saw the rebirth of the State of Israel as the beginning of the process. "Historically it's never happened that a nation is exiled twice and returns: Babylon (Iraq), back; Rome, back a third time. And the miracles of the State today, I think, are pretty clearly supernatural. Of course there's much more to do: many faults and problems, and we're clearly not sovereign: all the wars we've been at. But it's the beginning."

Part 8: A Person or an Era?

For the Rambam, Mashiach is an actual person: "he will come", "greatest of all kings". Not just a concept or an era. But some thought otherwise.

Midrash Tanchuma: אמר הקב״ה: בעולם הזה הייתם נושעים על ידי בני אדם: in Egypt Moshe; under Sisera Devorah and Barak; against Midian… the Judges; ועל ידי שהיו בשר ודם, הייתם חוזרים ומשתעבדים: human saviours, so it didn't last. לעתיד לבוא: אני בעצמי גואל אתכם, ושוב אין אתם משתעבדים, as it says ישראל נושע בה׳ תשועת עולמים.

Rav Menachem Kasher (Torah Shelemah, the last 150 years):

- Hoshea, Yeshayahu, Michah, Yirmiyahu and Zechariah speak of a specific human Mashiach.

- Nachum, Tzefaniah, Chavakuk, Malachi, Yoel and Daniel don't.

So perhaps not necessarily a person. "I think that's very, very difficult. The Rambam would certainly disagree."

Part 9: Rabbi Hillel's "No Mashiach"

Sanhedrin 99a: רבי הלל אומר: אין להם משיח לישראל, שכבר אכלוהו בימי חזקיה. There's no Mashiach for Israel; they already "consumed" him in Chizkiyahu's day.

אמר רב יוסף: שרי ליה מריה לרבי הלל! May his Master forgive Rabbi Hillel! Chizkiyahu was in the First Temple, yet Zechariah, in the Second, prophesied Mashiach still to come.

Rashi: אין להם משיח לישראל, אלא הקב״ה ימלוך בעצמו ויגאלם לבדו: Rabbi Hillel didn't deny a Messianic era, only a personal individual Messiah; the prophecies of a person were fulfilled in Chizkiyahu. "Already in Rashi: collective redemption rather than an individual leading the way."

Rav Yosef Albo's challenge (Sefer HaIkkarim, the opening)

"A book of principles that begins by saying principles are dangerous."

The Rambam counts Mashiach as a principle, and whoever denies a principle is a heretic with no portion in Olam HaBa. וזה דבר מתמיה מאד: Rabbi Hillel, אחד מחכמי ישראל הנזכרים בתלמוד, said ein Mashiach, so by the Rambam he'd be a kofer. Then why does the Gemara give him that honoured title? "When the Gemara cites someone to refute him, it's in derogatory terms. Not here."

Albo rejects the reinterpretations ("may he be forgiven" means he was wrong) and concludes: בלי ספק רבי הלל לא היה מאמין בביאת המשיח כלל, ואף על פי כן לא היה נקרא כופר, because ביאת המשיח אינה עיקר לתורת משה: the Torah can exist without it.

Mashiach is…

Denier is…

The Rambam

A principle of faith

A heretic, no portion in Olam HaBa

Albo

A true belief every Jew must hold, but not foundational

Wrong, not a kofer, like one who holds creation from pre-existing matter (Principle 4)

"That's one of Albo's big proofs against the Thirteen Principles." (Others: Rashi's era reading; or Mashiach without miracles.)

"I'm relying on Rabbi Hillel": the Chatam Sofer

The Chatam Sofer (R. Moshe Sofer, ~250 years ago) was asked exactly this. Today you would be a kofer.

"Like halachah: two views in the Gemara, sometimes on huge matters; once the psak is given, it must be followed. A chacham who sticks to his view against the Sanhedrin's ruling: terrible things can happen to him."

"The same in theology. Everyone now holds there will be a Messianic era. You can't say I eat chicken and dairy because Rabbi Yosei HaGelili did."

And Rabbi Hillel himself? "Then there was no psak: it was his view, and he probably changed it once he saw the consensus."

"Today everyone would agree a person must believe in Mashiach. Deny it: per the Rambam, a heretic; per Albo, wrong but not a heretic, like other beliefs, e.g. Gan Eden, which the Rambam says clearly is not one of the principles."

Part 10: The Seed of Shlomo?

Rav Kafich, "authoritative on the Rambam's thought in many ways": not to be taken literally.

- He doesn't know the Rambam's source, and some Gemarot suggest otherwise.

- Throughout Mishneh Torah, whenever the Rambam discusses Mashiach, he says only the House of David, never Shlomo.

- His suggestion: the Rambam added Shlomo to counter the Christian genealogy of Jesus through Natan son of David. "I wouldn't say hyperbole, but not an essential part."

Part 11: How Do We Recognise Mashiach? (Hilchot Melachim 11:3–4)

"Don't think the Mashiach must perform miracles, do new things, or revive the dead."

Proof: Rabbi Akiva, a great chacham of the Mishnah, was Bar Kozba's arms-bearer, and he and many sages of his generation thought him Mashiach until he was killed. They never asked him for a miracle.

Jesus and "the Yishmaeli who followed"

The Rambam: people thought Yeshu HaNotzri was Mashiach, and he was killed. Why would Hashem allow that? "Machshevot haBorei: we don't fully understand Hashem's ways."

"Notice: whenever the Rambam refers to Jesus he names him; Muhammad he never names": וכל הדברים האלו של ישו הנוצרי ושל זה הישמעאלי שעמד אחריו. "He had no respect for Muhammad."

אינן אלא לישר דרך למלך המשיח: only to pave the way for the King Mashiach. Christianity dominates the world, and so the idea of Mashiach has spread everywhere; "when the true Mashiach comes, they'll realise they were wrong."

"The wars of Hashem": physical or spiritual?

"Famously, many believed the Lubavitcher Rebbe was Mashiach. The son of Rav Mordechai Eliyahu, Rav Yosef Eliyahu, told me his father used to say the Lubavitcher Rebbe maybe was Mashiach in his time. But he's died. And he went to no wars that I know of. So Chabad say the wars of Hashem are spiritual wars."

"I don't think that's what the Rambam means: it's in the same paragraph as Bar Kozba, who fought physical wars, the revolt against Rome, successful for a time. The simple reading is physical wars. Front line or in the background? I don't know. He leads a people who win wars and take back sovereignty of Eretz Yisrael."

"Nowhere in Mishneh Torah or this principle does the Rambam say Mashiach needs prophetic powers or to be a miracle worker."

Will he be a rabbi?

"He studies, knows and teaches Torah and keeps the mitzvot: if that's not a rabbi, what is? But he needn't have the title. Many people do all of that and aren't rabbis, and they'd be fully able to be Mashiach." "A rabbi and a warrior."

Part 12: What Happens Then? (Hilchot Melachim 12)

Life continues as usual, with two differences:

1. Sovereignty

"No more UN resolutions, no more wars: completely sovereign"

2. Freedom from the burdens of making a living

"Not that money grows on trees, nor clothes, as some passages indicate; nor the wolf literally dwelling with the sheep: metaphors for peace and tranquillity"

Hilchot Melachim 12:4: לא נתאוו החכמים והנביאים ימות המשיח… אלא כדי שיהיו פנויין בתורה וחכמתה, ולא יהיה להם נוגש ומבטל, כדי שיזכו לחיי העולם הבא.

We long for Mashiach only to be free for Torah and wisdom, with nothing to hinder us, so as to merit Olam HaBa.

"This is key. As we explained last week, Olam HaBa is the direct consequence of one's moral and intellectual development. In Messianic times we'll have more time for it. A person today who's financially stable and free to sit and learn is already, in a sense, living in the times of Mashiach."

"Free will remains. Life continues as usual. But Israel is sovereign, and we're far freer to develop our intellect, pursue wisdom and keep the mitzvot."

Summary

  1. Principle 12, five elements: Mashiach will come; await him even if he delays; don't calculate; he'll be greater than all kings; from David (and Shlomo).
  2. Torah sources: Nitzavim (the ingathering) and Balak (two Messiahs: David and the last).
  3. Mashiach = the anointed king, the anointed one.
  4. Waiting is part of the principle (צפית לישועה; the Brisker Rav): we do it in the Amidah. "Every day" is the Ani Ma'amin's addition.
  5. Calculating pushes him away. Rav Saadia, the Ramban, Abarbanel and the Malbim did it, as "ripe times", and were all wrong. The 6,000 years isn't a deadline, and the Rambam doesn't take it literally.
  6. A gradual process: Rav Kook and the State of Israel as its beginning.
  7. A person, not just an era (vs Rashi on Rabbi Hillel, Rav Kasher). Rabbi Hillel's denial: Albo says not a kofer, because Mashiach isn't foundational; the Chatam Sofer: after the psak, today you would be.
  8. Seed of Shlomo: Rav Kafich, anti-Christian emphasis, absent from Mishneh Torah.
  9. Recognising him: Torah, mitzvot, leadership, the wars of Hashem (physical, the Rabbi argues) → presumed; Temple and ingathering → certain. No miracles required (Rabbi Akiva and Bar Kozba). Jesus and Muhammad "paved the way."
  10. Messianic times: sovereignty and freedom to learn, for the sake of Olam HaBa.
