Topic: The duty to study the laws of Pesah from thirty days before, and the names of the festival

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; the Ran and the Rashba; the Torah (Parashat Ki Tissa); the tefillah; the Rambam, Hilchot Hames UMassah

Daily Halacha: Purim, Monday 25 March 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara, in two places, says that from 30 days before Pesah we must ask, discuss and expound its laws. The source: Moshe taught about Pesah Sheni, 30 days after Pesah, already on Pesah Rishon. A second opinion says only from Rosh Hodesh Nisan, when Moshe taught the laws of the korban; the Shulhan Arukh follows the first.
  • The Ran and the Rashba held this duty applies only to rabbis who instruct (morei horaah); the halacha is that it falls on every person.
  • The enactment originally concerned the korbanot of Pesah, which were more numerous than on other festivals, but because the laws of Pesah are so vast the duty still applies, and these weeks will go systematically through them.
  • The Torah calls it Hag HaMassot, for the massot we left Egypt with, and Hag HaPesah in Parashat Ki Tissa, for HaShem passing over Jewish homes, hence Passover.
  • "Hag HaAviv" is not a name in the Torah, which says to keep Pesah in the month of spring. That verse is the whole reason for this year's extra Adar; without it Pesah would have been yesterday.
  • The tefillah calls it Zeman Herutenu. Other nations overthrowing their masters sought to enslave them; Israel only wanted to be free, the first time in history freedom appears as a moral value. Perhaps this is why the reigns of Jewish kings were counted from Rosh Hodesh Nisan, to remind them not to enslave their subjects.
  • The Shulhan Arukh calls these Hilchot Pesah, while the Rambam calls them Hilchot Hames UMassah, seeing hames and massah as the essence of the halacha.

Sources quoted

In one line

From thirty days before Pesah everyone must study its laws, a festival named for the massah, the passing over and our freedom.
