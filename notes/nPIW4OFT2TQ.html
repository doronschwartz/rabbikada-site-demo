Topic: Davar haaved for businesses: reputation, employees' wages and daily newspapers

Sources: Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Friday 7 November 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The poskim, and Hakham Ovadiah too, write that a non-food shop closed for eight or nine days may suffer long-term damage to its reputation as a serious business. If there is a real (not remote) chance of this, even if doubtful, it may count as davar haaved and the shop may open.
  • A few hundred years ago the poskim discussed a book printer with many employees, whose work had nothing to do with the moed, but who would still have to pay salaries if closed.
  • Some held this is not davar haaved, since the loss of wages is not directly related to the melakhah.
  • Hakham Ovadiah and other poskim held it certainly is: one loses by paying staff to sit idle, even if indirectly. So one with employees may be lenient.
  • Hakham Ovadiah therefore allows daily newspapers in Israel to print on Hol HaMoed: closing would mean paying salaries (if paid per article, simply do not produce it), and readers expecting a daily paper might turn to inappropriate ones, a public need (sorkhei rabbim), a separate heter.
  • Davar haaved can be flexible and broad, and each person must genuinely weigh the consequences of not working. Employees follow next.

Sources quoted

In one line

A business may stay open on Hol HaMoed when closing risks its reputation or means paying idle employees, as Hakham Ovadiah rules.
