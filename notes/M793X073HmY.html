Topic: Why the age for marriage is 18 when misvot begin at bar misvah

Sources: The Mishnah ("ben shemoneh esreh lahuppah"); the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 23 January 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Although all misvot apply from bar misvah, the Mishnah gives 18 as the age for marriage. The Rambam reads "ben shemoneh esreh" as entering the 18th year, that is from 17.
  • Why later than 13? First, to allow years of full dedication to Torah study before the burden of a family.
  • Second, one need not spend more than a fifth of one's assets on a positive misvah (as with an expensive etrog); the burden of a family at so young an age is comparable.
  • Third, most misvot have a set time, but this one spans a lifetime, so it is reasonable to wait for maturity.
  • The Rambam and Shulhan Arukh, from the Gemara, set the latest age at 20; the reasons and exceptions follow in the next note.

In one line

Marriage is set at 17 or 18 rather than 13 to allow Torah study and maturity, and should happen by 20.
