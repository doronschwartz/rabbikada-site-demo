Topic: Introducing the halachot of Hol HaMoed: their place in the series, their length and their nature

Sources: The Torah

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Monday 8 September 2025

The points

  • Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur and Sukkot were covered in previous years and can be found in the archives. The series continues with the Moadim, having covered the positive misvot of Yom Tov, the melakhot, Yom Tov Sheni and select halachot of the Three Weeks.
  • What remains is Hol HaMoed: what we must do, and what is forbidden.
  • All the halachot of Yom Tov, including Yom Tov Sheni, apply to Rosh Hashanah too; Yom Kippur has the status of Shabbat for melakhah. Hol HaMoed exists only on Pesah and Sukkot.
  • Pesah is seven days in the Torah: Israel has five days of Hol HaMoed (days 2 to 6), outside Israel four (days 3 to 6).
  • Sukkot is seven days plus Shemini Aseret: Israel has six days of Hol HaMoed (days 2 to 7), outside Israel five (days 3 to 7). This is a substantial part of the year.
  • The Torah calls these days holy callings (mikra kodesh). Moed comes from the root "to meet": a meeting between us and HaShem. Yet certain melakhot are permitted, so the days are both holy and mundane.

In one line

Hol HaMoed, four to six days on Pesah and Sukkot, is both a holy meeting time with HaShem and partly mundane.
