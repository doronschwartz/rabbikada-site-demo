Topic: The custom of standing for the reading of the Ten Commandments

Sources: The Gemara; Rambam, Teshuvah; Keter Shem Tov; Moroccan Hakhamim; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Monday 22 May 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • On the first day of Shavuot we read the Aseret HaDibberot from Parashat Yitro, and in some communities people stand.
  • The Gemara says there was a wish to add the Ten Commandments to the daily Shema, but it was not done because of heretics who would claim they are the main part of the Torah. Every word of Torah is equally important.
  • The Rambam, in a famous Teshuvah, uses this to rule that one should not stand, as it implies this part is more important.
  • Among Ashkenazim the custom to stand developed. The Keter Shem Tov records that the Spanish and Portuguese in London and Amsterdam stand (also in Yitro and Vaethanan during the year), and Moroccan Hakhamim record the same in Morocco. As far as he knows, Middle Eastern communities did not accept it and should not.
  • His theory: the Rabbi usually receives this Aliyah, the congregation stands for him and stays standing. In London's Spanish and Portuguese community they stand for the Rabbi, sit for the verses before, then stand again.
  • The poskim write that those without the custom should not take it on.
  • Hakham Ovadiah: one who does not stand, in a community that does, should stand, not separating from the community, but from the start of the Aliyah, to show he stands for the whole Aliyah. Conversely, one who stands should stay seated in a community that sits.

In one line

Keep your community's custom on standing for the Ten Commandments, and elsewhere follow the congregation, standing from the start of the Aliyah.
