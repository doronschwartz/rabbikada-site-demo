Topic: May women count the Omer, and may they say the Berakhah?

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; the Rama; the Ben Ish Hai; the Mishnah Berurah

Daily Halacha: Sefirat HaOmer, Wednesday 21 April 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Women are exempt from any positive time-bound misvah (lulav, sukkah, shofar). If they choose to do it they are still rewarded, which is why women hear the shofar and take the lulav.
  • The debate is whether a woman may say a Berakhah on a misvah she is not obligated in. The Shulhan Arukh rules she may not, since the Berakhah says "and commanded us"; the Rama rules she may.
  • Ashkenazi women generally do say the Berakhah. Sepharadi practice varies by community, but the mainstream practice, following the Shulhan Arukh, is without a Berakhah (shofar, sukkah).
  • So seemingly women may count the Omer, Ashkenazim with a Berakhah and Sepharadim without.
  • Two qualifications. For Sepharadim, the Ben Ish Hai rules that women should not count at all, on the Kabbalistic notion that counting is a masculine misvah. For Ashkenazim, many women do not count with a Berakhah.
  • Final ruling: Sepharadi women may count but without a Berakhah. Ashkenazi women generally count without one, but a woman who understands what she counts and will count every night may count with a Berakhah.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: the principle that women are exempt from positive time-bound misvot.
  • Shulhan Arukh and the Rama: whether a woman says a Berakhah on a misvah she is not commanded in.
  • The Ben Ish Hai: women should not count the Omer at all.
  • The Mishnah Berurah: reasons Ashkenazi women do not say the Berakhah, that she may not understand the count, which is integral to the misvah, or may forget other nights.

In one line

Women may count the Omer; Sepharadi women without a Berakhah, Ashkenazi women generally without, unless she understands the count and will keep it every night.
