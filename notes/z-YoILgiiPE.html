Topic: What a Jew or a Jewish vet college may ask a non-Jew to do regarding neutering

Sources: The Taz (Turei Zahav); the Teshuvot Bemareh HaBazak

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • One may not ask a non-Jew to castrate an animal, whether the reason is Amirah LeAkum or Lifnei Iver. So a Jewish college may not require non-Jewish students to perform forbidden castration.
  • The Hakhamim were so severe that an animal castrated at a Jew's request may not be benefited from.
  • Three things may be asked of a non-Jew, including non-Jewish students or vets.
  • First: giving a male hormones that make the semen non-viable permanently (a Jew should prefer temporary).
  • Second: removing a female's reproductive organs, since for a Jew this is likely only rabbinic. Amirah LeAkum does not apply to rabbinic prohibitions outside Shabbat, which is unique, and Lifnei Iver applies only to Torah prohibitions. One may add the Taz, who sees no prohibition with females at all.
  • Third: neutering a male indirectly, such as radiation or chemotherapy, which is only rabbinic.
  • Forbidden: asking a non-Jew, or requiring even a non-Jewish student at a Jewish college, to remove a male's reproductive organs directly, which is Doraita for a Jew.

Sources quoted

  • Taz: no prohibition of castrating female animals, added as a further reason to be lenient.

In one line

A non-Jew may be asked to neuter females, neuter males indirectly or make semen non-viable, but not to remove a male's organs.
