Recap

  • The Rambam scatters principles of faith throughout his halachic works wherever he can. His four great introductions: the Mishnah (this series), Avot (done), Perek Helek, the Mishneh Torah.
  • "If you understand this introduction, your whole perspective on the Oral Law, the Talmud and the Mishnah changes."
  • Part 1: every mitzvah came with its explanation, taught so that everyone heard it four times, over forty years, with a final 37-day review. The tradition is intact.

So why so many disputes?

Talmudic disputes are a different thing from later ones (Rishonim, Acharonim, or today's question of electricity on Shabbat). The question here is: why can't you read a page of Mishnah or Gemara without he says this, he says that?

Why do we say Eizehu Mekoman every morning? Before Baruch She'amar we learn a little Torah (the Korbanot), a little Gemara (Rabbi Yishmael), and a little Mishnah. Of all the chapters on sacrifices, why this one? Because it's the only chapter in Shas with no dispute. No Rabbi Meir, no Rabbi Shimon; just straight halachah. Which shows how full of disputes everything else is.

The Rambam's answer (Part 1): nothing received from Sinai is disputed. Disputes arise only where something wasn't given to Moshe and had to be derived by analysis and comparison (iyun vehekesh).

Whenever you see a dispute anywhere in Shas, it's something that was not handed down from Moshe.

Part 2: A Prophet Has No Advantage in Interpreting Torah

ודע כי הנבואה אינה מועילה בעיון פירושה של תורה ובהוצאת ענפים בשלש עשרה מידות.

What Yehoshua and Pinchas could do, Ravina and Rav Ashi can do.

  • Yehoshua and Shmuel are exactly equal to Ravina and Rav Ashi, the close of the Gemara 1,500 years ago, in how they derive law.
  • Two prophets vs three Sages: follow the three, the majority.
  • "The whole premise of Torah is extrapolation, and that's done through intellect. Not prophecy, not something spiritual or secret. Using the wisdom Hashem gave us to bring out what we believe is the truth of Torah: that's what Torah is."

"These and these are the words of the living God"

The Maharal of Prague on אלו ואלו דברי אלהים חיים. People take it to mean "everyone's right." But some questions have one objective answer.

How many roofs did the Mishkan have? Tannaim disagree: three or four. "If I went back in time and looked, there was only one answer." Say it was three. The one who said four is still divrei Elokim chayim, because his method of deriving it is a true method of Torah study, even if the answer is objectively wrong.

The proof from Birkat HaTorah: we follow it with יברכך so the blessing falls immediately on Torah. But you could say the blessing and then learn opinions the halachah rejects, or ones objectively wrong, and you've still fulfilled the mitzvah, "because the method of extrapolation was the true one. That's what Torah is."

"So what is special about a prophet?"

חי נפשי: it is one of the great principles on which our faith rests.

But first the Rambam must explain the kinds of prophecy and how a prophet is verified, because many people err here, "even special people," meaning Rav Saadia Gaon, who held that a prophet is verified by miracles. The Rambam: wrong.

(From here the class switched to Rav Shilat's text, "basically the most precise one.")

1A and 1B: idolatry

והנביא ההוא או חלם החלום ההוא יומת (Re'eh). We ask him for no sign. Even if he performs miracles we've never heard of, he is put to death: כי מנסה ה׳ אלהיכם אתכם, Hashem is testing you.

עדות השכל המכחיש טענתו חזקה מעדות העין הרואה מופתו

The testimony of reason that refutes his claim is stronger than the testimony of the eye that saw his miracle, since it is plainly proven that only One may be served.

"This is why, when people tell you this rabbi did that, I saw him do this: miracles are lovely, sometimes needed, Eliyahu and Elisha did them. But they're no proof of who you are."

The Kotzker Rebbe on the Maariv line אותות ומופתים באדמת בני חם: "miracles and wonders" belong "in the land of the children of Ham". Plenty of people do miracles. It's irrelevant.

2A: changing the Torah "because Hashem told me"

Examples:

- Orlah: "Hashem told me that from now on, wait four years, not three," or two.

- Against tradition: "An eye for an eye has always meant money; Hashem has now told me it means literally an eye."

ויחס זה לנבואה, the key words: he attributes it to prophecy. He is a false prophet, put to death by chenek, because he ascribed to Hashem what He didn't say.

Why? Moshe already told us: לא בשמים היא, it is not in heaven; and בפיך ובלבבך לעשותו: by your mouth (the verses we know) and your heart (our intellect, what we derive). Nothing else.

The crucial distinction. A Sage sees people being careless with orlah, waiting only two and a half years, and says add a year as a safeguard. That's fine. That's what a Sage does, not a prophet: עשו סייג לתורה, ושמרתם את משמרתי. Avraham, the Gemara says, kept even the rabbinic laws. "Just make clear it's not from prophecy."

Poultry and milk: a prophet who says Hashem has now extended meat-and-milk to poultry is put to death. A rabbi who says Hashem forbids poultry and milk isn't claiming prophecy, but he still violates a prohibition (as the Rambam writes in the Mishneh Torah) by ascribing to Hashem what He didn't say. Once the Torah was given, it's ours to derive.

"Hashem gives prophets messages like should you go to war or not. He doesn't give messages on how to interpret Torah."

2B: the true prophet

He calls people to keep the Torah, promising reward and warning of punishment, like Yeshayahu, Yirmeyahu, Yechezkel, and gives specific instructions (fight this city on this day; don't go to war today).

  • Once verified, ignoring him is מיתה בידי שמים, death at the hands of Heaven (Shoftim).
  • But if he makes exactly these claims and fails verification, he's put to death, even though he only told people to do good. "Being a prophet is a dodgy business. More likely than not you'll be put to death."

How a Prophet Is Verified

וכי תאמר בלבבך איכה נדע את הדבר אשר לא דברו ה׳… אשר ידבר הנביא בשם ה׳ ולא יהיה (Shoftim)

If he predicts in Hashem's name and it doesn't happen, he's not a prophet.

Note: the verse says only that a failed prediction disqualifies him, not that a fulfilled one qualifies him. So one or two hits leave him in doubt, until he is right every time, again and again (no number given). As with Shmuel: וידע כל ישראל מדן ועד באר שבע כי נאמן שמואל לנביא לה׳.

Mark: isn't there a chazakah of three? No. "We don't find that. Three seems not to be enough."

Why that's possible: people asked prophets everything

In those days people went to the prophet for everything: Shaul went to Shmuel about lost donkeys. Why? Because the Torah forbids astrologers, diviners and fortune-tellers:

כי הגוים האלה אשר אתה יורש אותם אל מעננים ואל קסמים ישמעו, ואתה לא כן נתן לך ה׳ אלהיך. נביא מקרבך מאחיך כמני יקים לך ה׳ אלהיך, אליו תשמעון.

Yirmeyahu vs the false prophets: he prophesied disaster; they promised all would be well. "I'm safe," the Rambam has him say: if bad news doesn't come, Hashem relented. But if your good news fails, you're false.

Mark: so to be verified, you must be consistently right on positive predictions, since negatives can be "explained away"? Correct, or on negative ones that do happen. Unfulfilled bad predictions neither verify him nor diminish him.

Then why was Yaakov afraid of Esav?

Hashem had promised him: ואנכי עמך ושמרתיך בכל אשר תלך. The Sages: he feared his sins had cost him the promise. The Rambam: that can happen to an individual, for a personal promise. But a public prophecy, once spoken in Hashem's name, must come true.

The Prophet's One Real Advantage

Once verified, if he commands us to suspend any mitzvah, positive or negative, we must obey. Whoever disobeys is punished.

Doesn't this contradict 2A? The class answered: temporarily, and it must lapse afterwards.

בכל אם יאמר לך נביא עבור על דברי תורה שמע לו, חוץ מעבודה זרה.

Three conditions:

1

Temporary (hora'at sha'ah), never permanent

2

Never idolatry, not even "just for this hour"

3

He makes clear it's a one-off. Asked are you overturning Moshe?, he says no, only now

Examples

  • Eliyahu on Mount Carmel offered a sacrifice outside the Temple, forbidden on pain of karet (השמר לך פן תעלה עלתיך בכל מקום אשר תראה). Asked by a beit din may we do this in future?, he'd say no, it's karet; I'm doing this once, to refute the prophets of Baal.
  • Elisha in the war with Moav told them to cut down fruit trees, despite לא תשחית את עצה. Asked is this mitzvah annulled?: if he said forever, death; only now: obey him.

The Rambam's striking example: Shabbat

A verified prophet (say Eliyahu or Shmuel) stands in the square on Shabbat morning: everyone, men and women, light fires, take up arms, and go to war. Not a life-threatening emergency; just Hashem told me.

We must get up at once, without delay, with alacrity and diligence, transgressing Shabbat, lighting fires, waging war, and expect reward from Hashem for doing so. אליו תשמעון.

"I'm an old man." One who considers himself pious, an elder "with his shtreimel and bekishe on Shabbat," who has never broken Shabbat in his life, says: I don't dispute the prophet, but I won't go. Plenty of others can. He has transgressed Hashem's word and is liable to death at the hands of Heaven. The One who commanded Shabbat commanded us to obey the prophet: והיה האיש אשר לא ישמע אל דברי אשר ידבר בשמי, אנכי אדרש מעמו.

But if, while lighting fires and readying weapons, someone ties a knot or does the washing, anything the prophet didn't command, he is liable to stoning. The prophet's instruction covers only what he said.

And the next week, the same prophet says: Hashem told me the Shabbat boundary (techum) is now one amah less, 1,999 amot, attributing it to prophecy, not reinterpreting. He's a false prophet. We put him to death, "that same prophet we all broke Shabbat for last week."

ובזה לבדו נבדל הנביא משאר בני אדם בתורה

In this alone does a prophet differ from everyone else regarding Torah.

Even the greatest Sage or chief rabbi who says I feel Hashem wants us to break Shabbat today; I had a dream: forbidden, unless he's a verified prophet.

In Interpretation: Just Another Sage

But in explaining the laws and their fine points, he is like the other Sages. If he gives an interpretation and the Sages another, and he says Hashem told me mine is right: we don't listen to him.

Even a thousand prophets, all like Eliyahu and Elisha, against a thousand and one Sages: אחרי רבים להטות. The halachah follows the 1,001 Sages, not the 1,000 great prophets.

  • The Gemara: אילו אמרה לי יהושע בן נון בפומיה, לא צייתנא ליה: even if Yehoshua bin Nun said it himself, I wouldn't listen.
  • There is no adding to the Torah by way of prophecy. A prophet who claims my interpretation is right because I'm a prophet is another kind of false prophet, put to death: שאין תורה אחרת תבוא מאת ה׳.
  • For understanding, we're never sent to prophets. The verse doesn't say go to the prophet, but ובאת אל הכהנים הלוים ואל השופט אשר יהיה בימים ההם: go to the Sage of your day.

Summary

  1. Disputes arise only where nothing was handed down (e.g. the sukkah's minimum size). Eizehu Mekoman is the one dispute-free chapter.
  2. Prophecy gives no edge in interpretation. Yehoshua = Rav Ashi; follow the majority of Sages. אלו ואלו: a rejected view is still Torah, because its method is true.
  3. Four kinds of prophet: idol-prophets (1A, 1B), and the prophet who changes the Torah in Hashem's name (2A), are put to death, even with miracles. Reason outweighs the eye. A Sage may add a safeguard; a prophet may not attribute a change to prophecy.
  4. Verification: every prediction must come true, small and great. Fortune-tellers are "better liars"; one miss makes a false prophet. Bad news may be cancelled by teshuvah; good news never.
  5. The prophet's one advantage: a verified prophet may order a temporary, non-idolatrous, explicitly one-off suspension of a mitzvah (Eliyahu at Carmel, Elisha's trees, war on Shabbat), and we must obey. But one amah off the techum "from Hashem," and he's false.
  6. In interpreting Torah he's just another Sage: 1,001 Sages outvote 1,000 prophets.
