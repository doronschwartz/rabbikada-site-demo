Topic: The duty to give when asked, and the value of not taking sedakah unless needed

Sources: The Hakhamim; the Mishnah; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Tuesday 22 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Refusing a poor person who asks is a negative prohibition, on two conditions: that one has money on hand (otherwise one can only give verbal support), and that he is a genuine ani.
  • Coming days will cover who is entitled to receive and how to know that someone is genuinely poor.
  • The Hakhamim say repeatedly that one should do one's best not to depend on others: rather make one's Shabbat plain than take charity for a fine Shabbat.
  • The Shulhan Arukh says even an honourable hakham who became poor should take a job, even a lowly one, rather than ask for charity.
  • The Mishnah says one who takes sedakah without needing it will come to need it.
  • On the other hand, one who needs sedakah, such as the ill, the suffering or the elderly, and refuses out of pride, gets no reward for that independence. A balance is needed.
  • This concerns asking. A gift given unasked, out of honour or friendship, may be accepted.

In one line

One must give a genuine ani who asks, but should strive never to take sedakah unless truly in need, nor refuse it when one is.
