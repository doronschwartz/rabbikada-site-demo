Shiur: Part 5 of the series on the Rambam's Introduction to the Mishnah, given by Dayan Daniel Kada on Zoom, Tuesday 10 June 2025 (after Shavuot)

These notes are based on the Sofer.ai transcript of the recording.

Recap

  • The Oral Torah: Moshe received it, taught it at least four times, and reviewed it before he died. Each generation's Sages (not prophets: prophecy gives no interpretive authority) derived new laws, sometimes differently.
  • Rabbi Yehudah HaNasi, in the third century CE, about 1,700 years ago, gathered it all into the Mishnah, in five categories: from Moshe with a scriptural source; from Moshe without (halachah leMoshe miSinai); derived (disputable, more so over time); fences (poultry and milk, by majority); takkanot (e.g. prozbul, so people can lend).
  • The structure (two weeks ago; "to the best of my knowledge the Rambam is the only one who gives a reason for every tractate's place"): Zera'im with Berachot first; Mo'ed, "a meeting," for the festivals are meeting times with Hashem, and Shabbat too (מועדי); Nashim from Yevamot to Kiddushin; Nezikin, where Bava Kamma first fixes the hazards in society, Bava Metzia covers relationships the Torah addresses (guardians, disputed articles), Bava Batra those it largely doesn't (land). Then Sanhedrin, Makkot… up to Avot.

Part 1: Why Avot Is Here

We've just finished six weeks of Pirkei Avot, "but its messages are timeless." This is exactly the kind of opening where the Rambam loves to digress into ethics. Why put ethics in an order about torts and damages?

Reason 1: so we accept the Sanhedrin

להודיעך אמיתת הקבלה, שהיא אמת, שקבלוה רבים מרבים.

To show that the tradition is true, received by many from many.

So Avot opens with the chain: משה קבל תורה מסיני ומסרה ליהושע, ויהושע לזקנים, וזקנים לנביאים, ונביאים מסרוה לאנשי כנסת הגדולה, then the Sages by name, generation after generation. "Look in Tanach and you'll see the link." The Great Assembly included Daniel and Ezra; Shimon HaTzaddik was its last member.

Why here? The previous tractates are all the Sanhedrin's: it administers Bava Kamma, Metzia and Batra, lashes (Makkot), oaths (Shevuot), testimony (Eduyot), capital cases for idolatry. Where does it get that authority? Avot answers: an unbroken chain back to Moshe.

שהוא בדורו כמו הם בדורותיהם. Each generation's Sanhedrin is like the Sages of Moshe's time. יפתח בדורו כשמואל בדורו: the leaders of each generation are, for that generation, like Moshe and Shmuel in theirs. "Otherwise, where would you stop?"

ובזה יש מוסר לבני אדם, שלא יאמרו: משפט פלוני נקבל, ובתקנת פלוני נאמין? אין הדבר כן, כי המשפט אינו לפלוני השופט, אלא לה׳ אשר צונו בו.

Judgment doesn't belong to so-and-so the judge. It belongs to Hashem, who commanded it.

"You may think: I'd have accepted Moshe's Sanhedrin, Yehoshua's, maybe Shmuel's. But our rabbis are the same as us. No. They have an unbroken tradition."

Which authority, and when?

(The Rabbi gave a whole series on rabbinic authority today a few years ago.)

For the Rambam, this binding authority ended with the Talmud, about 250 years after the Mishnah, around 500 CE. It belongs to the Sanhedrin.

A thought experiment. "If we had a Sanhedrin today of the 71 gedolei hador (whether that's possible is another discussion) and it enacted something, it would bind us, and ignoring it would violate לא תסור. One I've always thought of: if you're keeping two days of Yom Tov in Israel, you should have to wear a tie. Everyone around you is working; it's so easy to jump on a bus, take out your phone. Make a takkanah!"

But if one Sage, or three, or seventy, or a letter signed by a hundred rabbis, says something: "It's important to listen and see if there's something we need to take on. But if other rabbis tell you otherwise and you don't, that's fine. That's not lo tasur." "That's the Rambam. Others disagreed."

Today's batei din have very limited halachic power: no fines and penalties (dinei kenasot); they can arbitrate, do conversions, divorces, marriages. Much of what the Sanhedrin did requires semichah, a direct chain of ordination.

Reason 2: so judges behave

לומר בזה המסכתא מוסרי כל חכם מחכמי האומה.

To record the ethics of every Sage of the nation, so we learn from them.

That's for everyone, but judges need it most:

עמי הארץ, אם לא יהיו בעלי מוסר, לא יזיקו לציבור, אלא יזיקו לעצמן בלבד. והשופט, אם לא יהיה בעל מוסר וחינוך, יאבד עצמו ויאבד בני אדם כולם.

An ordinary person without good character harms only himself. A judge without it destroys himself and everyone.

"How can you administer capital punishment or lashes, or judge between people, when you're not living up to the highest standards? Pirkei Avot gives the highest standards."

What a judge must have at his fingertips (all from Avot)

The balance

הוו מתונים בדין

Be deliberate. Don't rush; you can be tricked or miss things

…but no עינוי הדין

Don't delay a clear case. "You have the answer. Give it. Cut to the chase."

Witnesses

Be precise with them, and don't phrase questions so they sense the answer you want and lie to supply it

Litigants

Don't teach them what to say. ("The halachic status of lawyers: a talk for another time")

Dignity

No light-headedness with ordinary people, lest he be demeaned

…but not aloof

Don't hide from people. The Sanhedrin are עיני העדה, the eyes of the community: "not the leader in the ivory tower."

No luxury

Don't pursue leisure and pleasure, lest desire pull him from judging (Avot ch. 6)

Don't seek cases

אל תהי חושך עצמך מן הדין: someone always eager to be dayan invites suspicion of getting something on the side

Compromise

Always try for pesharah. Never ruling, always compromising: "that's the best." Only if that fails, be firm and to the point

Patience

Hear people out, even when long-winded, until they're clearly off-topic

The Sephardi way. "In the Ashkenazi world the rosh yeshivah is the highest level, far above a community rabbi. For us, all our greatest Sages were always community rabbis, because the greatest psak and wisdom come from living with the people and hearing them. But you can't be one of them either: you have to keep that authority."

"Like an expert doctor"

The Rambam, a physician, "always likes to compare things to medicine":

Doctor

Judge

If food and drink can heal, no medicine. ("Nowadays: any problem, take this pill. That's not a good doctor. A good doctor first tries changing your diet")

Compromise

If not, mild remedies close to food (sweet potions)

A ruling given with mercy and compassion

If that fails, the strongest medicines (scammony and other Arabic-named drugs; the class suggested "antibiotics")

Be firm and sharp

"I once saw someone I really respect, the kindest person, the best middot, behaving so sharp, so strong, so difficult, because the case had dragged on and nobody would compromise. However kind you are, if compromise doesn't work, you're strong."

Last: no pursuit of wealth and display: מלך במשפט יעמיד ארץ. The Gemara: if the dayan is like a king, who needs nothing from anyone, let him judge; if he needs from others, he shouldn't.

So Avot comes after Sanhedrin: it holds the ethics every judge needs.

And Horayot last

הוריות: mistaken rulings of the Sanhedrin or beit din. מי שיש לו טבע בשר ודם אי אפשר שלא ישגה: no human can avoid all error. Last, because hopefully we won't need it. Nezikin: eight tractates.

The shape: 1–4 concern intrinsically holy bodies; 5–6 financial holiness; 7–8 the sin and guilt offerings; 9–11 description and rare cases.

Pronunciation: "Many tractates' names are completely mispronounced in the yeshivah world. It's not Arochin; it's ערכין, Arachin. There's no tractate called Kreisus; it's כריתות, Keritot."

Keeping a list. On inadvertent karet sins, one posek wrote that since we can't bring a chatat today, you should write each one down, so that when the Temple is rebuilt you'll bring a chatat for each. "This was written. Literally."

The leper and the dead

What separates humans from animals is the power of speech, our consciousness expressed in words. One who destroys his faculty of speech (tzara'at comes from lashon hara) is, as a human, dead, though alive, breathing, eating and drinking. So the metzora sits alone outside the camp seven days, tears his clothes, lets his hair grow: he sits shivah for himself, mourning the identity he lost until he regains it.

The totals

61 tractates, 523 chapters. "Only the Rambam could do something like this, before you could put it into AI or Google." Why? "Before we go into the detail, I need to give you a framework of the whole Mishnah. And how beautiful it is."

Part 4: From Mishnah to Talmud

The Tosefta and other baraitot

Rebbi wrote as concisely and precisely as possible, for memory and transmission. But he wrote for people like himself, and some found it hard.

Work

By

Purpose

Tosefta

Rabbi Chiya, Rebbi's student (early 4th century), and similarly Rabbi Oshaya

Explain the Mishnah and add what can be derived from it "only with hard work"

Sifra and Sifrei

Rav (late 3rd / early 4th century)

Also explanatory, adding material

But none of these baraitot come close to the Mishnah in the precision of its words and its brevity. ראוה בנות ויאשרוה, מלכות ופילגשים ויהללוה: when compared, all praise the Mishnah. "The Mishnah is the head."

Ravina and Rav Ashi

Each generation tried to understand the Mishnah better, disagreed on its explanation, and added:

עד שהגיע הזמן לרבינא ורב אשי, והם סוף חכמי התלמוד.

Until the time of Ravina and Rav Ashi, the last Sages of the Talmud.

By then (around 500 CE, about 250 years after the Mishnah) there was so much explanation and disagreement that Rav Ashi decided to do for everyone after Rebbi what Rebbi had done for everyone from Moshe to himself:

וקיבץ כל מאמרי האומרים, והתבוננות המתבוננים, והשמועות השומעים… וחיבר התלמוד.

He gathered all the sayings, reflections and teachings, and composed the Talmud.

The four contents of the Talmud

"Whenever you learn Talmud, it has to be one of these four things. Pay attention to this."

#

Purpose

What it covers

1

Explanation (perush)

Explaining the Mishnah; the disputes over how to explain it; each side's proofs for why its reading is better. "Often pages of the Gemara"

2

Ruling (psak)

Deciding (a) disputes in the Mishnah; (b) disputes about how to explain it; (c) disputes over what to derive from it, just as the Sages derived from the Torah

3

New law

Everything derived from the Mishnah, and the gezerot and *takkanot added from 250 to 500 CE*, that weren't in Rebbi's day

4

Derashot

דרשות מתאימות לענין כל פרק ופרק: aggadah, "flowery" teachings that take a verse, turn it on its head, and draw out an idea or moral that isn't its plain meaning at all

Summary

  1. Avot sits in Nezikin for two reasons: (1) its unbroken chain from Sinai shows why we must accept each generation's Sanhedrin: judgment belongs to Hashem, not "so-and-so" (for the Rambam, that binding authority ended with the Talmud); (2) judges above all need its ethics: deliberate but prompt, dignified but approachable, unworldly, compromise first, "like an expert doctor." Horayot (judicial error) comes last.
  2. Kodashim: animal and flour offerings → ordinary meat → firstborns (the order of Re'eh) → valuations and exchanges → Keritot (karet ↔ chatat) → Me'ilah → descriptive Tamid and Middot → rare Kinnim. It's Arachin and Keritot.
  3. Taharot: define a utensil → the dead (most severe) → tzara'at, "sitting shivah for oneself" → purification → lesser impurities → mikveh → one-gender impurities → hands → stems.
  4. 61 tractates, 523 chapters.
  5. Tosefta, Sifra, Sifrei explain and add, but the Mishnah is the head. Rav Ashi and Ravina compiled the Talmud, whose four contents are explanation, ruling, new law and derashot.
