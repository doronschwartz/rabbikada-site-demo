Topic: The Rambam's view on believing someone who says they are Jewish

Sources: Sefer Misvot Gadol (the Semag); the Rambam, Mishneh Torah and a Teshuvah; the Maggid Mishneh; Tosafot; Rabbenu Tam

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 26 February 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Semag writes that people regularly came saying they were Jewish and their shehitah and wine were accepted.
  • All of this concerns an unknown person. One who is muhzak, long assumed to be non-Jewish, must bring proof everywhere, in Israel and outside. If, say, the Prime Minister suddenly said he was Jewish, he would need proof.
  • The Rambam agrees about one who is muhzak. He adds that one who says "I was not Jewish and converted in city X" is believed, because the mouth that forbade is the mouth that permitted: we only knew he was not Jewish from him.
  • But the Rambam limits this to Israel in times when most people there were Jewish; elsewhere, or in his day, he is not believed. This might suggest someone who simply says "I am Jewish" is also not believed.
  • The Maggid Mishneh gives two readings: no difference between the cases, or only the convert needs proof.
  • A recently found authentic Teshuvah of the Rambam settles it: a claimed convert needs proof, since we do not know if the conversion was done properly, but one who says "I am Jewish" is believed. The case was a woman raising ransom from captivity, believed despite the financial gain, and he extends it to marriage.
  • So the Rambam holds like Tosafot, Rabbenu Tam and the Rishonim that such a person is believed.

Sources quoted

  • The Semag: such claims were commonly accepted.
  • The Rambam, Mishneh Torah: the convert is believed only where most are Jewish.
  • The Maggid Mishneh: two ways to read the Rambam.
  • The Rambam's Teshuvah: one who says he is Jewish is believed, even for marriage.

In one line

According to his Teshuvah, the Rambam believes a person who says he is Jewish, but requires proof from one who claims to have converted.
