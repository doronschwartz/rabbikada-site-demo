Key Principle: Whatever is Kosher from the Torah, to Matir (untie) it is also from the Torah. The same applies to Rabbinic prohibitions and permissions.

Kosher in the Mishkan

Source 1: Repairing the Curtains (Yeriyot)

  • When the curtains/roofs of the Mishkan had tears, they would knot them up
  • These knots would remain permanently
  • This is the primary application of Kosher in the Mishkan

Source 2: Making Nets for the Chilazon Fish

  • The Techelet dye was needed for the bottom roof of the Mishkan (Pasuk: "Techelet ve'Argaman")
  • Nets were required to catch the Chilazon fish for the dye
  • Making nets required tying knots
  • Similarly, Matir (untying) was needed when adjusting nets

The Most Important Principle: Kesher Shel Kayama

Definition

A Kesher Shel Kayama means a permanent knot.

The Rule (According to All Opinions)

It is only forbidden from the Torah if one does a Kesher Shel Kayama.

Example: If one ties a knot intending to untie it in half an hour, they have not violated a Torah prohibition – regardless of the type of knot made.

Why is Kosher Unique?

Question: Why do we only find this principle (requiring permanence) specifically with Kosher?

Contrast with other Melachot:

  • If I build something properly (Binyan D'Oraita) but plan to demolish it half an hour later, it is still forbidden
  • Cooking is irreversible anyway
  • But other reversible Melachot don't have this "permanence" requirement

Answer: The nature of Kosher is different:

  • When tying, you are not creating something new
  • You are simply putting together two existing things (threads, ropes)
  • For this to have the status of a Melacha, it must be permanent
  • In contrast, building creates something new from disparate materials – the act itself is the prohibition, regardless of later demolition

Summary: The prohibition of Kosher is specifically that two things stay together. If your intention is for them not to stay together permanently, it's not forbidden from the Torah.

The Major Machloket: Understanding Kesher HaGamalim

The Three Categories in the Gemara

There are three Beraitot discussing knots:

  1. Chayav (Liable) – Forbidden from the Torah
  2. Patur (Exempt) – Forbidden from the Rabbis
  3. Mutar (Permitted) – Allowed to make

The Example from the Mishna: Kesher HaGamalim/Kesher HaSapanim

Kesher HaGamalim (Camel Drivers' Knot):

  • A ring is placed in the camel's nose
  • A permanent knot is tied to that ring
  • Each time you want to move the camel, you tie something else (temporary) to it
  • The first initial knot is a professional, craftsman knot requiring expertise
  • This knot stays permanently
  • The Mishna says this is forbidden from the Torah

Kesher HaSapanim (Sailors' Knot):

  • Similarly, a professional permanent knot tied to secure other things

The Two Major Opinions

Opinion 1: Rif and Rambam

What makes Kesher HaGamalim forbidden from the Torah?

It requires TWO properties:

  1. Kesher Shel Kayama – Permanent
  2. Kesher Uman – Professional/Craftsman knot

It is solely due to it being a Kesher Shel Kayama (permanent).

The Halachic Rulings

Shulchan Aruch

Follows the Rif and Rambam straight out.

Rama

Does not simply follow Rashi. Rather, he takes on the stringencies of both opinions.

Result according to Rama:

  • Professional knot for half an hour: Assur (because of Rif concern)
  • Simple knot for extended time: Assur (because of Rashi concern)

The Shulchan Aruch Text (Siman 317)

D'Oraita Level

הקושר קשר של קיימא ומעשה אומן חייב

"One who ties a permanent knot AND it's a professional knot – is liable."

Examples given:

  • Kesher HaGamalim (camel drivers)
  • Kesher HaSapanim (sailors)
  • Knots that cobblers make inside shoes when manufacturing them

D'Rabbanan Level

אבל הקושר קשר של קיימא ואינו מעשה אומן פטור

"But if one ties a permanent knot that is NOT professional – exempt [but still forbidden rabbinically]."

Examples:

  • A broken strap that you knot
  • A broken rope that you knot together
  • Tying a rope to a bucket (for drawing water from a well)
  • Tying reins to an animal

Key phrase:

וכל אדם קושר אותם לקיימם

"And every person ties them for permanence" – indicating the norm is what matters.

Mutar Level

וקשר שאינו של קיימה ואינו מעשה אומן מותר לקושרו לכתחילה

"A knot that is NOT permanent AND is NOT professional – is permitted to tie initially."

Also:

וכל קשר שאינו של קיימה, קשרו קשר אומן הרי זה אסור

"Any non-permanent knot that IS professional – is forbidden [rabbinically]."

Special Leniency for Mitzvah Purposes

ולצורך מצוה כגון שקושר למדוד אחד משיעורי התורה מותר לקשור קשר שאינו של קיימה

For a Mitzvah purpose (like measuring a Mikveh), one may tie a professional knot that is not permanent.

Source: Gemara Shabbat (end of Daf 157):

דקושרין ומודדין ופוקקין לצורך מצוה

Question: Why doesn't Rambam mention the reverse – tying a permanent simple knot for a Mitzvah?

Pri Megadim's Answer: Because there's no need! Just intend to untie it after Shabbat, and it becomes non-permanent.

The Question of Intent vs. Norm

The Issue

What if I tie a knot intending to untie it after Shabbat, but it's a type of knot that normal people tie permanently?

Does my personal intention matter, or does the norm determine the status?

Taz's View

It goes according to your da'at (intention). Your genuine intention at the time of tying determines the status.

Be'ur Halacha's View (and Simple Understanding of Rambam)

The norm determines whether it's a Kesher Shel Kayama, not personal intention.

Proof from Rambam:

וכל אדם קושר אותם לקיימא

This language indicates that the norm for such knots is what matters.

Practical Implications

The Eruv Example:

  • If the eruv falls on Shabbat, can you tie it back?
  • Normally, eruv knots are intended to be permanent
  • Even if you intend to re-tie after Shabbat, is that sufficient?

The Tzitzit Example:

  • If all knots on your tzitzit unravel on Shabbat
  • You're wearing a four-cornered garment without tzitzit
  • Can you tie a simple knot intending to re-tie properly after Shabbat?

According to Taz: Permitted According to Be'ur Halacha: Questionable, as the norm for such knots is permanence

Time Frames According to Rif and Rambam

The Question

If we follow Rif/Rambam (as Sephardim do), and the knot is simple (not professional) and not permanent – why should there be any time limit?

Pri Megadim: According to Rif and Rambam, even six months should be permitted if:

  • It's a simple knot
  • You intend to untie it (not permanent)

The Acharonim's Position

Knesset HaGedolah (Benvenisti): Even according to Rif/Rambam, can't exceed 7 days because that could be considered "kayama."

From Yerushalmi: 50 days might be considered permanent.

Chacham Ovadia's Ruling

Despite the logic allowing longer, Chacham Ovadia rules: Up to 7 days for simple knots.

Reasoning: Sfek Sfeka (double doubt):

  1. Doubt: Halacha like Rif or Rashi?
  2. Doubt: Even according to Rashi – 24 hours or 7 days?

Result: Can be lenient for 7 days.

The Double Knot Question

The Rama's Ruling

דיש ליזהר שלא להתיר שום קשר שהוא שני קשרים זה על גבי זה

"One should be careful not to tie (or untie) any knot that is two knots one on top of the other."

Reason:

ואין אנו בקיאים איזהו מיקרי קשר של אומן

"We don't know what constitutes a professional knot" – perhaps a double knot qualifies.

Why This is Strict

According to Rashi/Rosh: Double knot for less than 24 hours should be fine (not permanent).

But the Rama follows both opinions. According to Rif/Rambam, a professional knot even for half an hour is D'Rabbanan.

Since we're unsure if a double knot is "professional," the Rama forbids it even for short periods.

Ashkenazi practice: No double knots on Shabbat, even for half an hour.

The Counter-Argument: Can't We Say Safek D'Rabbanan L'Kula?

Answer: No, because this is a safek b'yedi'ah (doubt due to lack of information about reality), not a doubt in halacha.

However, there are multiple doubts:

  1. Safek: Halacha like Rashi/Rosh or Rif/Rambam?
  2. Safek (even according to Rif/Rambam): Is a double knot professional?

The Chida's Position on Double Knots

The Famous Chida (Berkei Yosef)

בגלילותינו פשט המנהג לקשור קשרים בחגורותיו שבמתניו

"In our areas, the custom spread to tie two knots on one's belt."

שאינו של קיימא והוא של הדיוט

"It's not permanent AND it's a simple knot."

ולית דחש לה אלא מיעוטא דמיעוטא דבטלו במיעוטם

"No one is concerned about this except a minority of a minority, and they are insignificant."

Interpretation

Chida's view: A double knot is not considered a Kesher Uman (professional knot).

Ben Ish Chai's interpretation: The Chida was just recording local practice, not necessarily permitting it.

Chacham Ovadia's ruling: Double knot is definitively not a Kesher Uman. Everyone can tie a double knot – no expertise needed.

Chacham Ben Tzion Abba Shaul's View

Permits based on Sfek Sfeka:

  1. Safek: Rashi/Rosh vs. Rif/Rambam
  2. Safek: Is double knot professional?

Permits: Even a woman's snood tied with double knot for up to 7 days.

Special Case: Spanish & Portuguese Sifrei Torah

The Practice

  • S&P (and some Moroccan) communities use Sifrei Torah like Ashkenazi style (not in a case)
  • They use a beautiful silk cloth around the Sefer Torah
  • The cloth is tied with a double knot
  • Only trained members perform this Mitzvah

The Question

  • Shabbat: Tied, then untied next Shabbat (7 days) – permitted?
  • Yom Tov: Tied on Rosh Hashanah, not untied until Pesach or next year – problem?

Analysis

If we follow that double knot is a simple knot:

  • Up to 7 days should be permitted
  • Longer periods would be problematic
