Topic: The obligation to give when a poor person asks

Sources: The Torah; Shulhan Arukh; the Rambam; Rav Kanievsky; the Gemara

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Tuesday 1 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Beyond the small yearly amount every man and woman must give unasked, the more practical obligation is the prohibition on refusing one who asks: "do not harden your heart and do not close your hand".
  • The Shulhan Arukh codifies that one asked by a genuinely poor person must give something, however small. Not every request is from a genuine ani, which will be discussed. One who ignores him violates a Torah prohibition, and the Shulhan Arukh uses harsh words, calling him a "beliyaal".
  • The poskim write this applies even after giving a tenth of one's income. Once one has given a fifth, it is debatable.
  • The Rambam speaks of seeing a poor person asking and ignoring him. From this Rav Kanievsky explains the prohibition applies only to an explicit request, the ani standing before you; a letter or text message is not a formal request.
  • If one has no money (today we often carry only cards), the Shulhan Arukh says to explain kindly that you would like to give but cannot, and not upset him.
  • The Gemara says giving brings six blessings, and speaking to the heart of the ani and raising his spirits brings eleven more, even without giving.

In one line

When a genuine poor person asks you in person, you must give something however small, and if you have nothing, explain kindly and lift his spirits.
