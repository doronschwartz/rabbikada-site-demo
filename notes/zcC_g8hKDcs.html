The halacha

  • The mishteh ("yemei mishteh vesimhah") is a misvah from the Neviim, equal to the other misvot of Purim. Since the verse says days, the Gemara, Rambam and Shulhan Arukh rule that a seudah held only on the night of Purim does not fulfil the misvah.
  • Some Rishonim, such as the Avi HaEzri, compare it to the Megillah, whose main misvah is by day but which the Hakhamim added at night, and hold there is a lesser misvah of a seudah at night too. This is not in the Rambam or Shulhan Arukh, but the Rama brings it.
  • "Yemei", in the plural, simply reflects the 14th outside walled cities and the 15th in Yerushalayim. But some Rishonim saw in it a misvah for each place to mark the other day with a lesser meal and festivity, as the Rama codifies. It is not strictly required, but a nice minhag showing unity with the rest of Am Yisrael.
  • The Rambam writes the mishteh should in the first instance be a full festive meal with meat, not just a little food.
  • In the first instance it is with bread. This is not explicit in the Shulhan Arukh, but most poskim understand a seudah comes with bread.
  • The Magen Avraham, about 400 years ago, held bread is unnecessary: bread on Shabbat and Yom Tov stems from kavod, while Purim has a din of simhah, not kavod. So those who struggle with bread have what to rely on.
  • This year Yerushalayim holds its seudah on Sunday, so outside Yerushalayim there is a misvah to mark Sunday with some festivity. In the first instance the meal is with meat and food that brings simhah, or whatever brings a person simhah. Timing comes tomorrow.

Sources quoted

  • The Rama: a seudah at night as well, and marking the other day of Purim.
  • The Rambam: the mishteh in the first instance with meat.
  • The Magen Avraham: no need for bread on Purim, since its law is simhah, not kavod.

In one line

Hold the Purim seudah by day with meat and, in the first instance, bread, while marking the night and the other day of Purim in a lesser way.
