Topic: Why the siege, a seemingly minor event, is a fast: tracing tragedies to their roots

Sources: The Chief Rabbinate of Israel's Yom HaKaddish HaKelali (1949)

Daily Halacha: Asarah BeTevet

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The fast mainly marks Nevukhadnessar's siege of Yerushalayim. Compared with Tisha BeAv (the Temple in flames) or the 17th of Tammuz (the walls breached), a siege during which life went on seems minor.
  • In 1949 the Chief Rabbinate of Israel set 10 Tevet as Yom HaKaddish HaKelali, a day to light a candle and say Kaddish for those killed in the Holocaust whose date of death is unknown. Yom HaShoah was later fixed by the Knesset on 27 Nisan, the date of the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising, but some still remember the Holocaust on 10 Tevet.
  • The link: after a tragedy we must trace its sources and the stages that led to it. The seeds of Tisha BeAv were planted on 10 Tevet. History teaches the same: the First World War began with one assassination.
  • The same applies to the Holocaust, and to the 7th of October: it is not enough to remember what happened; we must also understand the roots that led to it.
  • The root always contains everything in it, which is why we mourn the beginning, not only the end.

In one line

Asarah BeTevet teaches us to look for the roots of dark times, not only at the final catastrophe.
