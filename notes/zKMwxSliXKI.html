Topic: The berakhah "Meshanneh HaBeriyot" on seeing an elephant or a monkey

Sources: The Gemara; Rashi; the Meiri; the Raavad; the Tur; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; the Vilna Gaon; the Hida's travel diary; Rav Kanievsky

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Wednesday 22 January 2025

The halacha

  • The Gemara says one who sees a pil (elephant), kof (monkey) or kifof blesses "Barukh... Meshanneh HaBeriyot", who diversifies His creatures. The Rishonim translate kifof as a bat, vulture or owl, so being unsure, we do not say it on such birds, and the Shulhan Arukh records only the elephant and monkey.
  • The Meiri, 13th century, explains these creatures resemble humans in some way; clear for a monkey, less so for an elephant. Rashi seems to agree, describing the kifof's cheeks as human-like. Some midrashim link these creatures to the generation of the flood.
  • Some poskim say any astonishing creature takes this berakhah, but since others limit it, as the Meiri does, the halacha is that it applies only to elephants and monkeys.
  • The Raavad says it once only, the first time, when the astonishment is greatest; the Tur and other Rishonim say every 30 days. The Shulhan Arukh follows the Raavad, so Sepharadim say it once in a lifetime, not on every zoo visit.
  • The Rema, the Vilna Gaon and others say every 30 days, so Ashkenazim have what to rely on, though the final ruling is not entirely clear.
  • He believes one who has seen these animals before but never said the berakhah may say it once the next time.
  • The Hida wrote in his travel diary of visiting a zoo in London and seeing a 100-year-old eagle. Which zoo is unclear, as London Zoo opened only in 1828. The practice is to go to a zoo to say this berakhah; Rav Kanievsky took his children to say it on elephants and monkeys.

In one line

On an elephant or a monkey say Meshanneh HaBeriyot, for Sepharadim once in a lifetime, and not on other creatures however astonishing.
