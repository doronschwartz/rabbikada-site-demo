Topic: Why one waits 24 hours after a utensil's last use before Hagalah

Sources: Rashi; Tosafot and the Rosh; Shulhan Arukh and the poskim

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Monday 31 March 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Before turning to practical appliances: it is good practice not to use a utensil for at least 24 hours before kashering it.
  • The Rishonim ask: if boiling heat expels the forbidden taste, why does it not reabsorb it into the utensil?
  • Rashi answers that while the taste is being expelled it cannot be reabsorbed, so one may kasher at any time.
  • Tosafot and the Rosh answer that there is normally 60 times as much water as absorbed taste, so it is nullified at once. Since this is not always so, the Shulhan Arukh and poskim write that the best practice is to leave the utensil unused for 24 hours.
  • After 24 hours the taste is Notein Taam LiFgam, not a beneficial taste, so even if it were reabsorbed without 60 times the water it would not forbid anything. Waiting covers all opinions.
  • Public kashering cannot rely on people waiting, so soap or detergent is added, which makes the expelled taste bad at once.
  • Since we usually clean utensils with soap anyway, one who kashered without waiting 24 hours is fine provided soap was used. In practice the recommendation remains to wait 24 hours.

Sources quoted

  • Rashi: the taste being expelled is not reabsorbed.
  • Tosafot and the Rosh: reabsorption is no concern because of 60 times the water.
  • Shulhan Arukh and poskim: best practice is to wait 24 hours.

In one line

Wait 24 hours before kashering so any reabsorbed taste is spoiled, though if soap was used kashering without waiting is still fine.
