The halacha

  • The Torah says the Korban Pesah is eaten "al massot umerorim", so the Gemara explains maror depends on the Korban Pesah. Today it is rabbinic, instituted as a remembrance of the Beit HaMikdash.
  • Massah remains a Torah misvah because of a separate verse, "baerev tokhelu massot".
  • The Hakhamim instituted the berakhah "al akhilat maror" on this rabbinic misvah.
  • The berakhot on massah and maror also cover the massah and maror of Korekh. So the Shulhan Arukh rules that from the berakhah at Mosi Massah until after Korekh one may not speak of anything unrelated. Asking for haroset, massah or more lettuce is fine.
  • The maror is dipped in haroset, a remembrance of the mortar. The Rambam lists wine, figs and other ingredients; the Rema, for Ashkenazim, apples and cinnamon. What matters is a mortar-like texture.
  • The Shulhan Arukh says to submerge it fully in the haroset, as if submerging into servitude, then shake it off so as not to lose the bitter taste. Tasty Sepharadi haroset is for other times, not now.
  • Since maror recalls "they embittered our lives", it is eaten without reclining. One eats a kazayit, about one lettuce leaf.

In one line

Maror is a rabbinic remembrance eaten without reclining: dip a kazayit in haroset, shake it off, and keep silent on other matters until after Korekh.
