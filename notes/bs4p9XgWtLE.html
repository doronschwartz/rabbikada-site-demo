Topic: The duty to study the laws of Pesah thirty days before, and the names of the festival

Sources: The Gemara; the Ran; the Rashba; Shulhan Arukh; Parashat Ki Tisa; the Rambam, Hilchot Hames UMassah

Daily Halacha: Pesah

The halacha

  • The Gemara, in two places, says that 30 days before Pesah we must discuss and expound its laws. The source: Moshe taught the laws of Pesah Sheni, 30 days after Pesah, already on Pesah itself. A second opinion says only from Rosh Hodesh Nisan. The Shulhan Arukh follows the first.
  • The Ran and the Rashba held this duty falls only on the rabbis who instruct the public. Others hold, and this is the halacha, that it falls on every person.
  • The enactment concerned the Korbanot of Pesah, but since the laws of Pesah are so vast the duty still applies, and the coming weeks will go through them systematically.
  • The Torah calls the festival Hag HaMassot, after the Massot we left Egypt with, and Hag HaPesah (Parashat Ki Tisa), as Hashem passed over the Jewish homes.
  • "Hag HaAviv" is not a name in the Torah, but Pesah must fall in spring, which is why a leap year adds a second Adar.
  • The Tefillah calls it Zeman Herutenu. Israel overthrew its masters without enslaving them: freedom as a moral value for the first time. Perhaps this is why Jewish kings counted their reign from Nisan, as a reminder not to enslave their subjects.
  • The Shulhan Arukh calls these Hilchot Pesah, but the Rambam calls them Hilchot Hames UMassah, seeing these as the halachic core of Pesah.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: study the laws of Pesah 30 days before, learned from Moshe and Pesah Sheni.
  • The Ran and the Rashba: the duty is only for those who instruct.
  • The Rambam: names the laws Hilchot Hames UMassah.

In one line

From thirty days before Pesah everyone should study its laws, for a festival of Massot, of Hashem passing over, and of freedom.
