Topic: Meat at the Purim seudah, and what the obligation to drink "ad delo yada" means

Sources: The Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Gemara; the Rishonim; the Meiri; the Rama

Daily Halacha: Purim, Monday 14 March 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The seudah, one of the four misvot of Purim, includes washing and bread. The Rambam rules, and the Shulhan Arukh implies, that it should include meat, since "ein simhah ela bebasar".
  • The Gemara says one is obligated "livsumei", roughly to become intoxicated, until one cannot distinguish between "arur Haman" and "barukh Mordekhai". The reason is that the miracle came about through feasts of wine (mishteh hayayin), as the Megillah keeps mentioning.
  • Some Rishonim hold this is not the halacha, because of a difficult story in the same passage of a rabbi who became drunk and, read literally, killed his friend and then revived him. But the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh rule it as halacha, and we accept the obligation.
  • The Rambam writes one drinks until one falls asleep in one's intoxication: asleep, one cannot distinguish between cursed and blessed.
  • The Meiri, of the Rambam's school, says the Hakhamim cannot have meant behaviour unbefitting a person: one should not get drunk and display it, but drink a little and fall asleep. The Rama rules this way for Ashkenazim, disagreeing with the Shulhan Arukh, who quotes the Gemara verbatim and whom the Rama understood literally.
  • The Gemara's word "bePuraya" may allude to "puraya", bed, as in the things one may lie about: one cannot distinguish while asleep in bed.
  • The drinking is only within the seudah. One drinks a bit more than usual, says Birkat HaMazon and goes to sleep. Getting drunk to the point of losing control is asur, wrong and dangerous; one must keep one's dignity.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: "Hayyav inish livsumei bePuraya ad delo yada".
  • The Rambam: drink until one falls asleep.
  • The Meiri and the Rama: drink a little more than usual and sleep, without unseemly drunkenness.

In one line

At the Purim seudah drink a little more than usual and then sleep, never becoming drunk to the point of losing control.
