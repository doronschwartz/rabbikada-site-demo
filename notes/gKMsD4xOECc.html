The halacha

  • Every berakhah falls into one of three categories, and the category has halachic ramifications.
  • Birkot hanehenin: on benefit from this world, including food, fragrance and seeing something beautiful.
  • Birkot hamisvot: said before a misvah, usually containing "asher kiddeshanu bemisvotav vesivanu", such as the berakhah on reading Hallel.
  • Blessings of thanks, praise and request, probably the largest group: the nineteen berakhot of the Amidah, and also those on seeing the sea or hearing thunder and seeing lightning.
  • All berakhot are rabbinic apart from Birkat HaMazon, but ancient: Ezra instituted their text some 2,400 years ago. The Gemara says one who changes the text the Hakhamim set errs, so we may not modify or modernise it.
  • The Rambam writes clearly that a precise translation of the berakhah into the vernacular fulfils the obligation. The Shulhan Arukh writes that Hebrew is better in the first instance, so we encourage Hebrew.
  • Advice for those still learning Hebrew: say berakhot in English to get used to thanking and praising Hashem, and move to Hebrew over time.

In one line

Berakhot are of benefit, of misvah, or of praise and request, their ancient text is fixed, and a precise translation fulfils the duty though Hebrew is preferred.
