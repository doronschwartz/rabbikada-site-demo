Topic: Sedakah pledges as vows, paying promptly, and saying "bli neder"

Sources: Rambam; Shulhan Arukh (laws of vows, of sedakah and of taking on a fast); the Torah (bal teaher; "kol nediv lev"); the Rema

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Tuesday 1 March 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Rambam and Shulhan Arukh say sedakah falls under nedarim, which the Torah treats very severely.
  • One who vows a korban must bring it as soon as possible, and at the latest before three festivals pass; delaying violates bal teaher. Whether the festivals must come in a set order is discussed.
  • A sedakah pledge is the same: give as soon as possible, and at the latest before three festivals pass.
  • So the Shulhan Arukh writes, in the laws of vows and of sedakah, not to vow even for charity unless one has the money to give immediately. If one must pledge, say "bli neder".
  • When a Mi Sheberakh is made for a donor, it is good to say he gives "bli neder", so that if he forgets he does not transgress.
  • From "kol nediv lev" in last week's parashah, some argue that with sedakah even a firm decision in one's heart, without thinking "bli neder", is a vow.
  • The Shulhan Arukh brings two opinions, and normally the halacha follows the second (that it is not a vow); but in the laws of taking on a fast he seems to indicate it binds. The Rema says to be stringent and treat such a decision as a pledge.

In one line

Pay sedakah pledges promptly, say "bli neder" when pledging, and know that according to some even a firm decision in the heart must be kept.
