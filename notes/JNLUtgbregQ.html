Topic: The Rambam's principles for teaching children: when to start, schools and class size

The halacha

  • A father's duty to teach his son Torah starts as soon as the child can speak, at two or three. He first teaches "Torah sivah lanu Moshe morashah kehillat Yaakov" and "Shema Yisrael", then more basic, important Pesukim.
  • Proper learning begins at six or seven, depending on the child. Things differ today, but these are the Rambam's principles.
  • From then he is taught as much as possible by day and a little at night, to instil the duty to learn day and night.
  • Every town must have a school for children. If it does not, it must arrange one, and otherwise we no longer have a relationship with it, since the world depends on the Torah of young children.
  • Based on the Gemara, a class should have no more than 25 children per teacher; beyond that an assistant or another teacher is needed. Remarkably, this is still roughly the class size today.

Sources quoted

  • Rambam, based on the Gemara: the first Pesukim to teach, starting age, a school in every town and 25 per teacher.

In one line

Teach children Pesukim from when they speak, properly from six or seven, in a school in every town with no more than 25 per teacher.
