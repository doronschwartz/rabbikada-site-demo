Topic: What "Barukh Atah" means and why a berakhah moves from second to third person

Sources: The Gemara; the Rashba; the Maharal of Prague; a Hasidic master

Daily Halacha: Berachot, Wednesday 10 January 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • One who takes from the world without a berakhah diminishes it, as if stealing from HaKadosh Barukh Hu; one who uses nature to recognise Him brings down more shefa, replenishing what he took. The Gemara says one who wants to be pious should be particular about berakhot.
  • "Barukh Atah Hashem" is usually translated "You are blessed, God", as if we praise Him, but God has no needs. The Rashba and others explain it as recognising Him as the source of all blessing, so the berakhah testifies to man's dependence on God.
  • The root also means increase and flow, as "berekhah" is a pool or spring: everything flows from Him.
  • The Maharal of Prague notes that bet (2), kaf (20) and resh (200) each begin the next unit after alef, yud and kuf, the single units, so they symbolise multiplicity and addition. Recognising God as the source makes His presence manifest and brings more light and blessing.
  • A berakhah opens in the second person ("Blessed are You") and ends in the third (as in "shehakol nihyeh bidvaro"). One Hasidic master explains that the closer one comes to God, the more one realises He cannot be grasped; sometimes the highest understanding is that one cannot understand.

In one line

A berakhah is not our praise of God but our recognition that all blessing flows from Him.
