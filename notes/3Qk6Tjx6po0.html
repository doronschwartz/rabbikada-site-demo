Topic: When Shehiyanu is said on a new house, furniture, clothes and shoes

Sources: The Mishnah; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Rama

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Tuesday 25 February 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Mishnah, the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh list three things for Shehiyanu: a new house one will live in, new furniture or utensils, and new garments.
  • A new house and beautiful new furniture are by default occasions of joy, so one says Shehiyanu.
  • Clothes are more mundane, so two conditions apply: it must be something people ordinarily rejoice over, and the person must himself be happy. A nice shirt or tie may qualify for one person and not another; socks do not, even if he is pleased. A new suit is usually joyous, though for a wealthy person who buys suits often it rarely is.
  • Shoes: the common belief that Shehiyanu is not said because leather comes from a slain animal is not correct. The poskim explain that in the past a shoe was simply functional and gave no joy. Some poskim write that today, for beautiful shoes that make one very happy, one does say it, subjectively.
  • The animal reason appears in the Rama about the wish people give one another (wear it and get new ones), not the berakhah, and the Rama calls it a weak reason.
  • Clothes are personal, so almost always Shehiyanu; a house or furniture shared with others takes HaTov VeHaMetiv.

Sources quoted

  • The Rama: some avoid the wish to wear out the garment and get a new one because of killing another animal, which he calls a weak reason.

In one line

Shehiyanu on clothes depends on real, ordinary joy; shoes were excluded for lack of joy, not because of the leather.
