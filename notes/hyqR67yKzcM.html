Topic: Why the Torah gives no date for Matan Torah, and Shavuot as a day of preparation

Sources: The Torah; the Ramhal, Daat Tevunot; the Azharot; Rambam, Sefer HaMisvot

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Wednesday 20 May 2026

The points

  • Shavuot has five names: in the Torah Yom HaBikkurim, Hag HaKasir and Hag HaShavuot; from the Hakhamim Aseret and Zeman Matan Toratenu. Without the Temple and the agricultural aspects, the giving of the Torah has become its main theme.
  • Why does the Torah give no date for something so monumental?
  • The Torah was not all given on Shavuot. The Ten Commandments with their commentary were given on 6 Sivan. Moshe then went up for 40 days to learn the Written and Oral Torah; after the Golden Calf he went up twice more, coming down on Yom Kippur, 120 days later.
  • He then taught the rest of the Torah over the following months while the people stayed at Har Sinai, almost a year. So "given at Sinai" means the Ten Commandments were given there and all of Moshe's learning took place there.
  • The Ramhal writes in Daat Tevunot that the Torah was not entirely given there: it was a general preparation to receive all the Misvot and the potential to keep them.
  • A fixed date would invite us to celebrate and study on that day and then set the Torah aside for a year. Shavuot is a preparation to engage with the Torah all year.
  • Hence the custom of reading the Azharot or the Rambam's Sefer HaMisvot: not to understand every Misvah that night, but to open the potential to study them in the coming year.

In one line

The Torah gives no date because Shavuot is not a commemoration but a preparation to engage with the Torah all year.
