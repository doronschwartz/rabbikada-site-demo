Topic: When carrying is permitted on Yom Tov, carrying for a non-Jew, and the role of an eruv

Sources: The Torah; the poskim; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Thursday 26 June 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Carrying, hosaah, is the 39th melakhah forbidden on Shabbat. On Yom Tov the Torah allows carrying food, as okhel nefesh, even without an eruv, so one can take food to friends.
  • By mitokh shehutrah, carrying is permitted even for non-food needs, just as one may boil water to wash one's hands. So one may push a buggy or carry glasses in one's pocket.
  • Spiritual needs count too: a lulav, a shofar, and, the poskim write, even a shofar carried to blow for women on Rosh Hashanah. Women are not obligated, but it is a very old minhag and the right thing to do, so there is a need.
  • Without any need it is forbidden. One may not skip checking one's pockets on Yom Tov and walk around with something one has no use for; according to some Rishonim that could even be a Torah prohibition.
  • One may carry for another Jew, such as a friend's tallit, but not for a non-Jew, since the Torah permits it only for Jews. So if someone still in the conversion process asks you to hold his bag while he ties his shoelaces, you must be careful: it is forbidden according to all opinions.
  • The exception is a place with a reliable eruv. There one may carry on Yom Tov as on Shabbat, even without a particular purpose and even for a non-Jew, though never muksah, and one need not check one's pockets if sure there is no muksah in them.

In one line

On Yom Tov carry for any Jewish need, however small, but not without purpose or for a non-Jew, unless there is a reliable eruv.
