Topic: The origins of Simhat Torah and of hakafot

Sources: The Tefillah of the day; the Gemara; Rav Shimshon Pincus; the Beit Yoseph (Rav Yoseph Karo); Shulhan Arukh; the Rama; the Arizal

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Friday 6 October 2023

The halacha

  • The name Simhat Torah is not found before the 11th century.
  • In the Tefillah we never say "Simhat Torah" but "Shemini Hag HaAseret", on both days abroad and in Israel too.
  • The Gemara records differing customs: some finished the Torah yearly, while in Israel it took three years. Even in 11th or 12th century Egypt there were two communities, one of each custom.
  • The essence of the day is Shemini Aseret: HaShem asks us to stay one more day. It has no misvah of its own; Rav Shimshon Pincus compares it to yihud, intimacy after the sukkah as a kind of huppah with HaShem.
  • Celebrating the completion of the Torah was added later, since our closest bond with HaShem is through Torah. But we should not forget the day is Shemini Aseret.
  • Hakafot are not in the Rishonim, the Beit Yoseph or the Shulhan Arukh. They appear in the Rama as Ashkenazi custom; the Arizal endorsed them, so Sepharadim accepted them.
  • So the original Spanish and Portuguese custom was no hakafot, since it is not a Sepharadi custom and they do not adopt kabbalistic customs.
  • The essence is rejoicing in our relationship with Torah and with HaShem, completing and restarting the Torah with renewed love for it.

In one line

Simhat Torah is really Shemini Aseret, a day alone with HaShem that we celebrate through the Torah; hakafot are a later custom.
