Topic: Introduction to contraception: what is permitted and the three views on spacing births

Sources: The Gemara; contemporary poskim

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 5 February 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Contraception runs against "Peru urevu" and Shevet, so its limits must be understood.
  • Only methods in which the natural act takes place normally, with contraception acting afterwards, are under discussion. A method that prevents the seed from entering is forbidden in all cases, because of wasting seed.
  • Everyone agrees that with serious physical or mental illness, contraception is permitted for as long as needed, since danger to life overrides.
  • Spacing births for a healthy couple was hardly discussed by the Rishonim and only in the last century. In the past women rarely conceived within two years (the Gemara notes breastfeeding) and many children died. Today a woman can conceive year after year.
  • Three contemporary views: no break at all unless there is illness; up to two years after a birth, since the Gemara says a woman needs two years to recover; or one year as a middle view. Beyond two years, no poskim allow it without a substantial reason.
  • Each case depends on the couple and their circumstances.

In one line

Only contraception acting after a natural act is ever discussed; illness permits it without limit, and for healthy couples the poskim allow a gap of none, one or up to two years between births.
