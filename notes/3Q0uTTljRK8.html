Topic: The source of the obligation to marry, and why it rests on men

Sources: Bereshit ("Peru urevu"); the Mishnah and Gemara; Shulhan Arukh, Even HaEzer

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 19 January 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The opening halacha of Even HaEzer is the obligation to marry. The Torah never states it directly.
  • The first misvah of the Torah is "Peru urevu", be fruitful and multiply. Since the proper way to have children is through marriage, marriage becomes an obligation, secondary to the main one of having children.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules that every man must marry in order to have children. One who does not marry and have children is, says the Gemara, incomplete.
  • The Mishnah teaches that the obligation is on men, not women. The Gemara learns it from the verse "be fruitful... and conquer it": conquest was the role of men.
  • Some commentators explain that the Torah did not command women because childbirth involved great discomfort and, in those days, danger. The Gemara notes women often vowed during labour never to have children again.
  • What this means in practice, and whether women have any misvah in marrying, is discussed in the following notes.

In one line

The obligation to marry comes from the misvah to have children, and the Shulhan Arukh places both on men.
