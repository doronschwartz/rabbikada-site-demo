Topic: Where and for how long the ban of Rabbenu Gershom applies

Sources: The Rashba; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh and the Rema, Even HaEzer, Siman 1

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 12 February 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Though bigamy is illegal in most countries today, the scope of the Herem of Rabbenu Gershom still has practical consequences.
  • Geography: Rabbenu Gershom lived in northern France and Germany. The Rashba writes that his Takkanah was only for his place and was not accepted even in nearby Provence, let alone Spain or the Middle East.
  • The Rambam codifies that a man may marry two wives and does not mention the Takkanah at all.
  • The Shulhan Arukh records the Takkanah but writes that it did not spread; the Rema holds it spread everywhere unless one knows for certain it did not. The Shulhan Arukh still adds that it is good not to marry a second wife.
  • Time: the Rashba writes that it was only meant to last until the end of the fifth millennium (the year 5000, not 1000 years), roughly 1240 CE.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules similarly: an Ashkenazi who moved to a Sepharadi country may marry two wives. He records such a case in Yerushalayim, and that in Salonika, Constantinople and elsewhere no one objected when Ashkenazim did so.
  • The Rema holds that even if the Herem lapsed, Ashkenazim accepted it as a minhag, so it remains forbidden. That it is a minhag and not a real Herem has its own ramifications.

Sources quoted

  • The Rashba: the Takkanah was local and limited in time.
  • The Rambam: permits two wives without mentioning it.
  • Shulhan Arukh: it did not spread, and lapsed; still better not to marry a second wife.
  • The Rema: it spread, and binds Ashkenazim as a custom.

In one line

For the Shulhan Arukh and the Rashba the ban was local and time limited, while the Rema holds Ashkenazim bound by it as a custom.
