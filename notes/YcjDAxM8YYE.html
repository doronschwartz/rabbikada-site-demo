What is Melechet Dash?

The Av Melacha

Dash in the Mishkan is the fifth melacha. To make the dye for the Mishkan's roof:

  1. Plant
  2. Plow
  3. Harvest
  4. Gather the stalks
  5. Dash — thresh the seeds from the stalks
  6. Zoreh — winnow with wind
  7. Borer — select good from bad
  8. Tochen — grind
  9. Meraked — sieve
  10. Knead → Bake (as dye)

The essence of Dash: extracting something edible from an inedible encasement.

Key Principles

Only Gidulei Karka (D'Oraita)

אין דישה אלא בגידולי קרקע

Dash applies only to things that grow from the ground. Mid'Oraita, it doesn't apply to non-plant matter. However:

  • Milking mammals is still d'Oraita per most Rishonim, because animals (and humans) get their nutrients from the ground — so they are considered gidulei karka in an extended sense
  • Squeezing liquid from a woolen garment to use the liquid = d'Oraita Dash (wool is gidulei karka)
  • Squeezing liquid from a synthetic fiber = NOT d'Oraita Dash (synthetic isn't gidulei karka)

The Overlap with Melaben (Laundering)

This siman will also touch on Melaben (laundering) because an essential aspect of laundering clothes is the squeezing out of liquid.

The Three Categories of Fruit (Foundational)

The Gemara establishes three categories of fruit for the law of squeezing:

Category 1 — Olives and Grapes (Zetim v'Anavim)

Status: Forbidden d'Oraita to squeeze.

Why is this a Toledah of Dash?

  • You had a liquid contained within a solid encasement
  • You extract the liquid, leaving the solid behind
  • Just like Dash extracts wheat from stalks, this extracts juice from a solid fruit

Why specifically these two?

  • The Torah recognizes their liquids as mashkin (formal liquids)
  • They have their own status as liquids — special brachot, special halachot
  • Olive oil and wine are formal mashkin
  • Therefore extracting them = liquid from solid = Dash

Category 2 — Strawberries, Pomegranates, and Commonly Squeezed Fruits

Status: Forbidden mid'Rabbanan to squeeze.

Why only d'Rabbanan?

  • The Gemara identifies tutim v'rimonim (strawberries, pomegranates) as the prototypes
  • Rashi's reason: lav orchaihu behachi — it's not the common practice (in those days) to squeeze them, so what comes out is not formally a "mashke"
  • Treated as food from food (ochel from ochel) at the Torah level — therefore no d'Oraita Dash
  • But Chachamim forbade as a precaution against confusion with Category 1

The Chayei Adam's Challenging Position (200-300 years ago)

The Chayei Adam argued that nowadays — when oranges, apples, and many fruits are commonly squeezed — those fruits should be d'Oraita.

Almost all poskim rejected this. The Torah's category of "mashke" is fixed: only olive oil and wine. Modern fruits remain at d'Rabbanan level no matter how common their juice has become.

Practical Today (Category 2 includes):

  • Strawberries, pomegranates
  • Apples, oranges, grapefruits, pears
  • Cranberries, lychees
  • Any commonly-squeezed-for-juice fruit

Category 3 — Fruits Not Commonly Squeezed

Status: Permitted to squeeze on Shabbat.

Examples discussed:

  • Figs — fibrous and fleshy, ~80% water but high sugar content; not suited to juice; better for pastes, jams, dried (develot). The Mishna's reference to develot (dried figs) suggests the lack of juice tradition is ancient.
  • Dates — also generally not juiced; processed into silan/honey instead
  • Possibly melon/watermelon — though even this has become more common today; status debatable

Gezeirat Mashkin Shezavu (Liquids That Flowed Out)

A critical secondary principle: what about liquids that ooze out of the fruit on their own, without any active squeezing?

The Setup

  • A non-Jew squeezed olives on Shabbat (no instruction from you), or
  • Very ripe grapes/olives gushed liquid on their own
  • The liquid is sitting there — you didn't do anything

The Gezeira

The Chachamim were worried: if we permit such liquid, you might come to actively squeeze the remaining olives/grapes. So they forbade.

Designated to Eat

Designated to Squeeze

2 (tutim, etc.)

✅ Permitted

❌ Forbidden

The Muktzeh Dimension

The liquid that flowed out from Category 1 grapes/olives is also muktzeh — a double prohibition (can't drink it AND can't move it).

Why Even a Gezeira on Category 2?

This is a gezeira ligzeira (decree upon a decree), since squeezing Category 2 is itself only d'Rabbanan. The Chachamim still extended it — the limitation being that the designation must be for liquid.

Important Limitation

Surface moisture — a tiny amount of moisture on the surface is insignificant and not subject to this gezeira.

The Shulchan Aruch Seifim

Seif 1 — The Three Categories Codified

זיתים וענבים אסור לסחטן

Olives and grapes — forbidden to squeeze.

ואם יצאו מעצמם אסורים אפילו לא עמדו לאכילה

If liquid flows of its own accord — still forbidden, even if you only intended to eat the fruit (mid'Rabbanan, gezeirat mashkin shezavu).

ותותים ורימונים אסור לסחטן

Strawberries and pomegranates — forbidden to squeeze (mid'Rabbanan).

ואם יצאו מעצמן: אם עומדים לאכילה מותר, אם עומדים למשקין אסור

If liquid flows of its own accord:

  • Designated to eat → permitted
  • Designated to drink → forbidden

ושאר כל הפירות מותר לסחטן

All other fruit — permitted to squeeze.

The Rema — Local Customs and Category 2

ובמקום שנהגו לסחוט איזה פירות לשתות ממימיו מחמת צמא או תענוג דינו כתותים ורמונים

Where a particular fruit IS commonly squeezed for pleasure or refreshment, it takes Category 2 status in that place.

Example given by Rav Falk many years ago: Carrots in Israel. (Practically complicated by tochen issues, but theoretically the principle applies — what's "commonly squeezed" varies by region.)

The Mishna Berura's Modification

The Mishna Berura disagreed with the Rema's strict locality reading. He held:

  • If a fruit is not squeezed somewhere because of cultural/taste preference → Category 3 there
  • If a fruit is not squeezed somewhere because they don't have enough OR it's too expensive → still Category 2 universally (because if they had access, they would squeeze it)

Mishna Berura's example: Pears. Pears in some European cities ~100 years ago were squeezed; in others not — only because of supply. The Mishna Berura held pears were already Category 2 everywhere, and time has proven him right — pear juice is now widespread.

He also discussed apples, agreeing they are firmly Category 2.

Medicinal Squeezing Doesn't Count

אבל אם נהגו לסחטן לרפואה לבד אין לחוש

If a fruit is squeezed only for medicinal purposes, it doesn't establish Category 2 status — only squeezing for pleasure/refreshment counts.

Squeezing Into the Mouth — Derech Achila

A critical practical question: can I extract juice from a fruit by sucking it?

Universal Agreement: In the Mouth = Permitted

If you put a fruit in your mouth and squeeze with your teeth, extracting the juice in your mouth → everyone agrees this is permitted, even for olives and grapes.

This is derech achila — the way of eating.

The Disputed Middle Case: Sucking with Lips Outside the Mouth

וכל זה דווקא לסחוט אסור אבל מותר למצוץ בפיו מן הפירות המשקים שבהן

You hold a grape (or any fruit) at your lips and suck the juice out without putting the whole fruit in your mouth. Permitted?

Position

Source

Ruling

Permitted

Most Rishonim

It's like derech achila — lips are an extension of the mouth; or the action is so different from normal squeezing that it qualifies as a major shinui

Forbidden

Some Rishonim (Agudah and others)

Until the fruit is fully in the mouth it isn't derech achila — and you're still extracting liquid externally

Why Not Like Breastfeeding?

One might compare this to a mother squeezing milk into a child's mouth (which IS forbidden as we'll see later). But the cases are different:

  • A child sucking is the normal way the child drinks
  • That is derech yenika
  • Adults sucking fruit with lips is not the normal way of consuming

Halacha L'Maaseh

Category

Sucking with Lips Outside the Mouth

Category 1 (olives, grapes)

Should be machmir — don't do it

Category 2 and 3 (oranges, lemons, etc.)

Permitted

So if you have an ulcer and want to suck a lemon for relief → permitted. If you want to suck a grape with your lips → be machmir. If you put the grape fully in your mouth and squeeze with teeth → always permitted.

Lollipops

A separate question entirely. No extraction issue:

  • It's a single solid being dissolved in the mouth
  • No liquid is being extracted from a solid
  • Permitted

Seif 2 — Already-Crushed Olives and Grapes; Wine in a Barrel

Already-Crushed Fruits

זיתים וענבים שנתרסקו מערב שבת משקים היוצאים מהם מותרים

Olives and grapes that were fully crushed before Shabbat — the liquid that continues to ooze out on Shabbat is permitted.

Why Different from Seif 1?

  • Seif 1: Whole, intact olives/grapes — there's still a real risk you might squeeze further. So the Chachamim forbade the oozed liquid.
  • Seif 2: Already fully crushed before Shabbat. Not much liquid remains, and even if you squeezed, it would not reach d'Oraita levels. The Chachamim did not extend the gezeira here.

Grapes Above a Wine Barrel

ואפילו אם לא נתרסקו כל צרכן מערב שבת, אם יש יין בגיגית שהענבים בתוכה אף על פי שהענבים מתבקעים בשבת בגיגית מותר לשתותו

Even if the grapes weren't fully crushed — if they sit in a barrel of wine and ooze liquid that immediately mixes into the existing wine, you may drink the wine.

The principle: What oozes out is immediately batel (nullified) in the existing wine of the barrel. The drops that come out never have an independent existence as forbidden liquid — they enter the wine instantaneously.

Why Doesn't דבר שיש לו מתירין Block This?

Normally, a thing that will become permitted (דבר שיש לו מתירין — like Chametz after Pesach) does NOT nullify even in 1000 parts.

So why does the oozing wine nullify here, given that after Shabbat it would be permitted anyway?

Answer: דבר שיש לו מתירין applies only when the forbidden substance had a moment of independent existence before mixing. Here, the drops never had that moment — they emerged directly into the wine, instantly nullified.

Practical Implication

If the wine were flowing through a slope and only later reaching the barrel, the drops would have a moment of independent existence and become forbidden. Only direct, immediate mixing creates the heter.

Seif 3 — Wine from Grape Skins and Seeds (Temed)

חרצנים וזגים שנתן עליהם מים לעשות תמד מותר בשבת לשתותן

Grape skins and seeds that were soaked in water to extract their wine-flavor — permitted to drink on Shabbat.

Why Permitted?

  • Same logic as Seif 2 — the wine flavor immediately mixes with the water
  • No moment of independent forbidden existence

אפילו נתן בהם מים ויצאו מעצמם גם כן מותר לשתותן

Even if more wine flows out on its own from the soaked skins — still permitted to drink. Since only the skins and seeds remain (the main wine was already extracted), anything that flows out is permitted.

Seif 4 (Beginning) — Squeezing Onto Food

This is the major practical principle of the whole chapter. We'll cover the foundation tonight and complete it next week.

The Gemara's Foundational Statement (Shabbat)

אמר רבי זירא אמר רב חייא בר אשי אמר רב סוחט אדם אשכול של ענבים לתוך הקדרה אבל לא לתוך הקערה

Rav: One may squeeze a cluster of grapes into a kederah, but not into a kearah.

Vessel

Contents

Status

Kederah

Pot with food in it

✅ Permitted to squeeze into

Kearah

Empty bowl (no food)

❌ Forbidden

The Underlying Principle

משקה הבא אל האוכל כאוכל דמי

"A liquid that comes into food has the status of food."

The full logic:

  1. Dash forbids extracting a liquid from a solid
  2. When you squeeze grapes onto a salad, the liquid emerges and immediately enters the food
  3. The purpose is to add flavor to the salad — the liquid is integral to the food
  4. Halachically, that liquid is treated as food, not as a liquid
  5. Therefore the action is food from food, not liquid from food
  6. Food from food = no Dash

What Squeezing into an Empty Bowl Looks Like

The liquid emerges as a distinct, independent liquid → liquid from solid → full Dash → forbidden.

The d'Oraita position is difficult to explain — there's much discussion in the Rishonim about why this would be Torah-level since the absorbed liquid isn't even native to the vegetable.

Rabbeinu Chananel's Challenging Position

Rabbeinu Chananel made two radical claims:

Claim 1 — Halacha Follows Rabbi Yochanan

Generally הלכה כרבי יוחנן (when Rabbi Yochanan argues with Rav or Shmuel individually).

Claim 2 — Rabbi Yochanan Disagrees with the Earlier Halacha Too

Rabbeinu Chananel held Rabbi Yochanan is disputing not just kvashim but also the original principle (משקה הבא אל האוכל כאוכל דמי). So according to him, you cannot squeeze grapes onto a salad either.

Universal Rejection

All other Rishonim disagreed with both claims:

  1. On Halacha: Rav AND Shmuel both rule leniently → majority rules. The general rule of הלכה כרבי יוחנן applies when he disputes one Amora, not two.
  2. On Scope: Even if you posken like Rabbi Yochanan for kvashim, he's only disputing the kvashim case — not the broader principle of squeezing into food.

Practical Halacha (Tonight's Practical Conclusion)

The Shulchan Aruch in Seif 4:

מותר לסחוט אשכול ענבים תוך קדרה שיש בה תבשיל כדי לתקן האוכל, דהוי ליה משקה הבא לאוכל וכאוכל דמי, אבל אם אין בתבשיל אסור

You may squeeze a cluster of grapes into a pot that contains food, to enhance the food — because the liquid going onto food has the status of food. But into an empty bowl is forbidden.

The Shulchan Aruch then brings Rabbeinu Chananel's stricter view as a secondary opinion:

ולרבינו חננאל כל שהוא צריך למימיהן חייב חטאת, אפילו סחט לקדרה שיש בה אוכל, ולדבריו סוחט אשכול ענבים נמי אסור
