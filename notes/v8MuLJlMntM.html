Topic: Must a family be checked for pesulei kahal before marriage? Three views in the Rishonim

Sources: Gemara Kiddushin and Ketubot; Rashi; the Rambam; Rabbenu Tam; the Ramban

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 20 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Once both are known to be Jewish, must one check for pesulei kahal, such as a mamzer in the family, or a halalah where a Kohen is marrying?
  • The Gemara, in Kiddushin and Ketubot, says all families are presumed kasher, but there are contradictions, and the Rishonim reconcile them in three ways.
  • Rashi: a family known in the community, with no irur, needs no checks, for a Yisrael or a Kohen. For Rashi an irur is a rumour; the Rambam defines it as two witnesses testifying to a problem. Checks, which in the Mishnah can go back several generations, are needed only for an unknown family from afar. The Rambam basically follows this.
  • Rabbenu Tam, Rashi's grandson, is the most lenient: a Yisrael never needs to check, even with a rumour. Only a Kohen with a rumour about the family must check, since more restrictions apply to a Kohen.
  • The Ramban is the most stringent: a Kohen must always check, rumour or not, while a Yisrael checks only when there is a rumour.

In one line

Rashi requires checks only with a rumour or an unknown family, Rabbenu Tam only for a Kohen with a rumour, and the Ramban always for a Kohen.
