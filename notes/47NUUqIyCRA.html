Topic: Whether one who shaves daily may shave on Hol HaMoed

Sources: The Gemara; the Rishonim; Rabbenu Tam; Shulhan Arukh; Rav Moshe Feinstein; Rav Hayyim David HaLevi, Aseh Lekha Rav

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Monday 2 October 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara forbids haircuts and shaving on Hol HaMoed, lest people leave them for when they are off work and enter the festival looking unkempt.
  • Most Rishonim, and the Shulhan Arukh, forbid it even for one who cut his hair before the festival, since people will not know. Rabbenu Tam permits it then, but the halacha does not follow him.
  • Today many shave daily, so a clean-shaven man is assumed to have shaved before Yom Tov. On this basis some poskim permit daily shavers to shave; Rav Moshe Feinstein allowed it at least 50 years ago.
  • Many contemporary poskim forbid it, since a takkanah of the Hakhamim stands even when circumstances change.
  • Rav Hayyim David HaLevi, Sepharadi posek and Chief Rabbi of Tel Aviv (died 1998), in Aseh Lekha Rav stays on the fence: strong logic permits it, but it was a decree. He does not rebuke those who shave, and never speaks publicly against it.
  • Conclusion: it is a rabbinic decree; Rabbenu Tam permits after a pre-festival haircut; and even the forbidding Rishonim might permit it today. Together, one who shaves on Hol HaMoed, especially if not shaving detracts from his simhat Yom Tov, has what to rely on.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: no haircut on Hol HaMoed even after one before the festival.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: permits daily shavers.
  • Rav Hayyim David HaLevi: undecided; those who shave have what to rely on.

In one line

One who shaves every day and shaved before Yom Tov has what to rely on if he shaves on Hol HaMoed.
