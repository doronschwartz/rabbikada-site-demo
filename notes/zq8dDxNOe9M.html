Topic: Bows and single knots (Siman 317:5), and the practical rulings for shoelaces, curtains, headscarves and the bands of the Sefer Torah Source: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Siman 317, seifim 1 and 5, with the Rema and the Mishnah Berurah Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada in his Monday-night series on the Melakhot, Monday 11 March 2019 (Kosher and Mattir, Part 5)

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Summary so far

  • The Rema is strict like Rashi and the Rosh: a professional knot is always forbidden, and a simple knot only for a short time, 24 hours or seven days.
  • The Shulhan Arukh: a professional knot is forbidden even briefly; a simple knot is permitted if not permanent. In practice, for Sefaradim, seven days.
  • A misvah: a rabbinic knot is permitted when there is no alternative.
  • Double knots: the Rema, following the Shiltei HaGibborim, forbids them even for a short time. The Hida clearly permits them, and for Sefaradim, following the Hida and Hazon Ovadia, a double knot may be tied as long as it is not for more than seven days. A single overhand knot at the end of one thread has the same status as a double knot.
  • What counts is the norm. You cannot tie the top knot of a sisit and say you will untie it after Shabbat, since that knot is normally left for good.
  • The Biur Halacha rules that one is liable for untying even without intending to retie.

Seif 5: the bow

The Shulhan Arukh: a bow is permitted, because it is not a knot. This is explicit in the Gemara. A knot is two things twined together that must be undone by working them apart. A bow can be pulled and comes loose. So a bow may be tied even for years, and so may a bow on top of a bow, three or five of them, like the ribbon on a box of chocolates or on a dress.

The Rema: even if one tied a single knot underneath the bow, it is still permitted.

Two kinds of single knot. A single knot joining two things, the first cross of a pair of shoelaces, falls apart within seconds. It has no permanence and is permitted. A key tied to a string with one such knot may stay for good. But an overhand knot within a single thread is tight and, for the Rema, may be professional.

The plain reading of the Rema is that a single knot with a bow on it is not a knot, "non-knot plus non-knot equals non-knot", so ordinary shoelaces may stay tied for months or years.

The Mishnah Berurah brings Aharonim who disagree: the single knot is a knot, only without permanence. The bow holds it in place and makes it a real knot, so it follows the time limits. Two bows one after the other, however, are permitted.

The shoelace demonstration. The class tried it out on a pair of shoelaces. A single knot, then a bow: the bow can be pulled loose. A second bow made from the two loops is a bow on a bow, permitted. But tying the two loops together as a knot, as people do when "double knotting" their shoes, is a double knot.

Sefaradim. The Hida says the Shulhan Arukh agrees with the Rema, and Hazon Ovadia brings this: from the strict law, a single knot with a bow may be tied permanently, but it is good to be concerned for the stricter view and have in mind to untie it within seven days. A bow alone, or a single knot alone, may stay forever.

Intention. What counts is your intention when tying. If you meant to untie it in time and then forgot, you did nothing wrong. But an intention you never meant to keep is not a real intention.

For Sefaradim, the seven days for a single knot and bow is a double stringency, first following the Mishnah Berurah against the Rema, and then the strict reading of the Rambam. So it is certainly not forbidden for longer, and someone who tied his laces once and slips his shoes on and off for weeks has strong views to rely on. A double knot, however, really needs the seven days.

If before Shabbat you tied a double knot and cannot get your shoes off at night, even Ashkenazim may untie it, since sleeping in shoes is real discomfort.

A parent may tie a child's shoes with a double knot, intending that they be untied that night, by the child or anyone else.

Curtains tied back with a knot and bow may be left for a long time for Sefaradim; Ashkenazim would need to untie within 24 hours.

Headscarves

A woman's mitpahat is identical to shoelaces. Hazon Ovadia and Hakham Ben Sion both write that women may tie the scarf with a double knot, since a single knot slips, as long as they intend to untie it within the week. The Rabbi cautioned that this matters more than with shoes: a scarf can be slipped on and off for weeks without untying the knot, so the intention must be real.

The Sefer Torah bands

Ashkenazim and the Spanish and Portuguese tie bands around the Sefer Torah. The S&P also have a silk cloth across the Sefer Torah, beneath the bands.

Ashkenazim tie on Shabbat afternoon and untie on Monday morning. Tucking in a single knot is permitted, but some poskim do not like it, as it may not hold and is less honourable. A single knot and bow until Monday, or even until the next Shabbat, is permitted for four reasons together: 1. The opinion within Rashi that allows seven days. 2. The Rambam, for whom a simple knot for two days is certainly permitted. 3. The Rema, for whom a single knot with a bow is not a knot. 4. Perhaps a misvah need, though one could say coming in on Sunday to retie is an alternative.

A Sefer Torah used only on Rosh Hashanah and Kippur, not opened for a year, is harder for Ashkenazim: perhaps only two reasons remain, and some say six months is already permanent. They would need to come back within the week and tie it properly, or tuck it in.

Sefaradim. A single knot from one Shabbat to the next is certainly permitted, and even a double knot, since it is a simple knot for seven days. For a festival Sefer Torah, a double knot for a whole year should not be done. But a single knot with a bow until the next year may be permitted: perhaps it is not a knot at all; even if it is, it is a simple knot, tied for a misvah, and not permanent. The Rabbi gave a practical example: on a Shabbat when three Sifrei Torah are used, the third may not come out again until Rosh Hashanah, so tie it with a single knot and bow, not a double knot.

The Rabbi said the remaining cases, rubbish bags, the lulav and ties, would follow next week, and invited the class to bring other practical questions.

Practical summary

  • A bow, or bows on bows, is not a knot and may be left indefinitely. A single knot joining two things, with nothing on it, is also permitted.
  • For Sefaradim, shoelaces with a single knot and bow are permitted; it is good to have in mind to untie within seven days. A double knot needs that intention.
  • Ashkenazim untie a knot and bow within 24 hours and avoid double knots, but may undo a double knot from before Shabbat if it causes discomfort.
  • A headscarf may be tied with a double knot if one really intends to untie it within the week.
  • Sefer Torah bands may be tied with a single knot and bow, even for a festival Sefer Torah; a double knot only for a Sefer Torah that will be used again within the week.
