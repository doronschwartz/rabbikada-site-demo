Where We Are

Siman 129, the laws of the names, was finished last time, and this shiur begins Siman 130: the laws of the edim of the get and their signatures, and what happens if they cannot read or sign. The other group was already further along in the siman, so the aim was to move a little faster.

Until now the simanim dealt with writing the get: what it is written on, its details, the date (127), the place (128) and the names (129). Now we come to the hatimot, the signatures of the edim.

Part 1: Why Do the Edim Sign at All?

Edei mesirah or edei hatimah

We all know the famous mahloket:

Who effects the divorce

Rabbi Elazar

Edei mesirah karti: the witnesses who watch the get being given

Rabbi Meir

Edei hatimah karti: the witnesses who sign the get

The Gemara tells us clearly, in several places in Gittin, that the halacha is like Rabbi Elazar: it is the edei mesirah who make the divorce.

"Mipnei tikkun olam"

A second Mishnah says that the edim sign the get mipnei tikkun olam, for the good order of the world. Why? Perhaps the edei mesirah will die, and when the woman wants to remarry she will have no proof.

The Gemara asks: for Rabbi Elazar this makes sense, since a get could exist with edei mesirah alone. But for Rabbi Meir signatures are needed anyway, so what was the takanah? The answer: the takanah applies to both, in different ways.

  • For Rabbi Elazar, the takanah is that the edim should sign at all.
  • For Rabbi Meir, the takanah is that they should write out their names (mefaresh shemotehem), "Daniel ben Hayim", and not just a mark. That way it is clearer.

The Terumat HaDeshen's question

The Terumat HaDeshen, about 600 years ago, asks a very strong question. If we rule like Rabbi Elazar, and the whole reason for signing is the fear that the edei mesirah may die, then already in his time the Beit Din issued a document confirming the divorce, and the get was torn. So why do we still need edei hatimah at all? He gives an answer, but "this is a very strange idea": we rule like Rabbi Elazar, and nonetheless the edim still sign.

The Beit Shmuel says that even if we accept this, it still does not explain why today the edim must write out their names. That was the takanah only for Rabbi Meir. For Rabbi Elazar a plain signature should be enough. He leaves it as a question.

The Rabbi underlined the framework of the whole siman: strictly speaking, edei hatimah today are almost unnecessary, since the whole point was tikkun olam, and today we have the Beit Din's records.

Edei hatimah without edei mesirah

This really belongs to Siman 133, but in short:

  • The Rambam holds that bediavad such a get is kasher.
  • Tosafot hold that the get is batel.

Edei mesirah without any signatures

What about the reverse: a get given before edei mesirah, with no signatures at all?

  • Almost all the Rishonim say it is kasher, even rabbinically. Tosafot and the Rosh say the signing was never a real takanah, only an esah tovah, good advice.
  • The Rambam's language sounds much more like a real takanah.
  • The Pithei Teshuvah brings the Maharshal (in the Yam Shel Shlomo). In origin it is only good advice, but since everyone signs today, a get without signatures would be seen as a bad get. So if it has not been given, it should not be given; and even if it was given, one should only let it stand where she has already remarried.

The Rabbi's suggestion

The Rabbi suggested an answer to the Beit Shmuel's question. Tearing the get came later. From the time of the Gemara onwards, the edim always signed, so signing became part and parcel of the seder haget. To stop signing now would cast doubt on gittin (mosi laaz). So even though strictly a signature might be enough, we keep the full signing.

"That's very true." In Siman 133 the Shulhan Arukh rules that a get with edei mesirah and no edei hatimah is kasher. Lemaaseh, if such a get were given and she remarried, the children are not mamzerim; they are completely kasher. But we would insist on edei hatimah, with their names written out, because such a get would look terrible for the woman, and terrible for the Beit Din that wrote it.

Part 2: The Gap Between the Text and the Signatures (Seif 1)

צריך שיחתמו שני עדים כשרים זה תחת זה למטה משיטה אחרונה של גט, ולא יניחו כדי אויר שני שיטין בין שיטה אחרונה לחתימתם

Two kasher edim must sign, one under the other, below the last line of the get, and must not leave a space of two lines between the last line and their signatures.

Why it matters

This is a sugya in Bava Batra, and its main halachic home is in Hoshen Mishpat, in the laws of signing a document. The edim cannot sign exactly against the last line of text, so some space is accepted. But a large gap is dangerous: someone could later add a line between the text and the signatures. That is why in documents, and in the get too, we fill up the whole last line.

Once the gap reaches two lines, it is a problem. What counts as a line, with or without the tall and long letters reaching into the next line, is discussed in Bava Batra and ruled in Hoshen Mishpat.

If they did leave a gap

Opinion

Ruling

Tur (brought first in the Rema)

The get is pasul

Rambam, Ramban, Rashba, Ran

Kasher, through the edei mesirah, just as a get with no edei hatimah would be kasher

The stringent side, which the Rabbi explained from the Raavad, asks: we allow many flawed signatures where there are edei mesirah, so why not here? Because in those cases you can see from the document that something is wrong, and you will go and ask the edei mesirah. Here, once the line is filled in, it looks like an ordinary document. You will have no inkling that it may have been forged, and you may never come to ask.

Sign right underneath

The Rabbi mentioned that in older ketubot he has seen witnesses sign right at the bottom of the page, with a large gap below the text. Not every rabbi who does weddings knows this halacha. "It's really important to be aware": in ketubot and in gittin alike, sign right underneath the text.

Three reasons to be lenient bediavad

Obviously one should be careful not to do this. But if it happened, the Rabbi gave three reasons to be lenient:

  1. The Rambam and the Rishonim with him hold it is kasher.
  2. The Rashba, brought in the Beit Yoseph, says that today, when we write kedat Moshe veYisrael, it works like the closing sharir vekayam in Hoshen Mishpat, after which anything written below is irrelevant.
  3. The whole question only arises when she brings the get in order to remarry. Today she does not keep the get; it is torn, and she receives a document from the Beit Din.

The second witness signed first

The Rema: if the second witness signed first, and then the first signed above him, it is kasher, even though when the second signed there was a gap of two lines. But lekhathilah, do not do this.

Part 3: Who Should Not Sign (the Rema on Seif 1)

Relatives

  • The edim obviously cannot be relatives of the husband or the wife.
  • The Rema adds that lekhathilah one should not use even a relative who became distant, or people related only by marriage. They are completely kasher; nonetheless, do not use them.
  • It is good to make sure the sofer is not a relative of the husband or the wife. But where it is not possible, there is no concern.

Why the sofer? He is a third party, not really a witness, which is why no one raised it until later. The class discussed whether the sofer, by writing the get, is himself taking part in it. The Rema continues: some are stringent that the edim should not be relatives of the sofer, and that none of them should be relatives of the Rav HaMesader who arranges the get, and it is good to be careful about all this lekhathilah. "This is all a nice thing", but me-ikar hadin it is absolutely fine.

A baal mum

None of the edim should be suspected of arayot. The Rema also brings those who are stringent that the edim should not have a physical blemish (baal mum). The Rabbi said he has always found this very difficult to explain; the best explanation he found is in the Hatam Sofer, quoting the Maharil. "These concepts are obviously very uncomfortable for us today."

One set of edim

The Mordekhai and the Semag: the practice is that the edei hatimah are also the edei mesirah, and that they see the husband instruct the sofer, so that everything is done before one set of edim.

When does this matter? In a get shelihut, the same edim are not always able to come, and sometimes the Rabbi's Beit Din has had to use different edim. It is a good practice, and different edim are absolutely fine.

The same idea applies at weddings. If the hatan wants to honour a friend, let him sign the ketubah, and have your own reliable edim under the huppah. Two edim on the ketubah and two under the huppah is no problem at all.

Ink and pen

The edim should sign only with the ink and pen of the husband, like the sofer, as explained at the beginning of Siman 120.

Part 4: Where and How They Sign (Seifim 2-5)

Seif 2: no blank margin

The sofer should begin at the start of the line, so that no blank space is left in the margin before the signatures, where something could be added.

Seif 3: a greeting between the get and the signatures

לא יהיה בין הגט לחתימת העדים כתוב דבר שאינו מענין הגט, ואם כתוב כן, כגון שכתוב שאלו בשלומה, פסול

The Mishnah: if, between the get and the signatures, he wrote a greeting, "ask after her welfare", the get is pasul. Perhaps the edim signed only on the greeting: testifying that although he is divorcing her, he still cares about her shalom.

What does pasul mean here?

  • The Rambam: safek megureshet, since we do not know whether they signed on the greeting or on the whole get.
  • Another view: a doubtful testimony is no testimony at all. Testimony must be clear, so the get is batel; and in practice one should be stringent like the Rambam as well.

The Shulhan Arukh just writes pasul. The Beit Shmuel points out that no Rishon explains it that way: it is either safek megureshet or batel. The Rabbi suggested the Shulhan Arukh is simply quoting the Gemara, relying on what he wrote in the Beit Yoseph.

If he wrote "and ask after her welfare", with a vav, it is kasher: it joins on to what is above, and they signed on everything.

The Darkhei Moshe raises a related case: the first witness signs "Kohen". Do we say the second witness was signing only on that? He concludes there is no concern.

A practical point for ketubot. If there is a crossing-out near the bottom of a ketubah, never sign directly underneath it. Signatures go on what is above them, so it could be said that you signed only on the correction. Signatures do not go on what is beside them, so it is better to sign in a way that clearly covers the whole document.

The key question for all these seifim

What are these seifim talking about? We will learn in Seif 6 that if there are edei mesirah, the get is kasher. So all these cases must be where there are only edei hatimah.

  • For the Rif and the Rambam, a get with edei hatimah alone works, so a flaw in the signatures makes it a problem.
  • But Tosafot hold that a get with edei hatimah and no edei mesirah is batel. So what are these sugyot about?

The answer, a principle that will come up many times: when there are kasher edei hatimah, we assume there were kasher edei mesirah. How that assumption works is a big discussion in Siman 133. But here the signatures are flawed, so we cannot assume it, and the get is a problem.

Seif 4: signed above, beside or behind

חתמו בראש הגט או מצידו או בימינו או בשמאלו או מאחריו, פסול

  • The Rashbam: as the Rabbi understood him, because signatures go on what is written above them.
  • Tosafot: it could be made into a get mekushar (a folded, tied get).
  • The Rambam: it is batel (again, without edei mesirah), because signatures on the top, the side or the back are not attached to the get.

Obviously, if they signed on the side near the bottom, together with the other text written there, it would be fine.

The Beit Shmuel says the Shulhan Arukh was not precise: this is definitely a batel get, and that is how "pasul" here should be read.

Seif 5: the direction of the signatures

אף כשחותמין למטה צריך שיהיה גב החתימה כלפי הגט, ואם היו רגלי החתימה כלפי הגט, פסול

The edim must sign in the same direction as the get, with the top of their letters facing the bottom line of the get. If they turned the paper round and signed upside down, with the "legs" of the signature facing the get, the Rambam says it is batel. Signatures go on everything before them, and written upside down, what comes "before" them is the blank paper, not the get.

Part 5: When There Are Edei Mesirah (Seif 6)

כל אלו שאמרנו פסול, אם יש כאן עידי מסירה, כשר

In all these cases, if there are edei mesirah, the get is kasher.

The Rabbi recalled the Maharshal from the beginning of the shiur: a get without proper signatures should not be given; even if it was given, one should not rely on it and should write another, unless she has already remarried.

The Beit Shmuel disagrees, based on the Shulhan Arukh's word kasher. If he meant "kasher only once she has remarried", he would not have written simply "kasher". So in his view, a get given with edei mesirah, without proper signatures, is kasher.

So far: do not leave a gap between the text and the signatures; write nothing irrelevant in between; sign right underneath, not on the top, the side or the back; and sign in the same direction as the get. If any of this went wrong and there are edei mesirah, the get is kasher.

Part 6: A Get Over Two Columns (Seif 7)

שייר מקצת הגט וכתבו בדף השני והעדים מלמטה בסוף הדף השני, כשר

In Seif 4 the whole get was on one page and only the signatures were on the next. Here the get itself runs on to a second column, and the edim sign at the bottom of the second.

  • This is kasher, as long as it is a scroll where it is clearly recognisable that nothing was cut, and the sofer meant to finish on the second column.
  • If it is not recognisable, she is a safek megureshet, even if it was given before edim. Perhaps there were two gittin, and the end of one and the beginning of another were cut and joined. Edei mesirah do not help here, because they would not know that.
  • The Rema: if there are edim who know that it was one get from the start, and it was given before them, it is kasher.

Two sheets glued together

The Rema adds (the Rabbi said this comes from the Or Zarua): if two hides were glued together and the get written across them, it is pasul out of doubt. What is the concern? The class suggested it was the same as before, but the Rabbi explained it differently: the question is whether two pieces stuck together count as one document or two. The Rabbi compared it to a sefer made of several sheets sewn together, which is still one sefer.

The Pithei Teshuvah says paper today is absolutely fine, even though it is made by pressing fibres together, because that is how paper is made: it has become a new entity, one document. What would be a problem in practice is running out of space on the get and sticking another piece underneath.

The next seif opens a new sugya, so the shiur stopped here, to continue next time.

Summary of the Halachot

  1. Rabbi Elazar's edei mesirah effect the divorce, and we rule like him. The edim sign because of the takanah of tikkun olam, in case the edei mesirah die.
  2. Today signing is strictly almost unnecessary (Terumat HaDeshen), yet we sign and write out our names, perhaps because signing became part of the seder haget and dropping it would cast doubt on gittin.
  3. A get with edei mesirah and no signatures is kasher according to almost all the Rishonim (Tosafot, the Rosh: esah tovah). The Maharshal says not to give it lekhathilah; if she remarried, the children are completely kasher.
  4. No gap of two lines between the text and the signatures (Seif 1). If there is one: pasul (Tur), or kasher with edei mesirah (Rambam, Ramban, Rashba, Ran). The Rabbi gave three reasons to be lenient bediavad. Always sign right underneath.
  5. Relatives: lekhathilah not even a relative who became distant or by marriage; preferably the sofer and the Rav HaMesader are not related either, but me-ikar hadin it is fine. Some are stringent about a baal mum.
  6. One set of edim for the signing, the giving and the instruction to the sofer is the practice, but different edim are fine. Edim sign with the husband's ink and pen.
  7. Nothing irrelevant between the get and the signatures (Seif 3); signatures not above, beside or behind (Seif 4), and not upside down (Seif 5).
  8. Where there are edei mesirah, all these are kasher (Seif 6). The Beit Shmuel says it may be relied on; the Maharshal is stricter.
  9. A get continued on a second column (Seif 7) is kasher if it is clearly one scroll; if not, safek megureshet even with edei mesirah. Two glued sheets are pasul out of doubt (Rema); modern paper is fine.
