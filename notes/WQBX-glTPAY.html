Topic: Why there are three massot, why the middle one is broken, and why at Yahas

Sources: The Gemara; the Mishnah Berurah; the pasuk of the dough "bound up in their garments upon their shoulders"

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Monday 27 March 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The three massot are often linked to Kohen, Levi and Yisrael, a lovely idea, but the halachic reason is that we need two whole massot for lehem mishneh, as at every Shabbat and Yom Tov meal (recalling the double portion of mann), plus a third to break.
  • The Gemara explains we break it because the Torah calls massah lehem oni, bread of poverty, and the poor ration their food, eating half now and keeping half for later, for the Afikoman.
  • We break it now, before Maggid, because lehem oni is also "lehem she-onin alav devarim harbeh", bread over which much is said, so it should be broken while the Haggadah is recited.
  • The larger piece is put away for the Afikoman and the smaller piece goes between the two whole massot. The Syrian custom is to break it into the shapes of a dalet and a vav, for Kabbalistic reasons.
  • Many cover or wrap the Afikoman, as the Mishnah Berurah cites, because the pasuk says the people carried their dough bound up.
  • The Syrian custom, and that of many others, is for each person to carry the wrapped Afikoman on the shoulder around the table, answering "Where are you coming from? Egypt. Where are you going? Yerushalayim. What are you carrying? Massah": a beautiful way to feel as if leaving Egypt.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: massah as lehem oni, both bread of poverty and bread over which much is said.
  • The Mishnah Berurah: the custom of covering the Afikoman.
  • The pasuk of the dough bound up on their shoulders: the source for wrapping and carrying the Afikoman.

In one line

We have three massot for lehem mishneh plus one to break as bread of poverty, and break it just before Maggid because it is the bread over which the story is told.
