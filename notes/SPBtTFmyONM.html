Topic: Moving a mechanical clock forward on Shabbat or Yom Tov when the clocks change

Sources: The Mishnah Berurah, Hilchot Shabbat; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph, Yehaveh Daat 2:48; Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Thursday 25 March 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • This year in England the clocks go forward from GMT to BST on Mosei Shabbat, losing an hour on Sunday morning, which causes confusion about synagogue times. The question is about mechanical clocks only, not electric ones.
  • The Mishnah Berurah in Hilchot Shabbat writes that it may even be a Torah prohibition, but he refers to restarting a clock that has stopped, which may be fixing an item (metaken manah).
  • A working clock that is simply an hour slow is different: moving it involves no melakhah.
  • So Hakham Ovadiah (Yehaveh Daat 2:48) rules clearly that one may move a working clock forwards or backwards on Shabbat. Some said not to, and that was the custom in Jerusalem, but by the letter of the law it is allowed.
  • Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach also permitted it, and said that even according to the stricter view it is allowed for a misvah.
  • So moving a mechanical clock forward on Sunday morning to know the correct time for synagogue is certainly fine. Electric clocks are a separate discussion.
