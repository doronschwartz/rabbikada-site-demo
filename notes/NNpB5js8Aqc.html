Topic: The link between the shofar of Rosh Hashanah and the shofar of the Yovel

Sources: The Torah ("Yom teruah"; Parashat Behar); the Gemara

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Monday 6 September 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Torah never calls the day Rosh Hashanah, only Yom Teruah, a day of blowing, so its essence is blowing and hearing the shofar.
  • The Torah does not say what to blow. The Gemara derives it from the only other teruah, in Parashat Behar: "vehaavarta shofar teruah" at the start of the Yovel, the 50th year.
  • In the Yovel slaves were freed, everyone returned to their family, property reverted to its owners and economic ties were released: a great equaliser, a chance for everyone to begin again.
  • Since Rosh Hashanah's shofar is learnt from the Yovel, they must be closely linked. The word repeated in Behar is "tashuvu", return; teshuvah simply means returning.
  • So the shofar challenges each of us to return to our inheritance: to end personal slavery and the habits that hold us back, and return to our true self, the pure soul within that wants to be better.
  • The shofar calls us to reset, return to the starting line and begin again, as in the times of the Yovel, looking ahead to the great shofar of our freedom.

In one line

The shofar of Rosh Hashanah, like that of the Yovel, calls us to freedom by returning to our true selves.
