Topic: Why we read Ruth on Shavuot, and whether a Berakhah is said on it

Sources: The Midrash; the Mishnah; the Torah, Parashat Ki Tese; the Hakhamim (Moavi velo Moavit); the Vilna Gaon; the Beit Yoseph; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Wednesday 24 May 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Reading Ruth on Shavuot is an ancient custom, found in Midrashim.
  • The Midrash's classic reason: Torah must go with Gemilut Hasadim. Ruth and Naomi exemplify Hesed, and receiving the Torah means committing to become better people.
  • The Mishnah says whoever keeps the Torah in poverty will keep it in wealth: Ruth committed herself in hard times and became great-grandmother of David HaMelekh.
  • David HaMelekh passed away on Shavuot, and the book ends with his genealogy.
  • A fourth reason, showing the Oral and Written Torah are inseparable: Parashat Ki Tese bars a Moabite from entering the community even after conversion, and Doeg taunted David over his Moabite descent. Only the Hakhamim's reading, "Moavi velo Moavit" (a male Moabite, not a female), allowed Ruth in and preserved David's kingship.
  • The Vilna Gaon, with earlier sources, held one says a Berakhah. Some Ashkenazim, especially in Israel, read it from a parchment scroll with a Berakhah, some even with Shehehiyanu, like the Megillah on Purim.
  • The Beit Yoseph and the Rema say the custom is not to make a Berakhah, as Sepharadim and many Ashkenazim do.
  • Many Sepharadim read half on each day outside Israel; in Israel all is read on the first day.

In one line

Ruth teaches Hesed and the authority of the Oral Torah, and Sepharadim read it without a Berakhah.
