Topic: May a married Jewish woman serve as a surrogate?

Sources: Rav Moshe Feinstein; the Satmar Rebbe; Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach; Rav Shlomo Amar; Rav Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Sunday 19 July 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The case: a couple with a viable egg whose wife cannot carry; the fertilised egg is placed in a surrogate. A single surrogate poses no problem. With a non-Jewish surrogate, a conversion is done as a stringency (giyur lehumrah), because of the doubt over who the mother is.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: no forbidden act of intimacy, so no problem.
  • The Satmar Rebbe objected to a man's sperm entering a married woman. Here what enters is an already fertilised egg, so there is an argument that he too would permit it.
  • Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach: if the egg defines the mother, the surrogate is essentially an incubator with no halachic connection to the child. Even if the carrier is the mother, there is no direct connection between the man's sperm and the married woman, only an already fertilised egg.
  • On this analysis Rav Shlomo Amar, former Chief Rabbi of Israel, permitted a married woman to be a surrogate, and writes that Rav Ovadiah Yoseph agreed. This is now the practice in Israel.
  • This is very beneficial: surrogates must already have carried children, so unmarried candidates are mostly divorced or widowed. In Israel, unlike England where only expenses may be paid, women had been divorcing in order to be paid as surrogates.

In one line

By all three views a married Jewish woman may arguably act as a surrogate, and this is now the practice in Israel.
