Topic: Wearing a metal hostage pin or poppy on Shabbat where there is no eruv

Sources: Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Tuesday 12 November 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • With an eruv it is fine; the question is for one who does not carry in an eruv.
  • Anything that is neither a malbush (garment) nor a takhshit (adornment) counts as carrying on Shabbat.
  • The Hakhamim still forbade some garments and adornments: things one regularly takes off to show people, like rings; things that easily fall off, like a shoe a size too big; and things people mock, like going out in one shoe, which one will end up taking off and carrying.
  • The Shulhan Arukh notes that women do wear rings on Shabbat and justifies it, among other reasons, because we have no true Torah public domain today, so even carrying a ring removed would not breach a Torah law.
  • Even the ring prohibition concerns only something regularly taken off. A metal hostage pin, or a metal poppy fastened with a safety pin, is a takhshit adorning the garment, and one does not normally take it off to show people.
  • So a metal yellow hostage pin or metal poppy is absolutely fine to wear on Shabbat. Paper poppies raise more questions and follow tomorrow.

In one line

A metal hostage pin or poppy is an adornment not usually removed, so it may be worn on Shabbat without an eruv.
