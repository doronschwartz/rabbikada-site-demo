Topic: Having a non-Jew neuter one's animal, and selling an animal that will be neutered

Sources: The Gemara; the Rishonim; Shulhan Arukh; the Aharonim

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara says explicitly that one may not give one's animals to a non-Jew to castrate.
  • One reason: the rabbinic prohibition of instructing a non-Jew (amirah lenokhri), as on Shabbat, where one may not ask a non-Jew to write for him.
  • The other: lifnei iver, causing another to sin, as in handing wine to a Nazir who could not otherwise obtain it. On this view non-Jews are also forbidden to castrate, so asking one may be a Torah prohibition, depending on the circumstances.
  • The difference: selling an animal to a non-Jew who will certainly neuter it. By amirah lenokhri nothing was asked, so it is fine; by lifnei iver it would seemingly be forbidden.
  • The majority of the Rishonim, the Shulhan Arukh included, follow amirah lenokhri. But the Aharonim write that in the first instance one should also be strict for lifnei iver, and not sell knowingly unless circumstances are exceptional.
  • If the buyer will pass it to a vet to neuter, it is twice removed, and the Aharonim permit it even on the lifnei iver view. So in most cases selling is permitted.
  • Asking a non-Jew to neuter on one's behalf is forbidden in all cases, whether the animal is one's own, another's, or a non-Jew's.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: amirah lenokhri or lifnei iver as the reason.
  • Shulhan Arukh: follows amirah lenokhri.
  • The Aharonim: be strict for lifnei iver in the first instance, but twice removed is permitted.

In one line

One may not ask a non-Jew to neuter any animal, but may usually sell an animal even knowing a vet will neuter it.
