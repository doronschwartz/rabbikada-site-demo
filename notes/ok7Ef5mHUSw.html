Recap

  • Category 1 (serious danger): Tehillim, trust, prayer, every natural means. If a person truly lives with Hakadosh Baruch Hu, no danger can harm him (the snakes in Chukat after the teshuvah) — but never put yourself in danger: you may lack the merit, and even if saved, it deducts from your merits. In mortal spiritual danger, pray — and sometimes one must give up his life.
  • Category 2 (minor harms): do not employ bitachon (prayer is fine) unless one is of the greatest people — though Rabbeinu Yonah in Mishlei disagrees (a machloket Rishonim). In spiritual matters even small things can become very big, so bitachon is appropriate even for small spiritual dangers.
  • Category 3 begun (achieving essential matters — basic bread, clothes, shelter): employ bitachon, pray, and request the basics, like Yaakov Avinu.

The five categories: (1) avoiding extremely harmful situations, (2) avoiding less harmful situations, (3) achieving the essential, (4) achieving the important-but-non-essential, (5) the non-important and non-essential.

Tonight: the two questions Rabbi Avraham raises in the middle of Category 3 — and his answers.

QUESTION 1 — If Hashem Feeds Even the Animals, Why Bitachon?

The Question

Many verses state Hashem grants basic parnasah even to animals — זן מקרני ראמים עד ביצי כינים (from the horns of wild oxen to the eggs of lice). If Hashem sustains all creatures anyway, what practical need is there for humans to have bitachon?

The Answer — Free Will Changes Everything

אף על פי שפרנסת הבריות מובטחת... הנה לפעמים גורם העוון לידי הכרתת הפרנסה Even though parnasah is guaranteed to creatures by nature — sin can cause a person's parnasah to be cut off.

The default position is indeed that Hashem feeds everyone. But humans have bechirah — free will. Our obedience or disobedience changes the parnasah in three directions:

Direction 1 — Aveirot Can Remove Parnasah Entirely

  • The Torah's curses (Bechukotai etc.) explicitly warn of famine when we don't do Hashem's will

Direction 2 — Or Parnasah Comes, But Only Through Great Toil

  • Adam after the sin: בזעת אפך תאכל לחם... וקוץ ודרדר תצמיח לך — you'll have bread, but by the sweat of your brow, with thorns and thistles
  • Kayin: כי תעבוד את האדמה לא תוסף תת כוחה לך — the ground will no longer yield its strength to you
  • אוכלי לחם העצבים — you'll eat "the bread of sadness": parnasah delivered with difficulty and turmoil
  • King Asa of Yehudah: he turned to Ben-Hadad, king of Aram, for help against Baasha king of Israel — and the navi rebuked him: בהישענך על מלך ארם ולא על ה' אלהיך — because you leaned on the king of Aram and not on Hashem your God. When you relied on Hashem, He delivered the vast armies of the Kushim and Luvim into your hand; כי ה' עיניו משוטטות בכל הארץ להתחזק עם לבבם שלם אליו — Hashem's eyes rove the whole earth to strengthen those whose heart is complete with Him. נסכלת על זאת — כי מעתה יש עמך מלחמות: you acted foolishly; from now on you will have wars. (Not total removal — but everything becomes a fight.)

Direction 3 — Obedience Brings Broad, Easy Parnasah

וכנגד זה מביאה צייתות לסדר השם הרחבת הפרנסה וריבויה

  • Parshat Ekev: והיה עקב תשמעון... וברך פרי בטנך ופרי אדמתך דגנך ותירושך ויצהרך — Hashem blesses land, grain, wine, oil, cattle
  • The botei'ach is by definition obedient (bitachon presupposes and is obedience) — so Hashem rewards his bitachon with easy parnasah: כן יתן לידידו שנא — He gives to His beloved in their sleep (as explained a few weeks ago — you make your parnasah and sleep easily)
  • ודורשי ה' לא יחסרו כל טוב — those who seek Hashem lack no good

The Contrast Verses (from Birkat Hamazon and Shabbat mornings)

כפירים רשו ורעבו, ודורשי ה' לא יחסרו כל טוב What does "young lions went hungry" mean? Metzudat David: כפירים = the wealthy and the great — a metaphor. The wealthy went hungry because they forgot Hashem and their bitachon; while the seekers of Hashem lack nothing.

  • Chana: שבעים בלחם נשכרו — those who were sated with bread ended up hiring themselves out (they lost their trust)
  • Doeg (who maligned David to Shaul — initially a righteous talmid chacham): גם אל יתצך לנצח... וישחקו עליו — God will tear him down forever, and the righteous will see and laugh: הנה הגבר לא ישים אלהים מעוזו, ויבטח ברוב עשרו — behold the man who did not make God his stronghold, but trusted in his great wealth

The Explicit Mishnah (Kiddushin 82) — Which the Avi Ezer Surprisingly Doesn't Bring

רבי שמעון בן אלעזר אומר: ראית מימיך חיה ועוף שיש להם אומנות? והן מתפרנסין שלא בצער. והלא לא נבראו אלא לשמשני, ואני נבראתי לשמש את קוני — אינו דין שאתפרנס שלא בצער? אלא שהרעותי את מעשי וקיפחתי את פרנסתי

Have you ever seen a beast or bird with a trade? Yet they are sustained without anguish. They were created to serve me, and I was created to serve my Maker — surely I should be sustained without anguish! But I ruined my deeds — and forfeited my parnasah.

The kal vachomer is exactly Rabbi Avraham's answer: animals have no free will, so their sustenance flows automatically; humans chose badly, and so parnasah became hard.

Summary of Question 1

Hashem gives parnasah to all — but because of our free will, obedience improves and broadens our parnasah, while disobedience removes it or delivers it only through turmoil and hard work. That is the practical need for bitachon.

QUESTION 2 — Is Bitachon Natural to Everyone, or Rare and Praiseworthy?

The Question

Verses imply trust in Hashem is natural and universal — most classically (said before eating bread, in Ashrei):

עיני כל אליך ישברו ואתה נותן להם את אכלם בעתו — the eyes of ALL look hopefully to You

But other verses praise the one who trusts — אשרי אדם בוטח בך — and condemn the one who doesn't — הנה הגבר לא ישים אלהים מעוזו. If everyone naturally has bitachon, why is it praised or its absence cursed? The verses seem to contradict.

Rabbi Avraham gives two answers.

ANSWER 1 — "Kol" Means Majority; Bitachon Can Be Compelled or Voluntary

Two introductions:

  1. כל כוונתו רוב — "all" in Tanakh often means the majority
  2. הביטחון יכול להיות הכרחי או רצוני — bitachon can be compelled or voluntary (and voluntary bitachon can itself be general or specific — below)

Compelled Bitachon — "No Atheists in a Foxhole"

When life is good — wealth, health, everything — a person forgets Hashem. (The Mesillat Yesharim discusses this: poverty is a great test of trust — but the Ramchal suggests the test of wealth is the greater one.) Then the person loses the wealth or the health — and now he turns to Hashem:

בעלי עושר וכבוד... אם יתרוששו ויאבדו את עושרם וכבודם ישובו לביטחון When the wealthy and honored lose their wealth and status — they return to bitachon. Not from free good will, but compelled by circumstances — their transient walking-stick (עושר, כבוד, בריאות) was knocked away.

Compelled Bitachon Even Among Idolaters

ודבר זה מצוי תדיר אפילו אצל עובדי עבודה זרה

The Rambam's principle (opening of Hilchot Avodah Zarah): idolatry didn't begin with people believing idols have intrinsic power — it began with intermediaries: people who couldn't face approaching Hashem directly, so they put something in between. (This is why Moshe broke the Luchot on 17 Tammuz — and why even today religious people cling to amulets, red strings, blue charms: an avoidance of direct communication with Hashem, placing trust in an intermediary. That, essentially, was the golden calf.) Most ovdei avodah zarah serve their idols in order to come closer to the true Savior: כי ביטחונם בפסיליהם... הוא אמצעי בינם ובין השם המושיע.

Proof 1 — the sailors of Yonah: In the storm, ויזעקו איש אל אלהיו — each cried to his own god. But when Yonah told them עברי אנכי ואת ה' אלהי השמים אני ירא — וייראו האנשים יראה גדולה — they feared Hashem, and prayed to Him: אנה ה' אל נא נאבדה בנפש האיש הזה. A moment ago they were praying to idols — and they trace right back to Hakadosh Baruch Hu.

Proof 2 — Mesha, King of Moav: Under Israel's oppression he offered his firstborn son to his god — and there was קצף גדול על ישראל (Israel was blamed for having driven him to it). The horror doesn't contradict the fact that כוונתו היתה לשם... בצרתו — in his distress, his intent traced toward Heaven.

Hence Yirmiyahu: מי לא יראך מלך הגוים כי לך יאתה — who does not fear You, King of the nations — even idolaters ultimately trace back to You. And David: מבטח כל קצוי ארץ וים רחוקים — You are the trust of all the ends of the earth.

Voluntary Bitachon — The Choice of Yichidei Segulah

והביטחון הרצוני הוא ביטחונם של יחידי סגולה מיחדים את השם

Voluntary bitachon is chosen — not squeezed out by desperation: דבקים בתורה, מאמינים באמת, וכל עניינם שהכל מכל מהשם יתברך — they place their reliance on Hashem even while they have wealth, health, strength, and status.

Bitachon and Yichud Hashem: note the phrase מיחדים את השם — true bitachon is an expression of the unity of God. One who truly lives knowing that nothing can harm or help him apart from Hashem is living שמע ישראל ה' אלהינו ה' אחד. Bitachon strengthens yichud Hashem, and yichud Hashem strengthens bitachon.

Two Levels Within Voluntary Bitachon

Level

Description

הביטחון הכללי — general

The bitachon of ordinary people "in the thoughts of their hearts": we all say and feel we trust Hashem — though it may not manifest in our day-to-day activities. (Even Sancheriv, despite his heresy, said בלא ה' עליתי על המקום הזה — he recognized his campaign came from Hashem.)

הביטחון הפרטי — specific/rare

The bitachon of the special shomrei Torah who live it every moment: in every business dealing, every negotiation, their trust is in Hashem and not in their efforts — בטח אל ה' בכל לבך ואל בינתך אל תשען... בכל דרכיך דעהו — training every thought toward Hashem

(The avodah: never be compelled into bitachon — choose it voluntarily; and then work to raise the general into the specific, so it manifests in everything we do.)

The Resolution (Answer 1)

הפסוקים שבהם נאמר כי כל אחד בוטח בהשם — כוונתם לרוב בני אדם, והביטחון מהסוג המוכרח; והפסוקים האמורים על נדירותו של הביטחון — כגון אשרי אדם בוטח בך — כוונתם לביטחון הרצוני המובחר

  • "עיני כל אליך ישברו" = the majority of people do, at the end of the day, turn to Hashem — mostly compelled (when the going gets hard and there's no alternative)
  • "אשרי אדם בוטח בך" = praise for the one who voluntarily chose bitachon — that is the rare and praiseworthy kind

ANSWER 2 — Kol Really Means ALL: Everyone Trusts in Something, and Everything Traces Back to Hashem

(Rabbi Avraham himself loves this answer — he closes it with "this is one of the beautiful and correct interpretations.")

כי כל הגורמים חוזרים אליו יתעלה... נמצא הבוטח באותו גורם סומך בטחונו עליו יתעלה — בין אם הבין זאת... או שלא הבין

Everyone trusts in someone or something — and that someone or something traces back to Hashem. So in effect, even without realizing it, everyone indirectly trusts in Hashem.

The Three Examples

You trust in...

But...

Verse

Your wealth

Hashem produced it — and guards it

ה' מוריש ומעשיר, משפיל אף מרומם; and for the oleh laregel: ולא יחמוד איש את ארצך (Hashem guards your property while you're away)

Your strength / status

Hashem gave it

וזכרת את ה' אלהיך כי הוא הנותן לך כח לעשות חיל

Another person (your employer of thirty years)

Hashem created him, gives him life, and gives him his ability

כי יוצר הכל הוא; אשר בידו נפש כל חי; והעושר והכבוד מלפניך

הבוטח בגורמים האלו... הרי הוא סומך בטחונו עליו יתעלה — בין אם הבין... בין לא ידעו וכפר בו Whether you understand it, don't understand it, or even deny it — the theoretical truth is you are trusting in Hashem.

The Suckling Analogy (דומה לנער קטן)

One who doesn't grasp this is like a baby at his mother's breast:

  1. The infant — all he knows is his mother's milk; as far as he's concerned, his mother is the source of everything
  2. The child (~5) — matures a little: mother provides the food, but from father's money
  3. The teenager — father's money comes from his employer
  4. The mature adult — the employer, and everything, traces back to Hashem: וכן הלאה עד שיבין ויתפוס שהכל מאת השם יתברך

David HaMelech said it in his maturity about his own infancy:

כי אתה גוחי מבטן, מבטיחי על שדי אמי; עליך השלכתי מרחם, מבטן אמי אלי אתה You drew me from the womb; You were my security ON MY MOTHER'S BREASTS — upon You I was cast from the womb. As a suckling he couldn't picture it; grown, he recognized the truth: it was Hashem all along.

Extending It to the Animals — The Verses Finally Explained

Now those puzzling pesukim make sense. When do ravens or lions "call to Hashem"?

  • נותן לבהמה לחמה, לבני עורב אשר יקראו — He gives the beast its bread, the young ravens that call
  • הכפירים שואגים לטרף — ולבקש מאל אכלם — the lions roar for prey, seeking their food from God
  • ידע שור קונהו וחמור אבוס בעליו — the ox knows its owner, the donkey its master's trough

The animal relies on whoever tends and feeds it (the sheep trusts the farmer) — but the one it relies on depends on Hashem: הגורם שבו בוטחים הם תלוי בך, השם יתברך — והם לא יודעים זאת. So כולם אליך ישברון is literal: ALL — humans and animals — trust in Hashem, most without knowing it.

הבן את הדבר והשיגהו בשכלך, כי הוא מהפירושים היפים והנכונים — "Understand this and grasp it with your mind, for it is one of the beautiful and correct interpretations."

The Resolution (Answer 2)

  • "עיני כל אליך ישברו" = literally everyone (and everything): all trust something, and that something traces back to Hashem — indirect, unknowing trust
  • The praise-verses = those who trust Hashem directly and knowingly — cutting out the intermediary and placing trust straight from themselves to Hakadosh Baruch Hu

CLOSING SECTION — Which Jobs Make Bitachon Easier or Harder

One final teaching on Category 3. Whenever working to provide the basics, one is obligated in complete bitachon:

חובה מוטלת על הבוטח בהשם יתברך שבהשגת הצרכים האלו... ביטחון שלם בהשם — trust in Hashem and not in the job you're doing to get these things.

But the job itself affects how easy bitachon is:

Three Kinds of Livelihood

Type

Examples

Bitachon

High-chance occupations

דייג בים או צייד בשדה — fisherman, hunter; raising animals, farming, seafaring

Strengthens/eases bitachon — the hashgacha is out in the open; success is visibly chance-dependent. Yaakov (posing as Esav, asked how he returned so fast): כי הקרה ה' אלהיך לפני — "Hashem made it happen before me"

Human-dependent occupations

מי שתלוי פרנסתו במלך — one whose livelihood hangs on a king (or a single patron/employer)

Weakens/hardens bitachon — everything depends on finding favor in one person's eyes. Hence David: אשרי הגבר אשר שם ה' מבטחו ולא פנה אל רהבים — happy is he who trusts Hashem and did not turn to the powerful

In between

ההתעסקות במסחר — general commerce, buying and selling

The middle path between the two

Contemporary Application (Rabbi Kada)

  • Modern "hunters": careers of near-total chance — an actor, a footballer — very hard to make it; those paths demand enormous bitachon
  • The other extreme: a personal assistant to an extremely wealthy man — everything guaranteed through one human — bitachon is much harder
  • Self-employed vs. employed: the self-employed person, whose finances shift day to day with clients and luck, generally needs (and more easily develops) more bitachon than the salaried employee with a fixed, predictable income

The Crucial Caveats — Trends, Not Rules

אין כוונתי לומר שכל המתפרנס באמצעות בני אדם או הון אי אפשר שיהיה לו ביטחון... ובכל זאת יש לפעמים ביטחון מלא גם במצב כזה

  • Working for a king does not preclude bitachon — it's just not easy; reaching it there is a very high level:
  • Ovadiah rose to the highest bitachon while serving in the court of Achav
  • David HaMelech, with all his wealth, still said: כי ממך הכל ומידך נתנו לך — "everything is from You, and from Your hand we have given You"; העושר והכבוד מלפניך
  • And Iyov: אם שמתי זהב כסלי ולכתם אמרתי מבטחי — "if I made gold my confidence..." — not to trust one's own fortune while possessing it is a great level
  • Conversely, not every hunter or fisherman reaches the bitachon of Yaakov and Yitzchak — some leave it all to randomness and chance. The point: in the former it's easier, in the latter harder — be cognizant of which kind of livelihood you're in.

End of Category Three.

Summary

Question 1 — Hashem Feeds Even the Animals; Why Bitachon?

Because of free will: obedience broadens and eases parnasah (Ekev; כן יתן לידידו שנא); disobedience removes it (the curses; kefirim rashu; Chana; Doeg) or delivers it only through toil and war (Adam; Kayin; King Asa). The Mishnah in Kiddushin says it explicitly: אלא שהרעותי את מעשי וקיפחתי את פרנסתי.

Question 2 — Natural or Praiseworthy? Two Answers

  1. Kol = majority; bitachon is compelled or voluntary. Most people reach bitachon compelled (no atheists in a foxhole — the ruined wealthy; even idolaters, whose service is an intermediary tracing back to Hashem: Yonah's sailors, Mesha of Moav). The praise-verses speak of the voluntary botei'ach — who chooses trust while he still has everything (and within the voluntary: the general feeling most of us have vs. the rare, specific bitachon lived out in every deal and every step — בכל דרכיך דעהו).
  2. Kol = literally all. Everyone (humans and animals) trusts in something — wealth, strength, an employer, an owner — and every one of those traces back to Hashem: indirect, unknowing trust (the suckling analogy: mother → father → employer → Hashem; כי אתה גוחי מבטן). The praise is for trusting Him directly.

Jobs and Bitachon

High-chance livelihoods (hunter, fisherman — today: self-employment, acting, sport) ease bitachon; human-dependent livelihoods (serving a king — today: the fixed salary, the single patron) harden it; commerce is in between. But the trend can always be broken: Ovadiah in Achav's court, David in his wealth.
