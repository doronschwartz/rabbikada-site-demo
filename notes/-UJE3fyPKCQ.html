Topic: The laws of the final meal before Tisha BeAv

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; Eikhah

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Wednesday 19 July 2023

The halacha

  • The laws of Seudah HaMafseket apply only to a meal eaten after midday that is the final meal before the fast; earlier meals that day are unrestricted, provided one can fast properly.
  • Meat and wine are forbidden at this meal by the law of the Gemara; the Nine Days are only custom. Yemenites, without that custom, eat meat until this meal. Even a pregnant or nursing woman told to eat meat in the Nine Days must not at this meal.
  • The Shulhan Arukh records the custom not to eat fish either.
  • Only one cooked dish (tavshil), unlike Erev Yom Kippur. A dish normally eaten as one, though made of several cooked foods, counts as one: the Syrian custom is majaddara, rice and lentils with onion. Lentils are round, a sign of mourning: life comes round.
  • The meal is eaten in solitude, "let him sit alone and be silent" (Eikhah), so even three eating together do not make a zimmun.
  • It is customary, not required, to sit on the floor; the elderly or those unable may sit normally.

In one line

The final meal before Tisha BeAv is one simple dish without meat, wine or fish, eaten alone, without zimmun, and customarily on the floor.
