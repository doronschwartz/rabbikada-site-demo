Topic: Kashering a utensil according to its majority use (Rov Tashmisho)

Sources: The Rishonim; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Tuesday 25 March 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A key question is how we view a utensil's use. A Kiddush cup is used all year with cold wine or grape juice, so anything absorbed is cold and washing alone would suffice.
  • If one knows it was occasionally used for something hot with hames, such as soup, one could argue it needs full Hagalah.
  • Most Rishonim hold we follow Rov Tashmisho, the majority of its use: if it is mostly used in a way needing no kashering, or a lower level, that level suffices. Other Rishonim say one hot use requires kashering.
  • The Shulhan Arukh follows the lenient view, as do Hakham Ovadiah and Hakham Ben Sion. Sepharadim may use their year-round Kiddush cup on Pesah after washing it, even if it was used with hot hames once or more.
  • Likewise a knife normally needs Hagalah. Even if it was once stuck into meat on a barbecue (dry heat, which would need Libbun), the Shulhan Arukh rules Hagalah suffices, since its typical use is heat with liquid.
  • The Rema rules for Ashkenazim that even one use at the more severe level requires the full process, which is why Ashkenazim keep a separate Kiddush cup for Pesah.

Sources quoted

  • The Rishonim: majority view follows Rov Tashmisho; a minority requires kashering after any single hot use.
  • Shulhan Arukh: we go after the majority of a utensil's use.
  • The Rema: for Ashkenazim, even one severe use determines the kashering.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph and Hakham Ben Sion Abba Shaul: rule like the Shulhan Arukh.

In one line

For Sepharadim a utensil is kashered according to its usual use, so a Kiddush cup used with cold wine needs only washing for Pesah.
