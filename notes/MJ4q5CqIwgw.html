Topic: The duty to study Torah daily and how much Torah a father must teach his son

Sources: Rambam, Hilchot Talmud Torah 1:8; Gemara Kiddushin; Rashi; Shulhan Arukh; the Ramah (Rabbi Meir HaLevi Abulafia)

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Thursday 29 April 2021

The halacha

  • Shavuot has five names: Hag HaShavuot (Parashat Reeh), Hag HaKasir (Mishpatim), Yom HaBikkurim (Pinehas), and from the Hakhamim, Aseret and Zeman Matan Toratenu. The Gemara dates the giving of the Torah to 6 Sivan, so the coming weeks are devoted to the laws of Talmud Torah.
  • Rambam: every Jewish male must study Torah, poor or rich, healthy or not, young or old, even one married with young children, and must fix a time each morning and evening, even if short.
  • A father must teach his son Torah. The Gemara in Kiddushin limits this to Torah, not Mishnah or Gemara.
  • Rashi: Torah means the five books. Rambam: all 24 books of Tanakh, and a father who cannot teach must hire a teacher. The Shulhan Arukh follows the Rambam.
  • The Ramah, in early 13th-century Spain, held that the limit applies only to a father without time or means; one who has them should see that his son learns Mishnah and Gemara too. The Shulhan Arukh rules this way as well.

Sources quoted

  • Rambam, Hilchot Talmud Torah 1:8: every Jewish male must fix times for Torah, morning and evening.
  • Gemara Kiddushin: the father's duty is Torah, not Mishnah or Gemara.
  • Rashi and Rambam: the five books, or all of Tanakh.
  • The Ramah: with time or means, Mishnah and Gemara as well.

In one line

A father must teach (or pay for) his son's study of all Tanakh, and beyond it Mishnah and Gemara if he has the time or means.
