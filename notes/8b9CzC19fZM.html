Topic: The Gemara's conclusion that the child is kasher, and the Rishonim on marrying a Kohen

Sources: Gemara Kiddushin; the Rif (Rabbi Yishak Alfasi) and the Geonim; the Rambam; the Rosh; the Ramban

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 1 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Mid-discussion the Gemara says that even according to the view that the child is kasher, the child is blemished (pagum), mainly understood to mean that a daughter may not marry a Kohen.
  • The Gemara concludes "hilkheta": the child of a Jewish woman and a non-Jewish man is kasher and may marry a Jew. All accept this.
  • Unresolved: does "kasher" mean entirely, so a daughter may even marry a Kohen, or kasher with the earlier caveat that she may not?
  • The Rif, about a thousand years ago, records that the Geonim were divided on this, and leaves it as a doubt.
  • The Rambam: the child is completely kasher, with no caveat about a Kohen.
  • The Rosh, Rabbenu Asher: the opposite, the daughter may not marry a Kohen, since the Gemara relied on what it said earlier.
  • So the three pillars of the Shulhan Arukh hold three views. The ruling comes next time.
  • The restriction concerns a daughter, since a Kohen's marriage restrictions apply to males. The Ramban adds that a son's daughter may not marry a Kohen either; this view is not followed at all.

In one line

The Gemara rules the child kasher, but whether a daughter may marry a Kohen is debated: permitted by the Rambam, forbidden by the Rosh, doubtful for the Rif.
