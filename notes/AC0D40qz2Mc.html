Topic: The berakhah on seeing an outstanding Torah sage, and why many hesitate to say it

Sources: The Gemara; Rambam; the Rif; the Rosh; Shulhan Arukh; the Kaf HaHayim; Rav Hayim Palagi; the Ben Ish Hai; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Monday 27 January 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • One who sees the sages of Israel blesses Hashem "shehalak mehokhmato lireav", who has shared His wisdom with those who fear Him. The Rambam, Rif, Rosh and Shulhan Arukh record it as a full berakhah.
  • Over the last 500 years the Aharonim raised four main doubts. First, the text: the Gemara has "halak", the Rambam "natan", so the Kaf HaHayim says not to say Hashem's name. This is weak, since either text works.
  • Second, the most cited: the Rishonim define the sage as outstanding in his generation, but who exactly is that? Out of doubt some poskim say to omit Hashem's name.
  • Third, the poskim write elsewhere that we have no true hakham today, for example to annul vows alone, which is why three are used; by extension, some say, nor for this berakhah.
  • Fourth, one of the Aharonim notes "to those who fear Him": wisdom is not enough without fine character and perfection, and such a person is hard to identify and to find.
  • So Rav Hayim Palagi of Turkey, the Ben Ish Hai, the Kaf HaHayim and others were sceptical about the full berakhah today.
  • Yet it is in the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh and a shame to lose, so many Ashkenazi and Sepharadi poskim say it in full on meeting someone truly exceptional in intellect and character. Hakham Ovadiah describes one famed for Torah and fear of Heaven who rules for all Israel. The community in Vienna said it on the Rogatchover, Rabbi Yoseph Rosen.
  • Fame and many followers, even as a Hasidic leader with thousands, are not the criterion.

In one line

On seeing a sage outstanding in his generation in both wisdom and character, one may say the full berakhah, but popularity alone does not qualify.
