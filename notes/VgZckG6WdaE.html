Topic: The Torah prohibition of Hadash, new grain

Sources: The Torah, Parashat Emor; Mishnah Kiddushin

Daily Halacha: Sefirat HaOmer, Tuesday 27 April 2021

The halacha

  • Parashat Emor forbids eating Hadash from the five grains (wheat, barley, spelt, oats, rye). He says people are unaware of it, and Sepharadim need to be especially careful.
  • Grain that took root after Pesah may not be eaten until the 16th of Nisan of the following year has passed; outside Israel one waits until the third day of Pesah passes.
  • Grain planted and rooted before Pesah (for example in January) becomes permitted after the second day of Pesah and is called Yashan, old.
  • The reason resembles saying a Berakhah before eating: grain was the main sustenance, so first the Korban Omer from the new barley was offered on the second day of Pesah, and that permitted the new grain.
  • The prohibition applies outside Israel too, and to grain grown by a non-Jew as well as by a Jew. It is a biblical prohibition still in force, relevant when buying flour, bread and biscuits.
  • The practical application in today's market follows tomorrow.

Sources quoted

In one line

Grain that took root after Pesah is biblically forbidden until the next Pesah, even outside Israel and even if grown by a non-Jew.
