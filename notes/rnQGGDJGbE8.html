Topic: Requests from organisations, giving "what he lacks", and priorities in sedakah

Sources: The Torah ("what he lacks"); the halacha of precedence in sedakah

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Monday 28 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The obligation when asked (if one has not given 20%) is towards a poor person, whom the Torah says to give what he lacks. It does not extend to organisations collecting for buildings or other worthy purposes.
  • One giving 10% may give part of it to such organisations, but not giving them breaches nothing. The same applies to letters in the post and charity funds: wonderful and to be encouraged, but not obligatory.
  • Strictly, "what he lacks" means giving, up to one's 10%, until the poor person no longer lacks food, clothes and the like.
  • In practice this does not apply when he has asked others too, or when the community shares the burden. Only one-to-one, with no one else to help, would one have to give that much.
  • When choosing where to give: food takes precedence over clothes.
  • A woman takes precedence over a man, even if she asks for clothes and he for food, since it is always harder for a woman to ask.
  • Beyond that there is an order: poor relatives first, then one's own city, and so on.

In one line

Organisations are optional, the poor who ask are not, and in allocating sedakah food comes before clothes and a woman before a man.
