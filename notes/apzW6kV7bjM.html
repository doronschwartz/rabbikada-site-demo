Topic: Learning Siman 317:1 inside: how long a knot tied on Shabbat may last for Ashkenazim and Sefaradim, and knots for a misvah Source: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Siman 317, seif 1, with the Rema, the Mishnah Berurah and the Biur Halacha Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada in his Monday-night series on the Melakhot, Monday 25 February 2019 (Kosher and Mattir, Part 3)

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Review

Kosher in the Mishkan was tying the nets for catching the hilazon. Untying, mattir, is symmetrical: whatever the status of tying a knot, untying it has the same status.

The Rif and the Rambam: a Torah prohibition needs two conditions, permanent and professional. Asked what permanent means, a participant answered correctly: you do not have to intend it to last forever; it is a knot you have no intention of untying, which will normally last. One condition makes it rabbinic. Neither, a simple knot that is not permanent, is permitted.

Within the Rambam there are two readings. The plain reading, followed by the Mishnah Berurah and the Biur Halacha: a simple knot tied for a few weeks or months is permitted, as long as it is not permanent. The Shulhan Arukh HaRav (the Baal HaTanya): a simple knot for seven days or more is already rabbinic.

Rashi and the Rosh: permanent is Torah-level; a medium time is rabbinic; a short time is permitted. What is "medium"? Two opinions: more than 24 hours, or more than seven days.

"What I'm saying is for sure, it's definitely not 24 hours" according to the Rambam. Twenty-four hours is only an opinion within Rashi. Even the strict reading of the Rambam goes no lower than seven days.

Seif 1 inside

The Shulhan Arukh: one is liable only for a knot that is permanent and professional, like the knots of camel drivers, sailors, and the straps cobblers tie inside a shoe. The Mishnah Berurah explains: - A permanent knot need not be very tight. Even one you could undo with one hand is permanent if it will not come undone by itself and you intend it to stay. - The camel driver puts a ring-shaped strap through the camel's nose once and for good; each time he parks, he ties another strap to that ring and later unties it. The first knot is the professional and permanent one. The sailor's knot on the mast is the same. - Even these are not "forever": shoes are thrown away and ships are broken up. But no one intends to untie them, and that is what makes them permanent.

A knot normally left for good. The Taz holds that intending to untie it is enough, even if such knots are normally left. The Biur Halacha disagrees: it depends on how such knots are normally treated, and that is the ruling.

The Rema brings those who disagree with the Shulhan Arukh: any permanent knot is a Torah prohibition, as Rashi and the Rosh hold. The Mishnah Berurah identifies a second difference: a simple knot tied for a few weeks is permitted according to the Shulhan Arukh, but forbidden according to Rashi and the Rema. The Rema then gives two opinions within Rashi: a knot not untied the same day has some permanence; or up to seven days is still not permanent. The Mishnah Berurah adds a third, Rabbenu Yeruham, who says three days.

"The same day" does not literally mean the same calendar day. Laces tied on Friday night and untied on Shabbat afternoon are fine, since it is less than 24 hours.

How long is too long even for the Rambam? Someone asked about intending to untie a knot in a year. The Beit Yoseph brings Rabbenu Yeruham that six months or a year may already count as permanent.

Untying a knot that will not come undone. If you were permitted to untie a knot but it is stuck, you may tear the string at the knot, but not in public. A knot that formed by accident may be untied even if very tight, because only an intended knot has the status of a knot.

Examples. The Shulhan Arukh lists a torn strap, a broken rope, a bucket rope or an animal's reins retied: these are not professional but are normally permanent, so rabbinic. And a professional knot for five minutes is also rabbinic, even if you will untie it the same day, according to the Rif and the Rambam, though Rashi and the Rosh would permit it.

Practical rulings

Ashkenazim. The Rema rules like the Rambam and also like Rashi. So a professional knot, such as a double knot, is always forbidden. A simple knot is permitted in the first instance only with intention to untie it within 24 hours; where there is a need, such as a bandage that must stay on for three days, within seven days. The Rabbi showed this from the way the Rema quotes only the stricter opinions.

Sefaradim. - Those who follow the Ben Ish Hai rule like one opinion in the Rema, and untie within 24 hours. - Following the plain reading of the Shulhan Arukh, as most Aharonim read the Rambam, a simple knot may stay for a few months. "What the Beit Yoseph in Cairo thought when he wrote this is that you're allowed to tie my shoelaces for three months." - Hazon Ovadia rules seven days. The Rabbi found it hard to see why he limits it to seven days, since his own reasoning seems to allow longer.

His conclusion. Seven days is certainly fine for Sefaradim, since it satisfies both readings of the Rambam. Where there is a real need, there is room to be lenient for longer, as long as the knot is not professional and not permanent, which means not more than about six months. This will matter for a festival Sefer Torah, tied after Yom Tov and not opened until the next one.

Knots for a misvah

The Shulhan Arukh allows tying, for a misvah purpose, a knot that is not permanent, for example tying cords together to measure a mikveh that was not measured before Shabbat. Which knot? It must be a professional knot, otherwise it would be permitted anyway. The Hakhamim permitted a rabbinic prohibition for a misvah. The Mishnah Berurah adds that this is only when there is no alternative; if you can, use a simple knot. In this case, he notes, there is no difference between one day and a few weeks.

The other way round. May one tie a permanent simple knot for a misvah? The Rabbi said there is no reason to distinguish, since both are rabbinic. So for a misvah, both a professional knot that is not permanent and a simple knot that is permanent are permitted. Some Rishonim read the Gemara differently, but for those who follow the Shulhan Arukh, this holds. Again, this will matter for the Sefer Torah.

Double knots

The Rema quotes the Shiltei HaGibborim: since we do not know what a professional knot is, one should not tie a double knot even if it will be untied the same day. Even Ashkenazim, who allow a simple knot for 24 hours, never tie a double knot, because if it is a professional knot it is forbidden even for ten minutes. The Mishnah Berurah adds that a single overhand knot at the end of a thread is also treated as a double knot.

A bow is not a knot at all and may be tied even for years. The Rabbi left bows, and whether Sefaradim follow the Shiltei HaGibborim, for the next class, followed by practical cases: shoelaces, the Sefer Torah, rubbish bags and ties.

Practical summary

  • Untying follows tying in every case.
  • For Ashkenazim, a simple knot is permitted only with intention to untie within 24 hours, or seven days in case of need; double knots are forbidden.
  • For Sefaradim following the Shulhan Arukh, seven days is certainly permitted, and in real need longer, if the knot is not professional and not permanent. Followers of the Ben Ish Hai untie within 24 hours.
  • A knot that is normally left permanently is forbidden even if you intend to untie it.
  • For a misvah, with no alternative, a rabbinic knot is permitted.
  • An accidental knot may be untied, and a permitted knot that is stuck may be torn at the knot, privately.
