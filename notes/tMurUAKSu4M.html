The halacha

  • Tu BiShvat (Tet 9, Vav 6: the 15th of Shevat) is not really about eating fruit. It is a purely halachic date with three ramifications, two still relevant.
  • Orlah: a tree's fruit is forbidden for its first three years and permitted only in Yerushalayim in the fourth. Tu BiShvat starts the tree's new year, so a tree planted three and a half years ago, in the summer, may be eaten from on Tu BiShvat as the start of its fifth year.
  • Tithes, still taken in Israel: produce from before Tu BiShvat may not tithe produce from after it, or the reverse.
  • Bikkurim, the first fruits of the seven species brought to the Temple: one year's fruit may not be brought for another, with Tu BiShvat dividing the years. Without the Temple this no longer applies.
  • The custom of eating fruit evolved over the last three to four centuries; the first mention he knows is the Magen Avraham, an Ashkenazi codifier of about 400 years ago. The number 15 came later and does not matter.
  • The Talmud holds a person accountable for produce he could have eaten and did not, since eating God's food is an appreciation of HaKadosh Barukh Hu.
  • It is a nice custom, not a halachic requirement, so one need not go hunting for exotic fruits; for most people Tu BiShvat has no halachic significance unless they planted a tree or tithe in Israel.

Sources quoted

  • The Torah: Orlah, tithes and Bikkurim.
  • The Magen Avraham: the custom to eat much fruit.
  • The Talmud: accountability for permitted produce one did not eat.

In one line

Tu BiShvat is the new year for trees for Orlah, tithes and Bikkurim, and eating fruit is a pleasant custom kept in balance.
