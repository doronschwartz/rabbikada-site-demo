Topic: Why hames is not nullified in 60, and how Sepharadim and Ashkenazim extend that stringency differently

Sources: The Torah and the Hakhamim; the Rishonim; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Monday 28 March 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • During the year, non-kosher food that falls into kosher food is nullified in 60 (Bittul BeShishim): less than a sixtieth gives no taste, so the whole may be eaten.
  • On Pesah the Hakhamim ruled that even a drop of hames forbids the whole mixture. From the Torah the usual nullification applies.
  • Their reason: hames is a Davar SheYesh Lo Mattirin, something that becomes permitted after Pesah, so one can simply wait. Non-kosher food would never become permitted, so nullification is needed for it.
  • Broadly, the Sepharadi Rishonim kept this stringency to its own case, while many Ashkenazi Rishonim deduced from it that one should be strict on Pesah in other matters too. Four examples follow over two days.
  • First example: Notein Taam LiFgam. If milk food was mistakenly cooked in a clean meat pot unused for 24 hours, the food is permitted, since the taste expelled after 24 hours is no longer a good taste. One does not do this in the first instance.
  • On Pesah, if one mistakenly cooked Pesah food in a clean hames pot unused for a week or more, the Shulhan Arukh permits the food, as during the year.
  • The Rema, for Ashkenazim, forbids it: just as hames is not nullified in 60 on Pesah, even a spoiled taste after 24 hours is forbidden.

Sources quoted

  • The Hakhamim: no nullification of hames on Pesah, because it will become permitted.
  • Shulhan Arukh: Notein Taam LiFgam is permitted on Pesah.
  • The Rema: it is forbidden on Pesah.

In one line

The rabbinic rule that hames is not nullified stays narrow for Sepharadim, while Ashkenazim extend it, as with food cooked in an unused hames pot.
