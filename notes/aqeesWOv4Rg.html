Topic: Why the Seder recalls both slavery and redemption, and why the Hakhamim gathered at Rabbi Akiva's home

Sources: The Haggadah; Eikhah; Rabbi Akiva; Gemara Berakhot 60b, Sanhedrin 101a and Makkot 24

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Monday 22 April 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Mah Nishtanah and Avadim Hayinu summarise the whole evening. The child's four questions are one: is this night about suffering or redemption? Massah is both the bread of haste and the bread of affliction; we eat maror yet drink four cups and recline.
  • The answer: both. We were slaves and HaShem took us out, so we enact freedom and recall suffering, hence the paradox of the night.
  • Why talk of the slavery at all? Simply, only one who sat in darkness appreciates light, as the pasuk in Eikhah says.
  • More deeply, with full emunah we understand that exile and suffering are themselves part of the redemption. As Rabbi Akiva said, everything God does is for the best.
  • So we eat massah (freedom), then maror (bitterness), and then Korekh, eating them together: in the end we will see they are one.
  • The five Hakhamim gathered in Bnei Berak because Rabbi Akiva lived there. Stories in Berakhot 60b, Sanhedrin 101a and Makkot show him as the one who saw light within darkness.
  • After the destruction, and with the hopes placed in Bar Kokhba, whom Rabbi Akiva believed was the Mashiah, extinguished, the Hakhamim came to him for inspiration.
  • Pesah is about the unity of God, "I and not an angel, I and not a seraph". As a nation in a difficult time we keep faith that one day we will have our Korekh, seeing the bitterness as part of the sweetness.

In one line

The Seder holds suffering and redemption together, and like Korekh, faith teaches that the bitterness is ultimately part of the liberation.
