Topic: The origins of a boy's first haircut ceremony, and holding it on Hol HaMoed

Sources: The Torah (Yaakov and Esav; orlah); the Radbaz; Rav Hayim Vital (the Arizal); the Hatam Sofer; the Keter Shem Tov; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Wednesday 22 October 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Upsherin is Yiddish for a haircut. Halakah comes from halak, smooth, as Yaakov said: Esav is hairy and I am smooth.
  • To his knowledge the first source is the Radbaz (Rabbi David ben Zimra of Egypt, about 450 years ago), discussing a vow, who mentions the custom of a first haircut at the grave of Shemuel HaNavi, with no age given.
  • Rav Hayim Vital heard reliable testimony that the Arizal took his son to Meron, to the grave of Rabbi Shimon bar Yohai, for his first haircut with a seudah, again with no age.
  • The idea is hinukh: teaching the child the misvah of peot harosh by cutting the hair and leaving the corners, done at a sadik's grave for a good influence.
  • The Hatam Sofer, about 200 years ago, links age three to orlah: as fruit is forbidden for three years, the child's hair is left for three years. Many later sources mention no age, and the Keter Shem Tov saw the minhag in Israel at four, perhaps tied to the fourth year of fruit.
  • It is a recent minhag, and age three more recent still. Communities with the custom continue it; to his knowledge Sepharadim from Syria and Iraq did not have it. It is nice but not obligatory, and the timing is flexible.
  • The Shulhan Arukh permits children's haircuts on Hol HaMoed, since the decree concerns adults entering the festival unkempt. The poskim write one may even postpone the halakah to Hol HaMoed to increase the simhah of the festival.

In one line

A child's first haircut may be held on Hol HaMoed, and may even be postponed until then to add to the simhah of the festival.
