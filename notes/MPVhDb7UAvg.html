Topic: The custom of sitting low on Tisha BeAv and the Spanish and Portuguese custom of changing seats

Sources: The Gemara; the Raviah (Rabbi Eliezer ben Yoel HaLevi); Shulhan Arukh; Masekhet Soferim; the Keter Shem Tov (Gaguine)

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Wednesday 30 July 2025

The halacha

  • By the Gemara a mourner sits very low, near the floor, but the Gemara says nothing of this for Tisha BeAv.
  • The Raviah, Rabbi Eliezer ben Yoel HaLevi of Germany, around the 12th century, recorded the custom to sit on the floor like a mourner in synagogue and at home. Ashkenazi Rishonim brought it, and it spread through France, Germany and Ashkenazi lands.
  • What is forbidden on Tisha BeAv (washing, laundry, eating and drinking, Torah study, greeting) is forbidden all 24 hours. A custom can be limited in time, so this custom lasted only until the afternoon.
  • The Shulhan Arukh records sitting low on the night and day "until Tefillat Minhah", which many understand as until midday. That is the Ashkenazi practice and that of most Sepharadim; after midday we sit on normal chairs.
  • It is already found in Masekhet Soferim (7th or 8th century), brought by the Keter Shem Tov of Gaguine, with two customs: sitting on the ground, or changing one's usual place in the synagogue.
  • The Spanish and Portuguese communities of London and Amsterdam, unlike the Shulhan Arukh, do not sit on the ground but change their seats. He knows it only from them and would be interested to hear of others.
  • As it is a custom, an elderly person or one who finds sitting low difficult should simply sit in a different place, which suffices.

In one line

Most sit low on Tisha BeAv until midday, while the Spanish and Portuguese follow the ancient alternative of changing their seats.
