Topic: Washing with hot and cold water on Shabbat

Sources: The Hakhamim; Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Wednesday 11 June 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A common question, especially outside Israel when two days of Yom Tov run into Shabbat and one cannot shower from Wednesday afternoon until after Shabbat. Shabbat comes first.
  • One may not wash the entire body in hot water on Shabbat, by bath or shower, even if the water was heated before Shabbat and the boiler is off. People used to bathe in hot water on Shabbat and claim it was heated beforehand, so the Hakhamim made a comprehensive ban on the bathhouse and on bathing in hot water.
  • Separately, if opening the tap lets cold water into a boiler, there may be a Torah problem of heating or cooking the water. This concerns a manual tap.
  • Water heated before Shabbat may be used to wash one's face, hands and feet (panav, yadav veraglav), and no more. So one may open the hot tap, if the boiler is off and no new water is drawn in to be heated, and wash one's face and hands.
  • Water heated on Shabbat may not be used at all.
  • The Hakhamim did not forbid cold water. The Sepharadi minhag is that a manual cold shower on Shabbat is completely permitted. Ashkenazim generally did not, because of the laws of drying oneself, to be discussed.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein ruled, and this is accepted, that even Ashkenazim may take a full cold manual shower on Shabbat when there is a strong need, such as an extremely hot day causing real discomfort.
  • Yom Tov is different and comes next.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: Ashkenazim may take a cold shower on Shabbat when there is a strong need.

In one line

On Shabbat hot water heated beforehand is for face, hands and feet only, while a manual cold shower is permitted for Sepharadim and for Ashkenazim in need.
