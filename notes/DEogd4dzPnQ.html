Topic: Why Hallah is no longer given to the Kohen, and how to dispose of it

Sources: The Torah; Shulhan Arukh; Hazon Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Hallah, Wednesday 6 July 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Recap: Sepharadim say "lehafrish Hallah terumah"; Ashkenazim say "lehafrish Hallah" or "terumah", and some "lehafrish Hallah min haissah". Sepharadim take any small amount, some Ashkenazim a kezayit.
  • The Torah says to give Hallah to the Kohen, but it may be eaten only in purity. Today everyone, Kohanim included, is tamei through contact with the dead, directly or indirectly, and there is no parah adumah to purify, so Kohanim may not eat it.
  • Shulhan Arukh therefore says to burn it.
  • But Hallah is forbidden to eat, so burning it in an oven makes the oven absorb its taste (beliot). One may wrap it in silver foil and then burn it.
  • This takes time for so small a piece, so Hazon Ovadiah, and Ashkenazi poskim too, write that one may wrap it in a bag and throw it away.
  • This is not zilzul misvah: burning is only to make sure no one eats it, the bin does the same, and the misvah is already done. This is what most people do today.
  • If burning, ensure it does not make the oven treif.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: Hallah is burnt today.
  • Hazon Ovadiah: it may be bagged and thrown away.

In one line

Since Kohanim cannot eat Hallah today, it is wrapped and burnt or, as most do, bagged and thrown away.
