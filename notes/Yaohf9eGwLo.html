Topic: Why we wash hands without a berakhah at Urhas

Sources: The Rambam; Rashi; Tosafot; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; the Hida

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Thursday 23 March 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Liquids, chiefly water, transmit impurity, so in Temple times one washed hands before eating food dipped in liquid, just as for bread.
  • Most Rishonim, the Rambam, Rashi and others, hold this still applies today, with a berakhah like bread. Tosafot held it applied only in Temple times.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules one must wash before eating vegetables dipped in liquid, but without a berakhah because of Tosafot's view.
  • The Rema writes that many are lenient and rely on Tosafot, so many do not wash for dipped vegetables during the year. On Pesah night everyone washes, and many poskim learn from this that one should do so all year.
  • For Sepharadim, Urhas is nothing unique to Pesah or about arousing the children's curiosity; it is the year-round halacha, without a berakhah.
  • Some Ashkenazi poskim see it as unique to the Seder, recalling the Temple like the Afikoman and the egg, or doing something different to arouse curiosity.
  • Either way, everyone washes without a berakhah. Why we eat a vegetable at all follows tomorrow.

Sources quoted

  • The Rambam and Rashi: washing for dipped food applies today.
  • Tosafot: only in Temple times.
  • Shulhan Arukh: wash without a berakhah; the Rema: many are lenient during the year.
  • The Hida: Urhas is the only one of the fifteen simanim with a vav ("and wash"), showing the washing continues all year.

In one line

Urhas is the year-round halacha of washing before dipped food, done without a berakhah because of Tosafot, and everyone does it on Seder night.
