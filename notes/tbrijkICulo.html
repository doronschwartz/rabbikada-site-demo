Topic: The reasons for the misvah: gratitude, or the chain of tradition

Sources: The Talmud; the Abarbanel; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Honouring Parents, Thursday 7 January 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Most commentators say the misvah trains hakarat hatov, gratitude. The ego likes to see itself as self-sufficient, and admitting what parents gave us, our very existence, can be hard, especially for teenagers forming their identity.
  • The Talmud equates honouring parents with honouring HaShem. Gratitude to parents, traced back, leads to the Source of all existence; denying it leads to denying HaShem.
  • The Abarbanel, a 15th-century Portuguese statesman, says the basis is emunah: our faith is the Mesorah passed down by parents and grandparents back to Sinai, and we can only accept it if we revere those who hand it to us.
  • This explains why the misvah sits among the first five commandments, between man and HaShem. On the gratitude reason too, gratitude to parents leads to gratitude to HaShem.
  • A reminder to parents: think "upwards" about what we owe our parents, not "downwards" about what our children owe us. The Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh forbid a parent to be too exacting about his honour; better to create a home where children want to honour us.

In one line

Honouring parents builds gratitude and keeps the chain of tradition alive, and parents should not be too demanding of it.
