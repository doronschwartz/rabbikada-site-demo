Topic: What counts as Hames from the Torah and from the Rabbis

Sources: The Torah ("for seven days Hames shall not be eaten"; "any leavening you shall not eat")

Daily Halacha: Pesah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Torah forbids eating Hames for seven days, and "any leavening agent". So Hames includes something fermented on its own, fermented by an outside agent, or used to ferment other things.
  • Most food prohibitions carry lashes (Malkot); eating Hames on Pesah carries Karet.
  • From midday on Erev Pesah, when the Korban Pesah was offered, there is a separate Torah prohibition on eating Hames, still in force, but without Karet.
  • Since midday could be uncertain on a cloudy day without clocks, the Hakhamim forbade eating Hames two hours earlier, around 11 am, depending on the year.
  • Hames comes only from the five grains: wheat, barley, spelt, rye and oats. Rice, potatoes and the like are not Hames, whatever one's custom.
  • Hames Nukshah has begun to ferment without finishing and would be eaten only in great hunger, such as the flour and water glue of scribes. It is forbidden rabbinically, both to eat and to own.
  • Unusually, Hames that has spoiled beyond human use is still Torah Hames, since it can still leaven. Only once unfit even for a dog does it lose the status of Hames.

In one line

Hames is fermented produce of the five grains, forbidden with Karet on Pesah, from midday on Erev Pesah by the Torah and earlier by the Rabbis, until it is unfit even for a dog.
