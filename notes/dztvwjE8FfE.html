Topic: The historical origin of the second day of Yom Tov, and why there was never a second day of Yom Kippur

Sources: Mishnah and Gemara, Rosh Hashanah; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Tuesday 1 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Our calendar is lunar, and a lunar month is just over 29 and a half days, so months are either full (malei, 30 days) or deficient (haser, 29 days).
  • In the times of the Sanhedrin and the Bet Din HaGadol, Rosh Hodesh was fixed by when the moon was seen. With no instant communication, the communities outside Israel, especially in Bavel, had to be told.
  • At first bonfires on the mountains passed on the message; when that became problematic, messengers went out to the cities of Bavel, only for months that contained a festival.
  • By the seventh day of Pesah the messengers had almost all arrived, yet a second day was kept for it and for Sukkot too. The Gemara explains that, since the start of the festival was in doubt, they did not want to distinguish, and all the festivals were kept for two days.
  • The Rishonim ask why Yom Kippur was never kept for two days, even when there was a real doubt and safek deoraita lehumra would apply. The answer: Elul is almost always haser, and it would be inappropriate to make people fast two days, a danger to life, when one can follow the majority.
  • For the other festivals the Hakhamim did not want to rely on the majority, so that people would not end up doing the wrong thing on the wrong day in the minority of months.

Sources quoted

  • Mishnah and Gemara, Rosh Hashanah: the lunar calendar, sanctifying the month by sighting, and the bonfires and messengers.
  • The Rishonim: the question of why there is no second day of Yom Kippur, answered by the majority of years in which Elul is 29 days.

In one line

The second day arose from doubt over when the month began in the diaspora, but Yom Kippur followed the majority because a two-day fast is too hard and dangerous.
