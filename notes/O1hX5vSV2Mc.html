Topic: What Purim Katan is, why the main Purim is in Adar Sheni, and whether one holds a seudah

Sources: Mishnah Rosh Hashanah, chapter 1; the Gemara; the Mishnah (Adar Rishon and Adar Sheni); Tosafot; the Rambam; the Tur; Shulhan Arukh; the Rama

Daily Halacha: Purim Katan & Adar Rishon, Monday 14 February 2022

The halacha

  • The 14th of Adar Rishon is Purim Katan. Since there are seven leap years in every 19, it usually comes every three years but sometimes after two: 2019, 2022, then 2024, 2027, 2030.
  • It is not Purim Sheni, the local days some cities (Casablanca, Castile and others) fixed for their own miracles.
  • The Tur and the Shulhan Arukh call it simply the 14th of Adar Rishon. The name probably borrows from the Mishnah in Rosh Hashanah, which calls Pesah Sheni "Pesah Katan".
  • The Gemara debates which Adar holds Purim: the one after Shevat, or the one before Nisan. The halacha is Adar Sheni, to place the redemption of Purim next to the redemption of Pesah. Purim is never celebrated fully twice.
  • The Mishnah says the only difference between the two Adars is the Megillah reading and gifts to the poor. Some Rishonim inferred that the seudah applies in both, and Tosafot records a custom to hold one on Purim Katan.
  • The Rambam evidently disagrees, since he holds gifts to the poor far greater than the seudah, so without them there would be no obligation of a seudah.
  • The Shulhan Arukh says only that one does not fast or eulogise. The Rama, for Ashkenazim, says that since there is a dispute, one should feast, for it is always good to be merry; with this he closes the first section of the Shulhan Arukh.

In one line

On Purim Katan there is no Megillah or gifts to the poor, no fasting or eulogies, and according to the Rama a festive meal.
