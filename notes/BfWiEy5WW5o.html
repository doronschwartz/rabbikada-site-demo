Topic: Whether the congregation sits or stands for the first 30 blasts and for the berakhah

Sources: The Torah; the Gemara; the Bet Yoseph; Hakham Ovadiah; the Ben Ish Hai

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Thursday 15 September 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara links "Yom teruah yihyeh lakhem" to "usfartem lakhem" of Sefirat HaOmer: as the Omer is counted standing, so the shofar is heard standing. This is rabbinic with a scriptural support (asmakhta); biblically one fulfils the misvah even seated.
  • The Sepharadi custom, and the original one, is to sit for the first 30, called the "seated blasts" (tekiot meyushav). Since everyone will stand for the Musaf blasts, the Hakhamim did not trouble the sibbur to stand for these.
  • Those Rishonim who blew only ten in Musaf raise a question here, which the Bet Yoseph discusses.
  • Ashkenazim stand anyway, though they still call them the seated blasts.
  • One who will not hear the Musaf blasts, such as someone leaving early or hearing only 30 at home, must stand for the first 30 if able.
  • On the berakhah, Hakham Ovadiah and the Ben Ish Hai rule that the congregation stays seated, so as not to trouble the sibbur; the one blowing stands. The Spanish and Portuguese stand for the berakhah and sit for the 30. Ashkenazim stand for both.

In one line

Ashkenazim stand for the first 30 blasts, Sepharadim sit for them, and anyone not staying for Musaf must stand.
