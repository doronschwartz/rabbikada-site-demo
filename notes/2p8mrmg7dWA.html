Topic: Using maaser money for synagogue misvot, books and membership

Sources: Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the Taz (Rabbi David HaLevi Segal); the poskim

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Thursday 17 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Though maaser was originally for the poor, it may be used for misvot: donating to the synagogue, buying books for it, and, as Hakham Ovadiah rules, a child's education beyond age six, which one is not strictly obligated to pay for.
  • The poskim permit using maaser to buy an aliyah or another synagogue misvah, despite the personal benefit.
  • The Taz, about 400 years ago, explains that the giver has tovat hanaah, the right to choose the recipient; that includes using maaser as he sees fit, just as he benefits from the gratitude of the poor person he chose.
  • Maaser may not be used for what one is obligated in: matanot laevyonim on Purim, one's own arbaah minim, or building one's sukkah, as these are not communal misvot.
  • Books for a synagogue library are fine. Buying books for one's own library while letting anyone borrow them is dubious; some rely on it, but generally it is a problem.
  • Membership depends on the community. Where one is welcome to pray without paying, maaser may be used. Where membership is required for rights such as a bar mitzvah or brit milah there, it is almost an obligation and maaser may not be used.

In one line

Maaser may pay for aliyot and voluntary synagogue giving, but not for personal obligations or membership that is effectively required.
