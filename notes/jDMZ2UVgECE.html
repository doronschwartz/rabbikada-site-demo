Topic: The five Amidot and the Viddui as the core of Yom Kippur

Sources: The Ramban

Daily Halacha: Yom Kippur, Thursday 21 September 2023

The halacha

  • The Hakhamim instituted five Amidot on Yom Kippur, four by day and one at night, each with Viddui. These are the heart of the day; the Selihot and Piyyutim are not the focus.
  • In Temple times the Kohen Gadol confessed for everyone over the goat sent to Azazel. After the destruction, the Hakhamim had each person say Viddui himself.
  • Viddui is said 11 times: eight in the silent and repeated Amidot of the four day prayers, at Arvit, in the Selihot after Arvit, and at Minhah of Erev Yom Kippur, said before the final meal in case one chokes or does not reach Yom Kippur.
  • The ten essential ones are those within an Amidah (Minhah of Erev Yom Kippur, Arvit and the eight by day), corresponding to the ten times the Kohen Gadol pronounced the Shem HaMeforash.
  • Women with children, or the unwell, should at least pray the five Amidot with Viddui. If not, at the very least one Viddui as Yom Kippur begins and one at Neilah.
  • The Ramban held one also confesses as Yom Kippur begins, so Sepharadim say Lekha Eli Teshukati before Kol Nidre and Ashkenazim Tefillah Zakkah, both with words of Viddui. This is an extra.
  • Viddui must be said standing, unless ill or very weak. Bediavad sitting is valid, though some say not. Though Sepharadim may sit for much of the repetition, all should stand for the Viddui with the Hazan.
  • Bowing the head is a nice custom, not compulsory. Striking the heart is the custom of most of Israel, but not of the Yemenites or the Spanish and Portuguese.
  • The core is "Aval anahnu vaavotenu hatanu": Het (unintentional), Avon (intentional) and Pesha (rebellious), from least to most severe.

In one line

The essentials of Yom Kippur are the five Amidot and their Viddui, said standing; at the very least, confess at the start and at Neilah.
