Topic: The custom not to sleep during the day of Rosh Hashanah

Sources: The Rema; Talmud Yerushalmi; Rabbi Yehoshua ibn Shuaib; the Rambam; the Maharam of Rothenburg; the Ben Ish Hai; Hazon Ovadiah; the Arizal

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah, Wednesday 1 September 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Rema records the Ashkenazi custom not to sleep by day on Rosh Hashanah, based on a Yerushalmi quoted by the poskim: one who sleeps at the head of the year, his mazal sleeps. We do not have this text in our editions.
  • Israel is above mazal: one truly connected to HaKadosh Barukh Hu need not be concerned. But not everyone lives with that awareness, and for them the concern applies.
  • The first Rishon to record it seems to be Rabbi Yehoshua ibn Shuaib in fourteenth century Spain: the Rambam says the shofar awakens us from slumber, so sleeping on the Day of Judgment does the opposite. The Maharam of Rothenburg, by contrast, would sleep on Yom Tov day as on any Yom Tov.
  • The custom spread and is recorded by Sepharadi poskim too, up to the Ben Ish Hai and Hazon Ovadiah.
  • It is a custom, and must be balanced: if staying awake will leave one agitated, tired and unable to pray or behave properly later, that must be factored in.
  • Sitting idle is considered like sleep. If one will waste the time rather than spend it in Torah and Tefillah, it is better to sleep.
  • The Arizal slept after midday: the custom applies to the morning only, so after Hasot, the Tefillot and the Tekiot, one may sleep if needed.
  • One need not wake at dawn: the custom concerns putting oneself to sleep by day, not waking after dawn from the night's sleep.

Sources quoted

  • The Rema: records the custom not to sleep on Rosh Hashanah.
  • Talmud Yerushalmi (as quoted by the poskim): one who sleeps on Rosh Hashanah, his mazal will sleep.
  • Rabbi Yehoshua ibn Shuaib, based on the Rambam: the shofar is to wake us from slumber.
  • The Maharam of Rothenburg: slept on Yom Tov day as usual.
  • The Arizal: the custom is only until midday.

In one line

Avoid sleeping on Rosh Hashanah morning if the time is used well, but it is a custom: after midday, or when needed, one may sleep, and one need not rise at dawn.
