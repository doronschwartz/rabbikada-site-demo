Topic: Why no berakhah is said on sedakah and other misvot between people

Sources: The Torah (Birkat HaMazon); the Rishonim; the Rashba

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Wednesday 2 March 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Hakhamim instituted berakhot before misvot such as lulav and shofar; only Birkat HaMazon is from the Torah. Why is there no berakhah before giving sedakah?
  • One explanation in the Rishonim: no berakhah is said on a misvah that depends on another person. The poor person might leave or refuse before the berakhah ends, making it a berakhah in vain.
  • The Rashba gives the principle that misvot between man and his fellow (sedakah, visiting the sick, comforting mourners, honouring parents) have no berakhah, without explaining why.
  • One explanation: "asher kiddeshanu" speaks of God setting us apart, while these are expected of every decent human being, so there is nothing distinctively Jewish to bless on.
  • A second explanation, which he sees as a key principle: these misvot are meant to make us sensitive to others. A berakhah would say "I do this because God commanded me, not because I care about you", turning it into a selfish misvah and detracting from the relationship.
  • The Torah is the source of morality and tells us what is right; once we know it, we do these misvot because we care about the other person.

In one line

Sedakah has no berakhah so that we give out of care for the other person, refining our character, not merely to earn a misvah.
