Topic: The sacrifices as a "pleasant aroma": ritual as the start of inner transformation

Sources: Maaseh Hashem (Rabbi Eliezer Ashkenazi); the Torah; Yeshayahu chapter 1; Rabbi Yisrael Salanter; the Gemara

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Monday 12 August 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Torah repeatedly calls the offerings "Reah Nihoah", a pleasant aroma to Hashem, a strange phrase, as if Hashem needed a fragrance.
  • Rabbi Eliezer Ashkenazi (16th century), in Maaseh Hashem, explains it signals a limitation: lest people think offering a sacrifice achieves atonement, the Torah calls it only an aroma, which always tells of what is yet to come.
  • So the Temple service is not the ultimate worship but a foretaste, a medium to stimulate people's first steps toward inner transformation.
  • Hence the prophets castigate those who stop at the sacrifices. In the Haftarah of Shabbat Hazon Yeshayahu asks, "Who asked you to trample My courts?", of people who bring offerings and then oppress the orphan and widow.
  • Details are easier than spirit, and rituals easier than relationships. Rabbi Yisrael Salanter remarked it is easier to learn all of the Talmud than to change one bad trait.
  • If the Three Weeks are only about what we may and may not do, we perpetuate ritualistic Judaism. The purpose is a society that cares for one another.
  • On Tisha BeAv we think not only of the fast and the Beit HaMikdash but, as the Gemara says, of our actions: the content of what we do, not just its form.
