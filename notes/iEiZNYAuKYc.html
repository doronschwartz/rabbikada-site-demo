Topic: The leap year cycle, why Purim is in Adar Sheni, and the origin of the name Purim Katan

Sources: The Gemara; the Mishnah (Pesah Katan); the Tur; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Purim Katan & Adar Rishon, Wednesday 21 February 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • This year has an Adar Rishon, so Purim Katan falls on Thursday night and Friday.
  • There are seven leap years in every 19, almost once every three years, but sometimes after two: 2016, 2019, 2022, now 2024, then 2027, 2030, 2033.
  • The leap month keeps the lunar calendar in step with the solar one, since the Torah requires Pesah in the spring. A purely lunar calendar lets a festival move through the year, as Ramadan does.
  • The Gemara asks whether Purim belongs in the Adar after Shevat or the Adar before Nisan. The halacha is Adar Sheni, to put the redemption from Paroh next to the redemption from Ahashverosh.
  • The Tur and the Shulhan Arukh call the day just the 14th of Adar Rishon. The name Purim Katan seems only about 300 years old, probably borrowed from the Mishnah, which calls Pesah Sheni "Pesah Katan".
  • It is unrelated to the Purim Sheni of various towns (Castile, Casablanca, Zaragoza and others), which marked local miracles on any date in the year.

In one line

Purim Katan is a newer name for an old special day, the 14th of Adar Rishon, while the main Purim stays in Adar Sheni.
