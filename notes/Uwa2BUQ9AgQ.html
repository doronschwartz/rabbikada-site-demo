Part 1: Principle 8 Summed Up

#

Point

The Rabbi

1

Our Torah is Moshe's Torah

The script may have changed; keri u-ketiv and chaserot u-melot inconsistencies are acknowledged by Chazal. "But the actual text is the same text."

2

Entirely min HaShamayim: dictated by Hashem to Moshe; no autonomous authorship, not Moshe's, not anyone's after

Biblical criticism, the old academic theory of four documents compiled together, "now really shelved by most," is complete kefirah. (The Gemara entertains Yehoshua for the last eight verses; Ibn Ezra and some Rishonim felt one or two words perhaps weren't. "But generally speaking, it's the same Torah.")

3

All equally holy

Some parts contain more principles: you wouldn't learn the Torah's principles from the kings of Edom rather than the Ten Commandments or Shema. "That's fine to say." Not that one is holier

4

The Oral Torah is part of it

Below

"The beauty of our Torah": through exile to the four corners of the earth, Sephardim, Ashkenazim, Chassidim, Teimanim, Italians, Spanish and Portuguese, all with different customs, rites and rituals, the text of the Torah is identical.

The Ten Commandments in the Temple

"Already in the Beit HaMikdash they said the Ten Commandments every day, and it was cancelled, because people began ascribing more holiness to them than the rest of the Torah." Fundamentals that contain everything else: yes. Holier: no.

The Oral Torah: the class-notes analogy

"You go to a class and take notes. You understand them because you heard the class; others won't. The Torah is Moshe's notes from Hashem, complemented by the oral tradition."

Part 3: Principle 9: The Text

היסוד התשיעי: הביטול. והוא שזאת תורת משה לא תבטל, ולא תבא תורה מאת ה׳ זולתה, ולא יתוסף בה ולא יגרע ממנה, לא בכתב ולא בפירוש, שנאמר: לא תסף עליו ולא תגרע ממנו. וכבר בארנו מה שצריך לבאר ביסוד הזה בהקדמת החבור הזה.

  • This Torah of Moshe will never be nullified.
  • No other Torah will come from Hashem.
  • Nothing added, nothing removed, neither Written nor Oral: לא תסף עליו ולא תגרע ממנו (Va'etchanan, Re'eh).
  • "We've already explained this": earlier in the Introduction, a prophet who comes to add is put to death; "the Rambam went into great detail."

"Read the square brackets, not the round ones. Start with HaBitul: abrogation."

So what is the Brit Chadashah? All the commentators: a covenant renewed so it's never broken again, in the times of Mashiach: a new loyal bond between Hashem and Am Yisrael.

And logically: Christians and Muslims accept that God gave the Torah before 600,000 men. "If He wanted to change it, why reveal Himself one-on-one to Jesus, one-on-one to Muhammad? Surely He'd call everyone together again, as He did at the beginning."

Spot the difference

The Rabbi read two texts aloud: "Bonus if you can tell me where it's from. The prize? An extra five minutes of shiur at the end."

Text 1

Text 2

God has spoken to mankind in many languages: through Judaism to Jews, Christianity to Christians, Islam to Muslims. God is the God of all humanity, but no single faith is or should be the faith of all humanity.

God communicates in human language, but there are dimensions of the divine that must forever elude us. As Jews we believe God has made a covenant with a singular people, but that does not exclude the possibility of other peoples, cultures and faiths finding their own relationship with God within the shared frame of the Noahide laws. God is the God of all humanity, but between Babel and the end of days no single faith is the faith of all humanity.

Robert: "The first acknowledges that Christians and Muslims have some sort of prophecy; the second doesn't."

"Excellent. That is the key difference."

Both by Rabbi Jonathan Sacks, The Dignity of Difference, 2002. Text 1: the first edition, August 2002. "A huge uproar, understandably": it suggests God spoke through Christianity and Islam, and that they are equal to Judaism. Rabbi Rakow zichrono livrachah, Rav of Gateshead, called the book heresy and a deviation from authentic tradition, and demanded he repudiate it and withdraw it. Rabbi Sacks backed down; the revised edition, end of 2002, has Text 2.

The issue: Text 1 implies revelation to other faiths: against this principle. "He was clearly trying to be diplomatic."

Text 2 is fine. "Dimensions that elude us" = I can't explain why God appeared only to the Jews. Other faiths finding their own relationship with God: "The Rambam basically said that: Hashem allowed Christianity and Islam to bring people closer to God. They believe in a God; that's great. But you cannot acknowledge that God revealed Himself to them."

"This is why we're so opposed to Reform and Conservative: changing the Torah's laws to fit the society we live in, the liberal ideas of each century, goes against 9B."

But: interpretation and attitude can change between generations, not the law itself. "God forbid the Torah itself ever changes. What can change is our attitude to people who don't observe all of it."

2. The Sanhedrin's power to reinterpret

"Controversial, but sourced. Not everyone accepts it, but we have Chachamim who say it: the Supreme Court can reinterpret the Torah."

Example:* ayin tachat ayin. Read without the Oral Torah: *your eye for my eye. Bava Kamma spends two full pages, about eight arguments, showing it means money: the value of the eye, however assessed.

4. Rabbinic additions: bal tosif

How could the rabbis add so many mitzvot? The Torah forbids meat and milk; Chazal added poultry.

The Rambam (Hilchot Mamrim 2:9): "A court may decree a prohibition on something permitted, binding for generations. What then is do not add, do not detract? They may not add to the Torah's words or detract from them as if it were scriptural law. The Torah forbids cooking and eating meat and milk; fowl in milk is permitted by Torah law. If the court says fowl in milk is permitted, but **we forbid it as a decree: that's allowed. It's a safeguard, not an addition."

Says

Status

"The Torah permits chicken and milk; we forbid it as a fence"

Permitted: a gezeirah

"The Torah forbids chicken and milk"

Violates bal tosif

"That's why throughout the Gemara: d'Oraita or d'Rabbanan? Always know the source; don't be loose in what you say."

5. What will change: Nevi'im and Ketuvim

Hilchot Megillah 2:18: כל ספרי הנביאים וכל הכתובים עתידין ליבטל לימות המשיח, חוץ ממגילת אסתר, הרי היא קיימת כחמשה חומשי תורה וכהלכות של תורה שבעל פה שאינן בטלין לעולם.

All of Nevi'im and Ketuvim will be nullified in Messianic times, except Megillat Esther, which endures like the Five Books and the Oral Torah.

The Ra'avad disagrees ("you've misunderstood it"). But the Rambam codifies it. This principle covers only the Chumash and the Oral Torah.

Question: You've often said the other fasts are divrei kabbalah, not quite d'Rabbanan. So Ta'anit Esther stays, and the others disappear?

"Correct. We won't do them any more. That's exactly what the Rambam is saying."

6. Midrashim on change in the future

Midrash Tehillim 146 ("not clear who wrote it; apparently 8th–9th century; after the Gemara, authoritative to some extent"), on ה׳ מתיר אסורים: not only releasing the bound (the morning berachah; prisoners and hostages) but permitting the forbidden:

יש אומרים: כל הבהמה שנטמאת בעולם הזה, מטהר אותה הקב״ה לעתיד לבוא: non-kosher animals become kosher.

אין איסור גדול מן הנדה… ולעתיד לבוא הוא מתירה: "Hashem will permit niddah." "Why? I've got no idea. Really quite shocking."

Vayikra Rabbah, "much more authoritative," on the sacrifices in the future. "No shortage of rabbinic sources implying some change in Messianic times."

The Rambam doesn't imply that. He says Nevi'im and Ketuvim end then, but the Chumash doesn't: "the thrust everywhere in the Rambam: in Messianic times, law continues, Torah continues. Kosher animals won't change." How he'd deal with these Midrashim, reject or read metaphorically: "I don't know exactly."

Shabbetai Tzvi: "this is why the false Mashiach was so successful. He used these texts: how can you do this? — Mashiach is here, so the laws can change. If it's Messianic times, anything can change."

Question: "So in Messianic times we can eat pork?" "There's an opinion that says so, yes." "So why not now?" "We're not in Messianic times."

Summary

  1. Principle 8: same Torah, wholly divine (biblical criticism is kefirah), equally holy, with the Oral Torah (the Karaites denied it).
  2. The Kuzari's proof: אנכי… אשר הוצאתיך: a revelation to two million people, אתם ראיתם (Rashi), an unbroken chain of 100+ generations, אין אדם מנחיל שקר לבניו.
  3. Principle 9 (the uncensored text, HaBitul): (A) no other Torah, rejecting Christianity and Islam; (B) no individual law changes. Ani Ma'amin and Yigdal cover only A.
  4. Yirmiyahu's Brit Chadashah is refuted from its own verses; Rabbi Sacks's Dignity of Difference revision shows the line: other faiths may relate to God; they did not receive revelation.
  5. Part B: attitudes and interpretation shift, laws don't (Reform/Conservative); a prophet may suspend, never abolish (Eliyahu); the Sanhedrin reinterprets (ayin tachat ayin); Albo grounds immutability in the evidence of mass revelation; rabbinic fences must be labelled d'Rabbanan.
  6. Nevi'im and Ketuvim (except Esther) end in Messianic times (the Rambam vs the Ra'avad); Midrashim of future change are at odds with the Rambam, and fuelled Shabbetai Tzvi.
