Topic: The halacha and practice today on checking lineage before marriage

Sources: The Rambam; Rashi; Shulhan Arukh; the Hazon Ish

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 23 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Rambam mainly follows Rashi, and the Shulhan Arukh quotes the Rambam. So when a couple from known families come to a Beth Din or rabbi to marry, no checks are needed for a possible mamzer or halalah, for a Yisrael or a Kohen. This is the practice today.
  • An investigation is needed only if there is an irur, which the Shulhan Arukh, following the Rambam, defines as two witnesses, not a mere rumour. A rumour does not weaken the principle that all families have a presumption of kashrut.
  • If two witnesses testify they heard that a grandparent was a mamzer or similar, one must investigate, sometimes back four or five generations.
  • Less clear: an unknown family. Rashi requires checks even with no irur. Some read the Rambam the same way, others say he makes no such distinction, and the Shulhan Arukh is unclear, as he simply quotes the Rambam.
  • The contemporary poskim, the Hazon Ish and others, take the lenient view that even an unknown family need not be checked, and this is the practice.
  • In summary: once both are established as Jewish, no investigation of lineage is needed, even for a Kohen, unless two witnesses testify to a problem.

In one line

Today no lineage checks are needed before marriage, even for a Kohen or an unknown family, unless two witnesses raise a problem.
