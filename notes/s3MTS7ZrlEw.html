Source: The Rambam's Introduction to Perek Helek: Principle 2, Yichud Hashem

Shiur: Part 9 of the series on the Rambam's Introduction to Perek Helek, given by Dayan Daniel Kada on Zoom, early December 2025

These notes are based on the transcript of the recording.

The Text

היסוד השני: יחוד השם יתברך, כלומר שנאמין שזה שהוא סיבת הכל אחד. ואיננו כאחד הזוג, ולא כאחד המין, ולא כאיש האחד שנחלק לאחדים רבים, ולא כגוף הפשוט האחד במנין שמקבל החלוק לאין תכלית. אבל השם יתברך אחד באחדות שאין כמותה אחדות. וזה היסוד השני, מורה עליו מה שנאמר: שמע ישראל ה׳ אלהינו ה׳ אחד.

"When we say Shema every morning and evening, we think: God is one, not two. That's an element, but not all of it. It's a far subtler idea: a unity without parallel." Even one speck of dust divides: into halves, atoms, protons and neutrons. Not Hashem.

Two parts tonight: (1) what "one" excludes; (2) what "a unity unlike any unity" means.

Part 1: Five Things "One" Excludes

1. Polytheism: the enemy of Tanach

The first principle is never argued in Tanach; this one fills it. The Golden Calf; the cycle of the prophets: settle, serve idols, suffer, prophets warn.

  • Pharaoh: מי ה׳ אשר אשמע בקלו? The Rishonim: which god is this? We have many: what does yours control?
  • לא יהיה לך אלהים אחרים על פני.

"Thankfully few struggle with this today."

2. Dualism: the enemy of the Sages

Two gods: Zoroastrian light and darkness. "A convenient answer to evil": if God is omnipotent (Principle 1), why evil? Two gods! Some early Christian scholars even taught that the God of the Old Testament is darkness, of the New, light.

The Sages built the answer into the prayers, at the very moments day and night change:

Arvit

אשר בדברו מעריב ערבים… בורא יום ולילה, גולל אור מפני חשך

Shacharit (the main service begins here)

יוצר אור ובורא חשך, עושה שלום ובורא את הכל

That's why Shacharit is ideally at netz, sunrise, and Arvit at nightfall: so as day and night turn over, you don't think there's a god of each.

האומר מודים מודים משתקין אותו: a chazan who repeats Modim is silenced, lest he seem to thank two powers.

The Gemara (Sanhedrin 39a):

A Magian told Ameimar: from the waist up belongs to Hormiz, from the waist down to Ahormiz. Ameimar: then why does Ahormiz let Hormiz pass water through his territory?

"A cute story with a deep idea: an omnipotent God must control everything. Two gods means neither has full power."

The subtle dualism we all fall into

The Ramchal devoted much of his philosophy, and a whole book, Da'at Tevunot, to this. "Deep down, we're all subtle dualists. Every time we get angry, every time we sin."

A true monotheist knows nothing happens outside Hashem's will: so (a) when things go wrong, it all comes from Him; (b) temptation is an illusion, since nothing outside His will can benefit me. Every sin says: I'll gain more from this than from Hashem's will: a denial of His oneness.

Robert: isn't that denying God altogether, "zero" rather than "two"?

"You could see it either way, both bad. אין עוד מלבדו: there's no benefit outside His will. But Tanach's enemy was many or two, never zero."

The original sin. והייתם כאלהים יודעי טוב ורע: the snake promised benefit from what Hashem forbade. Refusing would have affirmed His unity; they denied it. All history rectifies this. When Israel truly recognises Yichud Hashem: the end of days.

Why cover our eyes for Shema? The simple reason: focus. The Ramchal's: in this world we don't see His unity; there seem to be other forces, evil and temptation. I cover my eyes and say: Hashem Echad. I believe what I can't see. "Hence mezuzot at every door, tefillin** every morning."

Angels' names on mezuzot. The Rambam: whoever writes them inside a mezuzah has no portion in Olam HaBa. "The whole mezuzah is Yichud Hashem. You take the very thing that declares there's nothing besides Him and write angels on it! If you must, on the back, which is why we have Shaddai there today. The Rambam's had nothing."

3. The Trinity

"Most Christians deny they're polytheists: one God with three parts. For us that's heresy."

Halachic consequences:

- The Letter on Martyrdom (learned last year): one forced to embrace Islam need not die, since Islam believes in one God. Christianity is arguably avodah zarah, so one would have to die rather than adopt it.

- One may enter a mosque, even pray there (Rav Ovadia permits), but not even enter a functioning church. (It also contradicts Principle 3, God has no body: next week.)

- Some Rishonim: for gentiles the Trinity is not avodah zarah: for them it counts as belief in one God. "That has halachic ramifications."

4. Deism

God created, transcends the world, and left it. "Probably more popular today: since science now accepts a beginning, atheism can't explain the first elements, so: a creator who then walked away."

Denies Principles 1 and 2: Yichud Hashem means there's no reality outside Him. If the world exists, He is sustaining it, every moment: the imagined universe that vanishes when attention slips.

Question: "the mouth of Bilaam's donkey was created at twilight of the sixth day": isn't everything set up front, God watching it unfold?

"Absolutely not. Whether all miracles were built into nature in the six days (the Rambam) or are new (the Ramchal: a machloket), maintenance is constant."

5. Pantheism

God is nature; nature is God: "really a subtle form of atheism." Deism puts God outside the world; pantheism identifies Him with it.

Spinoza, a member of the Amsterdam community, was excommunicated on 27 July 1656 by the Ma'amad, "with the rabbis' consent" (it's the board that excommunicates), in the sharpest terms: cursed by day, cursed by night…

London's own controversy. In 1703, Chacham David Nieto of Bevis Marks (author of Mateh Dan) preached that God and nature are one. Members of the board, "more knowledgeable than the rabbi, of course," accused him of Spinozism; Joshua Zarphati rallied people against "the heretic rabbi, the pantheist in our midst." It raged over a year. Nieto didn't step down: I meant God's force runs through all nature.

In 1705 the parnassim of Sha'ar HaShamayim asked the Chacham Tzvi (rabbi of Hamburg, briefly rabbi in London). His ruling (on Sefaria): we must be grateful to the great chacham David Nieto, who warned the people against the philosophers who say nature acts by itself and nothing else exists*. Yishar ko'ach. "*That killed the controversy."

טבע = nature, and also to sink, as in טבעו בים סוף. "It's easy to sink into nature: to think the world just runs by itself. Hashem is behind nature, not nature itself." טבע = 86 = אלהים in gematria.

A sixth? Panentheism

"Briefly, 30 seconds. There's a term for a strong Chabad belief: God is in everything: everyone has a piece of God in them; ultimately the world is an illusion. That's a major problem I have with Chabad. I think the Rambam would disagree, though it's not explicit here. A discussion for another time."

Part 2: "A Unity Unlike Any Unity"

The Mishneh Torah (Yesodei HaTorah 1): אלוה זה אחד הוא, ואינו שנים ולא יותר על שנים… אלא יחוד שאין יחוד אחר כמותו בעולם.

Ch. 2 (on His knowledge):

הקב״ה מכיר אמתו ויודע אותה כמו שהיא, ואינו יודע בדעה שהיא חוץ ממנו כמו שאנו יודעים, שאין אנו ודעתנו אחד. אבל הבורא יתברך, הוא ודעתו וחייו אחד מכל צד ומכל פינה ובכל דרכי יחוד… נמצאת אומר: הוא היודע, והוא הידוע, והוא הדעה עצמה, הכל אחד. ודבר זה אין כח בפה לאמרו, ולא באזן לשמעו.

He is the knower, the known and the knowledge itself, all one. The mouth can't say it; the ear can't hear it.

Reuven and his attributes. "Reuven is clever, handsome, kind: there's Reuven, who happens to have these. That's not unified. God isn't God plus wisdom, kindness, compassion. It's all one. The Ramchal calls it פשיטות, simplicity: no parts, one essence. Very hard for us to grasp, because everything we know is composite."

Yigdal: אחד ואין יחיד כיחודו, נעלם וגם אין סוף לאחדותו: hidden from us, endless. Ani Ma'amin: ואין יחידות כמוהו בשום פנים.

Then what about the Thirteen Attributes?

ה׳ ה׳ אל רחום וחנון ארך אפים ורב חסד ואמת: said "over and over" on Yom Kippur and all year. That's God plus compassion, plus truth. A plurality.

Negative theology

The Rambam: God has no positive attributes. Every attribute in the Torah tells us what God is not, because negations imply no plurality.

The Torah says

It means

Strong / able (יכול)

Not weak

Compassionate

Not hard-hearted

Eternal

Not caused

Wise / knowing (יודע)

Not ignorant

Willing (רוצה)

Not indifferent

Moreh 1:58: דע שתאור האל בשלילות הוא התאור הנכון: describing God by negation is the correct description. Positive attributes imply composition and deficiency; negations guide the mind without them.

"It changes your whole perspective on the Torah. On Yom Kippur, think El Rachum veChanun, that's fine. But theologically, His unity means complete simplicity."

Why the Ramchal puts everything on Yichud Hashem

We can't grasp any positive attribute: that would mean grasping God Himself. We can negate. And negating means: no other force. So the only thing we can truly understand of God is that He is one, and the purpose of life is to reveal Yichud Hashem.

Pesachim 50a:

ביום ההוא יהיה ה׳ אחד ושמו אחד (said daily in Aleinu). Isn't He one now? Not like this world is the world to come. Here, for good news we say הטוב והמטיב; for bad, ברוך דיין האמת: I don't see how it's good; Hashem is the true judge. In the world to come it will all be Hatov veHameitiv.

"Yichud Hashem means believing, philosophically if not practically, that even the bad is good, since it all comes from one source. One day we'll see it. Now we cover our eyes."

A caution about the Sefirot

The Rivash (Rav Yitzchak bar Sheshet, 14th century, "quoted in halachah everywhere") wrote of a great rabbi who prayed one prayer to one sefirah, another to another, each blessing of the Amidah aimed at a sefirah. To us, this is very strange; it looks like dualism.* A philosopher mocked: the Christians believe in three, the Kabbalists in ten.***

In Zaragoza the Rivash asked his friend Don Yosef ibn Shoshan, a great Talmudist, Kabbalist and tzaddik. God forbid, we pray only to the One. As with a king: for justice you go to the judge, for money to the treasury. "Like one prime minister with a Foreign Office, Home Office, Treasury." The Rivash: very nice, but better to pray without all of this**.

Never imagine parts of Hashem. "As it was explained to me years ago: one sunlight through different media comes out different colours. That's the sefirot: the same light of God through different media."

Summary

  1. "Hashem is one" excludes:
  2. polytheism (Tanach's enemy; who is Hashem?);
  3. dualism (the Sages', answered in יוצר אור ובורא חשך; Modim twice; Hormiz and Ahormiz), including the subtle dualism of every sin (Ramchal): the original sin, eyes covered at Shema, no angels in mezuzot;
  4. the Trinity (Islam vs Christianity; mosque vs church);
  5. deism (He sustains every moment);
  6. pantheism (Spinoza; Nieto vindicated by the Chacham Tzvi; טבע = אלהים).

(And panentheism, a Chabad view the Rabbi rejects; for another time.)

2. "A unity unlike any unity": knower, known and knowledge are one: simplicity, no parts.

3. So no positive attributes: every attribute is a negation (Moreh 1:58). The Thirteen Attributes say what He is not.

4. The only thing we can grasp is His oneness: the Ramchal's key to everything. In the world to come, all is Hatov veHameitiv.

5. Sefirot are one light through different media, never parts of God (the Rivash).
