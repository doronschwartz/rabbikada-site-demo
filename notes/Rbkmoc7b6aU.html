Topic: The Mabit's view that Kohanim today are certain, and the Shevut Yaakov's ruling

Sources: The Mabit (Rabbi Moshe di Trani), author of Kiryat Sefer; the Maharit; the Gemara; the Shevut Yaakov (Rabbi Yaakov Reischer)

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 24 April 2026

The halacha

  • The Mabit, Rabbi Moshe di Trani of the 16th century, author of Kiryat Sefer on the Rambam, disagreed: Kohanim today are definite Kohanim, biblically and rabbinically.
  • His proof: the Gemara says the courts could even execute on the strength of a hazakah, for example relations between a man and one presumed to be his daughter, with no written proof. So the presumption of Kohanim makes them full Kohanim.
  • The Kohanim in the Gemara had a weak hazakah because they took another family's name. Ours, with nothing weakening the hazakah, are better. We do not eat Torah Terumah only because the penalty is so severe. His son, the Maharit, says the same.
  • So in these centuries there was a great debate over whether Kohanim are doubtful.
  • The Shevut Yaakov, Rabbi Yaakov Reischer of Prague (died 1733), was asked about a Kohen who married a woman presented as a widow, then learned she had done halisah. Halisah, a quasi divorce, forbids her to a Kohen only rabbinically.
  • He allowed them to stay together: Kohanim today are doubtful, the ban is rabbinic, and they are already married.
  • He adds that the rule that one who believes he is a Kohen must act as one (shavya anafsheih hatikha deissura) applies only where he believes he has evidence. Today a Kohen knows only what his father told him, and no one is a certain Kohen.

In one line

Against the Mabit's view that Kohanim are certain, the Shevut Yaakov treated them as doubtful and let a Kohen stay married to a woman who had done halisah.
