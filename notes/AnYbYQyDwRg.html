Topic: Taking on maaser bli neder, the Beit Yoseph's view on expenses, and what maaser may be used for

Sources: Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; a teshuvah of the Beit Yoseph; the Rema; the Aharonim

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Thursday 3 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Since maaser is a minhag, one in financial difficulty one year is not in breach, provided he gives something each year and gives when asked.
  • So Hakham Ovadiah writes that one who starts giving maaser should state in the first year that it is "bli neder", a voluntary practice he may pause.
  • One who began without saying bli neder and has done it for some years needs no hatarat nedarim to pause for a year or two, but does to stop permanently.
  • In a teshuvah the Beit Yoseph suggests maaser may be due only on what is left after monthly expenses (utilities, rent or mortgage). Many Aharonim disagree, but Hakham Ovadiah says one in a difficult situation should at least fulfil this opinion.
  • The Rema writes maaser may go only to the poor or the support of Torah study, not, for example, to buying an aliyah. The Aharonim disagree, and since it is a minhag, we hold it may be used for any misvah, though most of it should preferably go to the poor.
  • Hakham Ovadiah adds that strictly one must feed one's children only until six, so school fees for children over six, as Torah education beyond the strict obligation, may come from maaser.

In one line

Take on maaser bli neder, and it may be used for any misvah, including, according to Hakham Ovadiah, school fees for children over six.
