Topic: The mountain held over Sinai as coercion and as protection: the love of Torah

Sources: The Gemara (kafah aleihem har kegigit); Mekhilta, Parashat Yitro

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Tuesday 11 June 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Gemara says HaKadosh Barukh Hu held the mountain over the people (metaphorically, of course): accept the Torah or be buried here. It is the image often taught to children, and a negative one.
  • The Mekhilta on Parashat Yitro reads "they stood under the mountain" differently: the people were afraid of the thunder, lightning and loud voices, so He covered them as protection.
  • The two go together. We were coerced, and the Torah is called a yoke we must keep, but the coercion was an act of love, as when a parent makes a child do what is right for him.
  • We can relate to the Misvot as a mountain about to bury us, or as a mountain over us that protects and elevates us, something to cherish.
  • On Shavuot we renew our commitment; we have no choice, but it is Hashem protecting us and the best and most beloved way to live. The Torah is our best friend.

Sources quoted

We were compelled to accept the Torah, but out of love and for our protection.
