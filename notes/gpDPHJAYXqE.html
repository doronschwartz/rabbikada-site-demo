Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 296, seifim 1-7 Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 28 March 2024

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

Havdalah is as important as Kiddush

Last time covered Havdalah in the Amidah; tonight is Havdalah on the cup. The Mishnah Berurah (1) brings the view that Havdalah, like Kiddush, is from the Torah: remembering Shabbat means distinguishing it as it comes in and as it goes out.

The Rabbi mentioned Rabbi Akiva Eiger's view that saying "Shabbat Shalom" on Friday night might fulfil Kiddush from the Torah, since it praises Shabbat, perhaps for someone with no siddur at all. But "Shavua Tov" on Saturday night does not fulfil Havdalah, since it says nothing about Shabbat being distinct.

Atah Honantanu in the Amidah fulfils the Torah requirement. Without it and without Havdalah on the cup, one has not done the misvah. "It's a shame that people do Kiddush on Friday night but don't necessarily always do Havdalah."

The order, and the custom of spilling

Seif 1: the order is wine, spices, fire, Havdalah. Spices and fire are separate blessings that the Sages instituted for Saturday night and added to the cup; they are not Havdalah. If they will only be available later, make Havdalah now and say those blessings later.

The later poskim explain the order by the senses: taste (wine), smell (spices), sight (fire), and finally intellect to distinguish holy from weekday.

The cup must not be "pagum", already drunk from, as with every cup of blessing. The verses said before Havdalah are a good custom, not part of it. Pay attention during Havdalah, looking at the cup or the candle.

The Rema's custom: pour some wine from the cup near the end of the blessing, for a good sign, based on the Gemara: "a house where wine is not spilled like water has no blessing." He also writes that one extinguishes the candle with the leftover wine and puts some on the eyes, out of love for the misvah.

The Rabbi made several points:

  • The source is the Rema's own custom, from about 500 years ago, not the Gemara or the Rishonim.
  • The Gemara does not mean we should spill wine on purpose. Wine was expensive; it means that if a household never lets a drop spill, it is too tight-fisted. A drop here and there shows abundance.
  • There is a principle against waste.
  • Pouring during the blessing is problematic. The Aharonim say instead: fill the cup to the brim, so that a little spills as you lift it. That is accidental, not in the middle of Havdalah, and the cup stays full.

The Mishnah Berurah (5) says not to do it during the blessing. The Rabbi's practical guidance: fill it, and a few drops spill as you lift it. "That's the best way of doing it."

Only at Havdalah. "There is no concept anywhere in halacha" of letting the cup overflow at Kiddush, Birkat HaMazon, a wedding or a brit. It is wasteful and untidy: "It's not the way of a talmid hakham." Pour it nearly full so it does not spill.

Asked whether this is a real waste when it has a purpose, he said: "I'm just saying, don't do it because you think there's a misvah in it."

Putting out the candle and wiping the eyes. The Rema's reason is love of the misvah. People also put it in their pockets, and the Syrians took this seriously, but "it's not magically going to increase your wallet." Putting out the candle with the wine shows that it was lit for Havdalah.

Women and the Havdalah cup

The Mishnah Berurah (6): one should sit to drink, and he records a custom that women do not drink from the Havdalah cup, and that the person making Havdalah drinks all of it himself. He suggests this ensures a full cheekful is drunk.

The Rabbi called the women's custom "a big myth" that "bothers me tremendously," because it led to the idea that women do not make Havdalah. A woman has a duty to make Havdalah, almost as much as a man. When a man is present, he usually makes it. If her husband is unwell, she should make it for him rather than skip it.

Two common mistakes:

  • People answer amen after "HaMavdil bein kodesh lehol" before the blessing has finished, and so have not heard Havdalah. Wait for the end.
  • People say "Shavua Tov" straight after the blessing. If you are relying on this Havdalah, wait until the person has drunk a cheekful.

Havdalah on beer: the beverage of the town

Seif 2: Havdalah may not be made on bread. Kiddush can be on bread, since Kiddush leads into a meal; Havdalah has no meal. One may make Havdalah on beer if it is the beverage of the town, but not on water. Better a small amount of wine, or a cup already drunk from, than the beverage of the town.

The source is the amora Amemar, who made Havdalah on beer. The Biur Halacha says that if you prefer the local drink to wine, you may use it even in the first instance. If wine and beer are equal to you, use wine. "Having wine" means it is ready at home. Going out to the shop is not required; whether the garage or cellar counts, "you have to decide."

What counts as the beverage of the town:

  • Many Ashkenazim do not require it to be alcoholic. "Hamar" means an intoxicating drink, so Rav Ovadia Yosef and Rav Ben Sion Abba Shaul were strict that it must be alcoholic. Grape juice counts as wine.
  • In England, beer is accepted. Whisky or vodka: it is hard to call them the beverage of the town, and one must drink about 43 ml in one or two gulps.
  • A mixed drink such as gin and tonic could count only where that is what everyone drinks.

He said Havdalah on Saturday night is more lenient here than Kiddush on Friday night, but one should try to use grape juice.

Before Havdalah, and with no wine at all

Asked about driving to shul before Havdalah, the Rabbi said: relying on "Barukh HaMavdil" alone for melakhah is not simple; it is relied on for women who do not pray. Better make Havdalah at home first, or rely on Atah Honantanu. This will be discussed at length in Siman 299.

Seif 3: away in the countryside with no wine or beer, may one eat? Some say yes, since the ban on eating applies only when one will make Havdalah that night. The Rosh says to wait if he expects wine the next day. The Mishnah Berurah: if you expect it only from the afternoon, you may eat now. Of course Atah Honantanu should be said first.

The next seifim: if diluting stretches the wine, do it. If you have only one cup for Shabbat, keep it for Havdalah and make Friday night Kiddush on bread.

Sitting or standing

Seif 6: Havdalah is said sitting. The halachic reason: whenever one person fulfils a misvah for others, sitting creates a fixed group, a "meeting of minds." Friday night Kiddush is the exception, said standing because it is testimony about Creation. Shabbat morning Kiddush should really be seated. The Rema says the custom is to stand, to honour the departing Shabbat Queen.

The Rema: hold the cup in the right hand; for the spices, move the cup to the left hand and take the spices in the right, then return the cup to the right hand. Asked if the spices interrupt between the wine and drinking, he said they do not, because the Sages instituted it so.

He ended by saying the next seif, about women and Havdalah, needs its own discussion next week.

Practical summary

  • Make Havdalah every Saturday night; it is as important as Kiddush.
  • Fill the cup so that a few drops spill as you lift it; do not let the cup overflow at other times.
  • Women are obligated in Havdalah and may drink from the cup when making it themselves.
  • In England, beer may be used for Havdalah; Sefaradim need an alcoholic drink.
  • Wait for the blessing to finish before answering amen, and for the drinking before saying Shavua Tov.
