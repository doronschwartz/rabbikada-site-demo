Recap: Chapters 25 and 26

Chapter 25, שכן: to dwell. It can mean living somewhere while moving around in it, or the constant presence of something, even something non-physical. Of Hashem it is always the second: the continued presence of His Shekhinah or His hashgahah. The Mishkan and the Beit HaMikdash are not places Hashem physically inhabits; the Shekhinah and the hashgahah rest there when we have a relationship with Him.

Chapter 26, דברה תורה כלשון בני אדם: the Torah describes Hashem with verbs of motion (stand, sit, go, come, dwell, pass) because it speaks in language people easily understand. People see movement as a perfection, but just as eating, drinking and sleeping are wrong descriptions of Hashem, so is every movement. All of them must be read metaphorically.

The End of Chapter 26: Ten Verbs in Revision

Chapter 26 ends: ואין ספק שמשנסתלקה הגשמות נסתלקו כל אלה, כלומר ירד, עלה, הלך, נצב, עמד, סבב, ישב, שכן, יצא, ובא, ועבר, וכל הדומה לכך. Once we accept that Hashem has no body, every one of these verbs goes too.

סבב: How Does God Surround Us?

סבב is the one verb in the list Rambam never explains. Two famous verses use it of Hashem:

  • ירושלים הרים סביב לה וה׳ סביב לעמו מעתה ועד עולם (Tehillim 125)
  • חונה מלאך ה׳ סביב ליראיו ויחלצם (Tehillim 34, said every Shabbat morning)

If Hashem has no body, how is He "around" His people? The class suggested: He guards and protects them, as you surround something to protect it; or He is everywhere.

Rambam's answer, at the end of Hilchot Mezuzah

חייב אדם להזהר במזוזה מפני שהיא חובת הכל תמיד. וכל זמן שיכנס ויצא, יפגע ביחוד השם שמו של הקדוש ברוך הוא, ויזכור אהבתו, וייקץ משנתו ושגיותיו בהבלי הזמן.

Every time you come in or go out, you meet the oneness of Hashem, remember your love for Him, and wake up from the distractions of the passing day.

אמרו חכמים, כל מי שיש לו תפילין בראשו ובזרועו, וציצית בבגדו, ומזוזה בפתחו, מוחזק הוא שלא יחטא, שהרי יש לו מזכירין רבים. והן הן המלאכים שמצילין אותו מלחטוא, שנאמר חונה מלאך ה׳ סביב ליראיו ויחלצם.

With tefillin, sisit and a mezuzah, a person is almost guaranteed not to sin, because he has so many reminders. These are the angels that surround those who fear Him. So סביב means that Hashem has given us symbols of Himself to surround us at all times: tefillin and sisit for men, and the mezuzah for everyone.

Look at the mezuzah

There is no halachah to kiss the mezuzah. The first mention of it is in the Kissur Shulhan Arukh, about 250 years ago. The mezuzah is there to be looked at: everything in my day comes from Hashem, and the purpose of life is my relationship with Him.

"Of course we should kiss the mezuzah." The danger is that the act replaces the thought: I've kissed it, I've done it, now I continue. One of the Rabbi's rabbanim in yeshivah told them sometimes not to say the Leshem Yihud aloud but to think it, because then you actually think it through.

"I challenge you: next time you come into your house, don't kiss the mezuzah, don't touch it, just look at it and stop for a second, and think about what we learned in the Rambam."

A question from the class

Couldn't ה׳ סביב לעמו also mean His Shekhinah is among His people, as Jerusalem is among the hills? "Completely acceptable." Once you have the principles, the meanings are interchangeable: Shekhinah, hashgahah, created light. And, as Chapter 27 is about to show, מלאך ה׳ can mean Hashem Himself. The one thing סביב cannot mean is something physical, God around us the way the hills are around Jerusalem.

Whenever you meet one of these verbs in Tanach, "stop and think: what did we learn in the Moreh about this?"

Chapter 27: Onkelos

Why Onkelos, and why here?

So far, apart from the Tannaim, Amoraim and the Neviim, Rambam has quoted very few people by name: Aristotle, Plato once, and Onkelos. Onkelos is the only one he quotes with great reverence. Three reasons the chapter comes here:

  1. Chapter 26 set out the principle of anthropomorphism. Onkelos applied it, "a thousand years before me."
  2. Onkelos often renders verbs of motion as שכינה, the subject of Chapter 25.
  3. The Rabbi's new thought, from a conversation with Doron: סבב and the מלאך (see below).

Who was Onkelos? A Roman convert of the late first and early second century, from an aristocratic family, a relative of the emperor Hadrian. The Gemara tells how the Romans sent men to bring him back, and he convinced each of them instead. He studied under Rabbi Eliezer ben Hurkanos and wrote the Targum in Aramaic, the vernacular of the time.

His method

Onkelos the convert was an expert in languages, and הוא נתן את לבו להסיר את ההגשמה מן השם. Whenever he meets a verb of motion said of Hashem, הוא מפרש את משמעות התנועה כהתגלות of a created light, that is, the Shekhinah or the hashgahah. (As before, the created light can be understood as a physical light, or more deeply as a light in the prophet's mind.)

זה עקבי בתרגומו. He is consistent throughout. The Rabbi suggests Rambam is also protecting himself: don't accuse me of turning the Tanach into metaphor; Onkelos did it before me.

The puzzle: אנכי ארד עמך מצרימה

In Vayigash Hashem tells Yaakov אנכי ארד עמך מצרימה, and Onkelos translates אנא איחות עמך למצרים: "I will go down", the word for a person going down. Why here, of all places? (The Ramban also raises the question.)

Rambam: זו פרשה מופלאה מאוד המורה על שלימות החכם הזה. It shows how great Onkelos was, and it opens a door to understanding prophecy.

Answer 1: it happened in a vision

The passage opens ויאמר אלהים לישראל במראות הלילה. מכיוון שבתחילת הדיבור נאמר שהוא היה במראות הלילה, לא חשש אונקלוס.

A verse that reports what was said in a vision is different from a verse that describes what happened. וירד ה׳ על הר סיני describes an event, so Onkelos must remove the movement. אנכי ארד is what Yaakov heard in a night vision; nothing moved, so the words can stay. וזה מופלא.

From here, Rambam says, there is a great difference between what is said בחלום or במראות הלילה, what comes במחזה, and plain וידבר ה׳ אלי. These are different levels of prophecy, which Section Two will develop.

Maor pointed out that at the Berit Bein HaBetarim the Torah says היה דבר ה׳ אל אברם במחזה, and Onkelos renders it בנבואה. Exactly: as we saw in Chapter 21, the whole covenant happened in Avraham's vision.

Doron asked: even in a vision, what does it mean that Hashem "goes down" with him? A fair point. But Onkelos is not explaining the depth; he has one goal, to remove crude anthropomorphism from our minds. If you read Hashem came down on Har Sinai literally, you will think God has a body. In a dream at night nothing came down, so there is nothing to remove.

Answer 2: it was an angel

לדעתי יתכן גם שאונקלוס מפרש שהאלהים האמור כאן הוא מלאך, and so he had no difficulty writing איחות (angels, by the way, do not have wings; what they are comes later).

But the angel says אנכי האל אלהי אביך! That style is common when an angel speaks for Hashem, as in this week's parashah: ויאמר אלי מלאך האלהים בחלום יעקב, and a few verses later the same angel says אנכי האל בית אל... אשר נדרת לי שם נדר. Yaakov made his vow to Hashem, not to an angel. So the angel means אנכי שליח האל: "I am the messenger of the God to whom you vowed." Rambam calls this השמטת הנסמך, leaving out the first word of the phrase.

אלהים can mean an angel

Rambam in Yesodei HaTorah (Chapter 2) lists the names of the angels: חיות הקדש, אופנים, אראלים, חשמלים, שרפים, מלאכים, אלהים, ובני אלהים. Angels can be called Elohim. These are the words of the piyutim before Kedushah on Yom Kippur, which very few people understand.

Back to סבב

The Rabbi's speculation: if מלאך ה׳ can mean Hashem, and Hashem can mean a מלאך, then perhaps ה׳ סביב לעמו is also השמטת הנסמך: an angel of Hashem around His people. "That's something to think about."

Chapter 28 (begun): רגל

Rambam returns to single words, but now a body part. Why foot, here? The explanation that satisfied the Rabbi: this chapter will discuss Onkelos and the purpose of his Targum at length, which flows straight from Chapter 27. (Face and hands come in the late 30s.)

רגל מונח רב משמעי. It has three meanings:

  1. A foot or leg: רגל תחת רגל. (Curiously, Hazal read this verse as money.)
  2. A follower: צא אתה וכל העם אשר ברגליך: everyone who walks after you. "Not just following someone on TikTok."
  3. A cause or basis: the foot is what the whole body stands on. ויברך ה׳ אותך לרגלי: because of me. כי דבר שהוא בגלל דבר מה, הרי אותו דבר מה סיבה לדבר.

לרגלי or בגללך? (Vayetse)

An idea in the name of Dayan Ehrentreu, quoted by Rabbi Korkoben. After fourteen years, Lavan says נחשתי ויברכני ה׳ בגללך. Yaakov answers ויברך ה׳ אותך לרגלי. Why the different words?

  • בגללך is a possible cause. Like a new CEO when the shares go up: it happened while he was there, but any CEO might have done the same.
  • לרגלי is a direct, sufficient cause. Without me, none of it would have happened.

Questions held over

  • שלש רגלים, the festivals, are "three times you use your legs": the pilgrimage. That is meaning one.
  • But Bilam's angel asks על מה הכית את אתנך זה שלש רגלים. Not three legs, not three followers, not three causes. Hazal saw a hint to the three festivals; what is the simple meaning? "Excellent question. Let's try to find an answer for next week."

Summary

End of 26

All ten verbs of motion are metaphors. Revise them.

סבב

Hashem surrounds us through tefillin, sisit and mezuzah, the "angels" that keep us from sin. Look at the mezuzah.

Chapter 27

Onkelos consistently removes movement from Hashem: ואתגלי, not ונחת.

The exception

אנכי ארד עמך: a vision, not an event; or an angel speaking for Hashem.

Chapter 28

רגל: a foot, a follower, a cause. לרגלי is a direct cause; בגללך only a possible one.
