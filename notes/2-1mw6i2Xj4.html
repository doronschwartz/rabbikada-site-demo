Topic: Using Maaser money for Mahasit HaShekel, where the money goes, and how to describe the gift

Sources: Hakham Ovadiah; the poskim

Daily Halacha: Purim, Wednesday 9 March 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • For Sepharadim, giving over £5 for each family member can add up, so may Maaser money be used?
  • As discussed in the laws of Sedakah, Maaser money may not be used for an obligation, such as Matanot LaEvyonim on Purim. Many extend this to Mahasit HaShekel, since although a custom it is one we always keep. Hakham Ovadiah agrees.
  • He adds: one who began giving Maaser "beli neder", without obligating himself, may use Maaser money for it. One who has given Maaser for many years without that stipulation should not.
  • Some say the money goes to the synagogue, as today's substitute for the Mikdash (upkeep, cleaning, refurbishment); others say to the poor. Either is fine, as long as it goes to charity.
  • Giving it to the poor does not count towards Matanot LaEvyonim, the obligation on Purim day itself, however much one gave.
  • The poskim warn not to call it Mahasit HaShekel, which would belong to the Mikdash and might have to be set aside until the third Beit HaMikdash is built. Either say nothing and have the custom in mind, or say clearly that it is "zekher leMahasit HaShekel", a remembrance, not the biblical Misvah.

Sources quoted

  • Hakham Ovadiah: Maaser money may not be used unless one began giving Maaser "beli neder".
  • The poskim: call it only a remembrance of the half shekel.

In one line

Give Mahasit HaShekel to the synagogue or the poor, not from Maaser (unless taken on "beli neder"), and only as a remembrance.
