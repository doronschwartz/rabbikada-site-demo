Topic: Whether maaser is taken from gross or net income, and which expenses may be deducted

Sources: Gemara Bava Batra; the Hida; Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Tsedakah & Maaser, Monday 7 February 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • For reasons of both sedakah and maaser, it is correct practice to give 10% of one's income each year. The question is what counts as income.
  • One may not first deduct living expenses such as accommodation and utility bills; the opinion that allowed this is rejected.
  • The Gemara in Bava Batra calls tax forcibly taken from a Jew by non-Jews sedakah. From this the early Aharonim, among them the Hida, deduce that tax is deducted first, and maaser is taken from what remains.
  • Some argue that today tax is different, since we receive services in return. Rav Moshe Feinstein rejects this: even one who pays no tax receives the same benefits, sometimes more. So maaser is from net, not gross, salary.
  • Business insurance, such as a doctor's medical insurance, is also deducted first.
  • So is a car needed for work, like expenses wholly and exclusively for the business in tax law.

In one line

Maaser is given from net income after tax and genuine work expenses, but not after personal living costs.
