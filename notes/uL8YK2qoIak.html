Topic: When the fast begins, and eating or drinking before dawn

Sources: The Gemara; Rav Ovadia Yoseph; Rav Ben Sion Abba Shaul; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Asarah BeTevet

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Unlike Tisha BeAv and Yom Kippur, the 17th of Tammuz, Asarah BeTevet, Som Gedaliah and Taanit Esther begin in the morning, at dawn (alot hashahar).
  • Sefaradim take dawn as 72 minutes before sunrise (Rav Ovadia Yoseph and Rav Ben Sion Abba Shaul). With sunrise at 8:06, the fast begins at 6:54. Some begin 90 minutes before (6:36), which is praiseworthy, but the halacha allows eating until 6:54.
  • On any day one may eat and drink freely before dawn, even a full meal: the ban on eating before Shaharit applies only once the time for Shaharit has begun.
  • On a fast, however, the Gemara rules that going to sleep means accepting the next day. So one who slept may not eat, even if he wakes before the fast begins.
  • The way out is to stipulate before going to sleep that one does not accept the fast until its proper time. Then one may eat and drink on waking.
  • Without a stipulation: Shulhan Arukh (Sefaradim) forbids even drinking; the Rema (Ashkenazim) allows drinking, which is natural on waking, but not eating.

In one line

The fast begins at dawn, but to eat or drink on waking before then you must stipulate the night before; without that, Sefaradim may not even drink.
