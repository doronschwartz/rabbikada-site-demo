The halacha

  • The Gemara says a ger may marry a mamzeret, and a mamzer may marry a giyoret.
  • It derives this from the verse that a mamzer may not enter the congregation of HaShem. The Hakhamim understand Kahal HaShem as a legal category of lineage (yihus), which does not include gerim. This in no way means converts are not fully Jewish or part of the Jewish community.
  • The Gemara's story: Rabbi Zeira taught in Mehoza, a place with many converts, that a ger may marry a mamzeret, and the listeners pelted him with their etrogim. Rava said that even true Torah need not be said in such a place.
  • Rava then taught there that a ger may marry the daughter of a Kohen, and they honoured him; when he added that a ger may also marry a mamzeret, he explained that they are privileged to be able to marry anyone.
  • Lessons: not every true halacha should be said to every audience, and the right framing lets it be accepted.
  • The halacha: a ger and giyoret may marry a mamzer or mamzeret, and a ger may marry a Kohen's daughter. But the child of such a marriage is a mamzer, whichever parent is the mamzer.
  • Some later poskim write that although permitted, it is not right to bring more mamzerim into the world.
