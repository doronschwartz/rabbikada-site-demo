Topic: How to dispose of hames this year, including leftovers on Shabbat, and when to say Kol Hamira

Sources: The Gemara; Rabbi Yehudah; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Tuesday 8 April 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara gives three ways to dispose of hames: burning it, crumbling and scattering it to the wind, or casting it into the sea. Rabbi Yehudah required burning, but most Rishonim and the Shulhan Arukh accept all three.
  • The Rema says it is good practice to burn it, which is the Ashkenazi custom and that of many Sepharadim. If that is hard, one may crumble it and remove it from one's possession, relinquishing ownership.
  • On Friday this year one uses the usual methods, preferably before 11:54 but any time on Friday is fine.
  • Leftover hames on Friday night or Shabbat: where there is an eruv, crumble it into pieces smaller than a kazayit and scatter it outside.
  • Without an eruv, and since one cannot burn on Shabbat, there are two options. Flush it down the toilet, the equivalent of the sea, unwrapped so that it breaks down. Or put it in the outside bin, but since it is still in one's possession, pour bleach or detergent on it so it is unfit even for an animal.
  • One should dispose of hames on Friday and set aside only what is needed for Friday night and Shabbat morning.
  • Bedikat Hames is on Thursday night, followed by the first Kol Hamira nullifying unseen hames. Nothing is said on Friday. The second Kol Hamira, nullifying seen and unseen hames, is said on Shabbat morning after finishing the hames.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: burning, scattering to the wind or casting into the sea.
  • Rabbi Yehudah: only burning.
  • Shulhan Arukh: all three are valid; the Rema: the custom is to burn.

In one line

Burn or otherwise dispose of hames on Friday, flush or spoil Shabbat leftovers, and say the second Kol Hamira on Shabbat morning.
