Background: Three Melachot of Selection

Borer doesn't exist in isolation — it's one of three melachot that all involve selection/sorting. Understanding the context:

The Process in the Mishkan

  • The first 11 melachot relate to preparing dyes for the roof of the Mishkan
  • The sequence: Plow → Plant → Harvest → Gather → Thresh (Dash) → then three kinds of selection:

Melacha #

Name

What It Is

How

6

Zoreh (Winnowing)

Separating pods from seeds

7

Borer (Selecting)

Removing chaff/unwanted material from seeds

Meraked (Sieving)

Ensuring everything is ground properly

Using a sieve

  • After these: Tochen (grinding, melacha 8) → then sieving (melacha 9) → kneading → baking

The Differences Between the Three

  • Zoreh — Very specific: selection through wind/air force only
  • Practical example today: blowing chaff off peanuts = de'oraita (using breath as air force to separate wanted from unwanted)
  • Not much else to discuss — very narrow melacha
  • Meraked — Very specific: selection using a sieve
  • Word comes from לרקוד (to dance) — you move the sieve up and down
  • Will be covered later in this siman
  • Borer — The broad category: ALL other kinds of selection
  • This is the main melacha we discuss — most practical, most commonly violated

The Three Conditions of Borer

Source: The Gemara (Shabbat)

תנו רבנן היו לפניו מיני אוכלין בורר ואוכל בורר ומניח ולא יבור ואם בורר חייב חטאת

  • The Baraita says three things: select, then don't select, then if you did select you're chayav
  • The Gemara asks: what does this mean?
  • Five answers given — first two rejected, three accepted
  • All three accepted answers are required together — the Rishonim agree on this

The Three Answers (= Three Conditions)

1. Rav Yosef — Type of Utensil:

  • Hand = permitted
  • Non-specialized utensil (funnel, dish) = forbidden rabbinically (patur)
  • Specialized utensil (sieve) = forbidden de'oraita (chayav)

2. Rav Hamnuna — Direction of Selection:

  • Selecting the wanted from the unwanted (ochel from psolet) = permitted
  • Selecting the unwanted from the wanted (psolet from ochel) = forbidden de'oraita

3. Abaye — Time Frame:

  • Selecting for immediate use (le'altar) = permitted
  • Selecting for later use = forbidden de'oraita (as if selecting for storage — לאוצר)

Why All Three Together = Permitted (Derech Achilah)

  • In principle, ALL acts of selection should be forbidden — because in the Mishkan they selected
  • But when all three conditions are met together → it's considered derech achilah (the normal way of eating)
  • Anything that's part of the act of eating is permitted
  • Analogy: squeezing grapes is forbidden on Shabbat → but chewing grapes in your mouth (which squeezes out juice) is fine because it's derech achilah
  • Same principle applies across all food-related melachot

The Prerequisite: There Must Be a Mixture (Ta'arovet)

  • Borer only applies when there is an existing mixture
  • If there's no mixture → no borer, even if you're "sorting"

Example — No mixture:

  • 10 identical pink lady apples, all in excellent condition
  • Taking 5 for now and leaving 5 for later = fine — there's no mixture here

What constitutes a mixture? (To be discussed in detail later)

  • Different types of nuts in one bowl = mixture
  • Different coloured apples? Different varieties?
  • Salmon: fried vs. roasted vs. coated — one food or a mixture of types?
  • Salmon with bones — is that a mixture? Or is it derech bri'ato (the way it was created)?

Seif א (Aleph): Utensils — Three Levels

The Shulchan Aruch's Ruling

הבורר אוכל מתוך פסולת... או שהיו לפניו שני מיני אוכלין ובירר מין ממין אחר

Important first point: Borer applies even when BOTH items are perfectly good food

  • Example: bowl of pistachios and peanuts — both are ochel (edible, good)
  • But right now I only want pistachios → the peanuts are "psolet" (unwanted) relative to me
  • Ochel/psolet = wanted/unwanted — NOT necessarily good/bad

The Three Levels of Utensils

Non-specialized utensil (קנון ותמחוי)

Funnel, dish, cone

  • Why the sieve is de'oraita: Using a specialized selection utensil = derech berirah (industrial/professional level). No longer considered part of eating — it's an act of melacha.
  • Why a non-specialized utensil is derabanan: It's not the professional way to select, but it's still using a tool — so Chachamim forbade it
  • Why the hand is permitted: Combined with the other two conditions → it's just part of eating

Seif ב (Bet): Immediate Use — The Time Frame

The Shulchan Aruch's Ruling

הבורר אוכל מתוך הפסולת בידו להניחו אפילו לבו ביום נעשה כבורר לאוצר וחייב

  • Even with your hand, even ochel from psolet → if you leave it for later in the day = chayav de'oraita
  • It's as if you're selecting for storage (לאוצר)

The Rema: What Does "Immediate" Mean?

וכל מה שבורר לצורך אותה סעודה שמסב בה מיד מיקרי לאלתר

  • Anything for the upcoming meal you're about to sit down to = le'altar (immediate use)
  • Even if it's for the second course, third course, or dessert
  • Don't need to stop mid-meal and go prepare each course separately
  • The norm: when you prepare a meal, you prepare ALL courses at once — that's derech achilah

Key Ruling: You Can Select for Others Too

ואפילו אחרים אוכלים עמו שרי

  • You can do borer for other people — not just yourself
  • One person can prepare and select for the whole table
  • The Mishnah Berurah: even for someone you won't be eating with — as long as for THEM it's wanted, with your hand, for their immediate use

The Rema's Practical Example

ולכן מותר לברור הירק שקורין סאלאטין מן העלים המעופשים

  • Therefore: permitted to select good lettuce leaves from bad/wilted ones when making a salad for the upcoming meal

Detailed Analysis: What Does Le'altar (Immediate) Mean?

Point 1: If You Didn't End Up Eating It All — Fine

  • Made a big salad with many acts of selection → only ate half → leaving rest for seudah shlishit
  • Permitted — because מלאכת מחשבת אסרה תורה — the Torah follows your intention at the time of the action
  • At the time of selecting → your intention was for the upcoming meal → that's what matters

Point 2: What If You Change Your Mind Immediately After?

  • Selected Brazilian nuts for the meal → finished selecting → then decided "too many calories, I don't want them"
  • Pri Megadim: Not correct to do this (doesn't explain why)
  • Sha'ar HaTziyun (Mishnah Berurah): Disagrees — "I don't understand his reasoning, he needs to prove this"
  • Chacham Ovadia: Agrees with the Mishnah Berurah — no problem
  • Ben Ish Chai: Rules it's forbidden (like the Pri Megadim)
  • Halacha: Permitted — at the time of the act, the intention was for immediate use. That's what counts.
  • Caveat: If in the back of your mind you always intended to leave it for later — that's genuinely assur. You can't trick Hashem.

Point 3: Preparing a Full Plate for Guests (Ben Ish Chai)

  • Making a big platter for guests → you know they won't finish it all
  • Permitted — the "use" is impressing your guests with a full table
  • That IS the immediate use — the presentation itself
  • Even if you're doubtful whether they'll eat any of it at all → still fine
  • The point is: I'm putting it out NOW for you to see NOW

Point 4: How Long Before the Meal? The Major Question

Three opinions:

Posek

How Long Before the Meal?

Ben Ish Chai

Up to 1 hour before (understood "sha'ah" in the Mordechai literally)

Rav Ben Tzion Abba Shaul

Up to half an hour (from "samuch le-Minchah" = half hour)

Chacham Ovadia

A few minutes — literally just before

Rabbi Kada's position: It's not about minutes — it's about mindset and engagement

  • Am I NOW doing actions for THIS meal?
  • If I have 50 guests → preparation takes 2 hours → that's all le'altar because I'm actively engaged the entire time
  • If I have 1 person → takes 2 minutes → that's le'altar
  • The frame: I am currently, actively, imminently preparing to eat

Point 5: Chacham Ovadia on Birkat HaMazon

  • Family does Birkat HaMazon in the middle of the meal → then wants dessert after
  • Someone goes to prepare dessert during Birkat HaMazon
  • Chacham Ovadia says: don't do this
  • Birkat HaMazon = ending a meal → you're NOT about to imminently eat
  • Even though dessert is 5 minutes away after benching
  • Wait until after Birkat HaMazon → then do your selecting → then eat dessert
  • This underscores: it's about mindset, not minutes

Point 6: Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach — Sometimes Hours Before Is Fine

  • Example: vegetables that need to soak in oil and lemon for 2 hours for optimal taste
  • I want to eat at 12:00 → this needs to start soaking at 10:00 → I need to select and prepare at 9:55
  • Permitted — because the ONLY way to eat this at 12:00 is to prepare it at 10:00
  • The preparation at 10:00 IS part of eating at 12:00 → it's derech achilah
  • Same logic: anything that's a necessary preliminary step for the meal = le'altar

Point 7: Borer for Animals

  • You can do borer for your animals as well — same conditions apply
  • Bi'ur Halacha discusses this — quite obvious but worth noting

Seif ג (Gimmel): Repetition of Principles

  • Repeats in different forms the first two conditions (utensil + time frame)
  • No new major chiddushim beyond Seifim 1–2

Seif ד (Dalet): Psolet from Ochel — The Third Condition

The Shulchan Aruch's Ruling

הבורר פסולת מתוך אוכל אפילו בידו אחת חייב

  • Selecting the unwanted from the wanted — even with your hand, even with ONE hand — chayav de'oraita

Why This Way Is Forbidden

  • The normal act of borer (in the Mishkan): you have good material in front of you → you remove what's bad
  • That's the industrial/professional way to select
  • When you do the OPPOSITE (take the good out from the bad) → you're doing it in a non-professional way → it's part of eating
  • Question from the shiur: "Aren't these two sides of the same coin?"
  • Answer: The norm of borer is taking away what's unwanted from a pile of what's wanted
  • Normally the majority = the ochel (wanted)
  • Taking away psolet from ochel = the way a professional would do it
  • Taking ochel from psolet = the way an eater would do it
  • That's the conceptual distinction — even though the result is the same

If You Violated Borer — Status of the Food

Intentional (Be-Mezid)

  • Standard rule of ma'aseh Shabbat: forbidden for you forever, for others until motzei Shabbat
  • Ben Ish Chai: Rules this way — the separated food has the standard issur

Can You "Undo" the Borer by Putting It Back?

  • If you put it back (mix it all together again) → then re-select properly:
  • Chacham Ovadia: Permitted — because you've removed all benefit from the original melacha
  • Similar to: if you washed a shirt on Shabbat intentionally → can wear it again after Shabbat if you dirty it first
  • Does putting it back count as teshuvah for the act?
  • Ben Ish Chai: No — you go after your machshavah at the time of the act. You did the melacha. Putting it back later doesn't retroactively undo the sin.
  • Nice gesture, but the melacha was done

Unintentional (Be-Shogeg)

  • Already learned: be-shogeg → permitted even on Shabbat itself
  • Most cases of borer violations on Shabbat are be-shogeg

Important Questions to Be Discussed in Coming Classes

  1. What counts as a "hand"? — cutlery, peelers, forks, spoons
  2. Borer with non-food items? — socks in a drawer, books on a shelf
  3. Solids mixed with liquids — a fly in soup
  4. One body = a mixture? — bones in chicken, stem on a tomato (derech bri'ato)
  5. How to define a "mixture" — different colours? different varieties? different preparations?
  6. Good from bad vs. bad from good — what if the majority is the psolet?

Key Practical Summary

  1. Borer = selecting from a mixture. No mixture → no borer.
  2. Three conditions must ALL be met: hand (not a sieve), ochel from psolet, and le'altar (immediate use).
  3. Le'altar = for the upcoming meal. All courses of that meal count. Preparing for others is fine.
  4. It's about mindset, not minutes. Am I actively, imminently preparing to eat?
  5. Don't prepare dessert during Birkat HaMazon — wait until after.
  6. If food needs soaking/preparation time → selecting earlier is fine because it's a necessary step.
  7. Ochel and psolet = wanted and unwanted — not necessarily "good" and "bad." Two good foods can be ochel/psolet relative to each other.
  8. Many people stumble with borer (Mishnah Berurah) — it's a melacha that requires careful understanding.
