Topic: Whether Hakhel applies today, and the modern commemoration at the Kotel

Sources: Parashat Vayelekh; the Gemara; Rav Eliyahu David Rabinowitz-Teomim (the Aderet), Zekher LaMikdash; Rav Kook; Rav Uziel; Rav Herzog; Hazon Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Friday 10 October 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • There is no obligation of Hakhel today. The Torah links it to "when all Israel comes to appear" at the Temple, and the Gemara learns it applies only when there is a misvah of aliyah laregel, which there is not without a Beit HaMikdash.
  • With the return to Erets Yisrael over the last 150 years, Hakhamim felt a commemoration would honour the land and encourage return.
  • An early one was the Aderet, Rav Eliyahu David Rabinowitz-Teomim, from Lithuania, later Chief Rabbi of Jerusalem (died 1905), who wrote Zekher LaMikdash. Rav Kook and the Chief Rabbis Rav Uziel and Rav Herzog supported it.
  • The first commemoration was in 1945, after the Shemitah year, and it has been held at the Kotel every seven years since. The last was in 2022, with the Chief Rabbis, the Prime Minister and President Herzog.
  • Some opposed it as a new practice before the Beit HaMikdash is rebuilt. Hazon Ovadiah held there are no halachic grounds against it and strengthened it, but no berakhot are said, since there is no obligation.
  • The next is in 2029, after the 2028 Shemitah. If we do not yet have the Beit HaMikdash, it is a beautiful practice to join if in Israel.
