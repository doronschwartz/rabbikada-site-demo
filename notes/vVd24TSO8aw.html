Topic: The Kohen status of a pesua dakka who married a convert, and whether he may marry a divorcee

Sources: A commentary on Even HaEzer; the Rambam; the Beit Meir; the Hazon Ish; the Helkat Mehokek; the Aharonim

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A Kohen who is a pesua dakka may marry a giyoret. Does he still say Birkat Kohanim, and may he enter a cemetery?
  • One of the main commentaries on Even HaEzer writes that since he may marry a giyoret he loses his Kohen status entirely: no Birkat Kohanim or Pidyon HaBen, and he may become impure to the dead.
  • The problem: the Rambam writes that a Kohen with a blemish (baal mum), including a kerut shofkhah or pesua dakka, may not serve in the Temple yet may eat kodashim. That could not be if he had lost his status.
  • So the later authorities, the Beit Meir, the Hazon Ish and many others, write that he loses his kedushah only for lineage and marriage, and keeps the full status of a Kohen in all else.
  • So today a Kohen pesua dakka married to a giyoret still says Birkat Kohanim and does Pidyon HaBen, and is forbidden to enter a cemetery. There are many contemporary teshuvot; sometimes they seek to avoid the status, and sometimes, for a Kohen who wants to marry a giyoret, to establish it.
  • May he marry a divorcee? The Helkat Mehokek and some Aharonim: yes, as with a giyoret. Others: no, since the prohibition of a giyoret for a Kohen is derived, while the divorcee is explicit in the Torah. The final halacha is not clear, and a practical case would need examination.

Sources quoted

  • The Rambam: a blemished Kohen may eat kodashim.
  • The Beit Meir and the Hazon Ish: he loses his status only for marriage.
  • The Helkat Mehokek: he may also marry a divorcee.

In one line

A Kohen pesua dakka may marry a convert but remains a Kohen in every other respect, and whether he may marry a divorcee is debated.
