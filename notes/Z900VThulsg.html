These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Where we are

This class finishes Siman 143, which is about conditions (tenaim) that a husband places in a get. The Rabbi began with a quick look at Seif 13: a condition that she not marry a man who is forbidden to her anyway. The Rema says that we are not worried that she will go on to have a forbidden relationship, so she may remarry. This differs from the case, noted at the end of the previous class, where the other man could force her, and there we do worry.

The rest of the class is one long topic: a husband gives a get on condition that his wife does or does not marry a particular man. To keep the names straight, the Rabbi called the husband Reuven, the man named in the condition Shimon, and a third man Levi.

Seif 14: "On condition that you marry Shimon"

Reuven divorces his wife on condition that she marries Shimon. This looks like a scheme, as if men were swapping wives, so the Gemara (Gittin 84a, following Rava) says she may not marry Shimon. But she also may not marry Levi or anyone else, because the condition requires Shimon.

The big question is what happens if she marries Levi anyway and has children.

  • The Rambam: the get is void and the children are mamzerim. The condition means she must go from Reuven straight to Shimon.
  • The Ravad, Tosafot and the Rema: the get is not void. Reuven only said that she must marry Shimon, not when. So she can divorce Levi and marry Shimon, and the condition is fulfilled after the fact. The Rema rules like the Ravad, and not merely as a stringency. The Gra agrees.

The Rabbi stressed how remarkable the Ravad's view is. Normally mamzerut continues through the generations, yet here children who were mamzerim become kosher once the condition is fulfilled. The whole dispute rests on one question: does "marry Shimon" mean "straight away" or "at some point"?

The Shulhan Arukh adds:

  • If she did marry Shimon despite the prohibition, then once he divorces her she is free to marry anyone, including Levi.
  • If she married Levi first, she must leave Levi and may never return to him. Then, according to the Rema, she can marry Shimon and the children become kosher.
  • If she only had kiddushin with Levi, she cannot go back to him while Reuven is alive, but she can after Reuven dies, because the problem is only a problem of marriage.

The Mordekhai: when money is behind the divorce

The Mordekhai extends the prohibition. Even without a formal condition, it is clearly wrong for a man to pay a husband to divorce his wife, or to pay her, in order to marry her. The Rabbi noted that the prohibition of coveting is not just jealousy. It means taking steps to get what is not yours, such as paying someone off or pressuring him to give her up. Even scheming about it is already a violation, and carrying it out adds another.

A question from the class: what about a couple in a long, drawn-out divorce, where one of them begins a new relationship before the get is delivered? The Rabbi distinguished between living together, which is plainly forbidden, and something much more subtle: both have agreed to divorce, they live apart, and one begins to date. The Pithei Teshuvah, based on the Mordekhai, discusses a very poor couple who cannot afford the divorce, and a man who wants the woman to waive her money, take the get and marry him. The Rabbi's view is that for a man, dating before the get is received is far easier, and it is not the end of the world. For a woman it is more of a problem. The leniency is bedieved only.

"Except" and "on condition"

The Rabbi reminded the class of the Gemara at the end of Gittin. The Mishnah has a dispute between Rabbi Eliezer and the Sages about a get that says "except for marrying Shimon" (hus). The Sages say that this is a flaw in the get, because the exception means she is only partly divorced. A condition ("on condition", al menat) is different, because it is external to the get. The plain reading is that the Sages agree that a condition works. The Ramban reads the Gemara differently, with the dispute applying to both. The Shulhan Arukh follows the Rishonim who allow the condition.

Can she marry others while the condition is open?

Take a condition that she not marry Shimon for as long as Shimon lives. May she marry someone else meanwhile? The Rabbi reminded the class of the three views, first met earlier in the siman:

  • Rabbenu Hananel is the strictest. Whenever there is any chance that she may end up unable to keep the condition, she may not remarry until it is settled, here until Shimon dies.
  • The Ramban and the Ran allow it when two things are true together: it is a prohibition ("do not marry") and not something she has to do, and it is in her hands, since she cannot be married to Shimon against her will.
  • The Rambam is the most lenient. She may marry immediately even if the condition is something she must do, such as giving money later.

The Shulhan Arukh rules like the Ramban and the Ran, though he also quotes a second opinion. The general rule is that the halacha follows the second opinion quoted.

The Rosh: can the husband cancel the condition?

Reuven divorced his wife on condition that she not marry Shimon. Now Reuven and his ex-wife both agree: she wants to marry Shimon, and Reuven does not mind. Can he cancel the condition?

The Rosh says no. The only way is for Reuven to remarry her, or at least betroth her again, and then divorce her without a condition. He cannot cancel the condition and he cannot give a new get, because she is no longer under his control. All the hold he has left is that she cannot marry Shimon.

The Rabbi explained the logic. With other conditions the husband keeps some control: "I may come back in twelve months" or "you must give me 200 pounds" leaves her dependent on him. With "do not marry Shimon" she is free in every other respect, and he has nothing left to cancel. In the discussion a student asked whether this is just two kinds of condition, and the Rabbi pointed out that the difference is who holds the control. The Beit Shemuel asks why this differs from other conditions that the parties can cancel together, and gives several answers.

If she married Levi and then Levi divorced her, she still cannot go back to Shimon. There is also the reverse of the Ravad's case. If she married Levi, divorced him and then married Shimon, the condition was broken, so the get is retroactively void. A child who grew up for eighteen years as perfectly kosher suddenly becomes a mamzer. As the Rabbi said, "Amazing."

If the condition was only "do not have relations with Shimon", and she did without marrying him, the get still stands and the children are kosher. It is a sin, but it does not affect the get.

Conditions about relations

The Shulhan Arukh next deals with "on condition that you have relations with Shimon". The Rishonim say this should not be done, because the first act would take place while she is still married and the get takes effect only afterwards. The class debated whether the get and the relations happen at the same moment, and whether the retroactive effect of the get removes the problem. The Rabbi raised several questions and gave no final answer.

The opposite condition, "do not have relations with Shimon", is different. Here the Shulhan Arukh says she may not remarry until Shimon dies, because Shimon could force her. Everyone agrees, even the Rambam: when the condition is not entirely in her hands, she must wait.

A condition that lasts forever

A condition cannot bind a woman for the rest of her life, such as "never eat meat or drink wine". But a condition not to marry Shimon for as long as he lives does work. The Gemara does not distinguish even if he is a child and she is ninety; fifty years is only an example. The Rambam and the Rashba dispute how to read "never marry Shimon" with no time given. The Rabbi explained it with the image of a wine that may be spilled or a tree that may be cut down. The Shulhan Arukh rules strictly: she may not marry, and if she did marry she would need a get from the second husband too.

In practice

The Rabbi mentioned the Sefer HaTerumah's advice that dayanim should not get involved in a get with a condition like "do not go to your father's house". In practice a woman cannot be controlled this way. The standard procedure for a get today leaves out conditions and compulsion, though if there is a real need, a conditional get can still be done.

Practical summary

  • A get with a condition about marrying a particular man is delicate, and a dayan should avoid it.
  • If the condition requires Shimon and she marries Levi first, the Rambam says the get is void and the children are mamzerim. The Ravad and the Rema say it can be repaired by divorcing Levi and marrying Shimon. The Rema's ruling is like the Ravad.
  • A husband who has already divorced his wife cannot cancel the condition or give another get. He must remarry her and divorce her again without a condition.
  • A condition that is not entirely in her hands, such as "do not have relations with Shimon", means she may not remarry until Shimon dies.
  • A condition binding her forever is a problem, but one tied to the lifetime of a named man works.
  • Paying a husband to divorce his wife, or paying a woman in order to marry her, is forbidden and can affect the get.
