Topic: The source of selling Hames, when it is appropriate, and why it must be a real sale

Sources: The Tosefta; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Vilna Gaon; the Bayit Hadash

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Wednesday 3 April 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The first source is the Tosefta: a Jew on a ship with a non-Jew as the deadline before Pesah approaches, with much Hames he does not want to throw overboard, may give or sell it to the non-Jew and buy it back after Pesah. The Rambam records it in this context.
  • The Shulhan Arukh omits the ship: one may give or sell Hames to a non-Jew, even if it stays in one's house, and buy it back after Pesah.
  • The Vilna Gaon, the Bayit Hadash and many poskim were concerned it would become routine, and ruled one should rely on it only for great loss (Hefsed Merubah).
  • So shops, which cannot clear their stock, may sell, as may someone with a large whisky collection. A regular person with some pasta or bread should not, in the first instance, sell minor things.
  • Individuals should do their best to dispose of all Hames; one left with a lot, which he considers a considerable loss, may sell it.
  • It is a real sale, not a formality. Originally the non-Jew was given the key; today the form gives the owner's phone number and address and exactly where the Hames is, so he can access it. Blocking his access would mean the sale is not real and one owns Hames on Pesah.
  • Because of the many details, the Rav now sells as agent (Shaliah) for everyone, but each person should take it seriously.

Sources quoted

  • The Tosefta: the Jew and non-Jew on a ship.
  • The Rambam: records it in that context.
  • Shulhan Arukh: records the sale without the ship.
  • The Vilna Gaon and the Bayit Hadash: rely on it only for great loss.

In one line

Selling Hames is a real sale, meant for significant loss, and the non-Jew must truly be able to reach what he bought.
