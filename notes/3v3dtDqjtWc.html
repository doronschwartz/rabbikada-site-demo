Introduction and Review

Five Categories of Muktzeh Covered So Far:

  1. Muktzeh Machmat Chesron Kis (מוקצה מחמת חסרון כיס) - Items set aside due to monetary loss concerns
  2. Kli Shemelachto L'Issur (כלי שמלאכתו לאיסור) - Utensils whose primary purpose is for forbidden work
  3. Kli Shemelachto L'Heter (כלי שמלאכתו להיתר) - Utensils whose primary purpose is for permitted work
  4. Muktzeh Machmat Gufo (מוקצה מחמת גופו) - Items that are inherently useless
  5. Muktzeh Machmat Issur (מוקצה מחמת איסור) - Items forbidden due to potential prohibition (introduced in this shiur)

Additional Principles Previously Learned:

  • Shivrei Kelim (שברי כלים) - Broken remnants of utensils retain their original status
  • Tzorech Mekomo (צורך מקומו) - Moving for the sake of the space
  • Tzorech Gufo (צורך גופו) - Moving for the sake of using the item itself

Siman 308, Seif 14 (שח, סעיף יד)

Halacha: Shoe and Mold

Text: מנעל חדש מותר לשומטו מעל הדפוס, אף על פי שמלאכתו לאיסור, כיוון דשם כלי עליו, מותר לטלטלו לצורך מקומו

Translation: A new shoe may be removed from its mold, even though the mold is a kli shemelachto l'issur, since it has the status of a utensil, it may be moved for tzorech mekomo.

Detailed Explanation:

  • A newly made shoe still within its manufacturing mold
  • The mold is classified as kli shemelachto l'issur because its primary purpose is making shoes
  • Making shoes on Shabbat is forbidden (either boneh - building, or makeh b'patish - finishing touch)
  • Despite this, one may extract the shoe because this constitutes tzorech mekomo (needing the space where the shoe is located)
  • This is a direct application of the principle that kli shemelachto l'issur may be moved for tzorech mekomo

Siman 308, Seif 15 (שח, סעיף טו)

Halacha: Broken Sandal Straps

Text: סנדל שנפסק רצועה פנימית, עדיין תורת כלי עליו ומותר לטלטלו. נפסקה החיצונה, בטל מתורת כלי ואסור לטלטלו

Translation: A sandal whose inner strap broke still retains the status of a utensil and may be moved. If the outer strap broke, it loses its utensil status and is forbidden to move.

Detailed Explanation:

Inner Strap Broken:

  • Sandal retains torat kli (status of utensil)
  • Still permitted to move and wear
  • Reason: People are not embarrassed to repair inner straps, so it retains potential use

Outer Strap Broken:

  • Loses torat kli status
  • Becomes muktzeh and forbidden to move
  • Reason: Highly embarrassing to wear, so no one would repair it, removing its practical utility

Special Allowance in Public (Karmelit):

Text: ואם הוא בכרמלית, מותר לכרוך עליו גמי או מאכל בהמה לתקנו שלא יפול מרגלו

  • In a public area (karmelit), one may tie bamboo or animal food as temporary strap
  • Purpose: Prevent the sandal from falling off while walking home
  • Limitation: ובחצר שאינו נשמר שם, אסור - In a private courtyard, this is forbidden

Key Principle: Kavod HaBriyot (כבוד הבריות)

Mishna Berura's Addition: In a courtyard with many people where walking without a shoe would be embarrassing, the temporary repair is permitted.

Fundamental Rule: Kavod HaBriyot overrides Muktzeh

  • When human dignity is at stake, muktzeh restrictions are overridden
  • Muktzeh is fundamentally rabbinic (d'rabbanan)
  • Human dignity takes precedence over rabbinic prohibitions
  • This principle applies beyond just this specific case of sandals

Modern Application:

  • Today's shoes: If shoelace breaks, typically still usable, so not muktzeh
  • If sole completely detaches: Becomes muktzeh
  • Heel falling off: Debatable - depends on whether people commonly repair such damage

Siman 308, Seif 16 (שח, סעיף טז)

Halacha: Garment with Reed for Drying

Text: חלוק שקיפצוהו ותחבו בו קנה לתלות בו ליבשו. יכול לשומטו מעל הקנה, אבל ליקח הקנה מתוכו אסור, לפי שאינו כלי

Translation: A garment that was laundered and had a reed inserted to hang it for drying - one may remove the garment from the reed, but taking the reed from within it is forbidden because it lacks the status of a utensil.

Detailed Explanation:

The Reed (קנה):

  • Used as one-time hanging device (not permanent designation)
  • Lacks torat kli status
  • Not commonly used for hanging clothes (irregular use)
  • Therefore: Muktzeh machmat gufo

Permitted Action:

  • Removing the garment: Allowed because this is tilul min hatzad (indirect movement)
  • The reed moves incidentally as a result of removing the garment

Forbidden Action:

  • Directly extracting the reed: Prohibited because this constitutes direct movement of muktzeh

If a Proper Utensil is Used:

Text: ואם תחבו בו כלי, מותר ליטול מתוכו אפילו מלאכתו לאיסור

  • If an actual utensil (with torat kli) was used instead of a reed
  • Permitted to remove even if it's kli shemelachto l'issur
  • Reason: This falls under tzorech mekomo (like the mold in seif 14)

Modern Application:

  • Modern hangers have torat kli status, so this entire discussion is irrelevant
  • The principle of tilul min hatzad remains relevant in other contexts

Major Principle: Migo D'Itkatzai (מיגו דאיתקצאי)

Mishna Berura's Important Addition:

Wet Garments at Shabbat's Onset:

  • Garment must have been dried before Shabbat began
  • If wet when Shabbat entered: Remains muktzeh for entire Shabbat, even after drying

Definition of "Wet" (טופח על מנת להטפיח):

  • Wet enough to make something else wet upon contact
  • If it cannot wet something else, it's not considered "wet" in halachic terms
  • This principle applies across many areas of halacha

The Principle: Migo D'Itkatzai L'Vein HaShmashot, Itkatzai L'Chulei Shabbat

Translation: "Since it was muktzeh during twilight, it remains muktzeh for the entire Shabbat"

Detailed Explanation:

  • If an item was muktzeh during bein hashmashot (twilight period), it stays muktzeh all Shabbat
  • Exception: This only applies when the change in status was uncertain at Shabbat's onset
  • If certain natural change will occur: The principle doesn't apply

Reasoning (סברא):

  • When something is muktzeh at twilight, you "set your mind aside" from it
  • Since the change was uncertain, your mental state removed it from consideration
  • But: If 100% certain it will dry/change during Shabbat, no mental removal occurs

Practical Applications:

  • Item will definitely dry due to sun, time, or temperature: Not permanently muktzeh
  • Uncertain whether change will occur: Remains muktzeh entire Shabbat
  • This principle extends to drying figs and similar items (to be covered in Siman 310)

Fifth Category of Muktzeh: Muktzeh Machmat Issur

Why is a Wet Garment Muktzeh?

  • Not due to any of the four previous categories
  • New category: Muktzeh Machmat Issur (מוקצה מחמת איסור)

Definition:

  • Items made muktzeh due to potential prohibition one might perform with them
  • Chachamim prohibited the item itself to prevent violation of the underlying issur

Wet Garment Example:

  • Concern: People might squeeze out water (melaben - laundering)
  • Solution: Make entire garment muktzeh, not just prohibit squeezing
  • Status: Higher level muktzeh than kli shemelachto l'issur

Comparison to Kli Shemelachto L'Issur:

  • Kli shemelachto l'issur: May be moved for tzorech mekomo and tzorech gufo
  • Muktzeh machmat issur: Cannot be moved for any purpose - more stringent prohibition

Siman 308, Seif 17 (שח, סעיף יז)

Halacha: Oven and Bench with Fallen Legs

Text: קירה שנשמטה אפילו אחת מרגליה, אסור לטלטלה. וכן ספסל ארוך שנשמטה אחת מרגליו, אסור לטלטלה, אלא אם כן ישב עליה פעם אחת מבעוד שבת

Translation: An oven from which even one leg has slipped off is forbidden to move. Similarly, a long bench from which one leg has slipped off is forbidden to move, unless one sat on it once before Shabbat.

Detailed Explanation:

The Prohibition:

  • Applies when legs slip off (not break completely)
  • Entire item becomes muktzeh machmat issur
  • Cannot be moved even for tzorech mekomo or tzorech gufo

Reasoning:

  • Concern: Person might reattach the leg (violation of boneh - building)
  • Chachamim's response: Prohibit moving the entire item to prevent this violation
  • Classification: Muktzeh machmat issur (not kli shemelachto l'issur)

Exception - Pre-Shabbat Usage:

Text: אלא אם כן ישב עליה פעם אחת מבעוד שבת

  • If one sat on the broken bench before Shabbat
  • Demonstrates acceptance of the item in its current state
  • Shows no intention to repair it
  • Therefore: Not muktzeh (no concern about fixing)

Distinction: Slipped vs. Broken:

  • Leg slipped off: Easy to reattach, so becomes muktzeh
  • Leg broken in half: Difficult to repair, so remains usable (can sit on half-bench)
  • Bench broke in two: Each half retains utility, not muktzeh

Modern Applications:

Eyeglasses:
  • If one side slips off: May become muktzeh machmat issur
  • Exception: If worn with one side before Shabbat (shows acceptance of current state)
  • Demonstrates comfort using item in current condition
Building Site Ladders:
  • Builders' ladders left over weekend: Likely muktzeh
  • Household ladders with multiple uses: Generally not muktzeh
  • Depends on primary intended use and circumstances

Siman 308, Seif 18 (שח, סעיף יח)

Halacha: Leftover Bricks

Text: לבנים שנשארו מהבנין, מותר לטלטלם, דמעתה לא קיימו לבנין אלא למסגע

Translation: Bricks remaining from construction may be moved, as they are no longer designated for building but for sitting.

Detailed Explanation:

Normal Status of Bricks:

  • Usually: Muktzeh machmat gufo (no torat kli, no regular use)
  • Raw materials: Not utensils, no standard function

Special Case - Construction Completion:

  • When building is finished and bricks remain
  • Automatic assumption: Leftover bricks intended for sitting
  • No special designation required: Status changes automatically
  • Reasoning: Neat arrangement + no more building = seating purpose

Condition for Permission:

  • Bricks must be arranged neatly (not in large construction pile)
  • Clear indication that building phase is complete

Exception:

Text: אם סדרן זה על גבי זה, גילה דעתו שהוא חס עליהם

  • If piled up in large stacks: Shows intention to preserve for future building
  • Remains muktzeh machmat gufo
  • Cannot be moved for any purpose

Key Point: This represents automatic designation without conscious decision - unique case where circumstances create the change in status.

Siman 308, Seif 19 (שח, סעיף יט)

Halacha: Dangerous Objects in Public Domain

Text: קוץ המונח ברשות הרבים, מותר לטלטלו פחות פחות מארבע אמות

Translation: A thorn placed in the public domain may be moved less than four amot at a time.

Detailed Explanation:

The Scenario:

  • Dangerous object (thorn, glass, etc.) in public street
  • Poses injury risk to pedestrians
  • Not pikuach nefesh (life-threatening) but harmful

In Reshut HaRabim (Public Domain):

  • Permitted: Move less than 4 amot (approximately 2 meters) at a time
  • Forbidden: Carry 4+ amot in one go (Torah prohibition)
  • Reasoning: Moving less than 4 amot is only d'rabbanan (rabbinic extension)

In Karmelit (Semi-Public Domain):

Text: ובכרמלית, מותר לטלטלו להדיה

  • Permitted: Move completely until danger is eliminated
  • No restrictions: Can carry as far as needed
  • Many modern streets fall into this category

Fundamental Principle: Heizek Rabbim (היזק רבים)

Text: משום דחיישינן שמא יזוקו בו רבים, ומקום הזקה דרבים לא גזור רבנן שבות

Translation: Because we are concerned that many people might be harmed by it, and in cases of public harm, the Sages did not decree their prohibitions.

Categories of D'Rabbanan:

  1. Pure D'Rabbanan: Complete rabbinic invention (like muktzeh)
  2. Torah-Rooted D'Rabbanan: Rabbinic extensions of Torah laws (like carrying less than 4 amot)

Even the most stringent d'rabbanan (Torah-rooted) is overridden for public safety

Comparison to Private Domain:

  • In house: Glass fragments may be moved due to personal danger
  • Only muktzeh issue: Overcome by any level of harm (even individual)
  • Principle: Muktzeh overridden even for single-person harm

Modern Applications:

  • Banana peel on sidewalk: Permitted to remove
  • Broken glass in street: May be cleared
  • Any public hazard: Removal permitted despite carrying restrictions

Key Distinction:

  • Individual harm: Overrides pure d'rabbanan (muktzeh)
  • Public harm: Overrides even Torah-rooted d'rabbanan (carrying restrictions)

Siman 308, Seif 20 (שח, סעיף כ) - Designation Principles Begin

Halacha: Palm Branches and Designation

Text: חריות של דקל שקוצצים לשריפה, מוקצה הם ואסור לטלטלם. ישב עליהם מעט מבעוד יום, מותר לשבת עליהם בשבת

Translation: Palm branches cut for burning are muktzeh and forbidden to move. If one sat on them briefly before the day [Shabbat], it is permitted to sit on them on Shabbat.

Detailed Explanation:

Initial Status:

  • Palm branches cut for fuel: Muktzeh (intended for forbidden use)
  • Classification: Muktzeh machmat gufo or machmat issur

Designation Methods (Three Levels):

1. Physical Action (Ma'aseh):

Text: וכל שכן כשמשמשים בהם למעשה

  • Actually performing the intended action (sitting, tying, arranging)
  • Strongest form of designation
  • Clearly demonstrates intention
2. Mental Designation (Machshava):

Text: או אם חשב עליהם מבעוד יום לשמשם לכך

  • Simply thinking about using them for the purpose
  • This specific case: Sufficient for palm branches
  • Reason: These items have dual common uses (fuel AND sitting)
3. Permanent vs. Temporary Designation:
  • For items with dual uses: Even one-time mental designation sufficient
  • For irregular uses: Requires permanent designation

Why Lenient Here:

  • Palm branches commonly used both for fuel and seating
  • Equal usage pattern: Makes designation easier
  • Not irregular: Both uses are normal for this item

Siman 308, Seif 21 (שח, סעיף כא)

Halacha: Pile of Building Stones

Text: אבל נדבך של אבנים, אף על פי שחשב עליהם מבעוד יום, אסור לשבת עליהם, אלא אם כן עמדום

Translation: But regarding a pile of stones, even if one thought about them before the day, it is forbidden to sit on them unless one set them up [physically].

Detailed Explanation:

The Contrast:

  • Previous case (palm branches): Thought alone sufficient
  • This case (building stones): Physical action required

Why More Stringent:

  • Primary purpose: Building (forbidden on Shabbat)
  • Secondary purpose: Sitting (very irregular)
  • Already arranged for building: Shows clear primary intention

Required Action:

Text: אלא אם כן עמדום... לסדרם

  • Must physically rearrange stones
  • Arrangement must show seating intention
  • Action demonstrates: Change from building purpose to seating purpose

The Stringent Opinion:

  • Rama brings different view: Some require physical action in all cases
  • Not the accepted halacha: Main ruling follows lenient view

Siman 308, Seif 22 (שח, סעיף כב) - Main Practical Rule

Halacha: Stone for Door/Barrel

Text: אסור לכסות פי חבית באבן או בקרש, לסגור בו את הדלת, או להחליד בו את הבריח. אף על פי שחשב עליהם מבעוד יום, אלא אם כן יחדה לכך לעולם

Translation: It is forbidden to cover a barrel's mouth with a stone or plank, to close a door with it, or to secure a bolt with it, even if one thought about them before the day, unless one designated it for this purpose permanently.

Detailed Explanation:

The Standard Case:

  • Items: Stones, planks for irregular household uses
  • Uses: Door stops, barrel covers, etc.
  • Status: Irregular usage pattern

Designation Requirements:

Insufficient - Temporary Designation:

Text: אבל יחדה לשבת זו בלבד, לא

  • Designating for one Shabbat only: Insufficient
  • Must be permanent mental designation
Required - Permanent Designation:

Text: אלא אם כן יחדה לכך לעולם

  • Must mentally designate "forever"
  • Practical meaning: Not necessarily actual forever use
  • Mental state: Willingness to keep it for this purpose indefinitely

Exception for Common Uses:

Text: אבל בכל מידי דאורחא בהכי... דהא מהני בייחוד דשבת אחת סגי

  • For common uses: One-time designation sufficient
  • Example given: Using stones to crack nuts (apparently common then)
  • Connects to: Palm branch case from seif 20

Practical Modern Example:

  • Lost doorstop scenario: Find rock outside
  • Required action: Mental designation as permanent doorstop
  • Result: May use for any purpose on Shabbat (not just doorstop)
  • Future: When better doorstop acquired, may discard rock
  • Key point: Mental permanent designation, not actual permanent use

Multiple Opinions:

  1. Main opinion: Mental designation sufficient for irregular uses (if permanent)
  2. Some say: No difference between common and irregular
  3. Most stringent: Always require physical action

Accepted halacha follows the main opinion

Siman 308, Seif 23 (שח, סעיף כג)

Halacha: Tree Branch as Fan

Text: מותר לחתוך ענף מן הדקל מבעוד יום, ומותר להניח בו על השולחן בשבת להבריח זבובים

Translation: It is permitted to cut a branch from a palm tree before the day, and it is permitted to place it on the table on Shabbat to chase away flies.

Detailed Explanation:

The Action Creates Status:

Text: כיון שלצורך חיתוכו, עשאו כלי גמור

  • Cutting before Shabbat: Shows intention to make it into a utensil
  • Action of cutting: Transforms it into a complete kli
  • Result: No longer muktzeh machmat gufo

Permitted Uses:

  • Fanning away flies
  • Mishna Berura adds: Scaring children
  • Any other appropriate use (now has torat kli)

Key Principle:

  • Pre-Shabbat action: Can transform muktzeh items into kelim
  • Cutting specifically for Shabbat use: Clear demonstration of intention
  • Complete transformation: Full kli status, not just designated item

Siman 308, Seif 24 (שח, סעיף כד)

Halacha: Prepared Medical Materials

Text: פשתן סרוק ומנופץ שניתן על המכה, אם חשב עליו מבעוד יום לצמצם המכה... יצאו לדם שם מוקצה, ומשום רפואה נמי ליכא

Translation: Combed and beaten flax that is placed on a wound - if one thought about it before the day to bandage the wound... it loses its muktzeh status, and there is also no problem of healing.

Detailed Explanation:

The Item:

  • Combed flax: Raw material typically muktzeh
  • Intended use: Medical bandage/dressing

Methods of Designation:

  1. Mental designation: Thinking about using it for wound care
  2. Sitting on it: Physical action showing intended use
  3. Dipping in oil: Preparation action
  4. Wrapping with string: Making it into a bandage

Two Issues Resolved:

  1. Muktzeh problem: Solved by proper designation
  2. Medical prohibition: Not considered medicine

Not Medicine Because:

Text: שאינו אלא כמין מלבוש

  • Functions like clothing/dressing
  • Not active medical treatment
  • Simply protective covering

Alternative Opinion:

Text: ויש אומרים דלעולם אסור עד שיעשה מעשה

  • Some require physical action always
  • Same stringent view from previous seifim
  • Not the accepted halacha

Siman 308, Seif 25-26 (שח, סעיף כה-כו) - Final Examples

Seif 25: Animal Skins

Text: עורות יבשים, בין של אומן בין של בעל הבית, מותר לטלטלם

Translation: Dry skins, whether of a craftsman or homeowner, are permitted to move.

Reason: Can be used as rugs and mats - have regular household function

Rama's addition: Some distinguish between large and small animals based on practical usage.

Seif 26: Wooden Planks

Text: נסרים של בעל הבית, מותר לטלטלם

Translation: A homeowner's planks are permitted to move.

Distinction:

  • Homeowner's planks: Often used around house, so permitted
  • Craftsman's planks: Forbidden unless designated before Shabbat (professional materials)

Summary of Major Principles from This Shiur

1. Kavod HaBriyot (Human Dignity)

  • Overrides muktzeh restrictions
  • When embarrassment/dignity at stake
  • Applies beyond specific case of broken sandals

2. Muktzeh Machmat Issur (Fifth Category)

  • Items forbidden due to potential violation
  • Higher level than kli shemelachto l'issur
  • Cannot be moved even for tzorech mekomo/gufo
  • Examples: wet garments, items with detached legs

3. Migo D'Itkatzai Principle

  • Items muktzeh at twilight remain muktzeh all Shabbat
  • Exception: When certain natural change will occur
  • Reasoning: Mental setting aside vs. definite expectation

4. Heizek Rabbim (Public Harm)

  • Overrides even strong d'rabbanan restrictions
  • Distinction between pure and Torah-rooted d'rabbanan
  • Practical applications for public safety

5. Designation Principles

  • Three levels: Common use, irregular use, very rare use
  • Methods: Action, thought, permanent vs. temporary
  • Key rule: Irregular uses require permanent mental designation
  • Result: Once designated, usable for any purpose

6. Tilul Min HaTzad

  • Indirect movement distinguished from direct movement
  • Permitted when primary action is for permitted item
  • Important for practical applications
