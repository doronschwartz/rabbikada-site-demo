Topic: Whether one may look at the Kohanim during Birkat Kohanim, and why

Sources: Gemara Hagigah; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Birkat Kohanim & Prayer, Thursday 16 June 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Children are often told that looking at the Kohanim's hands makes one blind. The source is Gemara Hagigah: in the Temple, when the Kohanim blessed with the Shem HaMeforash, one who looked at them had his eyes dimmed.
  • Then the Shekhinah was strongly present, and the Kohanim's hands were uncovered, so gazing was inappropriate.
  • Today there is no Bet HaMikdash and no Shem HaMeforash, so that punishment does not apply. Still, Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh codify that one should not gaze at the Kohanim while their hands are uncovered, because it diverts attention from the berakhah.
  • When the Kohanim cover their hands, some poskim allow looking; others still do not, as it distracts. It is about kavanah, not something mystical.
  • Some turn their backs to the Kohanim so as not to look. This is not correct: one faces forward with eyes lowered, as in the Amidah or Shema, or looks in the siddur. One does not turn one's back on those blessing him.

In one line

Do not gaze at the Kohanim during the blessing, to keep one's focus, but face them with eyes lowered rather than turning away.
