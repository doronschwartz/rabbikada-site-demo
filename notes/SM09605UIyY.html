Topic: Whether the name is Matityah or Matityahu, in a get and in Al HaNissim

Sources: Megillat Antiochus; Bet Yoseph; Terumat HaDeshen; Ezra; Divrei HaYamim

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Matityah had five sons who fought the Greeks, and as Megillat Antiochus relates he went to war himself. Some call him Matityah, others Matityahu.
  • The question arises in writing a get, where the Bet Din must write names precisely; an error can affect its validity.
  • The Bet Yoseph quotes the Terumat HaDeshen (Rabbi Yisrael Isserlein, Austria): write Matityahu, since that is the name in common use ("shem hamushar befihem"), even though Ezra and Divrei HaYamim write Matityah. This shows the medieval Ashkenazi pronunciation, reflected in their siddur.
  • The Bet Yoseph responds that Tanakh says Matityah and we call such people Matityah, as in our Al HaNissim, so a parent presumably gave that name; the get is written Matityah.
  • That is the halacha for Sepharadim today: a get is written Matityah, and our siddur says "Bimei Matityah ben Yohanan Kohen Gadol".
  • Later Ashkenazi poskim, because of the doubt, have two gittin given, one with each name.
  • For Hanukkah: for Sepharadim the hero is Matityah, as in Tanakh, the more correct form; Ashkenazim say Matityahu.

In one line

Sepharadim write and say Matityah, following the Bet Yoseph; Ashkenazim say Matityahu and give two gittin when the name arises.
