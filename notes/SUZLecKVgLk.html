Topic: The custom of saying the thunder and lightning blessings without Hashem's name, and further details

Sources: The Ben Ish Hai; Hakham Ovadiah; the Gemara; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Rainbows, Thunder & Lightning, Tuesday 1 November 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A proper blessing includes Shem UMalkhut, Hashem's name and kingship. An old custom, recorded by Sepharadi and Ashkenazi poskim, is to say these blessings in abridged form, "Barukh Oseh Maaseh Bereshit", without Shem UMalkhut.
  • The main reason: the time frame is only seconds, and some feared it would not be said in time. Another: a blessing is repeated only for a new storm, after the clouds have dispersed and returned, which is hard to know.
  • The Ben Ish Hai records this as the custom of Baghdad, and that a colleague in Yerushalayim said the same.
  • Hakham Ovadiah quotes many who disagree: it is a shame to lose a blessing the Rabbis instituted, as on the rainbow and the sea. Saying the full blessing is the custom of the Ashkenazim and some Sepharadim.
  • Those who follow the Ben Ish Hai have what to rely on. Without an established custom, the default, per the Gemara, the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh, is the full blessing.
  • One who missed the blessing may say it the next time he sees or hears, unlike Shehiyanu, whose moment passes once the joy is experienced. It must be within a few moments.
  • Standing is customary, not required; one may say it lying in bed. Woken at night, one may just rub one's hands and say it, rather than wait to wash and miss the time.

Sources quoted

  • The Ben Ish Hai: the Baghdad custom not to say the full blessing.
  • Hakham Ovadiah: one should not lose the full blessing.
  • The Gemara, the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh: the default is the full blessing.

In one line

Unless one's custom follows the Ben Ish Hai, the thunder and lightning blessings are said in full, at once, even lying in bed.
