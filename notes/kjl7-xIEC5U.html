Topic: Honouring parents after their death

Sources: The Talmud; the Ramban; Rav Moshe Feinstein; Rav Ovadia Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Honouring Parents, Thursday 21 January 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The misvah continues after a parent's death, as long as we live. The Talmud says one honours a parent in life and in death.
  • The Ramban says honour after death is even greater: in life it may be mixed with motives such as inheritance or fear; after death it is pure.
  • For the first twelve months, when quoting a parent one says "hareni kapparat mishkavo", "I am an atonement for his resting place", because the severity of Gehinnom lasts twelve months.
  • How can words atone? Rav Moshe Feinstein: it is not literal; it is an expression of respect. Rav Ovadia Yoseph: a child willing to take on suffering for a parent shows what kind of child the parent raised, and that itself elevates the parent's soul.
  • Saying Kaddish, the Hashkavah, lighting candles and the like all belong to this misvah.
  • After twelve months one says "zikhrono livrakhah" or "alav hashalom". In English, "my late father" or "my late mother" fulfils this; mentioning a deceased parent with no title or adjective does not.

In one line

Honour continues after death: "hareni kapparat mishkavo" for twelve months, then "of blessed memory" (or "my late father"), with Kaddish and memorials part of the misvah.
