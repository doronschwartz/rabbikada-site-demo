The halacha

  • Maggid is not a prayer but a springboard for retelling the Exodus. Reading words one does not understand is not reading, and telling the story in a language one does not understand does not fulfil this biblical misvah. So the key parts must be translated and explained.
  • The Gemara requires beginning with disgrace and ending with praise, and debates whether physical or spiritual; we rule both. Physical: Avadim Hayinu. Spiritual: "Mitehilah ovdei avodah zarah hayu avoteinu", Terah father of Avraham, until we became a people with the covenant and the Torah.
  • The essential parts are the question and answer, the spiritual past, and the three things one must say at the end. Next in importance: Bekhol Dor VaDor, Ha Lahma Anya and the Ten Plagues.
  • This is equally a misvah for women; it is a shame when those who worked hardest preparing do not engage with the Haggadah.
  • Sepharadi Haggadot follow the Rambam's text, "leharot et asmo", to show oneself, through actions that bring the story to life; Ashkenazi texts read "lirot", to see oneself, to personally experience it.
  • The misvah is not limited to the Haggadah text: one may read the Torah, Midrashim or books like Meam Loez, during the meal too. The Shulhan Arukh says one should talk about the Exodus until sleep overtakes him.
  • The misvah is for the father to tell the children. Children's divrei Torah from school are wonderful, but the Baal HaBayit should translate the key paragraphs and tell the story simply.

In one line

The Haggadah must be understood, not just read: translate the key paragraphs, discuss freely all night, and let parents tell the story to their children.
