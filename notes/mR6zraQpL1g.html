Topic: Whether one may say "good morning" or "good night" on Tisha BeAv

Sources: The Gemara; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Mishnah Berurah; Rav Hayim Palagi; the Kaf HaHayim; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Wednesday 14 July 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Besides the five prohibitions shared with Yom Kippur, Tisha BeAv forbids Torah study and greeting others (Sheilat Shalom).
  • The Rambam and Shulhan Arukh, based on the Gemara, forbid greeting and asking after someone's welfare the whole of Tisha BeAv, before and after midday. If greeted, one may answer solemnly so as not to slight the person, but not hold a full conversation.
  • Many poskim, Ashkenazi and Sepharadi (the Mishnah Berurah, Rav Hayim Palagi, the Kaf HaHayim), include "good morning" and "good night", which should then be avoided; one only answers solemnly.
  • Hakham Ovadiah brings a Rishon who held a mere "good morning" or "good night" is not inquiring about welfare ("how are you, how is the family"), and rules there is what to rely on, provided no full conversation follows.
  • So strictly one may give a simple good night after the service or good morning in synagogue.
  • Hakham Ovadiah and other poskim also allow wishing "Mazal Tov", for example on a birth, or a long life, and asking "how is your fast?".

Sources quoted

  • The Rambam and Shulhan Arukh: no greeting all of Tisha BeAv.
  • The Mishnah Berurah, Rav Hayim Palagi, the Kaf HaHayim: "good morning" is included.
  • Hakham Ovadiah: a simple "good morning" or "good night" is not.

In one line

On Tisha BeAv one does not ask after others' welfare, but a simple good morning, Mazal Tov or "how is your fast?" is permitted.
