Topic: Marrying more than one wife: the Torah's permission and the ban of Rabbenu Gershom

Sources: Shulhan Arukh, Even HaEzer, Siman 1; the Gemara; the Rashba; the Mordekhai

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Wednesday 11 February 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Though largely a moot issue today, the history is worth understanding. The Torah and the Hakhamim clearly allow a man more than one wife, as recorded in the first Siman of the Shulhan Arukh, provided he can sustain them financially and emotionally. The Gemara recommends no more than four.
  • Permitted is not ideal. In the Torah, Avraham married Hagar at Sarah's urging, Yaakov married Leah only because he was tricked, Bilhah and Zilpah came on the advice of Rahel and Leah, and Elkanah probably took Peninah because Hannah was barren.
  • The verse speaks of a man and woman becoming one unit, which is hard with more than one wife, and the Neviim compare the relationship of HaShem and Israel to one husband and one wife. No Tanna in the Talmud is recorded as having more than one wife.
  • Rabbenu Gershom (960 to 1040) famously banned polygamy. Reasons given: to prevent mistreatment of wives (Rashba); to prevent domestic strife (Mordekhai); to prevent half siblings from distant places unknowingly marrying; and to give the wife standing equal to the man.
  • One of the Aharonim wrote that the ban came from fear of Christian persecution in France and Germany, but this view has not really been accepted.

In one line

Halacha permits more than one wife but the ideal was always one, and Rabbenu Gershom banned it around a thousand years ago for several stated reasons.
