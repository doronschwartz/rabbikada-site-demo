Topic: Is a child from a mamzer's donated sperm a mamzer?

Sources: The verse "lo yavo mamzer bikhal HaShem"; Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 13 July 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The case, unlikely but with important ramifications: a single Jewish woman receives sperm from an anonymous donor known to be a mamzer.
  • The vast majority hold the child is still a mamzer, since mamzerut does not depend on a prohibited union.
  • The proof: a mamzer may marry a mamzeret or a giyoret in a fully kosher wedding, yet the children are mamzerim. The verse teaches that the blemish continues from generation to generation even where nothing forbidden was done.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein offered a remarkable hiddush: the continuing blemish is not part of the laws of lineage (yohasin) but a new law derived from the verse, so it applies only through actual cohabitation. A child conceived otherwise, such as from leftover semen in a bath, would not be a mamzer.
  • Taken on, this would be remarkable for mamzerim: a mamzer married to a mamzeret or a giyoret could avoid conception in their relations and have children through insemination, who would not be mamzerim.
  • The vast majority disagree: the prohibited union is what creates a mamzer, but here the seed itself comes from a mamzer, so the status continues.
  • A fascinating question: if such a case arose, would there be room for leniency?

Sources quoted

  • The verse: the blemish of mamzerut continues through the generations.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: the continuation applies only through cohabitation.

In one line

Most hold a child from a mamzer's donated sperm is a mamzer, though Rav Moshe Feinstein suggested it applies only through cohabitation.
