Topic: How the prohibition of melakhah on Yom Tov differs from Shabbat: okhel nefesh, mitokh, and doing it for the festival

Sources: The Torah (Parashat Emor); the Gemara; the Hakhamim

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Thursday 22 May 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Yom Tov has greater simhah than Shabbat, but Shabbat has greater kedushah, from which the prohibition of melakhah derives. So for Shabbat the Torah says "kol melakhah lo yeaseh", while for Yom Tov it adds "akh asher yeakhel lekhol nefesh", excepting what is for food, and Parashat Emor says "melekhet avodah".
  • So on Yom Tov one may bake, cook, raise the heat for cooking and take out food.
  • Generally, what is done on an industrial or large scale, or commonly by employees, is prohibited. There are three categories: completely permitted, completely forbidden, and a middle category permitted on a small scale with rabbinic elements.
  • The Gemara establishes mitokh: since melakhah is permitted for food, it is permitted even not for food. So one may carry a tallit to synagogue without an eruv, light a fire for light or heat, or heat water to wash dishes. All accept this.
  • Whatever is permitted must be lesorekh hayom, for the festival. Beginning to cook so late that the food cannot be ready before the hag ends violates a Torah prohibition, according to all opinions.
  • Cooking late in the afternoon intending it for the next day, when someone might still eat it on Yom Tov, is rabbinically forbidden but not forbidden from the Torah.

In one line

On Yom Tov melakhah for food, and by extension for other needs, is permitted, but only on a small scale and for the festival itself.
