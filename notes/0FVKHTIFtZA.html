Topic: The better mazal of Adar against "ein mazal leYisrael", and the Rambam's rejection of astrology

Sources: The Gemara (Rav Papa); the Ritva; the Hatam Sofer; Rambam, letter to the sages of Montpellier and Mishneh Torah

Daily Halacha: Purim, Wednesday 5 March 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Gemara continues, in the name of Rav Papa: since Adar is a month of joy, Israel's mazal is better, so a court case with a non-Jew should be scheduled in Adar if possible. The Rambam and Shulhan Arukh do not quote this.
  • Elsewhere the Gemara says "ein mazal leYisrael", and this seems to be the accepted approach: Israel is not under the influence of stars, planets and constellations.
  • The Ritva, 700 years ago: normally there is no mazal for Israel, but in Adar and Av there is, good in Adar and less good in Av, so one should be cautious in Av.
  • The Hatam Sofer (Rabbi Moshe Sofer): Israel has no mazal, but non-Jews do, and theirs is weaker in Adar.
  • The Rambam, in his Mishneh Torah and forcefully in a letter of around 1195 to the sages of Montpellier, rejects astrology as a pseudoscience that Jews do not believe in and should stay away from. Talmudic references to mazal must be understood allegorically. For him the idea does not apply in Adar.
  • Even if one accepts mazal, it applies only to one who places himself under it. One who goes directly to HaKadosh Barukh Hu need not worry about it.
  • So the focus in Adar should be our relationship with HaKadosh Barukh Hu, not changing our conduct because of mazal.
  • A siman, a good and auspicious sign, is different and acceptable, which is why Sepharadim wish each other "Siman Tov" rather than "Mazal Tov".

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: Israel's mazal is better in Adar.
  • The Ritva and the Hatam Sofer: two ways to reconcile this with "ein mazal leYisrael".
  • Rambam, letter to Montpellier: astrology is a pseudoscience.

In one line

The Jewish approach is not to live by mazal and luck but to go directly to HaKadosh Barukh Hu.
