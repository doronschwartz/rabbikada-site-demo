Topic: How the Gregorian reform moved the start of the request for rain from 22 November to early December

Sources: The Gemara; Bet Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • In 2022 the request for rain outside Israel began on the night of Sunday 4 December, continuing until Pesah; in Israel it began on 7 Heshvan.
  • The Gemara sets it 60 days after Tekufat Tishrei, traditionally 24 September, so the poskim of the 14th to 16th centuries, the Bet Yoseph explicitly, give 22 November.
  • In 1582 the Julian calendar was replaced by the Gregorian. The spring equinox, fixed at 21 March in 325, had slipped to 11 March. Gregory XIII removed ten days: 5 October 1582 became 15 October, so the date moved to 2 December.
  • To prevent further slippage, the leap day is dropped in century years not divisible by 400: none in 1700, 1800 and 1900, but one in 1600 and 2000. So the date moves three days every 400 years.
  • Old siddurim show the progression: 3 December in 1700, 4 December in 1800, 5 December in 1900. Since 2000 was a leap year, it stays at 5 December (the night of the 4th) throughout this century.
  • In a year before a civil leap year it is a day later: in 2023 the request starts on the night of 5 December.
  • From 2100 everything moves a day later: 6 December normally, 7 December before a leap year.

In one line

Until 2100 the request for rain outside Israel starts on the night of 4 December, or 5 December before a civil leap year.
