Topic: The status of a child born to a married woman who has committed adultery

Sources: The Gemara (striking a parent); the Aharonim

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 18 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The new topic: children of a married woman who acts promiscuously, or who is known for certain to have committed adultery. Are they kasher or mamzerim?
  • The Gemara raises it in an unrelated context: one who strikes a parent and causes a bruise is liable to death. How can a child be executed for striking his father, when perhaps the father is not really his?
  • The Gemara answers with the principle of majority: rov beilot ahar habaal, most of a married woman's relations are with her husband, so the child is assumed to be his. The same applies to inheritance.
  • So even where the wife committed adultery and gives birth nine months later, we need not worry that the child is the adulterer's. The child is assumed to be the husband's.
  • DNA is a huge topic for another time, but in practice we certainly do not rely on DNA to make a child a mamzer, even if a test clearly matches the man his mother committed adultery with.
  • The Aharonim understand the principle in two ways: a plain numerical count, since the great majority of her relations are with her husband; or that most pregnancies of a married woman come from her husband, since she would normally want to conceive from her properly married husband.
  • Next time: how this applies to civil marriage.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: the majority of relations are attributed to the husband, so a child who strikes his father is liable.
  • The Aharonim: two readings of the principle, numerical or about likelihood of conception.

In one line

A child of a married woman is assumed to be her husband's even if she committed adultery, and DNA is not relied on to make a child a mamzer.
