Class Notes — Rosh Chodesh Adar תשפ״ו

Sefer: Hamaspik l'Ovdei Hashem (Kifayat al-Abidin) — Rabbi Avraham ben HaRambam Chapter: Perek Aleph — "The Path of the Torah Which Has No End" Edition: Feldheim

Context

  • Full work = 2,500+ pages, four major sections; we are studying part of section two
  • 13 chapters total — each covers a different character trait and how to work on it
  • First three sections (which we don't have) = day-to-day halacha, regular behaviour → Derech Ha'am
  • This remaining section = going beyond the basics → Derech HaYachid / Chasidut

The Two Paths: Overview

דרך התורה המתמשכת והולכת בלי גבול — the path of Torah has no end

  • Divides into two distinct paths:
  • Derech Ha'am (דרך העם) — the common path
  • Derech HaYachid (דרך היחיד) — the unique/individual path

Path 1: Derech Ha'am — The Common Path

  • = fulfilling the 248 positive commandments + refraining from 365 negative commandments
  • Each person according to their specific obligations

Obligations vary by category:

  • Gender: Milah, Sukkah, Lulav — males only (women may do voluntarily)
  • Kehunah: Temple service, dietary rules, marriage restrictions — Kohanim only
  • Time/place: Mikdash laws — not applicable in Galut
  • Role: "לא תשא פני דל ולא תהדר פני גדול" — applies to dayanim specifically

Requirements for walking this path correctly:

  • Must understand what Hashem commanded to do and not do
  • Must consider how each mitzvah relates to you personally
  • Must accept the obligation and act on it
  • Must study — without knowledge, you can't succeed
  • Source: Avot — אין בור ירא חטא (an ignorant person cannot be God-fearing)

Titles earned on this path:

  • Tzaddik — righteous
  • Tam — wholesome
  • Yashar — person of integrity
  • Sar Mera — turns from evil
  • Kasher (Rabbinic term) — appropriate/fitting
  • Word "kasher" not in Torah or Nevi'im — only Ketuvim (Kohelet, Esther)
  • Esther to Achashverosh: "וכשר הדבר" = it is appropriate

HaRambam's three levels of conduct:

  1. Mishpat (justice) — enforceable in Beit Din
  2. Tzedakah (appropriateness) — the right thing to do, not enforceable but obligatory
  • E.g. returning a poor person's night garment used as collateral
  • NB: English "charity" is imprecise — implies no obligation; Torah's tzedakah = moral duty
  1. Chesed (kindness) — going beyond what is appropriate
  • Doing everything commanded = tzaddik
  • But not yet a chasid — that's the next level

Failure to keep this path:

  • Called: rasha, poshea, averayan, moreh
  • Label matches severity of failing
  • = debasing yourself — not satisfying your nefesh sichliyit (intellectual soul)

Path 2: Derech HaYachid — The Unique Path

  • = understanding the goals and reasons of mitzvot (תכלית המצוות וסודותיהן)
  • = learning from the ways of prophets and pious people
  • Title earned: Kadosh (holy), Chasid (pious)
  • Rabbi Avraham's three-tier hierarchy: Tzaddik → Chasid → Navi

Importance of Ta'amei HaMitzvot (reasons for mitzvot):

  • Comes directly from HaRambam:
  • End of Hilchot Me'ilah: "It is correct to try to understand the laws and their reasons"
  • End of Hilchot Temurah: even chukim deserve contemplation
  • אף על פי שכל חוקי התורה גזירות הם, ראוי להתבונן בהם
  • On chukim: HaRambam disagrees that chukim have no reason — every mitzvah has a reason; a chok is simply harder to understand
  • Temurah example: can't swap a dedicated animal even for a better one
  • ירדה תורה לסוף מחשבת האדם — Torah understands human rationalisation around money
  • Key insight: understanding the reason → understanding the direction → knowing how to take the mitzvah further → Chasidut

Key characteristics of Derech HaYachid:

  • Not everyone can reach it — "זוהי מדרגה שלא כל שומר תורה מגיע אליה"
  • Not explicit in Torah — "לא נזכר חיובה במפורש"
  • No human punishment for not reaching it — "לא יהיה נענש בידי אדם"

Examples: Tzaddik vs. Chasid

Shabbat

  • Tzaddik: keeps Shabbat, avoids 39 melachot, Kiddush, meals ✓
  • Chasid: additionally contemplates the meaning — creation, God as primal existence, renewal of the world

Tzitzit

  • Tzaddik: wears tzitzit ✓
  • Chasid: uses tzitzit as a tool to remember Hashem constantly → "והייתם קדושים לאלהיכם"

Kashrut

  • Tzaddik: eats kosher, avoids treif ✓
  • Chasid: understands the reason (Rambam, Moreh Nevuchim — not overindulging in food) → restrains even within permitted foods

Arayot

  • Tzaddik: refrains from forbidden relationships ✓
  • Chasid: understands the principle (minimise indulgence) → exercises additional restraint in permitted relationships too

Levels: Derech Ha'am vs. Derech HaYachid

In Derech Ha'am — minimal variation:

  • Mostly binary: keep Shabbat or don't; eat kosher or don't
  • Differences mainly quantitative:
  • Number of prohibitions (eating bugs = 4-6 issurim vs. basar b'chalav = 1)
  • Frequency (wore garment without tzitzit once vs. every day)

Exception within Derech Ha'am:

  • Some mitzvot have inherent gradation even at the common level:
  • Yirat Hashem — fear of punishment (lower) vs. fear of His exaltedness (higher)
  • Ahavat Hashem — love because He's good to me vs. unconditional deep love
  • Avodat HaLev — service of the heart

In Derech HaYachid — vast variation:

  • רחבים תחומיה עד מאד ורחוקים זה מזה כרחוק מזרח ממערב
  • Even in one mitzvah, even one time — enormous differences in depth

The Mashal: Three Chasidim Keeping One Shabbat

Chasid 1 — General Contemplation

  • Keeps Shabbat fully (baseline) +
  • Thinks generally about Shabbat's purpose
  • Reflects: heavens and earth created in six days; nothing before Hashem; God = primal existence

Chasid 2 — Detailed Contemplation

  • Everything of Chasid 1 +
  • Delves into specifics — universe from earth's centre to celestial spheres
  • Reflects on wisdom of the Creator through details of creation
  • Goes through each day of Bereishit
  • = Ma'aseh Bereishit (Yesodei HaTorah) — science, astronomy, natural world as path to God's wisdom

Chasid 3 — Total Absorption / Ecstatic Encounter

  • Everything of Chasid 2 +
  • Reaches true inner holiness (kedushah penimit amitit)
  • Filled with joy; shining countenance
  • Fully appreciates the bond (zikah) between himself and God
  • Understands Shabbat's purpose: ביני ובין בני ישראל אות היא לעולם
  • Refrains from eating/drinking — not ascetic choice, but genuinely absorbed
  • Does not hear sounds; does not register what he sees
  • כמו חלב ודשן תשבע נפשי — soul satisfied like richest foods
  • Parallel: HaRambam, end of Hilchot De'ot — lovesick for Hashem, כי חולת אהבה אני

How can not eating on Shabbat be permitted?

  • Gemara Pesachim: Mar brei d'Ravina fasted every day except Shavuot, Purim, Erev Yom Kippur
  • Rabbeinu David: not a formal fast — passion for Torah so great he forgot to eat until night
  • Even on Shabbat: Shabbat requires oneg — and this was his oneg
  • Shulchan Aruch siman 288: if fasting is your enjoyment, you may fast on Shabbat
  • Shabbat ≠ obligation to eat; Shabbat = obligation to experience delight
  • Analogy: absorbed in a book/film → forget you're hungry → not conscious refusal, thought simply doesn't arise

Key Term: Pegiah (פגיעה) — Encounter

  • Arabic original: wasul → Hebrew: pegiah = to encounter (cf. ויפגע במקום)
  • Chasidut is experiential — not just performative
  • Greater Chasidut = greater pegiah = deeper encounter with God
  • All character traits (perishut, anavah, etc.) have many levels of pegiah
  • Humility of Moshe ≠ humility of other tzaddikim; perishut of Eliyahu ≠ perishut of other nevi'im

Q&A Points

Is excessive chesed a problem?

  • In human relationships — yes:
  • גומל חסדים טובים — implies there is also bad chesed
  • "Gemilut chasadim" from "gamal" (camel) — best chesed = making someone independent
  • Torah calls sibling incest "chesed" (Acharei Mot) — natural love crossing into destructive territory
  • With HaKadosh Baruch Hu — possibly: if chasidut comes at expense of other obligations; needs further thought

Are we commanded to go beyond?

  • ועשית הטוב והישר — Chachamim derive lifnim mishurat hadin from this
  • Falls under tzedakah (appropriateness), not chasidut
  • We don't appear to be explicitly commanded to be chasidim
  • Chasidut = voluntary spiritual aspiration

Gadol metzuveh ve'oseh?

  • Applies to derech ha'am
  • Chasidut = realm of the mitnadeiv (volunteer) beyond obligation

Chapter 1 Conclusion

  • Chasidut can only be pursued when basics (Derech Ha'am) are firmly in place
  • Cannot be a chasid without first being a tzaddik
