Topic: Father before mother today? And why the manner of honouring matters most

Sources: The Gemara; Pithei Teshuvah, Yoreh Deah 240:14; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Honouring Parents, Tuesday 12 January 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara's rule that the father comes first when married parents ask together rests on the Talmud's division of roles: the husband provides for his wife, and she in turn honours him.
  • Some argue that today, when roles are no longer fixed and a woman may be the main earner, these mutual obligations no longer apply in the same way, so even married parents may be honoured in whichever order the child wishes. See the Pithei Teshuvah on 240:14. Most authorities still put the father first.
  • More important than what one does is how. The Gemara says one may feed his father fine food and still "drive him from the world", or set him to grind at a mill and earn great reward.
  • The son who served his father the best meat answered his question about the cost with "Old man, just chew and eat", showing it was a burden.
  • The miller whose elderly father was drafted by the king put his father at the mill and went into the king's service in his place: an act of love, though it looked like making his father work.
  • The Shulhan Arukh rules that the essence of honouring parents is to do it with a pleasant, cheerful face.

In one line

Most authorities still put the father first for married parents (some argue not today), and above all, honour must be given with a smile, not as a burden.
