Topic: The laws of a year in which Erev Pesah falls on Shabbat, from the Fast of the Firstborn to the Shabbat meals (Sefaradi practice, with Ashkenazi differences noted) Source: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim 444 and 470; Mishnah Berurah; Hakham Ovadia Yoseph Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada at the Shasha synagogue, Sunday 21 March 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

A rare year

This was the sixth shiur in a Pesah series, after classes on kashrut and cleaning, two on the Seder and one on the structure of the Haggadah. Erev Pesah on Shabbat is rare: the last time was 2008, and the next will be 2025 and 2045. The Rabbi went through the days in order and asked the class to raise questions as he went.

Thursday: the Fast of the Firstborn

The Masekhet Soferim says firstborn males fast on Erev Pesah. The Rambam does not require it, taking the Yerushalmi to disagree, while the Rosh reads the Yerushalmi as agreeing with Soferim. Because it is hard to fast and then come to the Seder weak, the custom developed that a firstborn who takes part in a seudat misvah (a berit milah, pidyon haben or siyum) is exempt, but he must really participate, eating at least a kezayit. The Shulhan Arukh mentions a custom that firstborn women fast, which Sefaradim never took on. The Rema mentions a father fasting for a firstborn son under bar misvah, an Ashkenazi custom.

This year the fast cannot fall on Shabbat, and one does not fast on Friday and enter Shabbat hungry, so the question is Thursday. The Shulhan Arukh brings two opinions, that it moves to Thursday and that it is cancelled. The Rema says to fast on Thursday. Sefaradim follow the rule that when the Shulhan Arukh brings "some say... and some say", the halakhah follows the second.

Thursday night: Bedikat Hames

By the Torah, nullifying the hames (bitul) is enough. Hazal added the search, normally on the night of the 14th. This year that is Friday night, when one cannot use a candle on Shabbat, so the search is on Thursday night and is a full obligation with all the usual laws. From half an hour before nightfall (about 7:10 pm in London) one should not begin a meal or a shiur, and the search is done as soon after nightfall as possible.

After the berakhah one may not speak about anything unrelated to the search. Instructions ("check this room, what is this medicine") are fine; answering the phone is not. Places already checked before Pesah, where no hames has entered, need not be checked again, but where children are around one checks again, only where they can reach. What we look for is a kezayit of hames, not crumbs.

After the search one says Kol Hamira, even though we will still eat hames on Friday and Shabbat, intending that anything unseen is nullified. Someone who could not search on Thursday night may search the whole of Friday with a berakhah. This is unlike other years, because the obligation from the night is carried over to the next day.

Friday: eating, disposing, selling

In a normal year hames is forbidden from midday, with a ban on eating from the end of the fourth hour and on owning from the end of the fifth, this year about 11 am. Since this year it is only the 13th, none of this applies: one may eat hames all Friday and dispose of it all day. Still, it is right to dispose of whatever will not be eaten on Friday night and Shabbat by the end of the fifth hour, about 11 am. Disposal means any normal permanent way: crumbling and pouring detergent or bleach over it, flushing it, throwing it into the sea. Sefaradim do not need to burn it. Kol Hamira need not be repeated on Friday, as it will be said on Shabbat morning.

For the sale, the Batei Din want to sell by the end of the fifth hour, but know people will still have hames later. So the sale is made at about 10 am, with a stipulation that hames the seller owns until just before Shabbat is included. Someone who finds a bottle of whisky at 3 pm on Friday is covered and may put it in the cupboard. Keep the quantity left over small.

Work and haircuts, forbidden from midday on a normal Erev Pesah, are permitted this year.

Cooking and the table

The Mishnah Berurah says to cook Shabbat food in Pesah pots, leaving aside only the hallah. The Shulhan Arukh permits hames pots but not greasy food, which sticks and is hard to clean. The Rabbi did not recommend it, and advised plain hallah or pitta, with no crumbs.

As long as there is another respectable place to eat the Shabbat meals, one may set the Pesah table on Friday afternoon and eat Shabbat in the kitchen. The Hida writes strongly against meals squeezed "between the ovens" on Shabbat HaGadol. If this is not possible and there is a real need, such as small children or elderly people, the Beit Meir allows setting the table from bein hashemashot on Shabbat, about 6:25 pm. Convenience alone is not enough.

One may pray Arvit early, going from one holy time to another, as Havdalah may be said from pelag haminhah (Shulhan Arukh 263), as long as one is not home before Shabbat ends. All Maror must be checked for insects on Friday. Do not store it soaking in water, as it may become kavush. Keep it in the fridge.

The Shabbat meals

Friday night is straightforward: hallah or matsah, even shemurah. Matsah is forbidden only from the morning of Erev Pesah, following the Rambam among three views on the Yerushalmi's "Erev Pesah". Hamotsi and Birkat Hamazon are said as usual.

Shabbat morning has three options. 1. Hallah. Pray early, come home, wash and begin the meal by the end of the fourth hour. Some say 9:25 am (Magen Avraham), but Sefaradim follow the Gra, about 10 am. One need only begin by then and may continue after. Sort the crumbs and say Kol Hamira before 11 am, and dispose of leftovers. 2. Matsah ashirah, made with fruit juice and no water. The Shulhan Arukh allows it to Sefaradim with a reliable hekhsher. It cannot fulfil the Seder misvah, so it carries no prohibition on Shabbat. But it is like a cracker, not bread, so one must eat a meal-sized quantity, about 160 grams or four or five matsot. The Rabbi was unsure how advisable this is. 3. Matsah mevushelet, the option of Hakham Ovadia Yoseph. Before Shabbat, cook ordinary square matsah (not shemurah) in soup or fry it briefly, keeping it intact. It can no longer fulfil the Seder misvah, and one makes Hamotsi and Birkat Hamazon on it. The Rabbi reported that Rav Ovadia would go into the kitchen only twice a year, to make haroset and this matsah.

Seudah shelishit is after 10 am, so hames is forbidden: matsah ashirah, matsah mevushelet or fruit. Pitta is forbidden. Sefaradim may eat seudah shelishit before minhah.

Teeth and other points

If one eats hames in the morning, wash the hands, use mouthwash and floss, unless the gums regularly bleed. Those who brush with toothpaste on Shabbat all year may do so now; others should not start, but rinse and floss. Mouthwash and toothpaste should have a Pesah hekhsher, because of the taste.

Shemurah matsah is mukseh on Shabbat, as it will not be given to children. One should not say "I am going to sleep to have energy for the Seder"; sleeping with the Seder in mind is fine. A clock is not mukseh for Sefaradim, but the recording ended before the question of moving a clock's hands was settled.

Practical summary

  • Sefaradim need not fast on Thursday or attend a siyum. Ashkenazim fast or attend a real siyum with a meal.
  • Check for hames on Thursday night, soon after nightfall, then say Kol Hamira. If missed, check all Friday with a berakhah.
  • Aim to dispose of leftover hames by about 11 am on Friday, though it is permitted all day. The Beit Din sale covers hames owned until Shabbat.
  • On Shabbat morning begin a hames meal by about 10 am (Gra), and say Kol Hamira and clear crumbs before 11 am.
  • After 10 am eat only matsah ashirah, matsah mevushelet prepared before Shabbat, or fruit.
  • Set the Pesah table and check the Maror on Friday, and pray Arvit early if needed.
