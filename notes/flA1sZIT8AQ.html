Topic: The Rasha's question, the word avodah, and why freedom is hard work

Sources: The Haggadah; the Ritva's commentary on the Haggadah; Shemot, chapters 1 and 3; Rabbi Sacks

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Tuesday 12 April 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Haggadah faults the Rasha for saying "Mah haavodah hazot lakhem", "to you", excluding himself. Yet the Hakham also says "you" ("what are the laws... commanded you"), and his question is praised.
  • One answer: the Hakham asks genuinely, while the Rasha makes a rhetorical statement.
  • The Ritva, a Spanish Rishon, in his commentary on the Haggadah at the end of his work on Pesahim, says the problem is not "lakhem" but "haavodah". Only the Rasha uses that word.
  • In Shemot chapter 1, avodah describes the back-breaking labour the Egyptians imposed. Yet when Moshe asks for a sign, HaKadosh Barukh Hu says, "you will serve God on this mountain": the same word describes our freedom.
  • The Rasha sees only work: we swapped Pharaoh for another Master, so what are we celebrating?
  • Rabbi Sacks noted that Pesah, the hardest festival to prepare for, is the most observed (a poll found 88 percent of Israelis held a Seder), while Shavuot, the easiest, is the least observed, with Sukkot in between.
  • When we value something we gladly work hard for it; a medical student who asks what all the work is for raises a red flag.
  • The Hakham knows serving HaShem is hard, but valuable. What costs little is valued little; once we see its purpose, avodah becomes a labour of love.

Sources quoted

  • Shemot: avodah as slavery in Egypt and as serving God at Sinai.

In one line

The Rasha sees service of God as just another bondage, while the Hakham knows that freedom, like anything valuable, is hard work done with love.
