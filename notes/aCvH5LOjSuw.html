It fills a gap in his Derech Hashem series at Tiferet Eyal, where these paragraphs were taught but not recorded.

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The framework and the motive

The class opens at the end of paragraph 5. Hashem gave us a framework for using the world, the misvot. We must eat, so first we exclude non-kosher food, then slaughter correctly, remove the blood and the forbidden fats, make a berakhah and not mix meat with milk. We must earn a living, so Hashem gives a framework for business, with honesty and integrity.

Motive matters too. Even sleep should be to strengthen the body for serving Hashem, not only for pleasure. This needs balance, the Rabbi said: there is also a misvah to enjoy the world and appreciate Hashem's work. But there should be some purpose beyond self-gratification. Personally, he finds it easier to see Hashem in a beautiful fruit than in a steak.

That is why the 613 misvot are the best system for us: Hashem created us and knows our deficiencies. A system we design ourselves will have shortcomings.

Paragraph 6: do not lose the painting

"The root of all service is that a person should always turn towards his Creator": he was created to cleave to Him and placed in this world to overcome his material side through the intellect, and all his actions should be for this.

The Rabbi brought an analogy he heard from Rabbi Sacks. You stand before a beautiful painting and move closer and closer, until you are absorbed in one detail and lose the beauty of the whole. The same with Torah: if you focus too much on one area, you lose focus on others. That is why people who become extreme about certain ritual matters sometimes neglect the basic human ones, between man and his fellow. The misvot are a means to an end, and the end is to cleave to Hashem.

Paragraph 7: two kinds of action

The Ramhal divides our actions into two categories: what we do because we are commanded, the misvot, and what we do out of necessity, such as eating, drinking and earning a living.

Misvot fulfil Hashem's will in two ways. Simply, He commanded and we did, which gives Him, as it were, satisfaction. And more deeply, a misvah perfects us, and so allows Hashem to give us the good He created the world to give. The Rabbi pointed to the line before Dayenu in the Haggadah: "Kamah maalot tovot laMakom aleinu." Why "to" Hashem and not "from" Hashem? The Nefesh HaHayim explains that when we do His will and He can bestow good on us, that itself is good "for" Him, as it were.

Necessities must first be permitted, and then done for the sake of the body's health, not for superfluous desire, so that the body can serve Hashem without being held back. When a person uses the world this way, the Ramhal says, the use itself brings perfection.

The Rabbi compared Maslow's hierarchy of needs: food, warmth and rest, then safety, then friendship and esteem, and at the top self-actualisation, which is where Torah belongs. You cannot think about spiritual things when you are hungry or homeless, and people who jump to the top without solid foundations often collapse. The Rambam wrote much the same 800 years earlier. So two people can eat and sleep exactly the same; one does it only for enjoyment, the other to have the strength to serve Hashem, and for him it becomes holy.

Paragraph 8: the love of Hashem

The main thing, after all this, is love and fear of Hashem. What does it mean to love Hashem? Participants suggested devoting your life to Him, and that ahavah comes from the root "hav", to give, as Rav Dessler explains.

The Rabbi showed the Rambam at the start of Hilchot Yesodei HaTorah, chapter 2: when a person contemplates Hashem's great and wonderful works and sees His infinite wisdom, he immediately loves and praises Him and longs to know Him, as David said, "My soul thirsts for God." The Rambam follows this with two chapters on the physical world, the science of his day, in a halachic work, because for him this is part of serving Hashem.

The class ends here; the love and fear of Hashem continue in the next class.

Summary

  • The misvot are both a framework and a matter of motive: we use the world within the rules and for the right purpose.
  • Do not focus on one area of Torah so much that you lose the whole picture.
  • Our actions are either commanded misvot or necessities; both can bring perfection.
  • Necessities serve Hashem when permitted, healthy and done to give us strength to serve Him.
  • Love of Hashem, says the Rambam, comes from contemplating the wonders of His creation.
