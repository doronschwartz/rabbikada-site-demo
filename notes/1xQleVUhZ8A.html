Topic: Whether women must say Hallel on Hanukkah, and with a berakhah

Sources: The Gemara; Rambam; Tosafot

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Reciting Hallel on Hanukkah is a misvah of the Hakhamim, the complete Hallel all eight days (on Pesah only the first two), showing the prominence of Hanukkah.
  • One might think women are obligated, as they are in the Menorah. The Gemara says that when a woman recites Hallel for a man he must repeat after her, as with hearing kiddush from someone not obligated. Rambam rules this for every Hallel: women are exempt.
  • Tosafot excepts the Hallel of Pesah, since "af hen hayu beoto hanes"; by extension the same would apply to Hanukkah, where women are obligated in the Menorah for that reason.
  • Why would Rambam exempt them? Hallel, like Al HaNissim, is mainly thanks for the military victory (the Menorah recalls the oil), and in those days women did not go to war.
  • The majority of poskim hold that Hallel on Hanukkah is not obligatory for women.
  • Broadly, Ashkenazi women may say a berakhah on a misvah they are exempt from, so they may say Hallel with a berakhah. Sepharadim hold that one who is exempt cannot say the berakhah.
  • For Sepharadi women it is a good practice to say Hallel on Hanukkah, but without a berakhah.

In one line

Women are not obligated in Hallel on Hanukkah; it is good for them to say it, Ashkenazim perhaps with a berakhah, Sepharadim without.
