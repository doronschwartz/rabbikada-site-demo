Topic: From which night of the month and which time of evening Birkat HaLevanah may be said

Sources: The Gemara; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; Shaarei Orah (Rabbi Yoseph Gikatilla)

Daily Halacha: Birkat Kohanim & Prayer, Tuesday 17 October 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara only says "one who sees the moon in its renewal". Rambam and most Rishonim allow it from the first night after the molad; others wait three days, when one can benefit from its light.
  • The Shulhan Arukh says to wait seven nights, based on Shaarei Orah by Rabbi Yoseph Gikatilla, a Spanish mekubal: a rare case of a Kabbalistic ruling recorded in the halacha. Most Sepharadim follow this; Temanim and some Moroccans say it from the third night, some even from the first.
  • It may be said only until the end of the 15th of the month, after which the moon is waning.
  • It is customary to say it on Mosei Shabbat, in Shabbat clothes, since it is like receiving the Shekhinah.
  • The Shulhan Arukh wrote in Safed, where skies are often clear. In England, waiting for both seven nights and Mosei Shabbat can push it to the 11th to 13th, leaving only two or three nights. Our poskim write that where this is a concern one should not wait seven full nights but say it earlier, even at six days or perhaps less.
  • Ashkenazim generally say it on the first Mosei Shabbat after three days.
  • In the evening it may be said once it is dark enough after sunset to benefit from the moon's light, even before full nightfall, as some do after Tisha BeAv before the fast may be broken.

In one line

Sepharadim wait seven nights and Ashkenazim three, but in cloudy countries one should not delay so long that the chance to say it is lost before the 15th.
