Topic: An egg donated by a married Jewish woman, analysed by the three views on insemination

Sources: Rav Moshe Feinstein; the Satmar Rebbe; Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 17 July 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Here the married donor's egg is fertilised by a man who is not her husband. None of the three poskim discussed this newer case, but their logic can be extended.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: mamzerut needs a prohibited act of intimacy (biat issur). There is none, so the child is not a mamzer.
  • The Satmar Rebbe forbade inserting another man's seed into a married woman. Here nothing is inserted into her: her egg is removed and fertilised in a lab, so by his logic one could argue it is permitted.
  • Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach: mamzerut reflects the severity of any connection between a married woman and another man, even without an act. So here there would seemingly still be a question of mamzerut, though in a lab.
  • Even by his view, the issue arises only if the egg donor is the halachic mother. If the carrier is the mother, the mother is the wife of the man whose sperm was used.
  • Because of the doubt, Israel, whose family law follows halacha, forbids egg donation from a married woman and allows it only from a single woman, avoiding the question, though whether there is mamzerut at all is arguable.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: no prohibited act, no mamzerut.
  • The Satmar Rebbe: his objection to inserting seed does not apply to a lab.
  • Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach: the connection itself may create mamzerut, if the egg defines the mother.

In one line

An egg from a married donor is arguably fine for two views but questionable for the third, so Israel permits only single donors.
