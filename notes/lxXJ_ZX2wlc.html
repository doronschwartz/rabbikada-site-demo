Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 299, seif 10 (with the Rema); 300; 301, seifim 1-3 Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 16 May 2024

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

Siman 299:10: Melakhah after Shabbat, before Havdalah

This was the last class on Havdalah and the end of Shabbat. The Shulhan Arukh rules that one may not do any Melakhah before Havdalah, but once you have made Havdalah in Arvit (Atah Honantanu) you may, even before Havdalah on the cup. The reason, as explained last week, is not that you will get busy and forget the misvah. Until some Havdalah has been said there is still a holiness left over from Shabbat. Once you have said something that marks the end of Shabbat, that is gone.

If you need to do Melakhah before praying Arvit, you may say "Barukh HaMavdil Bein Kodesh LeHol", without the name of HaShem, and then make proper Havdalah on the cup later. This does not help for eating, which still needs Havdalah on the cup.

The Rema. Women who do not pray Arvit should be taught to say Barukh HaMavdil Bein Kodesh LeHol before doing Melakhah after Shabbat, such as washing up or switching on lights. That is the source of the common practice.

The Rema then brings an opinion that only full Melakhah, such as writing or weaving, needs Havdalah first, while something small like lighting a candle or carrying does not. From this came the custom of lighting candles in shul as soon as the community says Barukh Hu. The main ruling rejects this distinction: who says carrying or lighting a candle is lighter than writing? Others, Rabbenu Yeruham among them, read that opinion the other way: even after Barukh HaMavdil, only light things are permitted, and serious work waits until Havdalah in Arvit or on the cup.

In practice. Saying Barukh HaMavdil is not a blanket permission to then do everything and pray Arvit later. Where possible, pray Arvit or make Havdalah first. Someone asked about making Havdalah at home in winter before driving to shul. The Rabbi agreed: say Barukh HaMavdil, then light the Havdalah candle, make proper Havdalah, and then drive. Driving is serious Melakhah, so one should not drive on the strength of Barukh HaMavdil alone unless necessary.

Drawing water. The Rema records a custom to draw water on Mosei Shabbat, because Miriam's well is said to travel to all wells then, and its water heals. The Rabbi linked this to the Syrian custom of throwing away any open water left from Shabbat (the urn, jugs on the table, but not sealed bottles) and drawing fresh water. One reason given previously was that the souls return to Gehinnom at that time; this source adds that the point is to draw new water. Tap water counts.

Extending Shabbat. Someone still keeping Shabbat may ask someone who has already ended Shabbat to do Melakhah for him.

Siman 300: Melaveh Malkah

Siman 300 has one paragraph, quoting the Gemara verbatim: a person should always set his table on Mosei Shabbat to escort Shabbat out, even if he only needs to eat a kazayit. - Mishnah Berurah 1: set the table with a cloth and as for the Shabbat meals, though old dishes are fine. The plain meaning is a meal with bread, and ideally meat. If you are too full, at least cake, and at the very least fruit. - It applies equally to men and women, as do all the misvot of Shabbat. - Mishnah Berurah 2: just as we honour Shabbat on its way in, we escort it on its way out, so do it as soon as possible after Havdalah. One should not settle into serious work before it. It may be done all night, but ideally before midnight.

The Rabbi acknowledged it is hard in a London summer, when Seudah Shelishit is late. He added that our Shabbat morning schedule makes this worse; ideally one would pray early, eat the morning meal earlier, and have Seudah Shelishit in the late afternoon.

The luz bone. The early books say there is a small bone, the luz, near the neck, that never decomposes and is nourished only by the meal of Mosei Shabbat. Since Adam ate from the tree on Friday, this bone was not part of the decree of decay.

Keep it in proportion. The Mishnah Berurah ends: do not equate this meal with the three Shabbat meals; this is only a misvah. The Rabbi told of a woman who skipped Seudah Shelishit so as to have room for Melaveh Malkah, because of a segulah. The segulah is from Rebbe Elimelekh of Lizhensk, that a woman who eats Melaveh Malkah will have an easier childbirth, and remarkably the Kaf HaHayim, writing in Iraq and Jerusalem, quotes it. Skipping Seudah Shelishit for it is "a stringency which comes to a leniency". He also told of the Vilna Gaon, whose wife fasted through the week, telling her that her fasts were not worth missing Melaveh Malkah.

Introduction to Siman 301: Why begin with carrying?

This ended the first part of Hilchot Shabbat, the positive laws (Simanim 242-300). Siman 301 begins the forbidden Melakhot, starting with carrying, even though it is the last of the 39. The Gemara opens Masekhet Shabbat with carrying too. One reason given by Tosafot is that people treat carrying lightly. "People treat hosaah very lightly. They think, what am I doing? I'm just carrying something." So it comes first.

Seifim 1-3: Running, jumping and puddles

Seif 1: one may not run on Shabbat except to a misvah, such as to shul. The Gemara learns from the same verse as "vedaber davar" (that Shabbat speech should differ from weekday speech) that your walking on Shabbat should not be like your walking on weekdays. The Rema adds not to take big strides. The Rabbi recalled reading the Torah in three London shuls on one Shabbat morning and running between them.

Seif 2: young men who enjoy running and jumping may do so, since that is their enjoyment and Shabbat is for enjoyment. This is not only for the young: running to see a friend you are excited to see, or anything you enjoy, is permitted. Someone asked whether an onlooker would misunderstand; the Rabbi said this is not a severe prohibition, so there is no marit ayin concern. The Rema adds that one may take long walks.

The Rabbi noted that it is hard to find where the prohibition applies: almost everywhere you go on Shabbat is either a misvah or an enjoyment. It would apply to something like a dutiful meeting you are not looking forward to.

Jogging and exercise. If you enjoy jogging and it helps your health or mental wellbeing, you may jog on Shabbat. What is wrong is exercising in order to sweat, or doing it purely to lose weight for a figure while not enjoying it at all. Weights purely for bodybuilding are not for Shabbat, while exercise a doctor recommends for your bones or health is fine. "Shabbat is about the present, not about the future." Shaar HaSiyun 7: one may take a long walk even to exercise and warm up. Wearing a tracksuit is not forbidden; that concern is only social.

Seif 3: if you come to a puddle or stream, you may jump over it, even a wide one that needs both feet. But you may not walk through it, because your clothes will get wet and you may come to wring them out, which is part of laundering. This continued in the next class.

Practical summary

  • Do not do Melakhah after Shabbat until you have said Atah Honantanu or Barukh HaMavdil; in the first instance, pray Arvit or make Havdalah before serious Melakhah such as driving.
  • Women who do not pray Arvit say Barukh HaMavdil Bein Kodesh LeHol before doing Melakhah.
  • Eat Melaveh Malkah, ideally with bread, as soon as possible after Havdalah; if not, cake or fruit. Never at the expense of Seudah Shelishit.
  • No running on Shabbat except to a misvah or for enjoyment; jogging you enjoy is fine.
  • Jump over puddles rather than walking through them.
