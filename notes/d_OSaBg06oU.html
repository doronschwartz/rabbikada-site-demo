Topic: When one must stand on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, and when standing is only a custom

Sources: The Rema; the Mishnah Berurah

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The repetition of the Amidah (Hazarat HaShas) was instituted for those unable to pray themselves, who relied on the Hazan. One who relies on the Hazan in this way must stand, since by answering Amen it is as if he is praying.
  • For those who have already prayed the silent Amidah, strictly speaking there is no halachic need to stand for the repetition.
  • The Ashkenazi custom, recorded by the Rema, is to stand for the repetition, but it is a custom.
  • For Sepharadim, standing is good if one can. But if one is tired and will focus better seated, one may sit for most of the repetition, certainly after Kedushah. One must stand at points such as Kedushah, Modim and Birkat Kohanim.
  • Halacha only requires standing when the Sefer Torah is being carried or moved. When it is in its place, on the Tevah being read or in the ark, there is no requirement to stand even if the ark is open, and this is true even for Ashkenazim.
  • The Ashkenazi custom is to stand when the ark is open, out of respect, but one who is weak or unable to focus, especially on Yom Kippur, has grounds to sit.
  • Among Sepharadim, especially the Spanish and Portuguese, there was never a custom to stand when the Hekhal is open, and people sit while it is open for many parts of the Tefillah. Everyone stands when the Sefer Torah is taken out.

Sources quoted

  • The Rema: records the Ashkenazi custom to stand for the repetition.
  • The Mishnah Berurah: writes clearly that even for Ashkenazim there is no halachic requirement to stand when the ark is open, adding in a footnote that it is customary to stand out of respect.

In one line

Standing for the repetition and for the open ark is a fine custom, but strictly one may sit, and where there is any need it is permissible.
