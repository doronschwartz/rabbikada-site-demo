Topic: How much Hames the search before Pesah requires

Sources: Parashat Bo; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Pesah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Parashat Bo gives two prohibitions on owning Hames over Pesah, Bal Yeraeh and Bal Yimase, even without eating it.
  • From the Torah it suffices to sincerely renounce ownership (Bitul) before Pesah, even with the Hames still visible at home.
  • The Hakhamim required searching and removal (Biur) because one may not truly renounce it (would one object if a non-Jew took the valuable whisky over Pesah?), and because one may forget and eat it.
  • One opinion requires searching even for crumbs, because of the concern of eating them.
  • Many poskim disagree: Karet applies only to eating a Kezayit, so the Hakhamim would not have required searching for crumbs, and the concern of eating stray crumbs is not real.
  • Hakham Ovadiah and many poskim rule that one must search for and remove only a Kezayit, about 27 grams: a piece of bread one might come to eat. Inedible Hames needs no search at all.
  • So cleaning for Pesah is not spring cleaning; we are looking for pieces of Hames, and no more.

Sources quoted

  • Parashat Bo: Bal Yeraeh and Bal Yimase.
  • Hakham Ovadiah: one is obliged to search only for a Kezayit.

In one line

The search before Pesah is for pieces of Hames one might eat, about a Kezayit, not for every crumb.
