Topic: The origins and status of going to the mikveh on Erev Rosh Hashanah and Erev Yom Kippur

Sources: Gemara on purifying oneself before the festival; the Shibbolei HaLeket; the Kol Bo; Rav Saadiah Gaon; the Shulhan Arukh and the Rema

Daily Halacha: Elul, Monday 23 September 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara obliges purification before the three festivals. Most Rishonim explain that this was so one could eat the festival offerings in purity, which means it applies only to the three festivals and is not relevant today.
  • The Shibbolei HaLeket, a late Rishon from Italy, understood a broader idea of purity for the festival, and argued that it applies all the more before Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. Most authorities disagree: there is no obligation to go to the mikveh before either day.
  • Erev Rosh Hashanah: the Kol Bo (about 700 years ago, author uncertain) writes of going to the mikveh to come pure to the prayers and the Shofar. The Shulhan Arukh does not record it; only the Rema does, for Ashkenazim.
  • Erev Yom Kippur: many Rishonim record the custom. Rav Saadiah Gaon even held it obligatory with a berakhah; everyone disagrees, and no berakhah is said. The Shulhan Arukh does record this minhag, so one should try to keep it.
  • Timing: some say after midday, but the Shulhan Arukh says any time during the day, so the morning is fine.
  • In case of need, one may fulfil the minhag with a shower of nine kabin (about 11 litres, a couple of minutes), with that intention.
  • This minhag is for men, not for women.

In one line

There is no obligation, but going to the mikveh on Erev Yom Kippur is a well-established minhag recorded by the Shulhan Arukh (any time that day), while Erev Rosh Hashanah is an Ashkenazi custom recorded by the Rema.
