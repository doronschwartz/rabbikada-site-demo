Topic: Shehiyanu or HaTov VeHaMetiv on buying a new house

Sources: The Mishnah; the Gemara and the Rishonim; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Ben Ish Hai

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Wednesday 26 February 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Mishnah, codified by the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh: one who buys a new house and moves in says a berakhah. This applies to buying a home to live in, not renting or buying property for income.
  • Bought alone, the berakhah is Shehiyanu. Bought with a spouse, equally invested and both rejoicing, it is HaTov VeHaMetiv; one says it for both and the other answers Amen. The same applies with one's children.
  • A house on a mortgage counts as one's own, provided there is a reasonable plan to repay and no real expectation of losing it.
  • This is the halacha of the Gemara, the Rishonim and the Shulhan Arukh, and seems to be the ruling for Ashkenazim.
  • In the last couple of hundred years some Sepharadi poskim, including the Ben Ish Hai, wrote that the custom is not to say it on a house but to include it with a new garment or new fruit. The reason is unclear, perhaps that this Shehiyanu is voluntary.
  • If one can join it to a new suit, dress or fruit, fine; otherwise one should say it, since it is clear in the sources and it would be a shame to lose it. If we say it on clothes and fruit, all the more on a new home.

In one line

On a new home one says Shehiyanu alone or HaTov VeHaMetiv when shared, ideally not giving up the berakhah because of the later custom.
