Topic: Why particular Tehillim are read on Sukkot and Shemini Aseret

Sources: Masekhet Soferim; Tehillim 76, 42, 43 and 12; the Abudarham; the Keter Shem Tov (Rav Shem Tov Gaguine); Rashi on Tehillim 42

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Sunday 12 October 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • A psalm for each festival is first mentioned in Masekhet Soferim, from around the Geonim (7th to 9th century).
  • The Ashkenazi custom follows Masekhet Soferim: Tehillim 76 ("Noda biYhudah Elohim"), which explicitly mentions His sukkah.
  • Sepharadim read Tehillim 42 and 43; the first source he found is the Abudarham, about 600 years ago. Neither mentions Sukkot.
  • The Keter Shem Tov gives reasons: going to the House of HaShem "with joy and thanks" (true of all three festivals); "my soul thirsts... when shall I come and appear", which Rashi reads as the yearning after the Temple's destruction to go up again.
  • The hint to Sukkot: "basakh", which Rashi explains in one reading as covered, decorated wagons, from the root of sukkah.
  • His own suggestion: three times the psalm says "why are you downcast... hope", fitting Sukkot, zeman simhatenu, the happiest time of the year.
  • Shemini Aseret and Simhat Torah: Sepharadim read Tehillim 12, which opens "al hasheminit", on the eighth.
  • Gaguine adds "imrot HaShem imrot tehorot", the words of HaShem are pure and refine us, fitting the completion of the Torah reading on Simhat Torah.

Sources quoted

  • Masekhet Soferim: source of the Sukkot psalm, Tehillim 76.
  • Abudarham: earliest source for Tehillim 42 and 43.
  • Keter Shem Tov: reasons for the Sepharadi psalms.
  • Rashi: Tehillim 42 as yearning for the Temple; "sakh" as covered wagons.

In one line

Sepharadim read Tehillim 42 and 43 on Sukkot for their yearning for the Temple and the hint of "sakh", and Tehillim 12 on Shemini Aseret for "the eighth" and the purity of the Torah.
