Topic: The Mishnah's ruling against teaching daughters Torah, its meaning, and the first exception

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Tuesday 4 May 2021

The halacha

  • The source is the Mishnah in Sotah. Ben Azzai held a father should teach his daughter Torah; Rabbi Eliezer said that whoever teaches his daughter Torah is as if he teaches her tiflut.
  • Rashi reads tiflut as deviousness: the depths of Gemara sharpen the mind and may teach improper behaviour. Rambam reads it as emptiness: most cannot fully concentrate and turn the words of Torah into idle matters.
  • Almost all the Rishonim, the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh rule like Rabbi Eliezer. The Riaz (Rabbi Yeshayah of Trani, Italy) ruled like Ben Azzai, but the halacha does not follow him.
  • For the Rambam this refers to the Oral Torah. The Written Torah does not carry the problem of tiflut, but it is still not encouraged.
  • There are four caveats. The first, from the Maharil (14th-century Germany): the Mishnah speaks only of a father teaching his daughter; if she wishes to learn by herself there is no problem at all.
  • The Perishah (Rav Falk, about 400 years ago) adds that the concern is about most women in a formal system; one who wants to learn shows she is not of that majority.
  • The remaining exceptions and the practical halacha come tomorrow.

Sources quoted

  • Mishnah Sotah: Ben Azzai and Rabbi Eliezer on teaching a daughter Torah.
  • Rashi and Rambam: two readings of tiflut.
  • The Maharil and the Perishah: a woman who learns by herself is not included.

In one line

The halacha discourages a father from teaching his daughter the Oral Torah, but a woman who chooses to learn by herself may do so.
