Topic: Why proof of Jewish identity is required in practice today

Sources: Shulhan Arukh, Yoreh Deah; the Rambam; the Shakh (Siftei Kohen); the Maggid Mishneh; Rav Hayyim of Volozhin; the Hazon Ish; Rav Herzog

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 27 February 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Shulhan Arukh, in Yoreh Deah, quotes the Rambam, and the Aharonim, mainly the Shakh, read him like the Rambam's Teshuvah and the Maggid Mishneh's second explanation: one who says he is Jewish is accepted, unless long known as non-Jewish. This is the view of most Aharonim.
  • Yet in practice today such a person is not simply accepted, for two reasons.
  • First, many people now want to be Jewish, especially for the right of return to Israel, so the situation differs from earlier times.
  • Second, since the 1800s there have been Reform conversions, which are not valid in halacha: such a person is not Jewish, clearly and unequivocally. People may sincerely say they are Jewish when a mother or grandmother converted that way.
  • Rav Hayyim of Volozhin wrote that one who could not speak Yiddish was presumed not Jewish; the Hazon Ish and others said that was only for his time and place, but it shows the rule was already not taken at face value.
  • Contemporary Rabbanim write that we cannot simply accept the claim, especially where there is a reason for doubt, such as knowing nothing of the misvot, or, as Rav Herzog discusses, being married to a non-Jew.
  • So in practice Batei Din worldwide ask for proof for marriage, community membership and burial.

In one line

Though the strict halacha believes one who says he is Jewish, Batei Din today require proof because of changed circumstances, including invalid Reform conversions.
