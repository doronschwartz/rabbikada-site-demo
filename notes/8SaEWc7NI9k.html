Topic: Can one fulfil the misvah of Megillah by hearing it over the telephone?

Sources: Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach; Rav Ovadiah Yoseph; Rav Moshe Feinstein; Rav Sevi Pesah Frank

Daily Halacha: Purim, Tuesday 16 February 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Reading the Megillah, the first of the four misvot of Purim, applies to men, women and children above the age of education (six or seven): one reads it or at least hears it from someone else.
  • The telephone question was discussed at least 50 years ago, and with new technology there are often different views on applying older law. There are three main opinions.
  • Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach: one does not fulfil the misvah, since a telephone mechanically reproduces the voice, and one must hear it from a human being obligated in the misvah. Rav Ovadiah Yoseph favoured this view.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein, probably the great halachic authority of 20th-century America: one does fulfil it. Even in person the voice reaches the listener as sound waves that strike the eardrum, so every voice is a reproduction and a phone is no different. This is debatable, since sound waves are the natural way we were created to speak, while a phone adds a mechanical step.
  • Rav Sevi Pesah Frank, Chief Rabbi of Jerusalem, also permits it, for a different reason: what is heard is not the voice itself but a grama, the result of the voice, and one can fulfil the misvah through the consequence of a human voice.
  • So Rav Auerbach and Rav Ovadiah Yoseph forbid, and Rav Feinstein and Rav Frank permit for different reasons. Zoom is somewhat different and comes tomorrow.

In one line

Three main views: the telephone does not work (Rav Auerbach, Rav Ovadiah Yoseph), or it does, either because every voice is reproduced (Rav Feinstein) or because a voice's result suffices (Rav Frank).
