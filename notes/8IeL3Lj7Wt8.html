Comprehensive Study Notes

Review of Muktzeh Categories

Before beginning Se'if 6, we reviewed the four main categories of muktzeh learned previously:

  1. Chisaron Kis (חיסרון כיס) - Expensive and sensitive items
  2. Kli Shemelachto L'Issur (כלי שמלאכתו לאיסור) - Utensils whose primary use is forbidden
  3. Kli Shemelachto L'Heter (כלי שמלאכתו להיתר) - Utensils whose primary use is permitted
  4. Muktzeh Machmat Gufo (מוקצה מחמת גופו) - Items intrinsically muktzeh (introduced in Se'if 6)

Siman 308, Se'if 6 - Broken Utensils and Introduction to Muktzeh Machmat Gufo

Main Halacha

Source: Shulchan Aruch Siman 308, Se'if 6

Text: "כל הכלים שנשברו אפילו בשבת מותר לטלטלם שבריהם, ובלבד שיהיו ראויים לשום מלאכה"

Translation: "All utensils that break, even on Shabbat, it is permitted to move their fragments, provided they are suitable for some purpose."

Key Details:

  1. Examples given in Gemara:
  • שברי ערבה לכסות בה פי חבית (fragments of earthenware to cover the mouth of a barrel)
  • שברי זכוכית לכסות בה פי הפך (glass fragments to cover the mouth of a vessel)
  1. Underlying Principle:
  • When Shabbat began, the item had Torat Kli Alav (תורת כלי עליו) - the status of a utensil
  • Even when broken, this status extends to the fragments
  • Normally, broken pieces would be muktzeh machmat gufo, but here they retain permissibility
  1. Contemporary Application:
  • Modern poskim debate whether this applies today since people rarely use broken glass/pottery for covering vessels
  • Ruling: Still permitted because there remains potential for use, even if uncommon in practice
  1. Complete Loss of Function:
  • If fragments have absolutely no purpose, they become muktzeh

Practical Applications:

Permitted Examples:

  • Empty crisp packets - retain torat kli status throughout Shabbat
  • Plastic bottles (when not thrown away before Shabbat)
  • Any packaging that housed food/items when Shabbat began

Exception - Israeli Milk Sacks:

  • Have no reuse value when empty
  • Must be disposed of immediately and replaced

Rama's Addition - Gref Shel Re'i

Source: Rama on Se'if 6

Text: "ואם נשברו במקום שיכולים להזיק, כגון זכוכית שנשברה על השולחן או במקום שהולכים, מותר לטלטל השברים כדי לפנותן שלא יזיקו בהם"

Gref Shel Re'i Principle:

  • Items that are disgusting, unpleasant, or potentially dangerous may be moved even if muktzeh machmat gufo
  • Examples:
  • Soiled diaper - can move to dispose of it
  • Dangerous glass fragments - can clear them for safety

Important Limitations:

  1. Once removed from problematic area - becomes muktzeh again
  2. Cannot move again - e.g., cannot take soiled diaper out of bin once placed there
  3. Must be causing active discomfort/danger - if moved to unused room, cannot move again

Distinction from Main Halacha:

  • Gref Shel Re'i: Can move and put away, then becomes muktzeh
  • Fragments with potential use: Can move freely throughout Shabbat

Siman 308, Se'if 7 - Fragments That Broke Before Shabbat

Main Halacha

Source: Shulchan Aruch Siman 308, Se'if 7

Text: "חתיכת חרס שנשברה בחול מכלי, או ראויה לכסות בה כלי, מותר לטלטלה אפילו במקום שאין כלים מצויים שם לכסותן בה"

Translation: "A pottery fragment that broke on a weekday from a utensil, if it's suitable to cover a utensil with it, it's permitted to move it even in a place where there are no utensils there to cover with it."

Key Points:

  1. Location Independence:
  • Even in reshut harabim (public domain) where no utensils are present
  • The potential for use is sufficient to maintain non-muktzeh status
  • Practical example: Using fragment to clean shoes in public area
  1. Potential vs. Actual Use:
  • Don't need actual utensils present to cover
  • The inherent capability maintains the status

Critical Exception - Disposal Before Shabbat

Text: "ואם זרקה לאשפה מבעוד יום, אסור לטלטלה, כיוון שנטלה מלהיות עוד כלי"

Translation: "But if it was thrown in the garbage while still day, it's forbidden to move it, since it was removed from being a utensil."

Practical Example:

Friday Afternoon Scenario:

  • Finish plastic water bottle
  • Throw in bin before Shabbat
  • Friday night, want to retrieve it
  • Result: Forbidden - became muktzeh machmat gufo

Shabbat Scenario:

  • Finish bottle on Friday night
  • Throw in bin on Shabbat
  • Later want to retrieve it
  • Result: Permitted - maintains torat kli throughout Shabbat

Summary Principle:

  • Disposal before Shabbat = loses kli status permanently for that Shabbat
  • Disposal on Shabbat = maintains kli status for duration of that Shabbat
  • Following Shabbat = loses status for subsequent Shabbatot

Rama's Addition - Wealthy vs. Poor Perspective

Source: Rama on Se'if 7

Text: "ואף על פי שחזי לעני, הואיל ובקצירה ובדעת הבעלים שלו, אף על פי שהם עשירים, דמוקצה לעשירים, מוקצה לעניים"

Principle: Muktzeh L'Ashirim, Havi Muktzeh L'Aniyim

  • We follow the owner's perspective, not the observer's
  • If wealthy owner discarded item (though poor person would reuse it), item becomes muktzeh for everyone
  • Owner's declaration that it's no longer a kli removes its status universally

Practical Application:

Scenario: Guest in wealthy host's home

  • Host throws away reusable plastic bottle Friday afternoon
  • Guest would normally reuse such bottles
  • Result: Guest also cannot move the bottle - follows owner's designation

Safek (Doubt) Cases:

Question: What if uncertain whether disposal was before or after Shabbat?

Answer from Biur Halacha:

  • Can be lenient due to:
  • Muktzeh being d'rabbanan (rabbinic)
  • Principle of leniency in safek d'rabbanan
  • Especially in public places
  • Practical ruling: Assume disposal was on Shabbat, therefore permitted

Continuation of Se'if 7 - Items Never Having Kli Status

Text: "ודווקא חתיכת חרס משום דעתיה משברי כלים. אבל דבר שאין בו שייכות כלי, כגון צרורות או אבנים, אף על פי שראויים לכסות בהם כלי, אסור לטלטלם"

Muktzeh Machmat Gufo Explicitly Defined:

Permitted: Fragments of actual utensils

  • Retain connection to original kli status
  • May be moved even with remote potential use

Forbidden: Items never having kli status

  • Examples: Rocks, stones, pebbles (צרורות או אבנים)
  • Even if suitable as bottle stoppers
  • Reason: Never designated (מיוחד) for specific use before Shabbat
  • Classification: Muktzeh machmat gufo

Rama's Addition - Complete Prohibition

Text: "וכל דבר שאינו כלי כלל, אסור לטלטלו אפילו לצורך גופו ואפילו לצורך מקומו"

Stringency: Items with no kli status cannot be moved for ANY purpose:

  • Not l'tzorech gufo (for its use)
  • Not l'tzorech mekomo (for its place)
  • Reason: Complete absence of kli status

Examples of Muktzeh Machmat Gufo:

  • Raw foods (to be learned later)
  • Natural objects without designated purpose
  • Any item lacking kli status and specific pre-Shabbat designation

Siman 308, Se'if 8 - Doors and Coverings That Fall Off

Main Halacha

Source: Shulchan Aruch Siman 308, Se'if 8

Text: "כל הכלים הניתלים בשבת, דלתותיהן שנתפרקו הן ניצלים. בין נתפרקו בחול בין נתפרקו בשבת"

Translation: "All utensils that are permitted to be moved on Shabbat - their doors that became detached are permitted [to be moved], whether they became detached on weekday or on Shabbat."

Key Principles:

  1. Doors of Small Utensils:
  • Maintain non-muktzeh status even when detached
  • Reason: Easy to reattach, continues connection to original kli
  • Examples: Small cabinet doors, cupboard doors, chair handles
  1. Common Misconception:
  • People often think detached parts become muktzeh
  • Halacha: Explicitly permits moving them
  1. Size Limitations:
  • Permitted: Small, easily reattachable items
  • Forbidden: Large doors/windows that are difficult to reinstall
  • Reason: Concern about coming to fix/build (גזירה שמא יתקע)

Practical Applications:

Permitted Examples:

  • Bedside cabinet door falls off hinges
  • Chair handle detaches
  • Small cupboard door comes loose

Forbidden Examples:

  • House door falls off
  • Window frame comes out
  • Large, structurally integrated elements

Siman 308, Se'if 9 - Reinstalling Doors

Main Halacha

Source: Shulchan Aruch Siman 308, Se'if 9

Text: "דלת של שידה, תיבה ומגדל יכולים לטלו מהם. ואסור להחזירה, גזירה שמא יתקע"

Translation: "The door of a chest, box, or cabinet - one can remove it from them. But it's forbidden to return it [to its place], a decree lest one come to fix it."

Detailed Permissions and Restrictions:

Permitted:

  • Moving the detached door
  • Using it for other purposes (e.g., propping up for privacy)

Forbidden:

  • Placing it back in its exact original position
  • Reason: Concern about coming to permanently fix it (bring hammer, etc.)

Middle Ground:

  • Can prop it up against the opening for temporary closure
  • Cannot actually reinstall it in working position

Special Case - Chicken Coops

Text: "ושולל של תרנגולים אסור, בין ליטול בין להחזיר"

Reason: These enclosures are attached to the ground

  • Involves: Binyan (building) issues
  • Result: Complete prohibition on moving

Siman 308, Se'if 10 - Covers of Pits and Holes

Main Halacha

Source: Shulchan Aruch Siman 308, Se'if 10

Text: "כיסוי בור ודות אין נטלים אלא אם כן יש להם בית אחיזה. דאז מוכח שהם כלי"

Translation: "Covers of pits and cisterns are not moved unless they have a handle, for then it's evident that they are utensils."

Underlying Principle - Binyan (Building):

The Handle Test:

  • With handle: Clearly designed to be opened/closed regularly
  • Status: Considered a kli
  • Result: Permitted to move
  • Without handle: Not intended for regular opening
  • Status: Part of fixed structure
  • Result: Forbidden due to binyan u'stirah (building and demolishing)

Comparison to Doors:

Question: Why are doors permitted to open/close on Shabbat? Answer: The derech (normal way) of doors is to open and close

  • Not considered binyan because it's the intended function
  • Rabbi Shlomo Zalman Auerbach's argument against Chazon Ish on electricity:
  • Opening/closing electrical circuits should be like opening/closing doors
  • Normal function shouldn't constitute binyan

Practical Examples:

Forbidden:

  • Manhole covers without handles
  • Street utility box covers (unless they have specific handles/keyholes for authorized access)

Permitted:

  • Covers with clear handles or gripping mechanisms
  • Utility boxes with keyholes indicating intended access

Covers of Utensils vs. Ground-Based Covers:

Text: "ושאר כלים אפילו הם מחוברים בטיט יכולים לטלטל אפילו אין להם בית אחיזה"

Utensil Covers (Always Permitted):

  • Can open/close even without handles
  • Reason: Part of a kli, no binyan issues
  • Principle: אין בניין וסתירה בכלים (no building/demolishing with utensils)

Ground-Based Covers:

  • Only permitted with handles
  • Reason: Connected to ground, involves binyan concerns

Siman 308, Se'if 11 - Complete Needles

Main Halacha

Source: Shulchan Aruch Siman 308, Se'if 11

Text: "מחט שלמה מותר לטלטלה ליטול בו את הקוץ"

Translation: "A complete needle is permitted to move it to remove a thorn with it."

Classification Analysis:

Expected Classification: Kli shemelachto l'issur

  • Primary use: Sewing (forbidden on Shabbat)
  • Normal rule: Only permitted l'tzorech gufo or l'tzorech mekomo

Actual Ruling: Permitted for removing thorns

  • Reason: Using it l'tzorech gufo (for its beneficial use)
  • Application: Removing splinters, thorns from skin
  • Status: Alternative permitted use makes it moveable

Broken Needle:

Text: "ניטל חודה או חור שלה, אסור"

Translation: "If its point or its eye is removed, it's forbidden."

Reasoning:

  • No longer functions as a needle
  • Cannot remove thorns effectively
  • New classification: Muktzeh machmat gufo
  • Result: Completely forbidden to move

Siman 308, Se'if 12 - Fragments of Mats and Cloths

Mats (מחצלאות)

Text: "שירי מחצלאות מותר לטלטלם. דחזי לכסות בו טינופא"

Permitted: Leftover pieces of mats

  • Reason: Can cover dirt/unpleasant things
  • Principle: Retains connection to original kli status

Exception: "אם זרקן לאשפה מבעוד יום אסור לטלטלם"

  • If thrown away before Shabbat, becomes muktzeh
  • Same principle as learned in Se'if 7

Cloths/Rags (מטליות)

Text: "שירי מטליות שבלו, אם יש בהם שלוש אצבעות על שלוש אצבעות מותר לטלטלן ואם לאו אסור"

Size Requirement:

  • Minimum: 3 fingers by 3 fingers
  • Reason: Sufficient size to be useful for cleaning
  • Below minimum: Becomes muktzeh machmat gufo

Reason for Distinction:

  • Rags don't have strong kli status like mats
  • Need sufficient size to maintain practical utility
  • Smaller pieces have no meaningful use

Lenient Opinion: "ויש מתירין אפילו אין בהם שלוש על שלוש"

  • Some permit even smaller pieces
  • Shows difference of opinion on minimum useful size

Special Case - Tashmishei Mitzvah

Text: "ובלבד שלא יהיו תשמישי מצווה"

Exception: Items used for mitzvot (e.g., tallit)

  • General rule: Leftover pieces of utensils can be used for cleaning
  • Mitzvah items: Cannot be used for undignified purposes
  • Result: Broken pieces of tallit become muktzeh

Reasoning:

  • Shulchan Aruch permits discarding tashmishei mitzvah when no longer usable
  • But forbids תשמיש של גנאי (undignified use)
  • Since cleaning would be undignified use, such fragments become muktzeh
  • Distinction: Tashmishei mitzvah vs. tashmishei kedushah (which require genizah)

Summary of Key Principles Learned

1. Muktzeh Machmat Gufo Categories:

  • Items never having kli status
  • Natural objects without designation
  • Broken items that lose all function

2. Fragments of Utensils:

  • Generally retain non-muktzeh status
  • Must have some potential use
  • Original kli status extends to pieces

3. Disposal Rules:

  • Before Shabbat = loses kli status
  • On Shabbat = maintains status for that Shabbat
  • Owner's perspective determines status for all

4. Doors and Covers:

  • Small utensil parts remain non-muktzeh
  • Cannot reinstall due to building concerns
  • Ground-based covers need handles

5. Size and Function Requirements:

  • Some items need minimum size for utility
  • Complete loss of function = muktzeh
  • Potential use sufficient even if uncommon

6. Special Considerations:

  • Gref shel re'i for dangerous/unpleasant items
  • Tashmishei mitzvah restrictions
  • Building concerns with structural elements
