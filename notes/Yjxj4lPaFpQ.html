These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

A night of transmission

The night of Pesah is the only night of the year on which Hallel is said, and it is said twice. It is the night on which we hand down our beliefs and our tradition to the next generation, which is why Pesah is so rich in customs that differ between communities and even between families. Everything in the class applies equally to women, since they too came out of Egypt, except where the Rabbi said otherwise.

The Seder plate

The plate holds the massot, the shank bone (zeroa), the egg, the maror and hazeret (Sefaradim use lettuce for both), the karpas (celery or parsley) and the haroset. The massot should be at the top of the plate, unless one's custom is to keep them separately.

  • Three massot. Kohen, Levi and Yisrael "are all very nice ideas", but the halachic reason is simple: on Shabbat and Yom Tov we need two whole loaves, recalling the double portion of manna, and we also break one massah at Yahas. So we need three, leaving two whole ones for Mosi Massah.
  • The shank bone and the egg recall the Pesah offering and the festival offering. The arm recalls the "outstretched arm". The egg is a sign of mourning: the first night of Pesah always falls on the same weekday as Tisha BeAv. Even on this happy night, we do not have the real Pesah offering. The Taz gives two reasons for the Ashkenazi kittel: it is regal, and it is a reminder of death.
  • Arrangement. Ashkenazim arrange the plate in the order the items are used. Spanish and Portuguese and Eastern Sefaradim have their own arrangements, some following the Arizal. "Follow your minhag."

Kadesh and the four cups

Many chant the fifteen simanim first, a custom, not a requirement.

Kadesh does two things: it is Kiddush, a Torah misvah every Yom Tov, and it is the first of the four cups. During the year, someone who hears Kiddush fulfils the misvah without drinking. On Pesah night each person must drink, because the cups are about experiencing freedom, and "you can't allow someone else to experience for you," just as no one can put on tefillin for you. Men and women must drink; children should not be made to drink four cups.

  • Others should pour for you, at least for the head of the household.
  • Drink a reviit, about 86ml, in two to four gulps. After the fact, a cheekful (about 45ml) suffices. Better a normal cup than a huge silver goblet, since some require drinking most of the cup.
  • Recline to the left at about 45 degrees, on something. "Leaning into the air is not leaning." Sefaradim who did not recline must repeat; Ashkenazim rely on the Raviah and do not. Sefaradi men and women recline; among Ashkenazim, only men.
  • Wine is better than grape juice, but someone who will not focus on the story after wine should use grape juice.
  • Shehiyanu covers all the misvot of the night, including the search for hames the night before.
  • Rinse the cup inside and out before Kiddush, even if it is clean. Between the four cups there is no need; the Kabbalists rinse the leader's cup each time.

Karpas, Urhas and Yahas

Karpas is dipped into salt water, lemon water or vinegar. It is done to arouse curiosity (normally after Kiddush one washes for bread) and as an appetiser in the manner of the aristocracy. Eat less than a kezayit, so that no berakhah is said after it and its borei peri haadamah covers the maror later. How the berakhah lasts for hours is debated. Rav Ben Sion Abba Shaul, perplexed by this, suggested eating a little more karpas periodically to keep the link.

Urhas. All year, whenever food is dipped into one of the seven liquids, the hands are washed without a berakhah. That is why we wash before karpas: "it's not to make the children ask."

Yahas. We break the middle massah because lehem oni is the bread of the poor man, who always saves some for later, and "the bread over which many things are answered" must be in front of us during Maggid. The middle one is broken so that the top massah remains whole for Mosi Massah. The larger piece is kept for the afikoman: the Ben Ish Hai says it shows that things will be better at the end. The Syrian custom is to break it into the shapes of a dalet and a vav, a Kabbalistic idea. The afikoman is wrapped in a napkin. Children "stealing" the afikoman was not traditionally a Sefaradi custom.

Maggid: the Haggadah is not a prayer

Telling the story is one of the two Torah misvot unique to this night. The Rabbi stressed what he says every year. In prayer, standing before Hashem is valuable even without understanding every word. But "you can't read a book and say you've read the book if you don't understand the language." Reading every word of the Haggadah without understanding does not fulfil the misvah.

  • Mah Nishtanah is one question in four parts: why is this night different? Avadim Hayinu is the answer. In the Syrian custom the leader announces in Arabic: "This is the answer."
  • The minimum, for men and women, is Mah Nishtanah, Avadim Hayinu and Rabban Gamliel's "Pesah, Massah and Maror". These must be translated and explained.
  • The rest is a springboard for discussion: the midrashim, the counting of the plagues, the story of Rabbi Eliezer. Use the text to prompt questions; do not treat it as set in stone. Before each paragraph, explain what it is for.
  • The parent should lead, not the children. Children's divrei Torah can be spread through the meal.
  • The Shulhan Arukh says that on this night one should talk about the Exodus until sleep overtakes you. Staying up on Shavuot or Hoshanah Rabbah is a custom; this is the one night the Shulhan Arukh speaks of.
  • Asking questions is the greatest expression of intellectual freedom, alongside the physical freedom of the cups, reclining and massah.

Massah, Maror and Korekh

Wash again, without a berakhah on the second cup for Sefaradim. Say hamosi holding all three massot, for lehem mishneh, then put down the bottom one and say al akhilat massah over the top and broken ones.

  • The leader eats a kezayit from the top massah and a kezayit from the broken one. Everyone else needs only one kezayit, with a piece of the top massah supplemented. A kezayit was given as about 27 grams; one is the Torah minimum for those who cannot manage more. The Rambam writes that the more massah one eats, the more praiseworthy.
  • Eat within about five minutes, reclining. Do not chat from the massah until after Korekh, though explaining the misvot is part of them.
  • Maror is rabbinic today: a kezayit, dipped into thick haroset recalling the mortar, then shaken off; no reclining.
  • Korekh follows the opinion that massah and maror are eaten together. Recline.

The meal to Nirsah

During Shulhan Orekh, keep talking about Egypt rather than "the football". Do not eat roasted meat, which looks like eating the Pesah offering outside the Temple. The afikoman recalls the Pesah offering, so eat a kezayit (some eat two) before halachic midnight, which that year was about 1.05am. Afterwards, only drinks such as tea, besides the third and fourth cups. Hallel is a misvah for women too, so they should not leave before it.

Kashering for Pesah

The Rabbi gave the basic rules quickly, inviting questions afterwards.

  • Earthenware and glazed china cannot be kashered and must be put away, even if used only cold.
  • Items used only cold with hames, such as a Kiddush cup, simply need washing. For Sefaradim, an item used mostly for non-hames does not need kashering even if hot hames was occasionally in it. Having special Pesah utensils is lovely but not the halacha. "It's very hard to make something taref."
  • Plates that only receive hot food from a pot are a keli sheni, which in theory does not absorb. Rav Ovadia Yosef writes that one should still kasher them in the first instance, and Ashkenazim do not accept the leniency.
  • Microwave: leave 24 hours, then heat water in it for about ten minutes.
  • Dishwasher: clean every groove and run an empty cycle. Rav Ovadia Yosef permits one dishwasher for meat and milk; in practice run separate cycles.
  • Oven: self-clean, or the highest setting for half an hour to an hour; the racks can stay in but may be ruined.
  • Glass does not absorb, following the Shulhan Arukh: wash it and use it. Plastic is debated.
  • Urn: fine if used only for water; if hallot were warmed on the lid, kasher the lid.
  • Countertops: pour boiling water or cover them, not both, and only where hot food is placed. Hotplates: clean and leave on for half an hour to an hour. Sink: pour boiling water over it in a circular motion from the bottom up.

Practical summary

  • Three massot: two whole for lehem mishneh, one broken at Yahas for lehem oni.
  • Everyone, men and women, drinks a reviit for each cup, reclining properly.
  • Karpas less than a kezayit; wash without a berakhah before dipping.
  • Explain the Haggadah. Mah Nishtanah and Avadim Hayinu are the heart of Maggid.
  • The leader eats two kezeitim at Mosi Massah, everyone else one; eat the afikoman before halachic midnight.
  • When kashering, learn the principles: cold use needs washing, earthenware cannot be kashered, and in most cases boiling water or high heat does the job.
