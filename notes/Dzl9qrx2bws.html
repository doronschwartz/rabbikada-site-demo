Topic: Why the shofar is not blown when Rosh Hashanah falls on Shabbat

Sources: The Rambam; the Rif (Rabbi Yishak Alfasi)

Daily Halacha: Rosh Hashanah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • From the Torah the shofar should be blown on the first day of Rosh Hashanah even on Shabbat. About 2,000 years ago the Hakhamim enacted not to blow, lest someone carry the shofar in a public domain to learn how to blow it. The Hakhamim have authority to stop a Torah act when a greater concern is involved.
  • In the Bet HaMikdash they blew even on Shabbat, since the decrees of the Hakhamim did not apply in the Temple and its vicinity, which according to the Rambam means ancient Yerushalayim.
  • After the destruction, Rabban Yohanan ben Zakkai instituted, as a remembrance of the Mikdash, that any place with a formal Bet Din would blow on Rosh Hashanah that falls on Shabbat.
  • The Rishonim differ on what Bet Din is meant: according to the Rambam a court of 71, according to others 23, and according to the Rif even three. The Rif, in eleventh century North Africa, blew the shofar with his Bet Din on Rosh Hashanah that fell on Shabbat.
  • Many Rishonim disagreed, so we hold it requires a Bet Din with semikhah going back to the Sanhedrin, which we no longer have. So even before the greatest Bet Din in Yerushalayim today the shofar is not blown.
  • About 100 years ago a Hungarian rabbi who moved to Israel tried to restore blowing in ancient Yerushalayim on Shabbat. He had some support but it did not take hold and was forgotten.
  • Today no community anywhere blows on the first day when it is Shabbat; this year the shofar is blown only on the second day, Sunday, on a rabbinic level.

Sources quoted

  • The Rambam: the Temple's vicinity is Yerushalayim, and the Bet Din required is one of 71.
  • The Rif: even a Bet Din of three suffices, and he blew with his own.

In one line

Because of the rabbinic decree against carrying, and with no Bet Din with true semikhah today, the shofar is not blown when Rosh Hashanah falls on Shabbat.
