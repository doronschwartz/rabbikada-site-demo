Topic: Why Pidyon HaBen applies only to a natural birth, and what this teaches about Pesah

Sources: The Gemara; the Torah, end of Parashat Bo; the Haggadah

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Wednesday 13 April 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • A firstborn boy of parents who are not Kohanim or Leviim is redeemed from a Kohen at a Pidyon HaBen. The Gemara and the halacha limit this to a natural birth: a baby born by caesarean has no Pidyon HaBen. There are scriptural sources; here he explains the rationale.
  • Childbirth, historically, brought a woman close to death; what looks and sounds like dying brings life. History follows the same pattern: Egypt, the "iron crucible", gave rise to a nation and Sinai; Purim turned annihilation into influence in Persia; three years after 1945 the Jewish people came home to Israel. A seed must rot before it sprouts.
  • Pidyon HaBen commemorates, as the end of Parashat Bo says, the killing of the Egyptian firstborn and the sparing of the Jewish firstborn. The Haggadah stresses this was done by HaKadosh Barukh Hu directly, with no agent.
  • A natural birth without human intervention reflects that direct divine hand turning near death into life, exactly the story of Egypt. A birth needing human intervention is no less special, but it is a different kind of birth, more suited to Shavuot, which commemorates human development.
  • Pesah gives hope: in every generation, out of darkness and fragility comes redemption.

In one line

Pidyon HaBen follows natural birth because it recalls God Himself turning near death into life in Egypt.
