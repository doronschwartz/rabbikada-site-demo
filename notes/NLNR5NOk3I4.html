Topic: The prohibition of making or owning a replica of the Mishkan's Menorah

Sources: The Torah; the Gemara; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Our Hanukkah Menorah (eight branches and a shamash) is not the Menorah of the Bet HaMikdash, which had six branches and a central stem, seven lights in all. They serve different purposes.
  • The Gemara, codified by Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh, forbids making a replica of the Mishkan or any of its vessels; this is a Torah prohibition.
  • The Rishonim give three opinions: only an identical copy is forbidden (so leaving out the decorative flowers and goblets would be permitted); anything that would be kasher in the Mishkan, even bediavad, is forbidden (the Hashmonaim's Menorah lacked the adornments); or any seven-branched Menorah is forbidden.
  • The Shulhan Arukh takes the middle view. So a seven-branched Menorah of any metal (ideally gold, but any metal would be kasher) may not be owned or possessed.
  • A seven-branched Menorah of glass, wood or earthenware, which could never be used in the Mishkan, is permitted. Hakham Ovadiah also permits an electric one, and one that holds only candles, not wicks, would be permitted as so different.
  • Before buying a seven-branched Menorah one should check, and ask if unsure.

In one line

An eight-branched Hanukkah Menorah is fine, but a seven-branched metal Menorah may not be owned, as it replicates the Mishkan's.
