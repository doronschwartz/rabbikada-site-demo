Topic: The two reasons for Mishloah Manot and the cases where they differ

Sources: The Rishonim; the Rama; the later authorities

Daily Halacha: Purim, Monday 22 February 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Mishloah Manot, the second of the four misvot of Purim, obliges men and women to give one other Jew two portions of edible food.
  • The Rishonim give two reasons: Mordekhai instituted it so everyone would have enough food for the seudah; or to increase friendship, rectifying Haman's claim to Ahashverosh that the Jews are a scattered nation lacking unity.
  • First case: you bring Mishloah Manot to someone, perhaps to repair a strained relationship, and he slams the door. By the food reason you have not fulfilled it, as he has no extra food; by the friendship reason you have done your part. The Rama rules one fulfils it, but later authorities say it depends on this question, so one should give to someone else.
  • Second case: you give it anonymously, and the recipient does not know who sent it. By the food reason you have fulfilled it; by the friendship reason you have not.
  • We try to satisfy both reasons, so in either case one should repeat the misvah, giving to someone who accepts it and knows it is from you.
  • Tomorrow: whether someone in isolation can fulfil it by giving within his own family.

Sources quoted

  • The Rama: one whose gift is refused has fulfilled the misvah, though later authorities make it depend on the two reasons.

In one line

Since Mishloah Manot serves both the seudah and friendship, a refused or anonymous gift should be followed by another that is received and known.
