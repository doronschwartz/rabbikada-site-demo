Topic: Whether one may charge for teaching Torah

Sources: The Gemara (Mah ani behinam); Parashat Vaethanan; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Gemara on Karna

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Tuesday 28 May 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • In Parashat Vaethanan Moshe says he taught "as Hashem commanded me". The Gemara derives: just as I learned for free, so you must teach for free.
  • The Rambam, followed by the Shulhan Arukh, limits this to the Oral Torah. One may charge for the Written Torah where that is the practice, as it is today: reading, the taamim, classes on the Parashah, bar misvah lessons. What Hashem taught Moshe over forty days was the Oral Torah, the explanations.
  • Classes on Gemara, halacha and Torah in general are Oral Torah, and the Rambam was categorically against charging for them.
  • The Shulhan Arukh, 500 years ago, already records the common practice of being paid for teaching Oral Torah, and gives justifications.
  • One justification: the payment is not for the Torah but for the time, which could have been used to earn a living elsewhere. The Gemara tells of Karna, a dayan outside the Land of Israel, who asked litigants to pay what he would have earned from his work, or he had no time to judge.
  • Whether one needs an actual alternative source of income is a debate.
  • So the charge must clearly be for the lost time, not for teaching the Torah itself.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: "mah ani behinam af atem behinam".
  • Rambam: Written Torah may be taught for pay, Oral Torah may not.
  • Shulhan Arukh: records the practice of charging and its justifications.
  • The Gemara: Karna asked for payment for the work he lost.

In one line

One may charge for teaching Written Torah, and for Oral Torah only as compensation for lost time, never for the Torah itself.
