Topic: Cooking for or inviting a non-Jew on Yom Tov, and the case of someone in the process of conversion

Sources: The Torah ("hu levado yeaseh lakhem"); the Rishonim; the Hakhamim; the Rosh; Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Monday 26 May 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Melakhah for okhel nefesh is permitted on Yom Tov, and by extension for all needs, but only for Yom Tov itself. The Torah says "lakhem", for yourselves, so it must be for Israel.
  • So it is forbidden to cook for a non-Jew on Yom Tov, even food he will eat that day. The Rishonim dispute whether this is from the Torah or rabbinic, but it is certainly forbidden.
  • The Hakhamim therefore decreed against inviting a non-Jew to a Yom Tov seudah, lest one find there is not enough food and cook extra for him. On Shabbat it is perfectly fine.
  • The Rosh writes, and this is the halacha, that a non-Jew who turns up of his own accord may be fed, since the food was already prepared; the prohibition is to invite.
  • Once a person has converted, he is part of "lakhem", and the Torah lists the ger among those to include in the simhah of Yom Tov.
  • Someone still in the conversion process has the status of a non-Jew, and some poskim forbid inviting him. Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach and other poskim permit inviting someone properly in the process, since the purpose is to teach him how Yom Tov is kept, so there is little concern of doing melakhah for him. This does not apply to someone who has merely expressed interest.
  • Since the prohibition is rabbinic, it seems one may rely on this. But it remains forbidden, perhaps even from the Torah, to cook or do melakhah specifically for him, since he is not yet Jewish.

Sources quoted

  • The Rosh: an uninvited non-Jew who turns up may be fed.
  • Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach: one may invite someone seriously in the conversion process.

In one line

Do not invite a non-Jew to a Yom Tov meal, though one who comes may eat, and someone seriously converting may be invited but not cooked for specially.
