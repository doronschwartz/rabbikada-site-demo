Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 299, seifim 1-6, 9 and the start of seif 10 (seifim 7-8 skipped) Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 2 May 2024

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

Seif 1: No eating or drinking before Havdalah

The first class after Pesah opened Siman 299: eating and doing Melakhah before Havdalah. From the Gemara, once it becomes dark one may not eat or drink anything except water until one has made Havdalah.

From when? The Shulhan Arukh says "from when it gets dark", which would normally mean nightfall. But the Mishnah Berurah brings the view of most Rishonim that the prohibition already begins at shekiah, sunset. The Shulhan Arukh says "dark" because of the next line: if you began eating before, you need not stop, and may continue even after nightfall. That is why we can extend Seudah Shelishit well past the end of Shabbat.

When Minhah runs late. The permission to continue applies only if you started at a permitted time. If shul finished Minhah just after sunset, may you still wash for Seudah Shelishit? - Ashkenazim (Mishnah Berurah): because of the misvah of Seudah Shelishit, one may rely on the minority view that eating is only forbidden from nightfall, and wash and eat. - Sefaradim (Rav Ben Sion Abba Shaul): the prohibition begins at sunset. The poskim rule that one washes, eats a kazayit of bread, and stops. Water is always permitted.

This is why Minhah should be timed to leave enough time to wash and start eating before sunset. A couple of minutes either way is fine.

Only a meal with bread. If people were sitting over wine or whisky, or eating a meal without bread, they must stop at sunset. Someone asked about taking a little mezonot at Seudah Shelishit; the Rabbi agreed it is a problem after sunset: "Either you eat or you don't." There is an opinion that one should pause the meal at nightfall and make Havdalah, but no one does this; we continue to the end of the meal and make Havdalah afterwards.

Water but not Kiddush. Before Kiddush on Friday night even water is forbidden, while before Havdalah water is allowed. The concern with eating before Havdalah is that people get carried away with food and drink.

Seifim 2-3: Interrupting the meal

Seif 2: a basic rule of berakhot is that once your mind leaves the food (hesekh hadaat), you need new berakhot. If in the middle of the meal you said "let's do Havdalah", the Shulhan Arukh brings both views on whether new berakhot are needed. In practice no new berakhah is said, but one should avoid putting oneself into the doubt. The Rabbi added that nowadays saying "Shir HaMaalot" means "let's bentch", which is a much clearer statement that you have finished, so if you then eat or drink while waiting for the speaker to finish, you would need new berakhot.

Seif 3: if you made Havdalah in the middle of the meal, and you were already drinking wine with the meal, you do not say Borei Peri HaGefen again in Havdalah, because of the doubt in berakhot. He told of a Chabad custom on the last day of Pesah, where the meal runs on, Havdalah is made in the middle, and the meal continues. The same applies to Purim on a Friday: if the Purim meal runs into Shabbat, one covers the bread, makes Kiddush without Borei Peri HaGefen, and continues.

Reseh or Al HaNissim? In the Purim case one says Reseh VeHahalisenu in the bentching and not Al HaNissim, since Shabbat can be extended while Purim cannot, and one cannot say both. If you made Havdalah in the middle of a Mosei Shabbat meal, you can no longer say Reseh, since you have just declared Shabbat over. The simplest course is to finish the meal before nightfall so the question never arises.

Seifim 4-5: Bentching on a cup and late Havdalah

Seif 4: with ten men, Birkat HaMazon should be said over a cup. If you only have one cup of wine, you may bentch over it without drinking and then use it for Havdalah. Otherwise use two cups, one for each misvah, since we do not like doing two misvot on one thing.

May you drink the bentching cup after dark? Yes. The cup of Birkat HaMazon is the conclusion of the meal and counts as part of it. The Mishnah Berurah agrees, with the caveat that you regularly bentch over a cup; with ten men you always should. The Rabbi said it is incorrect to use the bentching cup for Havdalah when there is more wine, and hard to justify not drinking it.

Seif 5: if you did eat before Havdalah, intentionally or not, you still make Havdalah.

Seif 6: Until when?

All agree that Havdalah may be made all of Saturday night until dawn, the earlier the better. - The Shulhan Arukh's main view (the Rosh): until Tuesday, since Sunday to Tuesday still belong to the previous Shabbat and Wednesday to Friday to the coming one. - The Rif: only during Sunday.

Normally we follow the main view, but in berakhot we take the view that avoids a berakhah in vain. So the Ben Ish Hai, the Kaf HaHayim and Rav Ovadia Yoseph rule that Sefaradim make a late Havdalah only until the end of Sunday, not on Monday or Tuesday. The Rema follows three days, which is why some Ashkenazi schools in England make Havdalah at Monday assembly for children who will not have heard it. A late Havdalah is made on the cup alone, without the candle or spices, even on Sunday morning.

Summer Shabbatot in London. When Shabbat ends very late, children and the elderly cannot stay up. One option is to make Havdalah on Sunday morning, which was the common advice in Gateshead. Another is that in real need Havdalah may be made from Pelag HaMinhah. The Rabbi said he was not convinced the Sunday morning advice is correct, and that "there's plenty to rely on" to make Havdalah before going to sleep.

A question came up about someone who forgot Havdalah and then Yom Tov began on Sunday night. The Rabbi explained that for Sefaradim the question does not arise, since the time for Havdalah ended with Sunday.

Seif 9: Covering the bread

If you plan to eat Melaveh Malkah straight after Havdalah, any bread on the table should be covered during Havdalah, just as on Friday night, because bread normally comes first. This only applies if you intend to eat it. As in all the laws of the order of berakhot, food in front of you that you do not want now does not affect you.

Seif 10: Melakhah before Havdalah

The prohibition of Melakhah before Havdalah is also from the Gemara, but for a different reason. It has nothing to do with distraction. Until one has made Havdalah, there is still an aura of Shabbat, so the rabbis said not to do Melakhah. Someone asked how that fits with adding to Shabbat; the Rabbi explained that adding to Shabbat is itself a misvah, and breaking an extension one has taken on is not like breaking Shabbat itself.

Once you have said Atah Honantanu in Arvit you may do Melakhah, even before Havdalah on the cup. That is why one may drive home from shul before hearing Havdalah, and why one can light the Havdalah candle. Eating still requires Havdalah on the cup.

If something urgent comes up before Arvit, one may say "Barukh HaMavdil Bein Kodesh LeHol" without the name of HaShem and then do Melakhah. This helps only for Melakhah, not for eating. The details continued in the next class.

Practical summary

  • From sunset on Shabbat afternoon, do not start eating or drinking anything except water until Havdalah; a meal with bread begun before sunset may continue.
  • If Minhah ran past sunset, Sefaradim wash and eat a kazayit of bread only; Ashkenazim may eat normally.
  • Drink the cup of Birkat HaMazon at Seudah Shelishit and use a separate cup for Havdalah.
  • Sefaradim who forgot Havdalah may make it on the cup only, until the end of Sunday.
  • Cover the bread at Havdalah if you plan to eat Melaveh Malkah.
  • Do not do Melakhah until Atah Honantanu or Barukh HaMavdil, and do not eat until Havdalah on the cup.
