Seifim 41-42: when an agent dies

The previous shiur reached Seif 40. Seif 41 is straight from the Gemara in Gittin. However many agents there are in a chain, once the husband dies every shlihut is cancelled, because the get has not yet been given. But if only the first agent dies and the husband is alive, the second agent carries on.

Seif 42 is the Rema: if the second agent dies, the first may take the get back from the heirs and either give it himself or send it with someone else. What if the second agent is alive and the first wants to take it back and deliver it himself? The Taz and the Beit Shemuel say he may, because the first agent's shlihut is not finished until the get reaches the wife. He still has a role, even after handing over. This extends down the chain: the first or second may take it from a third. The Pithei Teshuvah adds that the current agent should give his permission.

The Pithei Teshuvah brings a further dispute. May the first agent cancel the appointment of the second? The Taz says yes, since the first made him. The Get Mekushar says no: once appointed, the second agent acts with the husband's own power. The Pithei Teshuvah also discusses a husband who appointed an agent, cancelled him and appointed a new one. May the new agent pass the get back to the cancelled one? He says no, because there is an implicit condition: the husband cancelled that man for a reason.

Seifim 43-44: may the wife's agent appoint another?

For the shaliah kabbalah (the wife's agent who receives the get for her) there are three views:

View

May the shaliah kabbalah appoint another?

The Geonim

Only if the wife explicitly gave permission

The Ittur

Like the husband's agent: yes, if he is ill or prevented

The Rambam

Never, even with her permission, because "milei" (mere words) cannot be passed on to an agent

The Tur rules like the Geonim. The Shulhan Arukh brings the Rambam as the main view and the Geonim as "some permit", in which case the new agent must be appointed in front of two witnesses, as with every shaliah kabbalah. The Rabbi stressed the practical point: a shaliah kabbalah should not appoint another agent, and if he did and the get was given that way, the woman would only be a safek megoreshet.

Seif 44 is a different case. If the wife herself appoints several agents, there is no problem of milei, and any of them may receive the get. If she appointed one and the next day appointed another, the Rambam holds that the second appointment cancels the first. The Rosh and the Shulhan Arukh hold that it does not.

Seif 45: an agent who changes his instructions

From Seif 45 to Seif 52 the siman deals with an agent who did something different from what he was told. The Gemara rules that if he changes, nothing he does counts: "you have overreached," as the Rabbi put it in legal terms. The cases are a different place (told to give it in Perth, he gave it in Sydney), a different room (told the house, he gave it in the loft) and a different hand (told the right hand, he used the left).

Why does the Shulhan Arukh say "only" for the second and third cases and not the first? The Beit Shemuel explains that a husband always cares about the place, because people talk about where a divorce happened. So "give it in Perth" is binding even without the word "only". "Give it in the house" or "with your right hand" is just advice unless he says "only". Someone asked why right and left are not obvious opposites. The Rabbi explained that "give it with your right hand" may be a mere preference, as many Sepharadim like to do things with the right hand for good mazal, not a condition.

The Pithei Teshuvah adds two practical points:

  • If the husband appointed an agent to take the get to the wife, and in the end she came to the Beit Din and the agent gave it to her there, it is valid. "This is relevant in our batei din," said the Rabbi: when the wife cannot attend, the husband appoints an agent, and she often comes to the Beit Din later to receive it.
  • If the husband said to give it in place X and the agent gave it in place Y, it is invalid, even though our harshaah says the agent may give it "wherever he finds her".

Seifim 46-49: second person, directions and dates

Seif 46 (from the Tur): "give it with your right hand" in the second person is stricter. It means the husband wants this agent personally, so he may not appoint another even if ill.

Seif 47: "give the get to my wife, who is in such a place" is not a condition, only directions. The Rabbi summed up three cases: "give it in Sydney" and "give it only in Sydney" both bind, but "give it to my wife; she is in Sydney" does not. One or two words make the difference, and this is why nowadays the Beit Din makes no conditions at all when a husband appoints an agent.

Seif 48 deals with a date. If the husband said to give it on the first of Adar, giving it earlier is invalid. The plain reading is that giving it later is fine. The Beit Shemuel compares Siman 144, where a get written after the stated time is invalid according to the Rambam and a safek according to the Shulhan Arukh. The Bah and the Torat Gittin distinguish: there the husband said "within" a time, here he only named a day, meaning not before it.

Seif 49 applies the same rules to the wife's agent.

Seif 50: receive it in Bavel, though you may find him in Damascus

If the wife says "receive my get in Bavel, but you may find him in Damascus", this is an in-between case. The agent may receive it in Damascus, but the divorce only takes effect when he reaches Bavel. Tosafot ask how a divorce can take effect later when the get is no longer in anyone's hand. One answer is an elaborate scheme in which the husband turns the agent into his own agent. The simpler answer, which the Ran also gives, is that she has made a condition, and a get may depend on a condition being fulfilled later.

Seifim 51-52: "take the ring and give the get"

Suppose the husband says: take back my ring (or any object) from her and give her the get. Everyone agrees the agent should not hand the job to someone else. The Rambam's reason is that the husband does not want his object in other hands; Rashi's is that a second agent may not be careful about the order.

The dispute is what happens if he did:

  • Rashi, the Rif and the Rambam: if the get was given before the object was received, it is invalid. If the object was received first, it is valid, even when done by a second agent.
  • Tosafot and the Rosh: if a second agent did it, it is invalid even if he took the object first, because the husband wanted this agent to bring it back.

Someone asked why this is stricter than "with your right hand". The Rabbi answered that here the husband wants something out of the divorce and is trusting a particular person with it. Someone else suggested that the first agent could take the object and a second agent give the get. The Rabbi agreed, and noted that the Rosh says exactly that.

The wording matters too. "Take the object and give the get" sets an order; "give the get and take the object" does not. The Beit Shemuel asks about a husband who said "make sure she agrees, then give the get". Whether another agent may do it depends on the two reasons: on the Rambam's reason there is no object to guard, but on Rashi's the second agent may not be careful. Seif 52 is the Rema quoting Rashi's reason. In practice, the Rabbi repeated, we make no conditions at all.

Seif 53: a get brought from abroad

An agent who brings a get from abroad and says the required words is believed, and the woman may remarry immediately. But she can collect her ketubah only from free property, not from property the husband sold, because we suspect a forgery against the buyers. If the husband comes and says the get is forged, then if there is no kiyum of the signatures and the witnesses are unknown, the Shulhan Arukh rules that she must leave her new husband and the child is a mamzer. The Beit Yoseph was hesitant, reading the Rambam as "close to a mamzer". The Beit Shemuel asks how, since kiyum is only rabbinic. The answer is that once the husband protests, the kiyum is required, and without it the child is a mamzer from the Torah.

Practical summary

  • When the husband dies, every agent's shlihut ends; when an agent dies, the chain continues.
  • A shaliah kabbalah should not appoint another agent; the Shulhan Arukh follows the Rambam.
  • An agent who changes the place invalidates the get; for other details it depends on the wording and on "only".
  • Small changes of wording ("give it in Sydney" or "she is in Sydney") change the law, which is why the Beit Din makes no conditions when appointing an agent today.
  • When the husband asks for an object back, the order matters, and the Rishonim dispute whether a second agent may complete it.
