Topic: Seeking forgiveness from those one has wronged, and why not to leave it to Erev Yom Kippur

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rambam, based on the Gemara

Daily Halacha: Yom Kippur

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Wronging another person, financially, physically or even verbally, is a Torah prohibition, and the Shulhan Arukh writes that even for hurt caused by words one must ask Mehilah and do Teshuvah. This applies all year round.
  • Such a wrong has two sides: against the person, and against Hashem who commanded us. Yom Kippur atones for the second only once the person has been asked and has forgiven.
  • That is why these laws appear in the context of Yom Kippur, and why it is customary to ask forgiveness beforehand, so that on Yom Kippur we can ask Hashem to forgive our sins against others too.
  • Asking only on Erev Yom Kippur, to enter with a clean slate, may not be genuine. A sincere apology is made whenever one feels sorry, without waiting.
  • The Rambam, based on the Gemara, and the Shulhan Arukh rule that if refused one asks again, not immediately, bringing three people and some gift or amends to show sincerity, and does this up to three times, each time before three people. Three requests in a row do not count. If, after all this, the person still refuses, one has done one's best.
  • The one asked should not be cruel and should forgive when he sees it is genuine. The exception is one who has been slandered (Mosi Shem Ra): since reputation cannot be restored, he has no duty to forgive.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: one must seek forgiveness even for hurtful words.
  • The Rambam, based on the Gemara, and Shulhan Arukh: the procedure of asking three times before three people.

In one line

Seek forgiveness genuinely and promptly all year, not just on Erev Yom Kippur, and guard especially against slander, which need not be forgiven.
