Topic: Three contemporary views on artificial insemination from a Jewish donor

Sources: Rabbenu Peres; Rav Moshe Feinstein; the Satmar Rebbe; Parashat Aharei Mot; Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Tuesday 7 July 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Rav Moshe Feinstein, perhaps the leading view, wrote several teshuvot and defended them strongly when attacked: it does not create a mamzer and does not forbid her to her husband. As Rabbenu Peres shows, without a prohibited union (biat issur) there is no mamzerut.
  • Yet he advised against a Jewish donor in the first instance: donors are usually anonymous, and the child might marry a sibling from the same father.
  • The Satmar Rebbe held the opposite: Rabbenu Peres speaks of something happening by itself, whereas here one acts deliberately. He brought the verse in Parashat Aharei Mot forbidding a man to give his seed to a married woman, so insemination itself is forbidden, she becomes forbidden to her husband, and the child is a mamzer.
  • A third view, of Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach: it is not prohibited to do, but the child is a mamzer, since mamzerut comes not from the karet but from the Torah's strong rejection of the outcome.
  • Both the Satmar Rebbe and Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach answer Rabbenu Peres in different ways.
  • Conclusion: it is certainly not right to use a Jewish donor. If done, the child's status is doubtful: kasher according to Rav Moshe Feinstein, not according to others. In practice this is not used.
  • Next: a non-Jewish donor, which is quite different.
