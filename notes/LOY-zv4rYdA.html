Topic: The source of "mishenikhnas Adar marbim besimhah" and why the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh omit it

Sources: Mishnah Taanit; Gemara Taanit (Rav); Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema; the Magen Avraham; Rav Yishak Rasabi; Talmud Yerushalmi; Talmud Bavli; the Beit Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Purim, Monday 3 March 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Mishnah at the end of Taanit says that when Av begins we reduce joy. The Rambam and Shulhan Arukh codify this, with practical laws from Rosh Hodesh Av and the week of Tisha BeAv.
  • On that Mishnah the Gemara, in the name of Rav, says that likewise when Adar begins we increase joy, but it does not say how.
  • The Rambam, the Shulhan Arukh and the Rema do not mention it in the laws of Megillah and Purim. Only the Aharonim, starting with the Magen Avraham about 400 years ago, bring it into halacha.
  • Some explain increased joy as more Torah, or a little more wine. These are weak: the Gemara does not say so, and they would require the opposite in Av.
  • Rav Yishak Rasabi, a contemporary Yemenite commentator on the Rambam, explains: the Yerushalmi lets a traveller read the Megillah from Rosh Hodesh Adar. The Bavli disagrees, holding the earliest time is the 11th, and the Beit Yoseph writes that the Rambam rules like the Bavli. The Shulhan Arukh brings the opinion but does not really follow it.
  • Rav's statement reflects the Yerushalmi's view that the whole month is a time for the Megillah. Since the Rambam does not rule that way, he also omits increasing joy in Adar.
  • A second explanation, and the link to the mazal of Adar, come tomorrow.

Sources quoted

  • Mishnah Taanit: when Av begins we reduce joy.
  • Gemara (Rav): when Adar begins we increase joy.
  • The Magen Avraham: first to bring it into halacha.
  • Rav Yishak Rasabi: the omission follows the Bavli against the Yerushalmi on reading the Megillah from Rosh Hodesh.

In one line

"Mishenikhnas Adar marbim besimhah" may follow the Yerushalmi's month-long Megillah, which is why the Rambam leaves it out.
