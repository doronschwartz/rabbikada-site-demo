Topic: Why one who claims to be a Kohen is not believed without a witness

Sources: The Gemara; the Rosh

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 13 April 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Siman 3 deals mainly with the Kohen. In Siman 2 we saw that, according to many Rishonim, a person is believed to say he is Jewish. Is one who turns up in a Beit Kenesset believed to say he is a Kohen?
  • Being a Kohen brings advantages: the first aliyah, Birkat Kohanim, prestige, and in some places in Israel gifts such as Terumah. It also brings disadvantages: extra prohibitions, impurity, and fewer women he may marry.
  • The Gemara, in at least two places, says clearly, and all accept, that a Kohen is not believed without at least one witness, and even one witness depends on the circumstances.
  • Why the difference from Jewish identity? The Rosh says that even though it could be verified, there is financial gain (Terumah) and prestige (the first aliyah and Birkat Kohanim).
  • He adds that it affects descendants: even if the gain today is very small, which is argued strongly today, accepting him means his children will be taken as Kohanim when it does become financially advantageous.
  • Practice and ruling today follow tomorrow.

In one line

The Gemara and Rishonim require a witness for one who claims to be a Kohen, because of the gain and prestige involved for him and his descendants.
