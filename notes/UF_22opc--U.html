Topic: Whether the hazan prompts the first word of Birkat Kohanim

Sources: Bemidbar 6 ("Amor lahem"); the Gemara; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; Bet Yoseph; the Ben Ish Hai; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Birkat Kohanim & Prayer, Tuesday 21 June 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • From "amor lahem", "say to them", the hazan prompts the Kohanim word by word and they repeat after him.
  • But the Rambam and the Shulhan Arukh, the simple reading of the Gemara, say the Kohanim begin "Yevarekhekha" without a prompt. The Bet Yoseph records this as the minhag of Egypt; it was the minhag in Iraq, and is that of Syrian communities and the Spanish and Portuguese today.
  • Ashkenazi poskim require every word to be prompted, so in Ashkenazi synagogues the hazan calls out "Yevarekhekha" too.
  • The Ben Ish Hai changed the Iraqi custom for two reasons: Kabbalistically each word should be prompted; and the Kohanim sometimes began "Yevarekhekha" before the congregation could answer Amen to their berakhah "levarekh et amo Yisrael beahavah".
  • Where the Kohanim begin by themselves, they must wait for the Amen. The reason for beginning at once seems to be to avoid an interruption between the berakhah and the misvah.
  • Hakham Ovadiah relates that as rabbi in Egypt and later in Tel Aviv he did not change the established custom of not prompting, though he preferred prompting. In Tel Aviv, after shiurim on the sources, the hazan and community asked to change and he agreed.
  • Later authorities prefer prompting all 15 words, but an established custom should be kept unless there is a strong reason to change it.

In one line

The Spanish and Portuguese, Syrians and Egyptians let the Kohanim start Yevarekhekha unprompted; Iraqis (after the Ben Ish Hai) and Ashkenazim prompt every word.
