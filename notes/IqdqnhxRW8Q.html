Topic: Who the Netinim were and whether they may marry into Am Yisrael

Sources: Sefer Yehoshua; Sefer Ezra; the Gemara; the Rishonim; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Wednesday 29 April 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Another category, unknown today but of interesting history, is the Netinim, "those given", from the word natan.
  • In Sefer Yehoshua, as Israel conquered the seven nations, the Givonim, a group within the Hivvi, pretended to come from a distant land and obtained a peace treaty. The deception was discovered only afterwards.
  • Yehoshua faced the misvah to drive out the seven nations against the treaty, so they were made part of the nation but set apart as woodchoppers and manual labourers.
  • They married only among themselves, not into Am Yisrael. In Sefer Ezra they still existed in the Second Temple period with the same role; after that they disappear.
  • The Gemara and Rishonim debate whether the ban is from the Torah or rabbinic. The Shulhan Arukh codifies that Netinim may not marry into Am Yisrael.
  • Today, by the majority principle (kol deparish merubah parish), a convert from any nation is not suspected of being a Netin and may marry in.
  • Next: a child of a non-Jewish man and a Jewish woman, who could be fully Jewish, Jewish but barred from a Kohen, a mamzer, or not Jewish. This unfortunately happens today.

In one line

The Netinim, descended from the Givonim, were barred from marrying in, but they no longer exist as a known group, so converts today may marry in.
