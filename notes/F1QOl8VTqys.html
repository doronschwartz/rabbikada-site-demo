Topic: Where the concept of the Three Weeks comes from

Sources: The Mishnah and Talmud; the Rambam, Mishneh Torah; Midrash Eikhah Rabbah; Rabbenu Hananel; Rav Hai Gaon, responsa

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Tuesday 22 July 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • After the series on Yom Tov (Hol HaMoed is still to come), a week and a half before Tisha BeAv he turns to the background of this period.
  • The Three Weeks are mentioned nowhere in the Talmud or the Rambam. The Mishnah tells of the calamities of the 17th of Tammuz and Tisha BeAv, and there are restrictions from Rosh Hodesh Av and the week of Tisha BeAv, but no Three Weeks.
  • The source is Midrash Eikhah Rabbah on "all her pursuers overtook her bein hamesarim": between the two breaches of the 17th of Tammuz and the 9th of Av we are pursued, and from this comes a three week period of mourning.
  • The author of the Midrash is unknown; from his research it seems to date from somewhere between the fifth and seventh centuries.
  • Rabbenu Hananel, in the 11th century, brings this Midrash. The very first known author to mention a three week mourning period is Rav Hai Gaon, the last of the Geonim in Bavel (died 1038), in one of his responsa, so in some places it was already widespread.
  • The Rambam does not mention it, perhaps because it had not reached him or because it is not from the law of the Gemara.
  • The restrictions of the Three Weeks will be discussed, but they are later institutions, not from the Gemara, which is always the ultimate source of halacha.

Sources quoted

In one line

The Three Weeks come from a Midrash and the Geonim, not the Gemara, so their restrictions are later institutions.
