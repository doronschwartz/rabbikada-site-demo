Topic: The leniency of davar haaved: its reasons, and loss versus lost gain

Sources: The Gemara; Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Rishonim

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Monday 3 November 2025

The halacha

  • Davar haaved: melakhah, even melekhet uman, is permitted on the moed to prevent something being lost. The heter appears in the Gemara, Rambam and Shulhan Arukh with many examples.
  • Reasons in the Rishonim: knowing one will lose out by staying passive detracts from the simhah of the moed; and, as the Gemara says, the Torah cares for the property of Israel.
  • The general principle (with exceptions) is that it covers losing something one already owns, not loss of gain. Lost profit from not trading on the moed is not davar haaved.
  • The Shulhan Arukh's examples: grapes in one's vineyard that will fall and spoil may be harvested even all day; plants that will deteriorate may be watered.
  • By contrast, the Shulhan Arukh writes that one may not go to great effort to retrieve a valuable lost article seen drifting past on the tide, since by being passive one only forgoes a gain.
  • Rambam writes "a substantial loss", implying only a substantial loss qualifies; this follows next.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: the Torah cares for the property of Israel.
  • Shulhan Arukh: harvesting grapes and watering plants permitted; retrieving a lost article not.

In one line

Davar haaved permits melakhah on Hol HaMoed to avoid losing what one already has, not to secure a gain.
