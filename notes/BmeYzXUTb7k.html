Topic: The Ashkenazi approach to rain on the first night, and saying Shehiyanu on the sukkah later

Sources: The Rama; Rav Hayyim Palagi; Hazon Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Monday 14 October 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Sepharadim: if it rains on the first night (or second outside Israel), Kiddush and the meal may be at home; if it stops at any time that night, one goes out, washes again and eats a kezayit of bread in the sukkah.
  • Ashkenazim hold there is an obligation to eat in the sukkah on these nights even in rain, though a doubtful one. They first wait half an hour or an hour to see if it clears. If not, they make Kiddush and eat a kezayit in the sukkah, then finish the meal at home; if it later stops raining, they go out and eat a kezayit there again.
  • Shehiyanu on Sukkot is said for the new festival (in Kiddush, at home or in the sukkah) and for first taking the Arba Minim. The Shehiyanu of Kiddush in the sukkah also covers sitting in the sukkah.
  • If one could not sit in the sukkah that night, does one say Shehiyanu again on first sitting there, later that night or the next day?
  • Most poskim say yes. This is the ruling of the Rama and the Ashkenazi custom, even after Shehiyanu at night and on the Arba Minim.
  • Among Sepharadim, some, including Rav Hayyim Palagi, agree. Hazon Ovadiah is concerned for the view that the night's Shehiyanu covers the sukkah even when not sitting in it, and says it is better not to say it.
  • It is unresolved; one decides by one's custom and ruling.

Sources quoted

  • The Rama: a further Shehiyanu on first sitting in the sukkah.
  • Hazon Ovadiah: better not to say it again.

In one line

If rain kept one out of the sukkah, Ashkenazim and some Sepharadim say Shehiyanu on first sitting in it, while followers of Hazon Ovadiah do not.
