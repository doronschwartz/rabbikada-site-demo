Topic: The reward of long life, and why Beit Din does not enforce this misvah

Sources: The Torah's "so that your days may be lengthened"; the Talmud; Shulhan Arukh; Rav Saadiah Gaon; the Or HaHayim (Rabbi Hayim ben Attar)

Daily Halacha: Honouring Parents, Friday 8 January 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Honouring parents is one of the few misvot where the Torah states the reward: long life. The Talmud debates whether in this world, the next, or both.
  • Where the Torah states the reward beside a misvah, Beit Din does not use its authority to enforce it: the only consequence of neglect is losing that reward. The Shulhan Arukh rules that the authorities do not intervene in failures of honour to parents; it is a great loss to the person himself.
  • Rav Saadiah Gaon: parents may live with their children for many years and become a burden. The Torah promises long life so that time "lost" caring for them is repaid.
  • The Or HaHayim, the great Moroccan rabbi of the 18th century: as you treat your parents, so your children will treat you. "Ish imo veaviv tirau" begins in the singular and ends in the plural: when one person reveres his parents, his children learn to revere him.

In one line

The Torah itself promises long life for honouring parents, so Beit Din leaves it to the person, and the way we treat our parents shapes how our children treat us.
