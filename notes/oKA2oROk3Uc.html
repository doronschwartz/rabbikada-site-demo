Sefer: Shulhan Arukh, Orah Hayim, Hilchot Shabbat Siman: 301, seifim 9, 11 and 14 (10, 12 and 13 skipped) Shiur: Part of Dayan Daniel Kada's Thursday Hilchot Shabbat class, Thursday 6 June 2024

These notes are based on a transcript of the recording.

The four rabbinic concerns

The Rabbi reviewed the principles. Something that is neither clothing (malbush) nor adornment (takhshit) is a burden, and carrying it is a Torah prohibition. Clothing and adornments are permitted, but the rabbis added four restrictions: 1. Something one commonly takes off to show people, such as a woman's ring. 2. Something loose that easily falls off, such as a shoe that is too big. 3. An adornment that looks as if it has another use, so people think you are carrying it to use it. 4. Something people will laugh at, such as walking in one shoe, so you may take it off.

Seif 9: Rings for men and women

Men wearing rings at all. Some contemporary poskim argue that since rings are mainly worn by women, a man wearing a wedding ring transgresses lo yilbash. Rav Moshe Feinstein writes at length that it is normal and permitted, and this seif shows clearly that men wore rings; the only question was Shabbat.

The Shulhan Arukh: a man may not go out with a ring that has no seal; if he did, he is liable from the Torah, because for men only a signet ring was an adornment that gave status. A signet ring is forbidden only rabbinically. Rashi explains that, as with women, he may take it off to show people. The Rambam says that concern applies only to women, who were more likely to show each other their things ("there's an element of truth there still nowadays," the Rabbi said, "although men also like to show off their stuff"). A ring worn by both men and women is forbidden.

Rings today. The Shulhan Arukh itself, in Siman 303, notes that women go out with rings and bracelets and nobody objects. The main reason is that our streets are not a real Reshut HaRabbim of 600,000 people, so even if she took it off and carried it, that would only be rabbinic, and the rabbis do not make a decree on top of a decree. The Rema adds that today jewellery is so common that people do not take it off to show it. Rav Ovadia Yoseph extends this to men: today men wear ordinary rings as adornment.

In summary: a man may wear his ring; a woman may wear hers. In a place that may be a true Reshut HaRabbim, such as Oxford Street, it is more of a question, but the Rema's reason may permit it even there, though that relies on a greater leniency.

Seif 11 and the Rema: Keys, glasses cases and Shabbat belts

Seif 11: something that is both an adornment and has a use, for example a necklace with a key at the end, is forbidden, because the onlooker thinks you are carrying it to use. Some permit it if the key is silver, since a silver key is clearly an adornment.

The Rema: one may not go out with a glasses case worn on a chain around the neck, even a silver one, because the glasses are the main thing. The case is made for the glasses, not the glasses for the case, so they are not batel to it.

Glasses. The Mishnah Berurah worried that spectacles of his day could fall off. Today's glasses sit firmly, and glasses you need are an extension of your eyes, just as a walking stick is for someone who cannot walk without it. They are permitted. - Reading glasses worn on the way to shul for use there are neither clothing nor adornment and are not an extension of you while walking. Wearing them without an eruv is a Torah prohibition. So shuls should keep reading glasses. - Prescription sunglasses: permitted, like any glasses you need. - Non-prescription sunglasses: Rav Ben Sion Abba Shaul forbids them, since sunglasses are often taken on and off. Rav Ovadia Yoseph permits them, since you need them in the sun. Even in England, where the sun comes and goes, one can rely on our streets being a karmelit.

Shabbat belts. Seif 11 also discusses a key tied to a belt. Mishnah Berurah 45: it works only if the key is an integral part of the belt, set into the middle of it. "A mistake people make with Shabbat belts: they think I can just take any belt and just link onto it a key. That's absolutely not how a Shabbat belt works." A key simply clipped onto a normal belt is not batel to it and may even be a Torah prohibition.

Watches

The Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halacha forbid wearing a wristwatch where there is no eruv: it is worn for use, not as clothing or adornment; the strap is not the main thing, the watch is; and one may take it off. Someone asked whether one could make the watch face itself a link in the strap; the Rabbi said that would not work. - Rav Moshe Feinstein argued that a watch today is part of what we wear, like a tie, but also wrote that a ben Torah should be strict. - An expensive watch, a gold watch or the like, is at least a takhshit, which men may wear today. - A plain plastic watch: Rav Ben Sion Abba Shaul forbids it. Rav Ovadia Yoseph, in Yabia Omer, writes that there is what to rely on, since it may count as a garment or adornment and we have no true Reshut HaRabbim. "He didn't say it's mutar. He says there is what to rely on."

The Rabbi, who does not wear a watch himself, said wearing an ordinary watch without an eruv is a real disagreement about a possible Torah prohibition and should be borne in mind. Imitation jewellery counts as jewellery, since its whole point is adornment.

Seif 14 and the Rema: Hats and rain covers

Seif 14: something worn as clothing may be worn even if its purpose is only protection from dust or dirt. The Rema: so one may wear a hat even just to keep off rain. But a woman may not put an extra cloth over her head covering only to protect it from rain.

The principle: clothing is permitted whatever the reason; something improvised to protect you is not. - A plastic carrier bag picked up in a shower and put on your head: forbidden. - Pulling up the hood of your coat: permitted, part of the garment. - A thin disposable raincoat: permitted, since it is made to be worn as a coat. - Pulling your jacket up over your head: permitted, an extension of what you are wearing. - Plastic covers over a sheitel: discussed by contemporary poskim; Rav Ben Sion Abba Shaul forbids them. The Rabbi felt that since they are manufactured to be worn they may be different. Someone suggested that for women who wear a tikhel over the sheitel, the plastic may replace the tikhel. - A plastic cover for a man's hat: someone argued it is not worn by the person but by the hat. The Rabbi agreed: "That's a protection for the hat. You're not wearing that in any way to protect you." Without an eruv in London he called it deeply problematic.

Practical summary

  • Men and women may wear their rings without an eruv.
  • Prescription glasses and prescription sunglasses are fine; never walk out wearing reading glasses you will only use when you arrive.
  • Ordinary sunglasses are permitted according to Rav Ovadia Yoseph.
  • A Shabbat belt must have the key built into the belt, not clipped on.
  • A plain watch without an eruv is debated; an expensive watch is an adornment.
  • Wear a coat or hood in the rain, not a bag or an improvised cover.
