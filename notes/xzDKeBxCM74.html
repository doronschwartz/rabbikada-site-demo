Topic: Three halachic exceptions that follow from the Megillah being so beloved

Sources: The Mishnah; the Gemara; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Purim, Wednesday 16 March 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Megillah is such a beloved misvah (haviva yoter) that we find three exceptions not found elsewhere in halacha.
  • Time: night-time misvot such as Shema must be done in the first instance before midnight, since, the Mishnah says, after midnight one is tired and will forget. With the beloved Megillah there is no such concern, so in the first instance one may read it any time in the night, though the earlier the better.
  • Several readers at once: normally two voices cannot be heard simultaneously, so one who hears Kiddush from two people reciting together has not fulfilled it, as one cannot hear the words properly. Yet the Shulhan Arukh says two or even ten people may read the Megillah together from their scrolls while the community listens, as long as one can hear, because one pays more attention to so beloved a misvah.
  • Mistakes: the reading of the Torah has strict rules about correcting errors, but the Shulhan Arukh, quoting the Gemara, rules we are less particular with mistakes in the Megillah. The concern with errors is understanding, and with so beloved a misvah we assume the listener still understands.
  • The Megillah is read night and day, and the day reading is the greater misvah.

Sources quoted

  • The Mishnah: night misvot before midnight lest one forget.
  • Shulhan Arukh: up to ten may read together; less strictness over mistakes, quoting the Gemara.

In one line

Because the Megillah is so beloved, it may be read all night, heard from several readers at once, and is less strict about mistakes.
