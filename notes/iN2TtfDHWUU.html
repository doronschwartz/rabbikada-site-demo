Where We Are

Last time the class learned about several women divorced with one get (Seifim 9-10), how the edim sign and the takanah that they write out their names, and why they must sign in each other's presence. Today's plan: Seif 16, "which is slightly complicated", the rest of Siman 130, and a start on Siman 131.

Part 1: Edim Who Cannot Read (Seif 16)

העדים שחותמין על הגט צריכים להיות יודעים לקרות ולחתום, ואם אינם יודעים לקרות קוראים בפניהם וחותמין, והוא שיכירו לשון הגט

The edim who sign the get must know how to read and to sign. If they cannot read, it is read before them and they sign, as long as they understand the language of the get.

The seif has two parts: edim who cannot read, and edim who cannot sign.

The Mishnah and the mahloket

The Mishnah says simply: korin bifneihem vehotmin, it is read before them and they sign. Note that korin is plural.

  • The Tanna Kamma makes no distinction between a get and other documents.
  • Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel holds that this is a special allowance (heter) for gittin; with other documents the edim must know what they are signing.

The Amoraim disagree about how to rule. And there is a further ruling in the Gemara that everyone accepts: for other documents, two non-Jews may read the text aloud, and the edim may sign on the basis of what they read.

The Rif, the Ri and the Rosh

Rules like

What it means in practice

The Ri

The Tanna Kamma

Korin means two readers. No difference between a get and other documents: there must always be two people reading before them

The Rif

Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel

For other documents two readers are needed. The special allowance for a get is that one reader is enough

So you would think the Rif, who rules like Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel, is stricter. "Lemaaseh, it comes out the exact opposite", as the Rosh explains: the Rif reads the Tanna Kamma's korin as simply "someone reads to them", and the heter of a get is that one reader suffices. The Beit Yoseph agrees that this is the Rif's view.

The Shulhan Arukh

The Shulhan Arukh writes korin bifneihem. The Taz points out that we cannot learn anything from this, since he may just be quoting the Mishnah. Lemaaseh, the Bah, the Taz and the Beit Shmuel all rule like the Ri and Tosafot, lehumra: two people must read the get before edim who cannot read, since it is like proper testimony.

Must they understand the language?

Vehu sheyakiru leshon haget: they must understand the language of the get.

  • The Beit Yoseph explains: even though they cannot read, they must understand the language when it is read to them. That is how the Shulhan Arukh seems to rule.
  • The Beit Shmuel disagrees: even if it is translated for them, that is good enough.

The Rabbi noted that today it is probably more common to have edim who do not understand the language than edim who cannot read. For the Beit Yoseph, you could not simply take two Jews from the community who do not understand what is read to them. Lekhathilah, the edim should understand the language of the get.

Part 2: Edim Who Cannot Sign (Seif 16, continued)

ואם אינם יודעים לחתום, רושמים להם הנייר ברוק וכיוצא בו מדבר שאין רשומו מתקיים, והם כותבין על הרושם

If they cannot sign, the paper is marked for them with saliva or something similar that does not last, and they write over the marking.

The Rema adds: some forbid this lekhathilah. Rather, blank paper is cut before them and they sign through the cut.

Which methods are allowed?

The Gemara gives several solutions: marking with saliva, with other liquids, and cutting the paper.

Opinion

Ruling

The Rif

Only the cut is acceptable. With the liquids, some remnant remains on the paper and they would be signing on top of it

The Rambam, the Rosh and others

The Gemara is only proving one of the solutions, not excluding the others. All are acceptable

The Rema

A middle way: lekhathilah use only the cut, but the other methods also work

No one rules straight out like the Rif, but the Rema leans towards him.

What does "cutting" the paper mean?

  • Rashi seems to understand that their signature is etched out, and they fill it in with ink.
  • The others object: if the signature is already etched into the paper and they only fill it in, that is ketav al gabei ketav, writing over writing, a problem. With saliva, which does not last, it is not considered writing. So the cut means something like a stencil: it marks out the place where they will sign, rather than carving the signature itself.

Leave room for their own hand

Tosafot make an important point, which the poskim accept: whichever method is used, do not make it exactly their signature. There must be enough room for the edim to express themselves in their own signature. Otherwise, how could there ever be kiyum hatimah (verification of a signature) on such a get? If someone else did the signature and they only filled in the ink, it is not their signature. "There's a balance here": you are helping them, but they must still sign themselves.

In short

  • An ed who cannot read: two people read the get before him, and he should understand its language.
  • An ed who cannot sign: lekhathilah a stencil, not an etching and not a liquid.

Seifim 17 and 18

These were learned last time: a get signed by an invalid witness is mezuyaf mitokho (Seif 17), and the sofer should not sign as a witness (Seif 18), a humra that goes back to the mahloket over omer imru in Siman 120.

Part 3: Non-Jewish Witnesses (Seif 19)

גט שנעשה בערכאות של כותים ועידי כותים חתומין עליו, אפילו כתבו ישראל ונמסר בעידי מסירה ישראל... אפילו הכי פסול

The Gemara

The Mishnah discusses documents made in non-Jewish courts. For ordinary documents, which are only evidence, they are accepted. A get, though, actually effects the divorce, so it needs Jewish edim. Rabbi Shimon says even a get is kasher.

The Gemara speaks of names like Lukos, a Roman name that no Jew would ever carry. How should this be understood?

  • One Rishon uses the Gemara as proof that we rule like Rabbi Shimon, and non-Jewish names may be used.
  • The Rambam, the Rif and the Rosh say no: non-Jews may never sign a get. The Gemara's question is about a get that comes before us with non-Jewish-sounding names on it. Can we assume they were Jews?

The paradox

The answer is surprising. Only if the names are ones no Jew would ever be called can we rely on the get. The people who arranged the get would never make such a glaring mistake; they must have checked, and found that these were Jews, the one in a thousand, as the Rosh says, who carries such a name.

So the Shulhan Arukh rules:

  • Writing a get with non-Jewish edim is pasul, even when it was written by a Jew, given before Jewish edei mesirah, and the names are clearly non-Jewish so no one would rely on them. It is mezuyaf mitokho.
  • A woman who brings a get signed with names that Jews never use may remarry with it: they must have been Jews.
  • If the names are somewhat like Jewish names, we are concerned that those who arranged it were mistaken, and she may not remarry with it.

"It's quite paradoxical." The Rabbi noted that this is hardly relevant today, since it is hard to think of a name no Jew carries. The class struggled to find one.

Part 4: One Witness and the Husband's Handwriting (Seif 20)

נתן לה הגט בינו לבינה ואפילו בעד אחד אינו גט, בד"א כשהיה הגט בכתב יד הסופר, אבל אם כתב הבעל הגט בכתב ידו וחתם עד אחד ונתנו לה, הרי זה גט פסול ופוסל לכהונה

Obviously two edei mesirah are needed; with only one, it is not a get. But if the husband wrote the get in his own hand, signed by one witness, it is a get pasul.

Rav and Shmuel

This comes from the Gemara's list of invalid gittin where im nisset havlad kasher, if she remarried, the child is kasher (we saw the list in Siman 127). One case is a get with a date but only one witness. What is that case?

Meaning

Rav

The get is in the husband's handwriting. His handwriting is like a hundred witnesses, since the point of edim is to clarify that he agreed. So midoraita it is kasher, and midrabbanan pasul

Shmuel

Even in the sofer's handwriting: a sofer would never write without the husband's instruction

In issurim the halacha is like Rav. So only the husband's handwriting with one witness is kasher midoraita: if she remarried, she may stay married, and the children are kasher. Lekhathilah, obviously, proper edim are needed.

"The ed ehad is lav davka." The essential element is the husband's handwriting. In truth, even with his handwriting alone, if she remarried and had a child, the child would be kasher.

"Lo tetse"

A recurring point: when we say lo tetse, she need not leave her second husband, this applies as soon as she has remarried, even before she has children. This is the psak of the Rambam and of the Shulhan Arukh throughout.

Why does this matter for Rabbi Elazar?

The Gemara itself says this Mishnah follows Rabbi Meir. We rule like Rabbi Elazar, so why does the Gemara need Rav and Shmuel's discussion?

  • The Rif and the Rambam learn from here that even for Rabbi Elazar, edei hatimah also work on their own. Edei mesirah are a good thing, in case the edei hatimah die, "but really both work".
  • Tosafot and the Rosh hold that edei hatimah never work alone. The point is that with kasher edei hatimah we assume there were kasher edei mesirah. Here the question is whether the husband's handwriting lets us make that assumption.

The Rabbi said the sugya fits much more smoothly with the Rambam and the Rif.

A riddle

How can a get have some effect without any edim at all, at least so that the children are kasher? Answer: according to the Rambam, the Rif and the Shulhan Arukh (Siman 133), a get in the husband's handwriting, which is like edim. She would not be allowed to remarry with it, but if she did, she could stay married. According to Tosafot and the Rosh this can never happen.

Part 5: Paying the Witnesses (Seif 21)

לא יטלו עידי הגט שכר יותר מכדי שכר בטלה דמוכחת

The edim of a get may take no more than compensation for proven lost time.

The Rema is much more lenient, with two reasons:

  1. We stipulate with the edim that if they spoil the get, they must pay for it. Taking on that responsibility allows them to charge more. We do not make this stipulation today.
  2. The edim may not marry the divorced woman, so they are limiting themselves, and may be paid for that.

On the second reason: me-ikar hadin, two edim who signed a get may marry the gerushah. We are not concerned that two people conspired together. A single shaliah, though, can never marry her, since there is always the concern that he lied in order to marry her, "and you tell the shaliah that". That the edim too may not marry her is a stringency, and the Rema's practice is to follow it.

Part 6: Dry Before Signing (Seif 22)

יש מי שאומר שצריך שייבש הגט קודם שיחתמו העדים

The Rabbi asked the class what was learned at the end of Siman 124: the get must be dry before it is given. That is the essential point, and it applies to the signatures too. Here is an extra humra: the get should be dry before the edim even sign.

The class discussed how to dry a get quickly when time is short. In the Rabbi's Beit Din, the edim always sign with the kulmus (quill), like the sofer. "This is why it's important to know all these obscure halachot": you are not always in a place like London, with sofrim, dayanim and edim on hand.

"That is Siman 130. It's a long siman, basically about all the hatimot."

Part 7: Siman 131: Lishmah (Seifim 1-4)

צריך שתהיה כתיבת הגט וחתימתו לשם האיש המגרש ולשם האשה המתגרשת, ואם לא נכתב לשמה אינו גט

Now we move on to how the get must be written and signed: for him and for her.

Seif 1: the practice get

A sofer wrote a get to teach or to practise, and a husband came along and found that the names in it matched his own, his wife's and his town's. He took it and divorced with it: it is not a get.

The Rabbi noted that in the time of the Mishnah they did not spend as much time on the names as we do. Today, with all the variations we write, finding a man and a woman, and their fathers, with exactly the same names is almost statistically impossible. But if it did happen, the get could not be used. That is one reason the practice developed to tear the get, so that it can never be used again.

The tofes and the toref

This ties in with the earlier mahloket over tofsei gittin: may the standard text of a get be written in advance, leaving out his name, her name, the place and the date?

  • The Tanna Kamma permits it; Rabbi Elazar forbids it, because we decree the tofes on account of the toref (the essential part). The Gemara rules like Rabbi Elazar, and so do most Rishonim.
  • The Rambam, in his Commentary on the Mishnah, ruled like the Tanna Kamma. The Rabbi mentioned that it was later found that he had amended this.
  • The Aharonim all rule that the tofes may not be written in advance without lishmah.

What if such a get was written and given? The Rabbi brought views that it should not be used, but that as with a rabbinic invalidity, if she remarried with it she need not leave, and the children are kasher.

Seif 2: written for one man, given by another

He wrote a get to divorce his wife and changed his mind. A man from his town said, "My name is like yours, and my wife's name is like your wife's", took it and divorced with it. It is not a get, even though it was written for the sake of a divorce.

Seif 3: two wives with one name

מי שיש לו שתי נשים ששמותיהם שוות, וכתב לגרש את הגדולה ונמלך וגירש בו את הקטנה, אינו גט

"Very strict." It is not enough that the get was written for his wife: it must be written for this specific woman. Written to divorce the older, it cannot divorce the younger, even though it was written for him.

Seif 4: "whichever I choose"

He told the sofer: "Write, and whichever one I want, I will divorce with it." Or: "whichever of them comes out of the door first." The Rabbi put it in human terms: a man with two wives who cannot provide for both, and will decide later.

This depends on bererah: can a choice made later reveal what was intended from the start? If we hold of bererah, it should work; if not, it does not. Since we are in doubt, she is a safek megureshet: do not do it, and if it was done, another get is needed.

The class broke for Pesah, to continue afterwards.

Summary of the Halachot

  1. Edim who cannot read (Seif 16): the get is read before them. Lemaaseh two must read it (the Ri, Tosafot, the Bah, the Taz, the Beit Shmuel), even though the Rif, as the Rosh explains him, allows one. They should understand the language (Beit Yoseph); the Beit Shmuel allows translation.
  2. Edim who cannot sign: the Shulhan Arukh allows a mark they write over; the Rema says lekhathilah use a cut stencil. Do not etch their whole signature: they must sign in their own hand.
  3. Non-Jewish edim (Seif 19) are always pasul. But a get that comes before us signed with names Jews never use may be relied on; names that are somewhat Jewish may not.
  4. The husband's handwriting with one witness (Seif 20) is kasher midoraita and pasul midrabbanan: if she remarried she need not leave, even before children. For the Rambam and the Rif, even his handwriting alone can have this effect.
  5. Paying edim (Seif 21): the Shulhan Arukh allows only lost time; the Rema allows more.
  6. The get should be dry before the edim sign (Seif 22), and certainly before it is given (Siman 124).
  7. Lishmah (Siman 131): a get must be written for this man and this woman. A practice get, a get written for another man, or for his other wife with the same name, is not a get. "Whichever I choose" depends on bererah: safek megureshet.
