Topic: Whether a berakhah is said on food and drink that is harmful or unhealthy

Sources: Gemara Berakhot; Rashi; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Berachot, Tuesday 16 January 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • One says a berakhah on any benefit from this world, but there are cases where it is inappropriate even though one enjoys the food.
  • The Gemara in Berakhot says no berakhah is said on plain olive oil because drinking it is harmful; this is the halacha in the Shulhan Arukh. Whether that has changed today is beyond this discussion.
  • Should one then bless on Coca-Cola, sugary or very oily foods? Rashi explains that harmful food gets no berakhah because it is not the normal way of eating; on his reading the question does not arise, since unhealthy food is, unfortunately, normal.
  • Many others explain that the Hakhamim did not institute praise for what is harmful, and on that reading one could strongly argue against blessing on very sugary food and drink.
  • Yet the Gemara requires a berakhah on raw barley, though eating it for 40 days harms the intestines. This distinguishes long-term harm from immediate harm; most unhealthy foods do not show an immediate effect, so there is room to say one still blesses, and the common practice is to do so.
  • Where food causes direct and immediate damage, such as a diabetic eating something sugary, it would certainly be wrong to bless: a berakhah in vain, since food is given for our benefit, not only enjoyment.
  • For the really unhealthy, such as children's lollipops, he thinks there is a strong argument that no berakhah should be made.

In one line

Food that causes immediate harm, like sugar for a diabetic, takes no berakhah, and there is a strong argument for the same with truly unhealthy treats.
