Topic: The laws of Birkat HaIlanot, the blessing on blossoming fruit trees

Sources: The Gemara; the Rishonim; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the Kabbalists, the Ben Ish Hai and the Hida

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Wednesday 30 March 2022

The halacha

  • The Hakhamim instituted a once-a-year berakhah on seeing two blossoming fruit trees ("hayose bimei Nisan"), thanking HaKadosh Barukh Hu for trees given for our benefit and enjoyment, a blessing of hope and renewal.
  • It is said only while the trees are in blossom, not once the blossoms have fallen and fruit is growing.
  • Most Rishonim hold it is not limited to Nisan: it may be said in Adar if blossoms come early, or in Iyar if late. In the southern hemisphere it will be around September or October. Hakham Ovadiah rules this way.
  • The Kabbalists, the Ben Ish Hai and the Hida require Nisan specifically, but the halacha by the plain sense is that it may be said before or after.
  • Accordingly, it is not time-bound and women are equally obligated.
  • The tree need not be one's own; it may be in a park or a back garden, and the berakhah may be said at night if the blossoms are visible.
  • Some go out of the city, reading "hayose", one who goes out, literally, but this is not required.
  • Since grafting trees is forbidden (though grafted fruit may be eaten), some advise against saying the berakhah on a grafted tree, and one should refrain.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: "hayose bimei Nisan", one who goes out in Nisan and sees blossoming trees.
  • The Rishonim and Hakham Ovadiah: not limited to Nisan.
  • The Kabbalists, the Ben Ish Hai and the Hida: only in Nisan.

In one line

Say Birkat HaIlanot on blossoming fruit trees, ideally in Nisan but otherwise when they blossom, women included, day or night, but not on a grafted tree.
