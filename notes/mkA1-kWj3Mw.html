Topic: Whether there is any acceptable way to watch a football match on Shabbat

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Rama

Daily Halacha: Shabbat, Monday 21 June 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Switching on a television on Shabbat is forbidden.
  • It is a big mistake to think one may ask a young child to do a melakhah: the Torah forbids using our children for forbidden melakhot, though a small child who acts of his own accord is another matter.
  • One may not ask a non-Jew to do even a rabbinically forbidden act, unless there are special circumstances. So asking a child or a non-Jew to switch on a television or radio for a match is absolutely forbidden.
  • The Shulhan Arukh permits starting work before Shabbat that continues by itself, like wheat in a mill, so for Sepharadi practice a television switched on before Shabbat, or on a timer, would be hard to call a violation.
  • The Rama, for Ashkenazim, forbids leaving anything that makes a noise running from before Shabbat, lest people suspect it was operated on Shabbat; seemingly this applies to a television or radio.
  • Even so, it is clearly against the spirit of Shabbat, which was not given for watching sport, and one should refrain.
  • Where the alternative is someone actually switching it on on Shabbat, programming it beforehand is the least bad option.

In one line

Watching a match on a set left on or timed before Shabbat may not break the letter of the law for Sepharadim, but it breaks its spirit.
