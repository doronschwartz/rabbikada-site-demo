Topic: The Gemara's misvah to marry one's niece, and how we relate to it today

Sources: Gemara Sanhedrin; the Rema; Rabbi Yehudah HaHasid, his will; the Sis Eliezer (Rav Eliezer Waldenberg)

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A Gemara in Sanhedrin says it is a misvah to marry one's niece, understood to include both a sister's and a brother's daughter. The reason is hesed: keeping the relationship in the family, and one will love, protect and provide for her more.
  • The Rema, about 450 years ago, also records it as a misvah.
  • Today we know of the genetic issues, so it is not advisable.
  • Rabbi Yehudah HaHasid wrote in his will not to marry one's niece, giving no reason. Whether he knew of genetics one can only speculate.
  • The simple understanding is that the Hakhamim lacked our knowledge of genetics.
  • The Sis Eliezer says differently: the misvah is the hesed of looking after one's family. When done for the misvah, HaShem protects the couple, but today it is very hard to have that intention, so it is not advisable. He concludes he cannot call it forbidden, since the Gemara permits it.
  • In practice it is clearly not advisable, though in Middle Eastern countries it happened until not long ago.

In one line

Though the Gemara calls marrying a niece a misvah, it is not advisable today, though not forbidden.
