Topic: Common misconceptions about Birkat HaLevanah, the blessing on the new moon

Sources: The Rambam and the Rosh; Shulhan Arukh; Shaarei Orah; Hakham Ovadiah; the Hida and the Ben Ish Hai

Daily Halacha: Peculiar Berachot, Thursday 20 February 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Hakhamim instituted this berakhah because the moon appears to us to be renewed every month.
  • According to most Rishonim, including the Rambam and the Rosh, one may say it from the first day after Rosh Hodesh, even on a thin sliver. Others require some benefit from its light, after three days. So according to basically all the Rishonim it may certainly be said after three days.
  • People wait seven days because the Shulhan Arukh rules like a Kabbalistic work, Shaarei Orah: seven days corresponding to the days of creation, when the moon is in its full force.
  • The Shulhan Arukh ruled this in Sefat, where skies are usually clear. In places like England, where one only has until mid-month, it is doubtful one must wait, and the poskim are generally lenient to say it after three days.
  • One should try to say it on Mosei Shabbat, when one is well dressed and scented. If that is the fifth or sixth night and the next one would be the twelfth, say it on the earlier Mosei Shabbat, as Hakham Ovadiah writes, if three days have passed.
  • One must see the moon and benefit from its light, so only at night or near nightfall. Most poskim allow a little cloud if the moon is clearly visible; the Hida and the Ben Ish Hai require a fully clear moon. Sepharadim prefer to wait for this, but if that would mean losing the berakhah, one may say it on a clearly visible moon with a few streaks of cloud.
  • The added mizmor and pesukim are because it is as if greeting the Shekhinah, which the moon represents, but the core of the misvah is the berakhah itself.

In one line

The berakhah may be said from three days, ideally on Mosei Shabbat, on a clearly visible moon at night.
