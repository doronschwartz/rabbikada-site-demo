Topic: Whether Birkat HaIlanot may be said on Shabbat

Sources: The Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; the Kabbalists

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Thursday 31 March 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • This Shabbat is Rosh Hodesh Nisan and Parashat HaHodesh, with three Sifrei Torah. Since Birkat HaIlanot is customarily begun in Nisan, may it be said on Shabbat or Yom Tov? Three concerns were raised.
  • First: the Gemara and Shulhan Arukh permit smelling a hadas on Shabbat but forbid smelling an etrog, lest one pluck the edible fruit. So one may smell inedible fragrant plants without touching them, but not edible ones. A late posek of the last century argued that looking at blossoms might likewise lead to plucking. Hakham Ovadiah and many others reject this as far-fetched: we do not invent our own gezerot, and the Hakhamim never forbade it.
  • Second: Kabbalistic works say the berakhah releases sparks and souls trapped in trees, and a late authority called this separating good from bad, a form of the melakhah of sorting. Hakham Ovadiah answers that spiritual concepts cannot be classed as melakhah; otherwise every misvah on Shabbat would be forbidden.
  • Third: one might carry a siddur outside without an eruv. That is an understandable concern, but with an eruv there is no problem, and again we do not invent decrees. It is a short blessing, and one simply must not carry.
  • Hakham Ovadiah writes explicitly that when Rosh Hodesh Nisan fell on Shabbat he said Birkat HaIlanot on Shabbat itself, to do the misvah at the first opportunity.
  • So one may say it this Shabbat, without carrying a siddur where there is no eruv and without cutting any leaves or blossoms.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara and Shulhan Arukh: hadas may be smelled on Shabbat, an etrog may not.
  • Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph: rejects all three concerns, and said the berakhah on Shabbat himself.

In one line

Birkat HaIlanot may be said on Shabbat, provided one does not carry without an eruv or touch the blossoms.
