Topic: Hol HaMoed outings in perspective, and the place of Torah study on Hol HaMoed

Sources: The Gemara, Sukkah; the Yerushalmi; Sefer HaHinukh; Rambam

Daily Halacha: Moadim, Thursday 11 September 2025

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • Hol HaMoed trips are a recent, modern idea. There is no obligation to go on one, though a family outing to a nice place is a form of simhah on Yom Tov.
  • The Gemara in Sukkah cites a sage who praised taking it easy on Sukkot rather than running around, since at home one enjoys the moed and the misvah of the sukkah more. Day-long trips that leave everyone exhausted leave no room for spirituality.
  • The Yerushalmi records a sage who would have permitted work on Hol HaMoed, because people end up wasting their time. The purpose of the prohibition is more time for Torah study, which matters even more.
  • Sefer HaHinukh notes there were no holidays then: the moadim were the holiday, a week of rest and of time for what one cannot do while working.
  • Trips are wonderful, but not at the expense of time, ideally half the day, for Torah.
  • Rambam describes the conduct: rise early, pray, a quick breakfast, back to the synagogue to study until midday, pray Minhah, then eat, drink and rejoice for the rest of the day.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara, Sukkah: staying at home on Sukkot to enjoy the sukkah.
  • The Yerushalmi: work would be better than idleness; the prohibition exists for Torah study.
  • Sefer HaHinukh: the moadim as time freed for spiritual pursuits.
  • Rambam: half the day for prayer and study, half for eating and rejoicing.

In one line

Hol HaMoed trips are fine, but half the day belongs to prayer and Torah study.
