The points

  • The series turns to the halachot of Yom Tov and Hol HaMoed, less well known than Hilchot Shabbat. Hilchot Yom Tov is much smaller but depends on Hilchot Shabbat, and there are differences to learn, especially with Shavuot in about three weeks.
  • Six days have the Torah status of Yom Tov: the first and seventh days of Pesah, the first day of Sukkot, Shemini Aseret, Shavuot and Rosh Hashanah. Days added outside Israel are rabbinic.
  • The Rambam is clear that Rosh Hashanah has the full status of Yom Tov. Yom Kippur does not: its halachot are like Shabbat, somewhat between Shabbat and Yom Tov.
  • The Ramhal in Derech Hashem measures a day's status by its aliyot: Shabbat seven, Yom Kippur six, the Yamim Tovim five, Rosh Hodesh and Hol HaMoed four, Hanukkah and Purim three.
  • The Torah's essence of these days is refraining from work and rejoicing; the Hakhamim add Torah study and gratitude.
  • The festivals look to the coming season: Pesah in spring, when things sprout; Shavuot, the harvest; Sukkot, the ingathering. Rav Shimshon Pincus compares them to birth, maturity (like a bar misvah) and completion, with Shemini Aseret as a yihud, the huppah at the end of the process.
  • The first Mishnah of Rosh Hashanah makes them days of judgment: Pesah for produce, Shavuot for fruit of the tree, Sukkot for the winter's water, and Rosh Hashanah for all humanity, with Israel leading.

In one line

The Torah gives six days of Yom Tov, for rest and rejoicing, and each festival opens a new season and is a day of judgment for it.
