Topic: The meaning of reducing simhah from Rosh Hodesh Av, and its practical applications

Sources: The Mishnah; Tosafot; the Mishnah Berurah; the Magen Avraham; the Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Gemara; Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Wednesday 12 July 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Mishnah says, "Mishenikhnas Av memaatin besimhah": when Av begins we reduce simhah.
  • Tosafot infer from the absence of a measure that one should not be happy at all; the Mishnah Berurah, the Magen Avraham and others codify this. The Rambam quotes the words as they are, and he understands him literally: a less happy spirit than usual.
  • Entertainment that is not a necessity, though permitted the rest of the year, is avoided; each person must judge necessity against luxury.
  • Building for simhah is forbidden (Shulhan Arukh, from the Gemara): a necessary extension for a family that has outgrown the house may continue, but a garden swimming pool, unless for medical reasons, may not.
  • Business purely for simhah is not allowed, such as buying during the Nine Days for a mundane party after Tisha BeAv.
  • No weddings from Rosh Hodesh. Engagements are permitted even on Tisha BeAv, as the Gemara says not to miss the opportunity.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: a new car as an upgrade is not allowed; if one has none, or it broke down, it is permitted. Without pressing need wait until after Tisha BeAv, unless the price will rise or it will not be available.
  • Haircuts remain permitted until the week of Tisha BeAv. Use the time for introspection about the exile.

In one line

From Rosh Hodesh Av we cut back on simhah: no weddings, building or purchases for pleasure, but needs and engagements are permitted.
