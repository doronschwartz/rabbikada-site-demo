Topic: Why we eat Korekh, why not only Korekh, and how it is eaten

Sources: Torah ("al massot umerorim yokhluhu"); Hillel; Shulhan Arukh

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Monday 3 April 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • "Al massot umerorim yokhluhu" was read yesterday to mean maror is required from the Torah only when there is a Korban Pesah. Hillel read it literally: the Korban Pesah, massah and maror are eaten together.
  • Korekh, the sandwich of massah and maror, recalls Hillel's view. It is only rabbinic, since there is no Korban Pesah today.
  • Why not just eat Korekh and cover everything? Massah is from the Torah and must be tasted on its own (taam massah). Since maror today is only rabbinic, eating them together would detract from the taste of the massah, and even Hillel would agree.
  • So we eat massah for the Torah misvah, then maror for the rabbinic one, then Korekh for Hillel's view.
  • Korekh is eaten reclining: though it includes maror, it recalls the Korban Pesah, a happy matter.
  • It contains a kazayit of massah and a kazayit of maror. This is the leader's third kazayit of massah and everyone else's second.
  • The maror is dipped in haroset again, but here there is no need to shake it off, since the misvah of maror is done and this is only a remembrance; one may enjoy the haroset.

Sources quoted

  • "Al massot umerorim yokhluhu": read by Hillel as eating all three together.
  • Shulhan Arukh: does not require shaking off the haroset at Korekh.

In one line

Korekh recalls Hillel's practice of eating massah and maror together, eaten reclining after each has been eaten alone, without shaking off the haroset.
