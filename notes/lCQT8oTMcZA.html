Topic: Artificial insemination from a non-Jewish donor

Sources: Rav Yehiel Yaakov Weinberg (Seridei Esh); Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Wednesday 8 July 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • With a non-Jewish donor there is no question of mamzerut, since there is no mamzerut from a non-Jew, even with a married Jewish woman.
  • Nor is there a concern of siblings marrying, since the donor's other children are not Jewish. So purely halachically, a non-Jewish donor could be argued to be better than a Jewish one.
  • The Seridei Esh, Rav Yehiel Yaakov Weinberg, agreed there is no mamzerut or sibling issue but still forbade it: people will think the child is the husband's. A later child of the couple would be thought a full sibling and this child would inherit; and if the husband dies, people would think the widow exempt from yibum and halisah, which follow the husband.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein was lenient: one cannot invent such new concerns, for by that logic adoption would be forbidden, since people often assume an adopted child is biological, and Hazal never forbade adoption.
  • So he permits it where there is real need: the couple cannot have a child together and the husband agrees.
  • The Seridei Esh's own concerns do not apply to a single woman, as there is no husband. Whether it is right hashkafically is for tomorrow.

Sources quoted

  • The Seridei Esh: forbidden, because people will assume the husband is the father.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: permitted in case of need, since the same concerns would forbid adoption.

In one line

A non-Jewish donor raises no mamzerut, and Rav Moshe Feinstein permits it in real need though the Seridei Esh forbade it.
