Topic: Whether owning shares in a company that trades in Hames counts as owning Hames

Sources: Rabbi David Hoffmann, Teshuvah; the Gemara (Hames in the mouth of a snake); Rabbi Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Thursday 28 March 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Owning Hames anywhere in the world is forbidden from the Torah, while Hames in one's home that one does not own is permitted if access is prevented. The question is whether a shareholder is a partner in the company's Hames or only a creditor.
  • Rabbi David Hoffmann, in Germany about 150 years ago, wrote in a Teshuvah that this question was set as an exam for students in the Beit Midrash, apparently for semikhah.
  • The Gemara discusses one's Hames in the mouth of a snake: though still technically one's own, one has no practical access to it, so there is no duty to retrieve it.
  • The poskim apply this to shares: a shareholder with no say in voting or management has no practical access to the Hames. Most poskim conclude that one with an insignificant holding may be lenient.
  • Some poskim were concerned and said to include shares in the sale of Hames. This has its own issues, but since many permit it anyway, adding them to the sale is fine.
  • A significant shareholding (Rabbi Moshe Feinstein said 30% and above) in a company trading in Hames on Pesah is a more serious issue that needs rabbinical advice; selling shares is not as straightforward as selling food.

Sources quoted

  • Rabbi David Hoffmann: the question was posed as an exam in the Beit Midrash.
  • The Gemara: Hames in a snake's mouth is not one's concern, as there is no access to it.
  • Rabbi Moshe Feinstein: a significant holding is 30% and above.

In one line

A small, passive shareholding in a Hames business is permitted (selling it is optional); a holding of 30% or more needs rabbinical advice.
