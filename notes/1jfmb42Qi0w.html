Topic: The laws of reverence (morah) for parents

Sources: The Talmud; Shulhan Arukh; the Tur and the Rosh; Shemuel I (David son of Yishai)

Daily Halacha: Honouring Parents, Wednesday 13 January 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Do not sit in a parent's fixed place (at the table, for study, and, the poskim write, in the synagogue) even when they are absent, without express permission.
  • Do not contradict a parent ("I disagree, that is wrong"). One may say that others hold differently. In Torah interpretation a son may disagree when sure he is right: the Tur, son of the Rosh, disagreed with his father in places, since Torah is a search for truth.
  • Do not interrupt a parent while speaking (true of anyone, all the more of a parent).
  • Do not call a parent by name without a title, even in their absence. Being called up as "Reuven ben Shimon" is allowed, since "ben" adds a title, as David answered Shaul: "the son of your servant Yishai".
  • The Talmud tells of a man whose mother tore his clothes and spat at him among the nobles of Rome, and he did not shame her; this is codified in the Shulhan Arukh. Even if a parent throws one's wallet into the river, one may not denigrate them, though one may later reclaim the money in court.
  • Whether this applies to a parent who repeatedly abuses a child is the next topic.

In one line

Reverence means not taking a parent's place, contradicting, interrupting or using their bare name, and not shaming them even when they shame you.
