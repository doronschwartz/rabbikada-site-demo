Topic: What the blemish of a child of a niddah means in practice today

Sources: The Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Rishonim; the Maharshal (Rabbi Shelomoh Luria); Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Monday 11 May 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The child of a niddah is not a mamzer, since kiddushin takes effect with her. The Rambam, followed by the Shulhan Arukh, says the child, boy or girl, is pagum: strictly no issue at all to marry, but not the ideal choice.
  • Where the Rambam got this is debated: perhaps a child of a prohibition cannot be entirely perfect, or he counted it among the children of the "nine traits" (bnei tesha middot), such as a child conceived when the husband secretly intends divorce.
  • The practical question: a fine boy or girl, perhaps a baal teshuvah, whose parents certainly never kept the laws of niddah. Should one look elsewhere?
  • The Maharshal writes that the Rambam's blemish applies only to a niddah forbidden by the Torah, not one forbidden only rabbinically.
  • There are strong grounds that a woman who immersed, even without the proper checks and preparations, is a niddah only rabbinically.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein builds on this: if the person excels, we may assume the mother was not a niddah from the Torah. She may have gone fully under water at the sea, or even in a swimming pool, whose drawn water (mayim sheuvim) by most opinions is only a rabbinic problem.
  • So in practice, though the Rambam and Shulhan Arukh call such a child pagum, if the person is good and the relationship is right, one may marry them without concern.

In one line

Following the Maharshal and Rav Moshe Feinstein, one need not hesitate to marry a fine person born to parents who did not keep the laws of niddah.
