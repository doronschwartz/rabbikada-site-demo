Topic: Sukkot as zeman simhatenu: the journey, flow, and the transience of life

Sources: Kohelet; Tehillim ("Lo hametim yehalelu Yah")

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Tuesday 15 October 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The Torah calls Sukkot a time of happiness twice; only Sukkot is zeman simhatenu, while Pesah is zeman herutenu. Happiness is not binary but a process, and Sukkot is the time to work on it.
  • Sukkot alone celebrates a journey, forty years travelling under HaShem's shelter, not an event in one place. We think happiness comes on arrival, but the journey itself is an essential part of it.
  • Flow: being immersed in a challenging task matched to one's abilities, not so hard it causes stress nor so easy it bores, gives fulfilment and happiness.
  • Sukkot is also linked to death. It is called Hag HaAsif, and "gathered to his people" is the Torah's phrase for dying. Ashkenazim read Kohelet, with its message of life's transience.
  • One reason is to temper our joy; the deeper one is that we appreciate life because it ends. Each moment brings us nearer the end, which can sadden, but it also teaches us to celebrate the moment.
  • Everything about Sukkot is transient: the sukkah is a temporary dwelling, the Arba Minim wither (the aravah is meant to be replaced daily), with the verse "the dead do not praise HaShem". Summer ends, leaves fall, it grows dark.
  • Living in the present and enjoying every moment HaShem gives can fill us with the greatest happiness.

In one line

Sukkot teaches that happiness lies in the journey and in valuing each passing moment.
