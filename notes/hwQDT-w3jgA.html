Shabbat Minhah: Uva LeSion and Vaani Tefillati

Siman 292 covers Minhah on Shabbat. The Rabbi noted again that the Shulhan Arukh's order suggests he was comfortable eating Seudah Shelishit before Minhah.

Seif 1: at Minhah we say Ashrei and Uva LeSion, take out the Sefer Torah and read ten verses from next week's parashah with three people, even when Shabbat and Yom Tov coincide.

Uva LeSion. On weekdays the Kedushah of Uva LeSion is said at the end of Shaharit. On Shabbat the morning service is long, so it was moved to the afternoon. Why also on Saturday night? The Tur, in the name of the Rishonim, says it gives the wicked extra time before they return to Gehinnom.

Someone once challenged the Rabbi for starting Minhah a few minutes before its time in winter. His answer: Ashrei and Uva LeSion are a make-up for what was left out in the morning, so there is nothing wrong with saying them before Minhah time. Whether the Torah reading may be done before Minhah time is a separate question, which he said is permissible, with sources to follow next week.

Vaani Tefillati. "May my prayer be at a time of favour." The midrash explains: other nations eat and drink and misbehave; we eat and drink and then return to shul. That makes it a time of favour. In Kabbalah, late Shabbat afternoon is the most favourable time of Shabbat.

Reading the Torah at Minhah

This is the first part of Shabbat Minhah with a source in the Gemara. Ezra instituted readings every three days (Shabbat, Monday, Thursday), and added Shabbat afternoon because of "yoshvei keranot", people who sit idly on street corners after eating and drinking. "Prayer doesn't get people back in synagogue. Torah does."

  • The minimum is ten verses with three people; one may read more. Often slightly more is read because an aliyah may not end within three verses of a paragraph.
  • When a Shabbat and Yom Tov coincide, we still read next week's parashah, since Yom Tov has no Minhah reading.
  • Travellers to Israel after Pesah, when England is a week behind, have no obligation to make up the missed parashah. The obligation goes with the community you are in. "I would do it as well, but you don't need to."

Asked why we read on Yom Kippur afternoon, the class agreed it is because it is a fast day: we read at Minhah only on Shabbat and on fasts.

Sidkatekha

Seif 2: Sidkatekha is said, from the Rishonim, three times. Two reasons were given:

  • Moshe, Yosef and David died on Shabbat afternoon, and since we cannot eulogise on Shabbat we say a Sidduk HaDin, affirming Hashem's justice, for each.
  • As Shabbat ends the wicked return to Gehinnom, and we affirm that Hashem's judgement is just.

Since it is a Sidduk HaDin, the poskim write that one should stand for it, as at a funeral. On a day when Tahanun would not be said on a weekday, Sidkatekha is omitted, just as Sidduk HaDin is not said on such days.

The Rema records a custom not to give a public class between Minhah and Arvit on Shabbat, in memory of Moshe, Yosef and David, when the study hall would close to mourn a sage. The Mishnah Berurah objects: if people will just chat outside instead, the custom should be abolished. The Rabbi said Sefaradim never had this custom. If people are sitting in shul with nothing to do, of course one should teach Torah.

Waiting for three stars

Siman 293, seif 1: Arvit on Mosei Shabbat is delayed to add to Shabbat. Just as we add time before Shabbat (in London 15 minutes; the minimum is about two minutes, as learned in 261), we add after it.

Seif 2: no melakhah until three small stars are seen together. If it is cloudy, wait until sure.

  • The halacha is three medium stars. Since we cannot tell what a medium star is, we wait for three small stars to be safe.
  • Requiring the stars to be next to each other, rather than scattered, is the addition for Tosefet Shabbat. The Mishnah Berurah says that waiting a couple of minutes after three medium stars would also be enough.

Why did the shul start Arvit before the published end of Shabbat? Because the published time already includes much more than the minimum, and the Amidah is reached after the time.

The Shulhan Arukh elsewhere seems to follow Rabbenu Tam's 72 minutes after sunset. The Biur Halacha suggests the stars are for someone who does not know when sunset was. The Rabbi said this is one of the proofs that the Shulhan Arukh does not really hold like Rabbenu Tam. In practice we rely on three medium stars together, plus a little extra.

Early Havdalah from Pelag HaMinhah

Seif 3: someone who must travel straight after Shabbat for a misvah and will have no time or wine afterwards may pray Arvit and make Havdalah from Pelag HaMinhah, while it is still day. This is from the Gemara.

  • He does not bless the fire, and may not do any melakhah until Shabbat ends.
  • Spices and fire are not part of Havdalah itself; Havdalah is the wine and "Hamavdil." The Shulhan Arukh implies besamim are said, but the poskim say not to, since there is no sadness while Shabbat is still here.
  • Havdalah means distinguishing Saturday from Sunday, like Kiddush distinguishes Friday from Shabbat. It need not wait until Shabbat has ended.

The poskim were uneasy about using this, fearing people would make Havdalah and then do melakhah. So it is rarely used, but the Rabbi gave two cases.

Children on long summer nights. In Gateshead, where Shabbat may end after 11 pm, boarding schools had boys make Havdalah on Sunday morning. The Rabbi said Sefaradim hold that Havdalah on Sunday morning is said without Hashem's name, so his ruling is:

  • Better to make Havdalah on Saturday evening, after Pelag HaMinhah, after praying Arvit.
  • Make it the last thing before bed: pray Arvit, get ready, make Havdalah, say Shema, sleep.

Someone added a reason: if you wake hungry in the night, you have already made Havdalah. Pelag in Gateshead can be about 9.15 or 9.30, so they must stay up until then.

An onen. From Rav Ovadia Yosef: if a close relative dies on Shabbat, there is no aninut on Shabbat. Once Shabbat ends, the onen is exempt from misvot and could not pray Arvit or make Havdalah. So he should pray Arvit and make Havdalah from Pelag HaMinhah while it is still Shabbat.

Lengthening the end of Shabbat. The Rema mentions drawing out "Vehu rahum" on Saturday night, as Ashkenazi hazanim do. The Arizal said to lengthen "Barekhu", and Rav Hayim Vital wrote this brings a good week.

Practical summary

  • Shabbat Minhah includes Uva LeSion and a reading of ten verses from next week's parashah.
  • Stand for Sidkatekha; omit it on days without Tahanun.
  • Shabbat ends with three small stars together, or a few minutes after three medium stars.
  • In real need, Arvit and Havdalah may be said from Pelag HaMinhah, without fire and without melakhah until Shabbat ends.
  • For children on long summer Shabbatot, Sefaradim should prefer early Havdalah before bed to Havdalah on Sunday morning.
