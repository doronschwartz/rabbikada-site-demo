Topic: Laundry for children, washing oneself and swimming in the week of Tisha BeAv

Sources: The Gemara; the Rishonim; Shulhan Arukh; Hakham Ovadiah

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Wednesday 7 August 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Recap: by the Gemara, and the Sepharadi custom, haircuts and shaving are forbidden only in the week of Tisha BeAv (Ashkenazim all three weeks); laundry likewise in that week (Ashkenazim from Rosh Hodesh).
  • Children who regularly dirty themselves may have clothes washed and wear fresh ones. Hakham Ovadiah says up to 3, other poskim 6, some 8; above 8 one should refrain, and below it depends on the child.
  • Washing oneself is forbidden by the Gemara only on Tisha BeAv. The Rishonim extended it to the week (Sepharadim) and Ashkenazim to the Nine Days. The Shulhan Arukh stresses being careful with each community's customs in these days.
  • Times have changed: the bathhouse was once a long treat (hence not before Minhah), whereas a shower today takes minutes. A relaxing bath is forbidden in this week unless for medical reasons.
  • One who showers daily or every second day and cannot function without it may shower, since that removes dirt rather than being washing for pleasure: briefly, with lukewarm water, without indulging. Human dignity, which the Gemara says can override certain restrictions, requires washing, for example where there is body odour.
  • Swimming for pure leisure is forbidden all Nine Days. For health it seems permitted, even for Ashkenazim, until the week of Tisha BeAv, but he does not think it permitted in that week. If medically required, for physical or mental health, it is permitted.

In one line

In the week of Tisha BeAv one avoids washing for pleasure and leisure swimming, but basic hygiene and medical needs are permitted.
