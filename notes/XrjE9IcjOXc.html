Five prayers, and Kol Nidrei is not one of them

This was the second evening on the High Holy Day services; the previous class went through Rosh Hashanah. Yom Kippur is longer but in a sense easier, because the same structure is copied across all five services. If you understand Arvit and Shaharit you can apply most of it to the rest of the day. You need a Yom Kippur mahzor to follow. The Hakhamim instituted five prayers: Arvit, Shaharit, Musaf, Minhah and Neilah. A common mistake, the Rabbi said, is to call the first night "Kol Nidrei night." Kol Nidrei is not a tefillah. It is one tiny, peripheral paragraph, and the real service begins later with Arvit.

The opening of the evening

The evening opens with a poem by Rav Hai Gaon, about a thousand years old, which is a nice introduction but not part of the service proper. Before Kol Nidrei comes a short paragraph in which, with the permission of God and of the congregation, we allow sinners to pray with us. It was introduced by the Maharam of Rothenburg in thirteenth-century Germany, probably because most of the community came only on Yom Kippur. The Talmud learns from the Temple incense, which included a bad-smelling spice, that every gathering needs a sinner too. The Rabbi noted that it is a harsh way to begin the day.

Kol Nidrei: a legal declaration

Kol Nidrei is not mentioned in the Talmud, which says only that someone worried about stumbling in his vows should annul forgotten vows once a year, at the beginning of the year. Most say it was composed in the sixth or seventh century. It is said on Yom Kippur because that is when most people come. Historians add that it also served Jews who had converted under duress and came back once a year to annul their vows to other religions. - It is a legal arrangement, not a prayer. It is in Aramaic, the language of the time, because you must understand what you are saying for an annulment to work. In prayer, the main thing is knowing you are standing before God. - It must be done before nightfall. Annulling vows is a judicial act, forbidden on Shabbat and Yom Kippur. The fast begins at sunset, but the time between sunset and nightfall is uncertain, so it was permitted then. - It works only for forgotten vows. To annul a particular vow you need three people to find an opening for regret: new information that, had you known it, would have stopped you vowing. Someone who vowed to fast every Monday and Thursday, and then learned from his doctor that it harms him, could be released. Someone who simply changed his mind could not. It is six lines, he said, and we have made a whole production of it. Asked why we do not say it in English, he agreed there is a strong argument for it, as it is not a prayer. "We say it three times just to make it a bit stronger. But really, we could do it once. Maybe the third time read it in English yourself."

Shehiyanu and Spanish and Portuguese customs

Shehiyanu is said here because there is no Kiddush on Yom Kippur. It is the only compulsory item before Arvit. Asked about candle-lighting, he said Ashkenazim say it then and Sephardim do not. Unique to the Spanish and Portuguese is a Mi Sheberakh for those interrogated by the Inquisition, said in Portuguese. Asked whether that still makes sense, he said it is not relevant any more and is kept as tradition.

The core: the Amidah and the Viduy

Arvit begins with the regular Shema and blessings. In the Amidah the first three blessings are regular, including the paragraphs on God's sovereignty discussed at Rosh Hashanah. The fourth blessing is the festival blessing with the addition of the day of forgiveness. Unlike Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur is essentially a regular festival prayer. If you had only an hour or two, the crucial part is the five Amidot and the confession within them, which are written in the Talmud. The Talmud says atonement comes by day, not at night, yet a confession was instituted in every prayer. There are eleven in all: two in each of the five services, and one in Minhah on the eve of Yom Kippur, in case someone chokes at the final meal. Arvit has no repetition, so its second confession follows the Selihot. In the day services it is said in the silent Amidah and again in the repetition with the hazan. Confession must be said standing, and on some views it does not count otherwise. The Viduy begins with a general confession. "Al Het" then moves from sins done under duress or willingly, secretly or publicly, inadvertently or intentionally, up through those that bring a sin offering, lashes, karet and the four death penalties. The Rabbi's main point is that the most important confession is your own. The text is there to awaken you. You must say the first paragraph, but after that you may say your own confession instead, and that could be more meaningful. A thousand years ago, he said, the service would have ended here. Yom Kippur was never a whole day in synagogue: the Talmud describes the girls going out to the vineyards. Selihot were added in the last eight hundred years or so to fill the time.

Selihot: what was added, and why

"If you had to miss something on Yom Kippur, this is the time to miss. These songs are just nice things to awaken us to come back to Hashem. The Amidah is the end itself, the Viduy is the end itself." - "Anenu" and the poems. Page 28 has a song by David Pekuda, his name hidden in the acrostic. - "El Melekh" and "Vayaavor." The thirteen attributes are said 26 times in the day, matching the letters of God's name. This is not in the Talmud, which says that after the golden calf God taught Moshe that when Israel recites them they will be forgiven. The Rabbi stressed that this is not a magic formula: as we say "merciful" and "compassionate," we should ask ourselves whether we are, and want to be. That is the greatest way to seek forgiveness. El Melekh, by Amram Gaon, introduces it. - The crucial paragraph. On page 32 the section from "Hatanu" is the most important part of the Selihot: acceptance of God's sovereignty, unity and eternity. We stand for it. In the Spanish and Portuguese rite the Viduy is said three times in each service. The Rabbi cited the Keter Shem Tov that one should not confess twice in succession, so the second was meant to be personal and quiet, and said he does not know how the third entered.

The day services

Everything said so far applies throughout, he explained. Shaharit is like a regular Shabbat morning with a few extra songs, including "Keter Malkhut" by Shelomoh ibn Gabirol. In the repetition the Spanish and Portuguese add the Kedushot of Yehudah HaLevi. This was controversial, he noted, because the Amidah is a fixed text, and in other Sephardi communities the sagan says them rather than the hazan. The Viduy Gadol of Rabbeinu Nissim, a long and moving confession, is said after Arvit by Middle Eastern Sephardim, not by the Spanish and Portuguese. The Torah readings. In the morning we read Aharei Mot, the Kohen Gadol's confession and the goat of Yom Kippur. With no Kohen Gadol today, each of us confesses. The Haftarah is about teshuvah. In Minhah we read the forbidden relationships, followed by Yonah. The Poskim give two reasons: people who come once a year should be reminded of what is forbidden, and many men and women are together in synagogue. A simple reason, he believes, is that it is the next section and rolling the scroll would be too much. Musaf is long because it describes the service of the Kohen Gadol. Neilah starts about 45 minutes before sunset, so it has few Selihot. The crescendo is Shema Yisrael, "Barukh shem," then "Hashem hu haElohim" seven times, for the seven heavens. The theme, as at Rosh Hashanah, is the unity of God. Then we blow the shofar, remembering the Jubilee shofar that freed slaves on Yom Kippur.

Questions

Asked when the prayer book was finalised, the Rabbi gave a very educated guess of the late sixteenth century, after which nothing was added. The Rambam's Yom Kippur siddur, he said, would be perhaps a third of this size, since he was not in favour of piyutim.

Summary

  • Yom Kippur has five prayers: Arvit, Shaharit, Musaf, Minhah and Neilah. Kol Nidrei is a legal annulment of vows, not one of them.
  • The essential part of the day is the Amidah with the Viduy in each service. Add your own confession.
  • Selihot, piyutim and the extra readings were added later to awaken us to teshuvah and are a means to an end.
  • The thirteen attributes should inspire us to act in the same way, not work as a formula.
  • Once you understand Arvit, the other four services follow the same pattern.
