Source: The Rambam's Introduction to Perek Helek, continued: where this-worldly reward, Gan Eden and Gehinom fit

Shiur: Part 3 of the series on the Rambam's Introduction to Perek Helek, given by Dayan Daniel Kada on Zoom, Tuesday 16 September 2025 (the week of Ki Tavo, before Rosh Hashanah)

These notes are based on the Sofer.ai transcript of the recording.

Recap

  1. Five views of the afterlife: Gan Eden, Mashiach, techiyat hametim, this world, all combined. All miss the real thing: Olam HaBa.
  2. Three readings of aggadah: literal; literal, so the Sages were wrong (which angered the Rambam); and metaphorical, the true view, held by very few. Read the Sages physically, and you'll imagine a physical reward.
  3. Olam HaBa: עין לא ראתה אלהים זולתך. Wholly spiritual, so a physical body can't comprehend it: the fish and heat, the blind man and colours. The Sages used graphic images so the wise could draw out something of the truth.

A correction, mid-sentence. "And that's what our focus is on… let me rephrase. The Rambam wouldn't be happy if I said that." We don't serve Hashem for Olam HaBa. We are עושי האמת מפני שהוא אמת: we keep the mitzvot because they're best for our character, identity, consciousness and intellect. Olam HaBa is what results.

Olam HaBa = spiritual identity, cleaving to Hashem: grasping Godliness. The more refined your intellect and character, the closer the connection and the greater the bliss.

The Queen. "If Queen Elizabeth, zichronah, announced she'd spend time with people according to how refined and 'royal' they'd become, the more you'd learned royal etiquette and refined yourself, the closer you'd get. Swap in whoever you'd most love to meet. That's Olam HaBa."

Tonight: where the other "rewards," with sources in Torah and Chazal, fit around it.

Part 1: Reward and Punishment in This World

The Torah's blessings and curses ("didn't we just read them on Shabbat?"):

היעודים הטובים והנקמות הרעות הכתובות בתורה, ענינם… כי הוא אומר לך: אם תעשה המצות האלה, אסייע לך על עשייתם והשלמות בהם, ואסיר מעליך המעיקים והמונעים כלם.

If you keep these mitzvot, I'll help you keep them and perfect yourself in them, and remove every obstacle.

לפי שהאדם אי אפשר לו לעשות המצות כשהוא חולה ורעב וצמא, ולא בשעת מלחמה. No one can keep mitzvot while ill, hungry, thirsty or at war. So the Torah promises those will go, so that they can complete their knowledge and merit Olam HaBa.

Maslow's hierarchy. "You can't think about culture or friendships before food and shelter. You can't think about Olam HaBa, about who you want to be, while you're fighting to put bread on the table or going from one illness to the next."

And the curses: abandon the Torah, and those very conditions are withheld, until you can't keep the mitzvot.

A new reading of Ki Tavo

תחת אשר לא עבדת את ה׳ אלהיך בשמחה ובטוב לבב מרב כל (Devarim 28:47)

Bang in the middle of the curses. The Rishonim ask: is the punishment for not serving, or for not serving joyfully?

Reading

The common reading

You served, but without joy, so you were punished

The Rambam's

You didn't serve Hashem when you had joy, a settled heart and plenty: no illness, no money worries, every ability to serve. Not serving while fighting to survive is one thing; with everything and still not serving, Hashem says: the conditions I gave you don't help you, so I'll take them away

אם עשית קצת מצות מאהבה והשתדלות, אעזרך לעשות כולם… ואם תעזוב דבר מהם דרך בזיון, אביא לך מונעים שימנעוך מעשותם כולם.

"The reward of a mitzvah is a mitzvah"

שכר מצוה מצוה ושכר עבירה עבירה (Avot 4:2)

For the Rambam, who holds mitzvot aren't done for reward, this is the simplest reading: the reward of a mitzvah is the ability to do more (Hashem grants livelihood, health, peace of mind); the consequence of a sin is losing that ability: under-development.

"Isn't this beautiful? According to the Rambam there's no reward and punishment written in the Torah at all."

  • Rabbi Akiva: שכר מצוה בהאי עלמא ליכא: no reward in this world. "Seemingly preposterous, when the Torah says do this, get that over and over. The Rambam: correct, because those aren't rewards."
  • בדרך שאדם רוצה לילך מוליכין אותו: Hashem leads you down the path you choose.
  • כל המקיים את התורה מעוני, סופו לקיימה מעושר; וכל המבטל את התורה מעושר, סופו לבטלה מעוני (Avot 4:9). Wealth in every sense: money, health, peace of mind.
  • Why doesn't the Torah mention Olam HaBa? (Every Rishon asks; Monday night's topic.) Because the Torah doesn't talk about reward at all.

"Don't lose your life"

Rabbi Levi Yitzchak of Berditchev saw a man run out of shul mid-prayer: I have to get to work, to make a living. While you're running to make a living, be careful you don't lose your life. "We're so busy with day-to-day things that we can't think about our mission in life. This season forces us to."

The paradox of progress. "We used to wash clothes in the sea; now we throw them in a washing machine. We should have so much more time to develop ourselves. But more time means more distractions. Without that free time there'd be no Hollywood. The question is what we do with it. Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur force us to ask."

A question: doesn't this make tzaddik vera lo worse?

If good deeds lead Hashem to remove obstacles, why do the righteous struggle?

"I hear the question, and I've never thought of it that way. You're right: the Rambam's approach does make it bigger, because you can't push it into some future calculation. Theodicy is the biggest question we've always had."

  • We never really know what's going on in another person.
  • The Rambam himself wrote in a letter that he suffered depression for a couple of years after his brother died.
  • Rav Chaim Vital (the Arizal's transcriber, the source of most Kabbalah we have) wrote that for two formative years (around 23 to 25) he couldn't learn, and wanted to study chemistry instead.
  • אין צדיק בארץ אשר לא יחטא; וסביביו נשערה מאד: Hashem may be stricter with those close to Him.

"Perhaps it's relative: it would have been harder." "That's always the way out, I suppose."

Part 2: Gan Eden

A show of hands: Gan Eden is after death? Or in this world?

"As a kid I always thought it was some beautiful place in heaven, after we die. Some Rishonim say so. The Rambam doesn't. He holds Gan Eden is a physical place on this earth."

וגן עדן הוא מקום דשן, מבחר הארץ, ורבים בו הנהרות והאילנות העושים פירות, יגלה אותו השם לבני אדם לעתיד לבוא, ויורה אותם הדרך ללכת בו, ויתענגו בו. ואפשר שימצאו בו צמחים נפלאים מאד, גדולי התועלת, ערבים מאד, מלבד הידועים אצלנו. וכל זה אינו נמנע ולא רחוק, אבל קרוב להיות.

A fertile place, the choicest on earth, full of rivers and fruit trees, which Hashem will reveal in the future and show us the way to it. Perhaps it holds wondrous plants, beneficial and sweet, beyond those we know. Not impossible or far-fetched, all the more so since it's written in the Torah.

  • "In the future" = Mashiach's days or the resurrection; not revealed now.
  • "Show us the way": because Adam was expelled: ויגרש את האדם, וישכן מקדם לגן עדן את הכרבים ואת להט החרב המתהפכת לשמר את דרך עץ החיים. "It seems a physical place, guarded by an ever-turning flaming sword. That's the simple reading, and the Rambam's."

"But Google Maps…"

"I think we've mapped every metre of the planet." (Don: a quick search says 97–99%.) Caves; the ocean is less mapped than outer space. "It is slightly problematic, I'll be honest."

  • The Ramban (Sha'ar HaGemul): also physical, below the equator. He'd heard reports from India of sightings of the ever-turning flame.
  • "It's in Iran!" "Where in Iran?" "I read it. Persian people told me." "Let's go and see it." "After we conquer Iran?" "Maybe that's why we don't know yet."
  • The Ben Ish Chai's difficulty: the Gemara says the world is 1/60 of the Garden, the Garden 1/60 of Eden. So if Gan Eden is on earth, it must be vast but take up no space, like the Ark and Keruvim that took up no room in the Holy of Holies. "I don't think the Rambam would accept that; he doesn't like impossibilities."

The key point: it's very physical: beautiful waters, drinks, "wine pouring out." So group 1 isn't wrong, but it isn't the ultimate. It's one stage.

Part 3: Gehinom

Three or four lines, against pages on Mashiach and resurrection.

ואמנם גיהנם הוא שם לצער ולעונש שישיג לרשעים, ולא פרשו בתלמוד תואר זה העונש.

A name for the pain and punishment of the wicked. The Talmud never described its nature.

Some say

Proof

The sun will draw near and burn them

הנה היום בא בער כתנור (Malachi)

A strange heat will arise in their bodies and burn them

רוחכם אש תאכלכם

External heat or internal heat. That's it.

The Shelah points out: in the entire Mishneh Torah there is no real mention of Gehinom, only in passing, quoting a Gemara that a careless judge may go there. Gan Eden appears only indirectly, in the Sheva Berachot: שמח תשמח… כשמחך יצירך בגן עדן מקדם.

They are not principles of faith. Deny resurrection, Mashiach or eternity, and you're a heretic. Deny Gan Eden or Gehinom: you contradict the Sages, but it's minor in the grand scheme. This is crucial.

The Ramban's reading of the Rambam

Some accused the Rambam, so brief on Gehinom, of not believing in it. The Ramban: not true. "I think his reading is correct."

First, recall: the ultimate punishment for the Rambam is karet, the soul ceases to exist. (Class: "to go to hell?" "No." "Karet." "Good.")

The Ramban's questions:

  1. Justice. One man kept the whole Torah but once, knowingly, ate on Yom Kippur and never repented; another ate every Yom Kippur. Both: karet. Fair?
  2. The Mishnah's list. Many sins carry karet: so what's special about the heretic, singled out as having no portion? (This one can be answered: there are levels of karet.)

His answer, in the Rambam's name: before karet comes a process: Gehinom, up to twelve months, proportionate to the severity. The worst (a kofer) gets karet and intense Gehinom.

Kaddish. "That's why people say Kaddish twelve months: the Gemara says Gehinom lasts at most twelve. People make a massive thing of it, every prayer. In the grand scheme, it's not a major thing."

Open questions

Who enjoys the Rambam's Gan Eden: the body or the soul? The body's in the grave!

"Excellent question." A participant: maybe they're spiritual places in this world, invisible now, physical later. "Like the Ben Ish Chai. But for the Rambam it's physical: one day we'll sit, eat and drink there."

So how do the dead wicked receive a physical Gehinom? The Rambam says resurrection is only for the righteous. Will the wicked be revived to be punished?

"I don't think the Rambam believes that. I need to think about it and check. I don't have an answer now."

Summary

  1. Olam HaBa is the core, but it's the result of doing truth, not the motive.
  2. This-worldly blessings and curses aren't reward or punishment: Hashem removes, or imposes, the obstacles to serving Him. Ki Tavo: you didn't serve when you had everything. שכר מצוה מצוה. So the Torah promises no reward at all. (It does sharpen tzaddik vera lo.)
  3. Gan Eden is a physical place on earth, to be revealed in the future. Real, but one stage, not the ultimate.
  4. Gehinom: a name for punishment, some burning, undefined. Per the Ramban's reading, a temporary, proportionate stage before karet.
  5. Neither is a principle of faith; the Mishneh Torah hardly mentions them. Resurrection and Mashiach are.
  6. Other Rishonim: Gan Eden and Gehinom are spiritual waiting rooms before a body-and-soul Olam HaBa. The Rambam's Olam HaBa begins at death.
