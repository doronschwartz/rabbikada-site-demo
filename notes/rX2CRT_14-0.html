Topic: Which parts of Selihot may be said without a minyan

Sources: Gemara on the Thirteen Attributes; Parashat Ki Tisa; the Gemara on praying in Aramaic; Rav Ovadia Yoseph

Daily Halacha: Elul, Wednesday 7 September 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The core of Selihot is the Yud Gimel Middot, the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy. The Gemara says that a prayer with them, said with the intention of emulating them, never returns empty-handed.
  • The Thirteen Attributes have the status of a davar shebikdushah, like Kedushah or Kaddish, which may only be said with a minyan. So an individual does not say them as part of the Selihot prayer.
  • They are, however, verses of the Torah (Ki Tisa, after the golden calf). Just as the verse of Kedushah may be said when studying Nevi'im, the Thirteen Attributes may be said alone as if reading from the Torah, with the reading tune. The custom without a minyan is to skip them, but one who wishes may read them this way.
  • The Aramaic passages of the Sefaradi Selihot (such as Rahamana Idkar Lan and Mahei UMasei) are also questionable alone, since the Gemara says one should not pray in Aramaic when praying alone. Rav Ovadia Yoseph rules that these too should be left out without a minyan.
  • Everything else in Selihot may and should be said alone, including while waiting for a minyan.

In one line

Without a minyan, say all of Selihot except the Thirteen Attributes (unless read as Torah verses) and the Aramaic passages.
