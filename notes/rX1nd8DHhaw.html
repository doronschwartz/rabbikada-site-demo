Topic: What sets Tisha BeAv apart from the other fasts and from Yom Kippur

Sources: Shulhan Arukh; the Gemara; the poskim

Daily Halacha: The Three Weeks, Thursday 20 July 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Tisha BeAv is the most severe rabbinic fast. Unlike the others it lasts 24 hours, not dawn to nightfall; more than eating and drinking is forbidden; and pregnant and nursing women, exempt from other fasts according to the Shulhan Arukh, must fast, or at least begin.
  • More lenient than Yom Kippur: any holeh may eat, such as one with flu who is ill in the whole body and cannot function, even if not literally in bed. On Yom Kippur only one whose life may be at risk eats.
  • Stricter than Yom Kippur: besides its five prohibitions (eating and drinking, washing, anointing, marital relations, shoes), the Gemara forbids greeting others and Torah study.
  • Greeting is forbidden by halacha, not custom, all 24 hours, not just until midday. Yom Kippur is, in the Gemara, one of the two happiest days of the year; Tisha BeAv is sad, hence these two extra laws.
  • An ill person on Yom Kippur eats less than a shiur at intervals; on Tisha BeAv, according to custom and most poskim, one who is exempt may eat normal amounts, though small amounts let one still say Aneinu.
  • One exempt eats only what is needed, basic food, and no meat or wine unless medically needed.
  • A woman after childbirth is exempt for 30 days, against 7 for Yom Kippur.

Sources quoted

  • Shulhan Arukh: pregnant and nursing women are exempt from the other rabbinic fasts.
  • The Gemara: greeting and Torah study are forbidden on Tisha BeAv; Yom Kippur is among the happiest days.

In one line

Tisha BeAv has Yom Kippur's five restrictions plus greeting and Torah study, yet any genuinely ill person may eat normally.
