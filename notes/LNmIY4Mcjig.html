Topic: Must one reveal what one knows of a serious invalidation in someone's family?

Sources: The Gemara (mishpahah shenitmeah) and Gemara Kiddushin; the Ran (Rabbenu Nissim); the Maharshal (Rabbi Shelomoh Luria); the Rema; Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 27 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara's principle: once a family has mixed in (mishpahah shenitmeah), it stays mixed in. The Ran draws an important practical rule from it, codified in halacha.
  • All agree that where one merely has a doubt, one does not investigate. In a case he had, someone claimed a groom's grandmother may have been born before her mother received a get, which would make her a mamzeret and him a mamzer; since it was only doubtful and the family is presumed kasher, there is no investigation.
  • The Ran goes further, from the Gemara in Kiddushin: even one who knows for certain, individually, that someone is a mamzer need not reveal it, even when someone else is about to marry that person. He himself may not marry them, since for him the prohibition is known.
  • This fits Rav Moshe Feinstein's explanation: the prohibition is marrying someone one knows is forbidden.
  • This applies to any serious invalidation, mamzer, non-Jew or halal: one may keep quiet.
  • The Maharshal disagreed: only a doubt may be kept quiet; a certainty must be revealed. But the Rema and contemporary poskim rule like the Ran.
  • The Ran allows telling discreet people (senuim) so they know to keep away, but it should not be publicised.

In one line

Even one who knows of a mamzer in a family need not publicise it, though he himself may not marry into it.
