Topic: The principle that a family mixed into Am Yisrael is not investigated

Sources: The Gemara (mishpahah shenitmeah); Rav Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Thursday 12 March 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Gemara, codified in halacha: once a family has mixed into Am Yisrael (mishpahah shenitmeah), even if someone within it may be invalid, a mamzer, an eved or even not Jewish, if we do not know who, we leave it and do not investigate. There are many details and caveats.
  • Why not assume everyone is tainted? One reason: from the Torah a safek mamzer is permitted; it is the Hakhamim who forbade it, and they did not apply their prohibition once a family has mingled in. This has difficulties.
  • A second reason: bittul berov, nullification in the majority, as with non-kosher food mixed into kosher. One might object that each person is important and so not nullified, but that rule is rabbinic. This too has difficulties.
  • A third, along the lines of the Gemara: it is a kindness from HaShem, so that Am Yisrael can continue without investigating everyone.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein: the prohibition is to marry someone knowing they are forbidden, going against the will of HaShem. Without knowledge there is no liability, so the Hakhamim said do not investigate and let them merge into the community.
  • None of this applies when one knows, or where there are specific doubts about someone.

In one line

Once a family is mingled into Am Yisrael and no one knows who is invalid, we do not investigate, for several reasons including Rav Moshe Feinstein's.
