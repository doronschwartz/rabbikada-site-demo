Topic: The Misvah of remembering Amalek: how, how often, when, and whether women are obligated

Sources: The Torah, Parashat Ki Tese; the Gemara; Sefer HaHinukh

Daily Halacha: Purim, Thursday 18 February 2021

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • "Zakhor" appears 69 times in the Torah, including remembering Amalek, ancestor of Haman and the first nation to attack Israel after Egypt. Though Amalek's identity has been unknown for over two millennia, most authorities hold the Misvah to remember still applies from the Torah.
  • The Gemara says it must be done verbally, by reading the three verses in Parashat Ki Tese.
  • Some hold the Torah requires reading from a Sefer Torah; others that reading from a Humash or speaking about Amalek fulfils it, and hearing it from the Torah is rabbinic.
  • Sefer HaHinukh (an anonymous 14th-century work) suggests once a year or even every three years suffices. Most hold once a year, the custom today, since after 12 months one begins to forget.
  • It may be done any time; it is read the Shabbat before Purim because Amalek was Haman's ancestor. One who cannot hear it this Shabbat should read the passage from a Humash at home and hear it from a Sefer Torah later, ideally when Ki Tese is read (mid-August this year).
  • Women: some say they are obligated, as the Misvah is not time-bound. Sefer HaHinukh, followed by many, exempts them, since the remembrance relates to waging war, in which women did not go out.
  • Since this is a dispute, a woman who can go to hear it should, but those who do not have what to rely on.

Sources quoted

  • The Gemara: the remembrance must be verbal.
  • Sefer HaHinukh: once a year or even three may suffice; women are exempt.

In one line

Hear Zakhor from a Sefer Torah once a year, or read it from a Humash and hear it later; women should hear it if they can.
