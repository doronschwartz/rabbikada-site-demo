Topic: Who separates Hallah, the wording of the berakhah, and how much to take

Sources: The Rambam; Shulhan Arukh; the Rama; the Kaf HaHayyim (Rav Yaakov Hayyim Sofer); Hazon Ovadiah; the Hida; the Arizal; the Ben Ish Hai

Daily Halacha: Hallah, Tuesday 5 July 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Hallah is not only for women. It became traditional for women, like Shabbat candles, but a man who kneads dough separates it with the berakhah, just as a single man or one whose wife is away lights candles.
  • The Rambam's berakhah is "lehafrish Hallah". Some Rishonim objected that the dough itself is called hallah, so it should be "lehafrish terumah"; the Shulhan Arukh rules so, against the Rambam.
  • The Rama adds that "lehafrish Hallah" is also fine; later Ashkenazi poskim prefer "terumah" as less ambiguous. Ashkenazim say either.
  • The Kaf HaHayyim and Hazon Ovadiah combine them: "lehafrish Hallah terumah", the general Sepharadi custom today.
  • Amount: the Rambam says the Torah requires any amount; the Hakhamim required one twenty-fourth of the dough.
  • Since today the Hallah is burnt, not given to the Kohen, the Shulhan Arukh says any amount suffices. The Hida records the custom of a tiny piece, and Hazon Ovadiah rules so.
  • The Arizal said to take one twenty-fourth still; the Ben Ish Hai suggests doing so once a year, perhaps before Yom Kippur, as a merit.
  • The Rama records the Ashkenazi custom of a kezayit (about 27 grams) whatever the size of the dough.

Sources quoted

  • Rambam: "lehafrish Hallah"; any amount from the Torah.
  • Shulhan Arukh: "lehafrish terumah"; any amount today.
  • Kaf HaHayyim and Hazon Ovadiah: "lehafrish Hallah terumah".
  • Ben Ish Hai: one twenty-fourth once a year.

In one line

Sepharadim say "lehafrish Hallah terumah" and separate a tiny piece; Ashkenazim say "Hallah" or "terumah" and take a kezayit.
