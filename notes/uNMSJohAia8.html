Topic: The custom of greenery and flowers on Shavuot and the Vilna Gaon's objection

Sources: The Maharil; the Rema; the Torah; the Mishnah; the Gemara (siftotav shoshanim); the Vilna Gaon; Rabbi Moshe Feinstein

Daily Halacha: Shavuot and Talmud Torah, Tuesday 23 May 2023

The halacha

  • The earliest source he knows is the Maharil (14th-century Germany): greenery in the synagogue, for the joy of the Hag. The Rema adds flowers in homes, to recall Matan Torah.
  • Reasons: the Torah forbade sheep to graze near Har Sinai, so there was greenery there; the Mishnah says trees are judged on Shavuot; and the Gemara on "siftotav shoshanim" says every word at Sinai filled the world with fragrance.
  • The Vilna Gaon, about 250 years ago, forbade trees in the synagogue as imitating the practices of non-Jews (uvhukotehem lo telekhu).
  • The majority view, codified in the halacha, is that the prohibition covers only practices with no logical reason, which may be rooted in idolatry. So Rabbi Moshe Feinstein permits doctors' gowns, and graduation gowns are likewise fine, since they have a clear purpose.
  • Despite Christmas trees, the Shavuot custom has clear reasons, so most poskim see no issue. The Vilna Gaon held any practice originating with non-Jews forbidden, even with a reason.
  • The custom has spread among Sepharadim and Ashkenazim, though many Ashkenazim, especially in Israel, do not put trees in the synagogue. The concern was trees; flowers at home pose no issue at all.
  • The halacha and accepted practice is that one may have them at home and in the Beit HaKnesset.

Sources quoted

In one line

Greenery and flowers on Shavuot are permitted and accepted, though followers of the Vilna Gaon avoid trees in the synagogue.
