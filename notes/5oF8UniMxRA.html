Topic: Why red wine is used on Seder night, and when white wine is preferable

Sources: The Gemara; Mishlei; the Midrash; Keter Shem Tov (Hakham Shem Tov Gagin); the poskim

Daily Halacha: Pesah, Tuesday 9 April 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Hakhamim in the Gemara derive from a pasuk in Mishlei that wine by default is red, so Kiddush on red wine is the better way (misvah min hamuvhar). If one prefers white wine, or it is more expensive, one may use it.
  • On Pesah the Hakhamim prefer red wine, recalling the Midrash that Pharaoh slaughtered Jewish children and bathed in their blood to cure his leprosy, and recalling the plague of blood.
  • One who gets migraines from red wine may use white wine or grape juice for Kiddush and the four cups.
  • Hakham Shem Tov Gagin, rabbi of the Spanish and Portuguese community about a century ago, records that in 1912, as a dayan in Cairo, he and his colleagues baked massot after hasot on Erev Pesah, an old custom, as well as Massah Ashirah, made with fruit juice or wine, which does not become hames even after 18 minutes.
  • A man from Damascus hid his wine in his coat so that non-Jewish workers would not touch it and make it forbidden. The workers saw him pouring a red liquid into the dough and called the police, claiming it was children's blood. Under interrogation it was shown to be wine, and they still baked their massot with Hallel and thanks to HaKadosh Barukh Hu.
  • The blood libel persisted for centuries, in the Middle East (Damascus and Aleppo) as well as Europe, despite the Torah's repeated prohibition of eating blood.
  • The poskim before him already wrote that where there are blood libels one uses white wine.

Sources quoted

  • Mishlei: wine by default is red.

In one line

Use red wine at the Seder unless it is hard to drink, or there is a risk of a blood libel, when white wine is used.
