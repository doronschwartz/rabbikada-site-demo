Topic: Eating and drinking on waking before the fast begins

Sources: Gemara Taanit; the Yerushalmi as cited by the Rishonim; Rav Ovadia Yoseph; Shulhan Arukh; the Rema

Daily Halacha: Asarah BeTevet

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Asarah BeTevet is the shortest fast in the northern hemisphere. Rav Ovadia Yoseph takes dawn as 72 minutes before sunrise, so in London it begins around 6:45.
  • The Gemara in Taanit rules that once a person goes to sleep, he has accepted the fast, even if he wakes before dawn. So he may not eat or drink.
  • The Rishonim, from the Yerushalmi, explain that this applies only if he did not stipulate. If before going to sleep he says that he does not intend the fast to begin until dawn, he may eat and drink on waking.
  • So it is a good idea, the night before, to make this stipulation.
  • Without it: Shulhan Arukh forbids eating and drinking; the Rema, for Ashkenazim, forbids eating but allows drinking.
  • The ban on eating before Shaharit does not apply before dawn, since there is no obligation to pray yet; one may have cake, a drink or whatever one wants.

In one line

Stipulate before going to sleep and you may eat and drink until dawn; otherwise Sefaradim may not, and Ashkenazim may only drink.
