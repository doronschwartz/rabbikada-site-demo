The halacha

  • Tu BiShvat, the 15th of Shevat (Tet is 9, Vav is 6), is not mainly about eating fruit but a purely halachic date with three ramifications, two still relevant today.
  • Orlah: the fruit of a newly planted tree is forbidden for the first three years, permitted only in Yerushalayim in the fourth, and freely from the fifth. Tu BiShvat starts a new year, so a tree planted three and a half years ago (in the summer) may be eaten from on Tu BiShvat.
  • Tithes, still taken in Israel: produce grown before Tu BiShvat may not be used to tithe produce grown after it, or vice versa.
  • Bikkurim, the first fruits of the seven species brought to the Temple: likewise one year's fruit may not be brought for another, with Tu BiShvat the dividing date. This no longer applies without the Temple.
  • Since this is irrelevant for most Jews, the custom of eating fruit evolved over the last three to four centuries. The first mention he knows is in the Magen Avraham, an Ashkenazi codifier of about 400 years ago. The number 15 came much later and is not important.
  • The Talmud says a person is held accountable for produce he could have eaten and did not, since eating God's food is an appreciation of HaKadosh Barukh Hu, so this is a fitting day for fruit.
  • It is a nice custom, not halachically required; there is no need to hunt for exotic fruits. It should be done in balance.

In one line

Tu BiShvat is the new year for trees for Orlah, tithes and Bikkurim, and eating fruit on it is a nice custom, not a requirement.
