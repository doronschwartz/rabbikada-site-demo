Class Notes — Rabbi Daniel Kada

Sefer: HaMaspik LeOvdei HaShem (Rabbenu Avraham ben HaRambam), Chapter 10

Chapter: Perek Yud (10) — Perishut, abstinence

Topic: Fasting is a useful preparation (hekhsher) for perishut, never a goal in itself, and only with conditions. The same rule applies to every outward abstinence. How this fits the Rambam's golden mean in the Shemonah Perakim, and the two great obstacles to perishut: leadership and family.

When: Monday 16 November 2026, week of Parashat Vayetse 5787, on Zoom.

Schedule note: The first part of the shiur was on the parashah: two ideas on Toledot and seven on Vayetse from Rabbenu Avraham's commentary on the Torah. They have their own episode and notes in the series Rabbenu Avraham ben HaRambam on the Parashah, and are only listed briefly below. The remaining 35 minutes were HaMaspik.

Part One — On the Parashah (in brief)

  • Toledot: Yaakov cooked lentils out of perishut, not poverty (against Ibn Ezra). Yishak named the well Esek because the dispute made him busy with something outside his spiritual routine.
  • Vayetse: (1) ויפגע means to reach; prayer is a meeting with Hashem. (2) Yaakov would tithe even bread and clothes, plus the Midrash that Levi was the tithe of his sons. (3) וישא יעקב רגליו: the will carries the legs. (4) Seven years felt like a few days because his love for Rahel was spiritual. (5) Dinah, from דין. (6) דלקת: pursuit in anger, from fire. (7) Yaakov swore בפחד אביו יצחק to stay clear of Lavan's idolatrous oath.

Part Two — Where We Are in Chapter 10

A quick recap before continuing:

  • Perishut is an important trait, and it is primarily in the heart: not what you do, but what you think.
  • It is reached intellectually, by recognising that the most important thing in a person is his intellect and that the real world is not the physical one;
  • and practically, by slowly, gradually weaning yourself off unnecessary luxuries. "Don't go too fast."
  • Last week: Yishak's blessings, which seemed to contradict this, and Rabbenu Avraham's explanation.

Now Rabbenu Avraham gives more practical examples, starting with fasting.

Part Three — Fasting

A preparation, not a goal

ואשר לצום, הרי הוא כהכשר חשוב לפרישות. Fasting can be an important hekhsher, a preparation for perishut. "Notice: it's a preparation. It's not a means in itself."

רק בתנאים מיוחדים, only with certain conditions.

The conditions

1. Not when it is harmful. שלא יצומו בימות הקיץ, משום חום מזיק לגשם ולחולי. Don't fast in the heat of summer, when it damages the body. On this the Gemara says כל היושב בתענית נקרא חוטא: whoever fasts at the wrong time is called a sinner.

2. No feasting before and after. שלא תאכל אכילה גסה לפני הצום אשר תבוא לידי כבדות, ובמשך הצום תשב ותחשוב על מיני מאכלים שתפסיק בהם את צומך. This is the way of many, and of them it is said יצא שכרו בהפסדו.

"How many times do we do this?" A huge meal the night before, so you sleep badly and wake with a stomach ache. All day you think about the evening's food. Then another huge meal. Whatever you gained from the fast, you lost before and after it.

3. Never on its own. אל תסמוך עליה בלבד אם חסרה לך ההכשרות השנייה. The outward preparation is worthless without the inner one: "Most important is the internal feeling."

What to do with the day

אלא צרף עליה את העיסוק הרב והתבוננות... בקריאת התורה, ספרי הנביאים, תולדות החסידים.

  • Torah and the Prophets.
  • Stories of the righteous. The Rabbi had not seen a Rishon say this before: there is value in reading and telling the lives of great people, as in the Hasidic custom of telling stories of saddikim on Mosaei Shabbat.
  • Understand what you read. תן דעתך על משמעות הדברים שאתה קורא. In the weeks of Shovavim (the winter weeks from Shemot, most pronounced in a leap year) many fast on Mondays and Thursdays and read the whole of Tehillim three times in a day. "That's an amazing thing. But what's the point if you don't understand what you're reading? Better to read less Tehillim than lots of Tehillim not understanding."
  • Prayer and service, with a settled mind and fear of Heaven. "Our mandatory fasts, apart from Yom Kippur: we spend the day sleeping, reading, wasting our time. What's the point?"

The reward

Done this way, the outward preparation supports the inner one: כי יפתחו לך שערי רחמים, ויזרו עליך אורות החכמה, ויתגלו לך צפונות חסדי אלוקים.

The Rabbi's practical view: "Nowadays I don't know how much we encourage fasting. Even the six mandatory fasts are difficult for people, and they don't use the time well." Unless you are confident you can fast and use the day well, it is not worthwhile. If you can, it is wonderful, but it is only a hekhsher.

Three dangers

והדבר טעון זהירות מרובה, as Shelomo said: שמור רגלך כאשר תלך אל בית האלקים. (The Gemara in Berakhot learns from this verse to relieve oneself before praying.) Rabbenu Avraham reads it: guard your feet on the spiritual path; don't jump, don't go too fast. There are three pitfalls:

Part Four — The Same Rule for Every Abstinence

ודע כי זו היא דוגמה לשאר כל מה שמסוג זה. Fasting is only the example. Apply the same rules (slowly, only when ready, watching for physical, emotional and spiritual harm) to everything:

  • Clothing: from silk and brocade to plain cotton and coarse wool.
  • Marital life: reducing physical pleasure. "Great, don't do it too fast."
  • Home: from palaces with gilded ceilings to a modest house that only keeps out heat, cold and sun, כגון בית אלישע. Elisha's little upper room was a great thing in his eyes: הנה חרדת אלינו את כל החרדה הזאת.
  • Even living in caves and deserts: if it comes to that, go slowly.

ולא נצטרך להאריך בבירורם: "I expect you to extrapolate for each one."

Part Five — Is This Against the Rambam?

The Rambam's golden mean

Three years ago we learned the Shemonah Perakim. In Chapter 4 the Rambam teaches the middle path, and writes of the hasidim who went to an extreme:

מה שעשו אותם החסידים בקצת הזמנים ובקצת המקומות... מנטות אחר קצה אחד: fasting, rising at night, abstaining from meat and wine and from their wives, wearing wool and hair, living in mountains and deserts. לא עשו דבר זה אלא לרפואה: only as a cure. Like bending a crooked stick the other way so it ends up straight: for a limited time only.

וכאשר ראו הכסילים שהחסידים עשו אלה הפעולות ולא ידעו כוונתם, חשבו שהטובות כיוונו אליהם. Fools saw this and copied it, afflicting their bodies (rolling in snow, letting bees sting them, practices found in some medieval works), כאילו השם יתברך שונא הגוף של עבדיו, as if Hashem hated the bodies of His servants.

The question

Rabbenu Avraham knew the Shemonah Perakim inside out; he quotes it in the next chapter. Yet here he presents fasting, simple clothes and simple homes as the way, and not just for a limited time. Is the son arguing with his father?

Mark's suggestion: the Rambam speaks of ordinary people, Rabbenu Avraham of very elevated people acting for purely spiritual reasons. "Okay, it's a start."

The Rabbi's answer

Rabbenu Avraham says again and again that all these things are only outward preparations. The Rambam attacks people who think there is intrinsic value in fasting and self-affliction. Rabbenu Avraham doesn't believe that either.

"There's nothing intrinsically good about fasting, or living in the desert." As Rabbenu Avraham told us a few weeks ago, a wealthy man with the nicest home can be on the level of perishut, if acquiring things doesn't move him. The outward practices are sometimes how you get there. "They work hand in hand. The Rambam would agree with Rabbenu Avraham, and Rabbenu Avraham with the Rambam." And to Mark's point: the simple person does it for its own sake; the real person knows it is for something else.

Part Six — The Obstacles: Leadership and Family

עיכובים בדרך הפרישות. So far we discussed obstacles within you, habits you work on. Some obstacles come from outside, and you cannot always control them: בין קבוצה גדולה... בדיני העיר או בפרנסת הציבור, ובין קבוצה קטנה כגון בני ביתו של אדם.

Leading a community

It depends on scale: אינו דומה ראש כפר בן עשר נפשות למי שכרך בן אלפי אדם, nor a man with a wife and a few children to one with many children, grandchildren, servants and relatives.

A leader cannot easily reach perishut: כי הזמן קצר הוא מאוד, his time goes on the public, וגם הלב נטרד, and his heart is distracted. היחתה איש אש בחיקו ובגדיו לא תשרפנה? Can you carry fire and not be burned? "It's possible, but it's very hard." (As we learned earlier, Rabbenu Avraham advises a livelihood with as little contact with people as necessary.)

What about King David?

ואל תביא לי ראיה מדוד. David was a king and still practised perishut, because:

  1. He was trained in perishut before he became king: when Shmuel came, David was out in the fields with the sheep.
  2. He was doing the will of Hashem, and שלוחי מצוה אינם ניזוקין: Hashem guards those on His mission. "When you're doing something for HaKadosh Barukh Hu, you get hashgahah."
  3. Even as king he said: אם אבוא באהל ביתי, אם אתן שנת לעיני... עד אמצא מקום לה': how can I rest in my house while the Beit HaMikdash is not built?

But David was unique. In general: מי ששואף למידת הפרישות חייב להתרחק מן השררה. Avoid leadership as far as you can, and if appointed, step back when religion will not suffer, as the Bnei Betera stepped down because Hillel could lead.

The Rabbi's caution: Rabbenu Avraham was himself a leader, head of the community and its representative to the government. "Like everything we learn, take it in balance: how much good am I doing by leading, against how much I'd gain working on myself?"

Family

חייב להמעיט בבני לוויה לאט לאט: gradually reduce your circle, first servants and attendants, then wider relatives, until you keep only those you are obliged to look after: your wife, young children, and elderly parents.

"Nowadays we measure importance by how many connections someone has on Facebook or LinkedIn. Rabbenu Avraham says the exact opposite: the fewer, the better."

But balance is essential. ואם יזנח אותם, הרי הוא בא לידי הזנחת חובה וביטול מצוה, וזה חמור. Neglecting them is abandoning a misvah, which is worse than the gain. As in Chapter 1: you cannot be a hasid before you are a saddik. "We all know ascetic people whose children grew up by themselves." Remember last week: Yishak went to meditate at Beer Lahai Roi only after Avraham died, because while his father lived he looked after him.

So: look after them, train them to be content with necessities, ask Hashem for help (as in Chapter 8, on Bitahon), work as usual, and הבא ליטהר מסייעין אותו.

Before marriage

חייב מי שבוחר ללכת בדרך הנשגבה הזאת... לפני שיהיה מטופל באישה וילדים. Build perishut before marriage:

  • The Gemara in Kiddushin: רחיים בצווארו ויעסוק בתורה? With a millstone round his neck, can he study Torah?
  • Ben Azzai: נפשי חשקה בתורה, and he did not marry.
  • Yaakov married at 84.
  • Eliyahu and Elisha never married.

The Rabbi: "I don't think he's condoning being a bachelor." In his commentary on the Torah Rabbenu Avraham writes several times that the Avot married late to grow spiritually first. Balance it against the risk of sin when unmarried, "like everything in life."

Summary

  1. Fasting is a hekhsher: a preparation for perishut, never the goal.
  2. Conditions: not when harmful; no feasting before or after; not thinking about food all day; always with the inner work.
  3. Fill the day with Torah, the Prophets, stories of the righteous, prayer, and understanding what you read.
  4. Three dangers: physical, emotional, spiritual (arrogance).
  5. The same rules apply to clothes, marital life and homes: slowly, and only when ready.
  6. Rambam and Rabbenu Avraham agree: outward abstinence has no value in itself; it is only a means to inner perishut.
  7. Leadership and family are the great external obstacles. Avoid leadership where possible; keep the ties you are obliged to; never neglect obligations; build perishut before marriage.
