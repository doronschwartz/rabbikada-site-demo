Topic: The continuing struggle after 165 BCE, from Yehudah HaMakkabi to Yannai and Shelomsion

Sources: The Talmud

Daily Halacha: Hanukkah

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The points

  • The miracle of the oil was in 165 BCE, but the struggle with the Greeks continued for at least another century.
  • The Greeks reconquered Yerushalayim and installed Hellenised Kohanim, but to avoid tension they allowed the misvot to be kept.
  • In Adar 161 BCE Yehudah HaMakkabi defeated and killed Nicanor; a huge Greek army retaliated and Yehudah was killed in battle.
  • His brother Yonatan reorganised the army; his pressure won the Hashmonaim autonomy over Yerushalayim and Judea, and in 152 BCE he deposed the Hellenised priests and installed a good Kohen Gadol. About ten years later he was murdered by the Greeks.
  • Shimon, the next brother, took over and was killed in 135 BCE by King Talmai. Shimon's son Yohanan Hyrcanus fought back, kept Yerushalayim and the Bet HaMikdash, became Kohen Gadol and strengthened the Sanhedrin; the Talmud says that at the end of his life he joined the Seddukim.
  • The Seddukim were a new threat: loyal to the written Torah and to Greek culture, they rejected the Oral Law.
  • The tragedy: Matityahu's great-grandson Yannai, Kohen Gadol and ruler of Judea, sided with them, persecuted the Hakhamim and willed that they be killed after his death. His wife Shelomsion prevented it and is remembered as a good queen; a Jerusalem street bears her name.

In one line

The victory of 165 BCE was not the end: the Hashmonaim fought on for decades, and the Hellenist threat returned from within through the Seddukim.
