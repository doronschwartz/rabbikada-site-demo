Topic: The essence of Rosh Hashanah: crowning Hashem, the two modes of judgment, and why we do not confess or ask for our own needs Sources: Bava Kamma 50a; Rosh Hashanah 17a; the Ramhal, Derech Hashem; the Zohar Shiur: Given by Dayan Daniel Kada, in person and on Zoom, Tuesday 12 September 2023

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

Coming to Rosh Hashanah stressed: two questions

This was the first of two shiurim before the Yamim Noraim, with Yom Kippur the following week. People come to Rosh Hashanah very stressed and anxious, the Rabbi said, with a whole list of things to pray for, and they miss the main point. If we understand this shiur and apply it, "it will completely transform our Rosh Hashanah."

  • Why no mention of sin? In all the prayers of both days, Shaharit, Musaf and Minhah, we never confess or ask forgiveness. The closest is the Arizal's teaching to confess in your mind during the shofar, which was debated and which many disagreed with.
  • Why judgment before atonement? It would seem to make more sense to have Yom Kippur first, be forgiven, and then come to court.

Then two Gemaras that seem to clash: - "Whoever says HaKadosh Barukh Hu is a vatran (one who overlooks), his life will be overlooked." You cannot say, "I will sin and Hashem will forgive me." - "Whoever overlooks wrongs done to him, all his sins are overlooked." This is the "maavir rishon rishon" we say in El Melekh.

People look for all kinds of segulot before the Yamim Noraim: travelling to a particular rabbi for a blessing, or to Uman. They overlook the segulah the hakhamim gave us in the Gemara itself: forgive others.

Two modes: Mishpat and Yihud

The key, he said, is the Ramhal in Derech Hashem: Hashem runs the world in two modes.

Hanhagat HaMishpat is consequences. He said he does not like the phrase "reward and punishment"; what Judaism teaches is consequences, like gravity making something fall. When a person does good, as defined by the Torah, he becomes better and closer to Hashem, and enjoys the Divine Presence more. A person who does not becomes too different from Hashem to connect. His analogy was a personal lunch with the late Queen. You would prepare and learn the royal etiquette, and the better prepared you are, the closer to her you sit. The one who did not prepare sits further back: not a punishment, simply the result.

Within Mishpat, Hashem's hands are, as it were, tied. A loving parent who makes house rules has to keep them, or they become a mockery.

Hanhagat HaYihud is the override button. After the golden calf, Hashem told Moshe there would be times He would be gracious and merciful, even to the undeserving. The parent says, "It's Pesah night. I made the rules, and tonight I'm suspending them." He also compared it to a presidential pardon. If the president pardoned everyone, the justice system would be a mockery; but in rare, extenuating cases he uses his full authority. That is Yihud: Hashem's complete sovereignty, not bound by anything.

The shofar as a coronation

Saadiah Gaon gave ten reasons for the shofar, but the central one, from Tehillim and the verses we say before blowing, is coronation. The Rabbi pointed to the coronation of King Charles that year, much of it drawn from Tanakh: "Zadok the Priest" comes straight from the anointing of Shelomoh, and the trumpet blast announces the monarch.

Why does coronation matter? Because recognising Hashem as King moves Him "from the throne of judgment to the throne of mercy." What is rahamim? If you have done teshuvah, you are atoned and do not need mercy. If you have not, why should you get it? Rahamim is precisely the switch from Mishpat to Yihud. When we truly recognise that there is nothing in the world except Hashem's will, "that is the greatest segulah" for suspending judgment.

Why we do not confess on Rosh Hashanah

  • Confessing means entering the mode of din, the details of what we did and did not do.
  • More important in his eyes: confession is introspection, and it makes Rosh Hashanah about yourself. "Rosh Hashanah is not about you."

Someone objected that we may not succeed. Exactly, he said, so we "hedge our bets." Rosh Hashanah is the focus, and then the rest of the ten days and Yom Kippur work through Mishpat. Yom Kippur does not work without teshuvah: you can fast and pray all day, but without a thought of teshuvah you have not done Yom Kippur.

Two ways to reach Yihud

  • Forgive others. The thirteen attributes of mercy are not a magic recital. We say them to learn to behave that way. When you go beyond the letter of the law and overlook what was done to you, even when you have the right to pursue it, Hashem says, "You go beyond the letter of the law, I'll go beyond the letter of the law as well." Asked whether this means beforehand or on the day itself, he said he could not give a user's guide, and that it is not binary: people are forgiving in some things and not others.
  • Make Hashem King through the tefillah. Malkhuyot, Zikhronot, Shofarot and the third berakhah. This is why the Zohar says that whoever prays on Rosh Hashanah for children, health and livelihood is like a dog barking at its master: "bark, bark, I need this, I need that." As the Tur and the Shulhan Arukh describe it, we come to Rosh Hashanah dressed in white, calm and confident.

Unity hidden and revealed The third berakhah is universal: humanity should recognise Hashem. Today the world looks like a duality: the righteous suffer, the wicked prosper. Yihud says it is all one, and one day the righteous will see it and evil will vanish like smoke. If we truly believed in Yihud we would never sin, because sin is the illusion that something outside Hashem's will is worth having. That is why we cover our eyes for Shema: we believe it but cannot yet see it.

Asked about the righteous suffering, he agreed that explanations such as the wicked using up their reward in this world are true, but said that on the surface the world still looks divided, because we live in a time of concealment. On Rosh Hashanah we pray for it to end.

At the end of Yom Kippur everyone says "Hashem Hu HaElokim" seven times: Hashem is mercy, Elokim is judgment, and they are the same One. Sefaradim then add one verse: "Karati bekhol lev, aneni Hashem, hukkekha essorah." Once you have truly recognised Yihud Hashem, you will keep His laws.

Why the requests were added, and questions

The requests in the mahzor, Avinu Malkenu included, were later additions from the time of the Geonim, because most people cannot relate to these lofty ideas. The Hida noticed that nobody answers amen loudly to the requests about redemption, but when it comes to healing and livelihood, everyone shouts. The risk is missing the message, but he called it a means to an end: better that than the whole day going over people's heads.

Questions - Judged on the coming year? Someone suggested Rosh Hashanah comes first because Hashem judges us on our intentions for the coming year. He agreed: it is the same idea, since living with Yihud Hashem is the best preparation for the year ahead. - No shofar on Shabbat? If the shofar is the essence, why not blow when Rosh Hashanah falls on Shabbat, as that year? He called it an excellent question. It shows how concerned the hakhamim were not to go against Hashem's will even by accident, which is itself Yihud Hashem. But they recognised the cost: the Gemara says a year in which the shofar is not blown at its beginning is at risk at its end.

Summary

  • Rosh Hashanah is about crowning Hashem, not about our own needs or our sins; those details belong to the ten days and Yom Kippur.
  • Hashem usually runs the world by Mishpat, consequences, but can override it through Yihud, His complete sovereignty, which is what rahamim means.
  • The shofar is a coronation that moves Hashem from the throne of judgment to the throne of mercy.
  • Two ways to reach Yihud: overlook the wrongs done to us, and focus the tefillah on Hashem's kingship.
  • Come to Rosh Hashanah relaxed, thinking about what we can do for Hashem rather than what He can do for us.
