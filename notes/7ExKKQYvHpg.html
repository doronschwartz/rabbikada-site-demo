Recap and Seif 14: no need to appoint the agent in front of witnesses

The Rabbi began with a short recap of the previous shiur: the shaliah holakhah (who carries the get from the husband to the wife) and the shaliah kabbalah (who receives it for her), and the question of whether the husband's agent must be appointed in front of witnesses. For the shaliah kabbalah the answer is clearly yes. For the shaliah holakhah the Rambam says no, because the get itself is in his hand. The Rosh says yes, based on the Yerushalmi. The Shulhan Arukh brings both views in different places.

Seif 14 says that the shaliah holakhah does not need to be appointed in front of witnesses. The Rabbi stressed a practical result. Appointing the agent in front of witnesses is enough even if the agent is somewhere else and does not hear it at the time. The agent can be told afterwards, provided he agrees. So if the wife is abroad and the husband must leave, the husband can appoint someone who is present and the get can be handed to her when she returns.

Seif 15: "write" is not the same as "give"

It is not enough for the husband to say "write a get". The appointment must include giving it to her. Usually this is explicit, or clear from the context. This is why our text always says that the agent should give the get to the wife wherever she may be. If the agent gave the get without the instruction to give, it does not work.

Seif 16 and the suicide case

There are circumstances where we read the instruction to give into the husband's words even though he only said "write". The Shulhan Arukh lists a husband who suddenly fell ill, a man going to prison, a man going on a journey, or a man setting off with a caravan or on a ship. In such cases he is in a precarious situation, and we assume he meant "write and give" but had no chance to say so.

The next case is a husband who said "write a get for my wife" and then took his own life immediately. The Beit Yosef and the Beit Shemuel explain that "immediately" means within the time it takes to write the get, which the Rabbi estimated today at a few hours. If it happened that quickly we assume it was deliberate, and therefore that he meant the get to be given. But if he only died by accident, such as a fall, the get does not work, because he never told them to give it.

A student observed that today we assume a person who takes his own life was not of sound mind, and asked what case the Shulhan Arukh then has in mind. The Rabbi agreed that this is a real question and that it may reflect a different understanding of mental health.

Seif 17: "Whoever hears my voice, write a get"

A husband in great danger says, "Anyone who hears my voice, write a get for my wife and give it to her." They give it to her even though he did not say to give it, and even though no witness recognised him; he only said, "This is my name and this is hers."

Normally the witnesses must recognise the husband and wife, which is why we ask for identification today. Here we rely on him because he is in danger, and we do not worry that a rival wife imitated his voice. The Rishonim say that the danger removes the need for recognition, though some still require some proof that the speaker is a person and not a spirit. The Rambam does not mention this, and the Shulhan Arukh follows the Rambam, though he also brings those who require it.

Seif 18: three people, or "all of you"

If the husband says to three people, "Write a get for my wife," each has a task: one writes and two sign. It is not necessary that all three do the same thing. If he said "all of you take it" to deliver the get, he meant one of them. If he said "two of you", he wanted two agents.

A father and son may act together as agents, though not as witnesses together. If he said "all of you must take it", each one must, and otherwise it is a doubtful get.

Nowadays we appoint the scribe clearly, and we appoint the agent clearly to give the get.

The harshaah

The Shulhan Arukh turns to the shtar harshaah, the document in which the agent is authorised. Even according to the Rosh, an agent who comes with a valid get and says "I am the husband's agent" needs no further proof. We write the harshaah today for another reason: it gives the agent full authority to appoint further agents without anyone having to be ill or under duress. Without it, an agent can appoint another agent only in such circumstances. With it, the get can pass through as many agents as needed, to another continent for example, without any difficulty.

Several details followed. An agent who holds the harshaah is trusted to appoint another agent even if the harshaah has not been confirmed. The text still says he may appoint another, as a reminder to him. We use the same witnesses for the get and the harshaah, because otherwise it becomes cumbersome.

If the witnesses of the harshaah turn out to be invalid, the agency falls according to many authorities. The Rabbi asked the obvious question: if the agency does not need witnesses, why does it fall? The Darkhei Moshe suggests that it casts doubt on how the get was arranged. The Get Mekushar says that once the get has left the husband's hand, it must be returned to him before he can appoint another agent.

Confirming the harshaah and the names

The Beit Din confirms the harshaah (kiyum) as they do for other documents. Strictly, recognising the signatures is enough, but we do this as a Rabbinic requirement. We also send the confirmation along with the get. The Rema adds that when there is a difference in the names, the custom is to write where it came from. The Rabbi said this is very relevant when a get is sent to another country: we include an explanation of how we arrived at the names that appear, almost like a ruling, so that the receiving Beit Din does not have questions.

Must the agent be an adult?

The Darkhei Moshe asks why the agent must be a gadol (an adult), when a husband who is a minor does not give a get anyway. The answer is that there are cases where a minor husband is still obliged to divorce, for example if he was alone with her after appointing the agent. The Rema therefore says we make sure that the agent is an adult, though this is a stringency. If someone looks very young, check that he is bar mitzvah, unless he clearly has a beard. For the witnesses to the get we must always check.

The Shulhan Arukh also notes the custom of a kinyan to appoint the agent. The Rabbi said that the Rishonim clearly do not require it. We do not do a kinyan in London, but only have the agent say the text in the seder get.

The shaliah kabbalah

A shaliah kabbalah is not used today. If one were used, his signature must be recognised in the place where the get is given, because he arrives with no get in hand, only the harshaah, so confirmation of the signatures is required. This is one reason why we stopped using them.

If the age of the woman's agent is in doubt, we rely on the signs of adulthood, and the Shulhan Arukh rules that this is needed.

The procedure for giving the get to a shaliah kabbalah: the Beit Din confirms the harshaah, the get is read and returned, the witnesses are told that the get is cancelled in the usual way, and it is handed to the agent.

Conditions with a shaliah kabbalah

Rabbenu Peretz says that a condition should not be added when using a shaliah kabbalah. The Beit Yosef asks why, since a condition works even if said out loud. His answer is that perhaps the witnesses will not tell the woman about it. With a shaliah holakhah, it is not a problem. The Rabbi noted that today we avoid conditions anyway, as we will see in Siman 143.

When a shaliah kabbalah is still used

The Beit Shemuel and the Taz say that if there is a real need the shaliah kabbalah is not ruled out. The Bach gives the case of a husband who is sometimes sane and sometimes not. By the time an agent reaches her he might again be of unsound mind, in which case the get does not work. So she appoints an agent near the husband, and he can hand it over at the moment the husband is sane.

The wording of the harshaah

The old text allowed the agent to give the get to the wife or to her agent. We no longer include that phrase, though it shows what was originally intended. A later line that lets the shaliah kabbalah appoint another is clearly a scribal error and must be erased, because a shaliah kabbalah cannot appoint another agent: unlike the shaliah holakhah, he has no get in hand. Nor do we write kinuyim (alternative names) in the harshaah, only the name as it appears in the get.

Practical summary

  • The shaliah holakhah does not need to be appointed in front of witnesses, but today we appoint both the scribe and the agent clearly in front of the Beit Din.
  • An instruction to "write" is not an instruction to "give". Only in urgent circumstances, such as illness, prison, a journey or immediate suicide, do we assume he meant both.
  • We always prepare a shtar harshaah, which lets the agent appoint further agents without illness or duress, and the Beit Din confirms it (kiyum).
  • The agent and the witnesses must be adults, and we check this.
  • When a get is sent abroad, include an explanation of the names and any possible questions.
  • We do not use a shaliah kabbalah today, and the harshaah text for the shaliah holakhah should not allow one.
