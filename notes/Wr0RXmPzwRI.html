Topic: Is there a limit to the misvah of continuing to have children?

Sources: Contemporary teshuvot (Rav Yoseph Messas and others); the Arukh HaShulhan; Rav Yehuda Herzl Henkin (Benei Vanim); Rav Eliezer Melamed

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Friday 30 January 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • Where there is physical or mental illness, it overrides even the Torah misvah; the question is for a healthy couple.
  • This question appears only in teshuvot of the last 50 to 100 years. One great posek of Israel of the last century told a man of 44 with eight children, whose wife wanted more, to continue: no limit. Rav Yoseph Messas told a man with four children, unsure of his livelihood, likewise to continue.
  • Another approach lets finances count: the Arukh HaShulhan writes that the misvah applies if he can support them.
  • Rav Henkin (Benei Vanim) argues there is a limit: as the Torah misvah is a boy and a girl, Hazal's addition is another boy and girl, so two boys and two girls fulfil it.
  • Another contemporary view: in the past, infant deaths and the spacing of births meant few families had more than five or six children, so the misvah is fulfilled with four or five; beyond that, if a couple have the means and peace of mind, they should continue.
  • The Rabbi adds that it is about quality as well as quantity: raising, educating and giving attention to each child. Only the parents can decide what is right for them.

In one line

Beyond a son and a daughter there is a misvah to have a couple more; after four or five, whether to continue depends on each couple's means and ability to raise them well.
