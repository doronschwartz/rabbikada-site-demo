Class Notes: Dayan Daniel Kada

Series: Rabbenu Avraham ben HaRambam on the Parashah: ideas from Rabbenu Avraham's commentary on the Torah (which survives on Bereshit and Shemot), taught each week at the Monday HaMaspik LeOvdei HaShem shiur.

Source: Taught at the HaMaspik shiur of the week of Vayishlah 5787, HaMaspik 10.6.

Topic: Six ideas on Vayishlah. The first and most striking: Yaakov's wrestling was a prophetic vision, reached through hitbodedut, and the limp the next morning was its effect on the body. Then the order of the camp, the children "graciously given", two places called Sukkot, Yaakov's two names, and the name Binyamin.

The Rabbi: "Some very interesting ideas this week's parsha. The most fascinating one, the most controversial one, is the episode at the beginning of the parsha."

1. ויותר יעקב לבדו: the wrestling was a vision

Yaakov's family has crossed the river, ויותר יעקב לבדו, and Yaakov is left alone. ויאבק איש עמו עד עלות השחר (32:25): a man wrestles with him until dawn and strikes his thigh. At sunrise Yaakov walks on, limping, and that is why we do not eat the Gid HaNasheh, the sciatic nerve. What was happening there?

Why was he alone? Rabbenu Avraham: אחר שהתבודד יעקב בלבדו התבודדות גשמית ולא נשארו אצלו עבדים וחפצים, נתעלה באותה התבודדות הגשמית התבודדות הרוחניות, אשר בסופה השיג השגת נבואה שנדמה לו כאילו יאבק איש עמו.

He had a wife and children, so why was he alone? Because he was practising hitbodedut, solitude: spending time alone, meditating and contemplating. This is the subject of the 13th and final chapter of HaMaspik. He was alone physically, with his family across the river, and then alone in his mind, התבודדות רוחנית. At that height he reached prophecy, and the wrestling, with the whole dialogue, was a prophetic vision.

That it never physically happened is his father's hiddush: the Rambam writes in the Moreh that it all happened in a vision at night. Rabbenu Avraham adds how Yaakov came to that vision: through hitbodedut.

Then why the limp? This is the Ramban's famous question on the Rambam. If it was only a vision, why does the verse say ויזרח לו השמש והוא צולע על ירכו? The Rambam does not address it. Rabbenu Avraham answers it:

ואל תתמה איך אירע זה במצב שאינו מציאות אלא בדמיון, שהרי האדם יראה בחלומות כאילו נוסע ויקץ עייף, כאילו היכוהו, ויקץ ברגש של כאב, לפי שהאיברים מתפעלים על ידי כח הדמיון. ואם יהיה כך על ידי השפעת החלום הרגיל, כל שכן שיהיה על ידי השפעת מראה הנבואה.

After a bad dream you can wake up sweating, even with a lingering pain, though nothing happened physically. The כח הדמיון, the imaginative faculty, can affect the body. If an ordinary dream can do that, all the more so a prophetic vision. The sun rose, the vision ended, and Yaakov limped from its aftereffects.

Three things in one comment: the vision (from the Rambam), how he reached it (hitbodedut), and why he limped (the power of the imagination). The Rabbi: "I think that's incredible."

2. The order of the camp: a procession

Esav comes with 400 men. וישם את השפחות ואת ילדיהן ראשונה ואת לאה וילדיה אחרונים ואת רחל ואת יוסף אחרונים (33:2): the maidservants and their children first, then Leah and her children, Rahel and Yoseph last.

  • Almost all the Rishonim (Rashi explicitly, the Radak, Ibn Ezra, Bekhor Shor): he was protecting those he loved most, אחרון אחרון חביב. Rahel was his favourite wife and Yoseph her child; Binyamin was not yet born.
  • Rabbenu Avraham: אין זה כדי להבריח את האחרון בהם אם יחמול עשיו ויהרוג את הראשון, שהרי הכל בניו ונשיו. They were all his wives and all his children. Rather: כך גוזר הסדר, שיכסה הפחות במעלה את הגדול במעלה ויתאחר היותר נכבד במעלה עד לבסוף, וזה מנהג נהוג עד עכשיו על פי הרוב למלכים וכיוצא בהם.

It is a procession. The lesser dignitaries walk in first and the most important last, as the kallah walks in last at a wedding (leaving the hatan aside, who walks in first). Yaakov is saying to Esav: I am making a procession in your honour.

3. הילדים אשר חנן אלהים את עבדך

Esav asks מי אלה לך: who are all these? Twenty-two years earlier Yaakov left with nothing. He answers only about the children: הילדים אשר חנן אלהים את עבדך. What about the women?

והשיב לו יעקב על הילדים והשתמט על הנשים, אף על פי שהיתה שאלה על הכל, לשני טעמים. האחד, מחמת ביישנות מלהזכיר את יחוס הנשים. והשני, שכאשר הזכיר את יחוס הילדים שהם בניו, נודע לעשו יחוס הנשים שהן נשיו.

  1. Modesty: you do not speak about your wives without good reason.
  2. It is obvious: once Esav hears these are my children, he can work out that their mothers are my wives.

Why חנן and not נתן? Rabbenu Avraham, quoting his father in the Moreh: חנינה means giving something to one who does not deserve it. Yaakov is saying: I do not deserve them, but HaKadosh Barukh Hu has given them to me. He lived recognising that everything, even his wives and children, was a gift.

4. Two places called Sukkot

After the meeting, ויעקב נסע סכתה: he built huts there and the place was called Sukkot. In Shemot, ויסעו בני ישראל מרעמסס סכתה, about 600,000 men.

Rabbenu Avraham: ואין זה סוכות אשר נסעו אליה ישראל ממצרים... אלא סוכות זו בארץ ישראל או קרוב אליה, ואותה סוכות היא על פני מצרים וגבוליה, ושם שתיהן סוכות. Yaakov's Sukkot, on the way from Padan Aram to Canaan, is perhaps further north; the Sukkot next to Ramses is near Egypt in the south. Two places with one name, unlike his grandfather, Rabbi Maimon, who took them as the same place.

A minor point, the Rabbi said, but he brought it to show that Rabbenu Avraham is "not afraid to argue with his grandfather."

5. לא יעקב יאמר עוד שמך: Yaakov and Yisrael

In Lekh Lekha, לא יקרא עוד שמך אברם, and Sarai becomes Sarah. Nobody is called Avram or Sarai today. Here the verse is almost identical, לא יעקב יאמר עוד שמך כי אם ישראל, yet people have been called Yaakov throughout the generations. Why?

  • Avraham: after the change, the Torah never again calls him Avram. So "no longer" means never again, and the Sages (Berakhot) say כל הקורא לאברהם אברם עובר בלאו. The meaning is כאילו עובר בלאו, as if it were a prohibition; not a literal one, but not the right thing to do.
  • Yaakov: the Torah keeps calling him Yaakov, ולא עוד אלא קורא אותו יעקב יותר מאשר קורא אותו ישראל, more often than Yisrael. So the verse means לא יקרא עוד שמך יעקב בלבד, אלא ישראל גם כן: no longer Yaakov alone, but Yisrael as well. His descendants are called בני ישראל, though they are the sons of Yaakov.

The Rabbi's addition, from Rabbi Sacks (he spoke about it in his synagogue that Shabbat): יעקב is from עקב, the heel, always on the back foot: born second, the blessings, running from Lavan. ישראל is כי שרית עם אלהים ועם אנשים ותוכל, standing up and winning.

With Rabbenu Avraham's reading, the lesson becomes a balance. You cannot go through life only working quietly and tactfully around people, and you cannot go through life only standing up to them. A wise person decides when to be Yaakov and when to be Yisrael. Hence both names, and even the name Yaakov Yisrael, common in the Ashkenazi world.

6. Binyamin: son of days

ויהי בצאת נפשה כי מתה ותקרא שמו בן אוני ואביו קרא לו בנימין. His mother called him Ben Oni, son of my pain; his father called him Binyamin, בן ימים, son of days (ימים can mean days or years).

  • His grandfather, Rabbi Maimon: כי יעקב כוון בזה כי קרב זמנו ונשארו מן הימים מעוטים. Yaakov was old and this was his youngest: I have only a few days, a few years, left to enjoy you.
  • Rabbenu Avraham: מה שאני רואה בזה... כי כוון בזה שהימים יגדלו אותו, אף על פי שחסרה אמו מגדלת אותו. Your mother is dying in childbirth and will not bring you up. The days, life's experience, will bring you up.

The Rabbi: "Next time you hear someone called Binyamin": either the few days left to enjoy you, or a child raised by the years.
