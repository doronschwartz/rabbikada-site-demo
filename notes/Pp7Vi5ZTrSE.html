Topic: Why Kohanim remove their shoes for Birkat Kohanim, and when one may be lenient

Sources: The Gemara (Rabban Yohanan ben Zakkai's enactment); Hakham Ovadiah Yoseph; Rav Hayyim Palagi

Daily Halacha: Birkat Kohanim & Prayer, Monday 20 June 2022

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • About 1,900 years ago Rabban Yohanan ben Zakkai enacted that Kohanim remove their shoes for Birkat Kohanim. The Gemara gives two reasons.
  • First, dirty shoes displayed on the dukhan before everyone are not respectful to the congregation (kevod hasibbur).
  • Second, a shoe strap might come undone and the Kohen might leave to fix it; people would suspect a flaw in his lineage, and the Hakhamim took yihus very seriously.
  • Some poskim discuss boots, but generally "lo plug": the enactment is comprehensive, applying to all footwear, with or without straps.
  • Hakham Ovadiah discusses one who finds it hard to remove his shoes, quoting Rav Hayyim Palagi: the Gemara speaks of going up to the dukhan in full view. A Kohen who blesses from floor level is less visible, so neither reason fully applies and he may keep his shoes on.
  • Hakham Ovadiah concludes that where it is difficult, for example an elderly Kohen, one may rely on this. Lekhathilah even a Kohen blessing from the floor removes his shoes.
  • Kohanim who remove their shoes should wear socks, as blessing barefoot is disrespectful. One without socks should keep his shoes on and bless from the floor.

In one line

Kohanim remove all footwear for Birkat Kohanim, but one who finds it difficult may keep his shoes on if he blesses from the floor.
