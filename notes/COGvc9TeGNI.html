Topic: Whether the travellers' exemption from sukkah covers leisure outings on Hol HaMoed

Sources: The Rambam; the Gemara; Shulhan Arukh; Rav Moshe Feinstein; Hazon Ovadiah; Gemara Sukkah

Daily Halacha: Sukkot, Tuesday 22 October 2024

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • The Rambam says on Hol HaMoed one should aim to give half the day to HaShem, in prayer and Torah, and half to eating, drinking and enjoyment, so outings are justified to an extent.
  • The Gemara and Shulhan Arukh exempt travellers from the sukkah while travelling; one who travels by day is exempt by day and obligated again at night.
  • Rav Moshe Feinstein and others hold this applies only to travel for a clear need, such as business or visiting someone, not pure leisure. On a leisure outing with no sukkah, one should not eat foods that require a sukkah, such as bread.
  • Others hold a traveller is a traveller once he has set out, even for leisure, and may eat sandwiches without a sukkah.
  • Hazon Ovadiah takes the stricter view with Rav Moshe Feinstein: it is better not to rely on the travellers' leniency for an outing, and to eat only foods exempt from the sukkah.
  • He ends his teshuvah with the Gemara in Sukkah praising those who stay home on the festival. Outings are fine, but constant travel should not crowd out the misvah of sukkah.

Sources quoted

  • Rav Moshe Feinstein and Hazon Ovadiah: leisure travellers should avoid bread outside a sukkah.
  • Gemara Sukkah: praise for those who stay home over the festival.

In one line

On a Hol HaMoed leisure outing without a sukkah it is better to avoid bread and eat only foods that need no sukkah.
