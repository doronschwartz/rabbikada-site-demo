Topic: Are Kohanim today certain Kohanim? The Gemara, the Rambam, and the Maharashdam's question

Sources: Gemara Kiddushin; Nehemiah; the Rambam; the Maharashdam (Rabbi Shemuel de Medina)

Daily Halacha: Even HaEzer, Wednesday 22 April 2026

These notes are based on an automatic transcript of the recording.

The halacha

  • A Kohen may marry a widow but not a divorcee. A Kohen who is divorced or widowed later in life often finds the only available women are divorcees. Are Kohanim today certain, making it a Torah prohibition, or doubtful, so other reasons for leniency might count?
  • The starting point is the Gemara in Kiddushin on the Kohanim descended from Barzilai HaGiladi, who took their wives' family name and, as the verse in Nehemiah says, had no records of lineage.
  • They could not serve in the Beit HaMikdash but were allowed to eat Terumah, which shows how strong a hazakah is: they had behaved as Kohanim. One opinion allows Torah Terumah, the other only rabbinic Terumah.
  • The Rambam rules that a Kohen meyuhas, with clear records, may serve in the Beit HaMikdash, while the Kohanim of his day are Kohanei hazakah, presumed Kohanim, who may eat only rabbinic Terumah.
  • The first to discuss the status properly was the Maharashdam, Rabbi Shemuel de Medina of Salonika in the 16th century, in a groundbreaking Teshuvah everyone refers back to.
  • Background: a woman taken captive may not marry a Kohen, rabbinically, lest her captor had relations with her, which forbids her to a Kohen for life. Being rabbinic, one witness that she was not defiled suffices.
  • He was asked about a witness who only heard from someone present that she was not defiled. His answer comes next time.

In one line

The Gemara and Rambam treat today's Kohanim as presumed, not certain, Kohanim, and the Maharashdam built a major ruling on this.
